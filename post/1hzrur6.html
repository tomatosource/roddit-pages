THE CASE FOR TYLER HERRO

It isn’t difficult to make an All-Star case for Tyler Herro. He’s done his job when it comes to making the task as simple as possible.

There are only seven players in the league this season posting a true-shooting percentage of .610 or better with at least a .240 assist percentage and .250 usage rate. Three of those players are leading Most Valuable Player candidates in Nikola Jokic, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and Giannis Antetokounmpo. The others are Darius Garland, former All-Star and starting point guard for the team with the best record in the league in the Cleveland Cavaliers, Steph Curry, Damian Lillard and Herro.

Now, before you try to say those are arbitrary statistical categories – the endpoints in these queries are always somewhat arbitrary, otherwise we wouldn’t have any terms to search with – what this is saying is that there are only five players in the league who have sustained a certain level of efficiency while using a quarter of their team’s possessions and assisting on a quarter of their team’s field goals while on the floor. Nothing complicated about that, and it gives us a good foundation to get started with. If you add in Herro’s total rebounding percentage of eight percent into that equations, it’s just him and the MVP candidates remaining.

From there, let’s simplify. Herro is one of 11 qualified (they’ve played enough minutes) players averaging at least 23 points, five rebounds and five assists this season. Here are those players sorted by their Win Shares.

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander (+7.7) Nikola Jokic (+7.1) Jayson Tatum (+5.4) Giannis Antetokounmpo (+4.5) Tyler Herro (+3.4) Franz Wagner (+3.2) Luka Doncic (+3.1) LeBron James (+3.1) Cade Cunningham (+2.2) LaMelo Ball (+1.7) RJ Barrett (+1.1) So Herro is in pretty good company to start with, and he’s not exactly barely squeezing over the barrier for entry. On top of that, Herro is just the fifth player in league history to shooting at least 40 percent on at least 9.5 attempts from three per game, something only Steph Curry has accomplished in more than a single season (he’s done it eight times). Only Curry, Herro and Anthony Edwards are meeting that criteria this season.

What about what Herro has meant to the HEAT specifically?

Miami has been 8.3 points per 100 possessions better on the offensive side of the floor with Herro on the court, that’s No. 12 in the league among players who have been on the court for at least 1,000 minutes. Miami’s offense hasn’t been truly elite with Herro playing, but without him their Offensive Rating has been the equivalent of No. 30.

Is that all enough to make him an All-Star? It’s certainly enough to get him in the conversation, at the very least, but as it doesn’t look as though he’ll receive enough votes to be a starter he’ll be at the mercy of the coaches selection for the four remaining bench slots – two backcourt, two wild card – available for guards. Maybe Herro gets in, maybe the coaches wait a year, difficult as it can be for a player to earn their first All-Star selection. Miami is in the playoff picture, but their seed isn’t high enough to ensure them a selection, and even though Adebayo isn’t having the same season he’s had the past two years he is well respected by coaches and it’s possible he splits some votes with Herro as well.

For now, Herro has more than done his job. He’s played like an All-Star. He’s made the impact an All-Star is supposed to make. But it’s a crowded field, and All-Star numbers on paper don’t guarantee you anything.

SEEING TWO

This is worthy of a far deeper look and we aren’t going to spend a ton of time on it today, but the most important thing happening with the HEAT on the court is that more and more teams – Utah, Sacramento, Indiana, Atlanta – have been sending a second defender at Herro with aggressive coverages in pick-and-rolls over the past few weeks.

This season Herro is seeing 6.4 blitzes or shows in pick-and-roll per 100 possessions, topping his career-high of 4.5, and in the past three weeks that number has skyrocketed to 12.2. Only six players – Anthony Edwards, Trae young, Jalen Brunson, Damian Lillard, Cade Cunningham and James Harden – have seen more aggressive two-on-the-ball coverage in pick-and-roll this season.
