The Deni Avdija trade has been…really bad for the Wizards, right?

One of the more quietly “not great” trades, it obviously doesn’t compare to Luka Doncic generational debacle. Nevertheless, let’s quickly go over what Washington has got out of the deal:

-Malcolm Brogdon: The recently retired Rookie of the Year, Brogdon was more or less salary filler to get the trade done and hopefully net some assets for the Wizards at last year’s deadline. Obviously, that didn’t work out and they got essentially nothing out of the 2022⁄23 6th Man of the Year.

-Carlton “Bub” Carrington: I’ll be honest, I liked the trade at the time because I was quite high on Bub. But thus far? He hasn’t worked out for them. The current holder of the worst +/- of all time, he’s currently shooting 35 percent from the field. Weirdly, he’s been good from 3 at around 43 for the year but if the field goal percentage didn’t give it away, it’s on very low volume. It’s too early to say he’s a bust but it isn’t looking good for the once-promising combo guard.

-2029 first (Second worst of the Blazers 3 2029 picks between their own, Bucks and Celtics): There’s also 2 seconds for additionaldraft capital but let’s be honest, the chances those turn into a player as good as Cory Kispert - let alone Deni or former 2nd ronder Nikola Jokic - are about as likely as you winning the lottery. If Washington manages to convert this pick into something meaningful via a consolidation trade for an All-Star should their rebuild be successful in the next 2-3 years then this could salvage the trade but otherwise it’s not particularly exciting.

Needless to say, Portland has been reaping the benefits of the Avdija trade thus far.
