Brad is a team builder not a trade winner

The term “in Brad we trust” did not come from nowhere. This was earned not just because he brought the Cs a championship, but the way he did it. While he certainly has had moves that everyone liked from the start, a lot of his most impactful moves have been ones where a lot of people thought he lost them in the moment. Not because the players he traded for were bad, but because they weren’t the “value” trades we had become so used to with Danny.

Danny was notorious for never losing a trade, to a point where there were rumors of other GMs not wanting to work with him because he would negotiate so hard for every asset, and hold out until he got the “win”. But his trades were not always basketball trades. Other than the Allen and KG trades, he never really made those trades that were more about the on court outcome than the off court accounting.

Brad on the other hand has been the opposite for the majority of his tenure, save this past “gap year”, where he was focused on getting under the tax to reset things. But look at the other moves he made:

He gave up a 2028 pick swap that many thought was an overpay for Derrick White.

He gave up a mid first round pick (which became Sengun) to dump Kemba Walker and bring back what most people thought was a washed Al Horford.

He “panicked” when things fell apart trying to trade Brogdon, and gave up the “heart and soul” of the team in Marcus Smart for the often injured and disappointing Porzingis.

What do these have in common? Brad ignored the fact that he was potentially losing the value play, and made the trades for players he thought fit what they needed. He could have probably gotten better value by taking on different players, or maybe he could have just called the other teams bluff and paid less. But he instead identified what he wanted and paid the price.

Brad seems to run the team as if he was still coaching it. He doesn’t want to trade for players he wouldn’t want to coach, just to “win the trade”, but instead identifies the players he wants and pays what is needed.

That’s not to say he doesn’t have his limits. But he is willing to pay a premium for someone who he believes is the right fit.

I say this all because I genuinely believe Brad targeted PG in a similar fashion. Could he have gotten more “value” by trading for someone different? Probably. But I think he looked at PG as the right fit to make the team better compared to the alternatives.

Let me be clear, I’m working from the assumption that for whatever reason they determined a move had to be made. None of us know what the reality was in the locker room, so I’m just going to make the assumption that bringing Brown back was not on the table.

I think Brad decided PG was the best option on the table that both gave them premium assets for the future while also having the potential to improve the team in the short-term because of his fit. It was a basketball trade by a coach who wants to build a team of players that fit together rather than a collection of assets that make the pundits give him an “A”.
