Three memos of things we need POE2 to really focus on during EA next phases.

On Content Reuse

POE2 has a problem where it both needs to use some of the goodness of its pedigree, leagues, and content but is challenged as a small development studio needing to find efficiencies to deliver.

In doing so some pitfalls appear to be emerging around reusing content - like Delirium at a fundamental level: when originally designed it was a new tech using a fluid system to draw a fog motion layer across the maps. This always had some issues with even POE1 on some maps, but these became far worse when the underlying system was reused into POE2. In POE2 where map styles deviated so significantly with new randomization approaches that the draw-a-path for the fog flow model to fail amazingly fast. In this simple example, the POE2 team must go back and critically examine Delirium and comparable features with a lens that will find them injecting drift between POE1 and POE2. The team must reflectively ask - does this feature actually work when ported to POE2? This is a risk to the small studio, but there are few ways around this. Many features in POE1 will not translate well, or as effectively as perhaps conceived initially without considerable tuning to make them valid and relevant within POE2.

Others who have commented in this space have pointed at Ritual as a comparable, where some of the map designs on POE2, and the hyper-simplistic few Ritual altar types in POE2 don’t work well on the map styles often due to spacing/crowding and design issues. This too is an artifact of an attempt at content reuse, with admitted modulation but incomplete tuning to arrive at a real next-generation concept model that fits. 

I empathize with the developers because they are stuck - needing to ensure they don’t break, or under resource POE1, while rapidly fixing and getting to a state of EA and release on POE2 where the company can shift over. It’s definitely an interesting case study. I hope they go engage with some college to conduct a business case study on a small company story-telling around the sheer challenge of a mono-product change-over; it’s worth sharing.

Hi, I would like to buy your…

POE2 has to commit to what it wants to provide for progression. Its competition that was able to start fresh has achieved something that POE2 is lacking - decisive direction on what GGG wants play to be. Last Epoch has injected the idea of supporting solo self found, and supporting trade players. While its systems aren’t perfect, it shows where POE2 needs to head and how it needs to advance its art and focus on standing on a next generation beyond POE1. 

Make a decision on trade: Either: trade is mandatory or it’s an optional element to support a style of play. I would argue that it is mostly mandatory for end game success - where the design of the game generally relies on the luck of the marketplace and an exchange mechanism to be able to advance your character. POE2 is weaker than POE1 in its early access form requiring even higher amounts of market interaction in its current currency-redesign setup. Other parties have given solutions for the trade problem, to encourage Community - focus on a StoreFront concept if you must; either forcing players into a trade hub, or forcing them to visit seller hideouts. But find a mechanism to advance the trade concept beyond POE1. You cannot exit early access without solving and firmly declaring – is trade mandatory in POE (if you want to do anything other than just play the game full time for success - or risk of blind luck).

Make a decision on currency: Either deterministic crafting options need to exist to help players avoid trade if they want to avoid it, or you must have pathways where deterministic crafting content can be arrived at via trade in a way that makes it seamless to interact with the market.

Advance the concepts of Gold: few will argue that Gold injection is a good way to reduce the price of failure, encourage experimentation and play among the player base trying to use POE for what it’s good for - a game that should encourage exploration. However end game Gold needs to potentially be a mechanism for dealing with issues above - such as a path to helping arrive at any type of deterministic crafting or some similar mechanism to reduce other currency eating sinks in map crafting, etc. If given outlets, Gold can afford many angles of use or even market flow control if it were a method to avoid sale saturation in a “marketplace” assisted storefront.

Don’t lose that Trade in POE1 and the POE community is both powerful and important for the culture of the players. There are few games I’ve enjoyed as much as the friendly trade community of POE for its simple community building - but it’s also a new generation game and supposed to advance us as players beyond POE1. Trade must be supported by ways that casual players can engage in the marketplace and benefit from sales; accelerate or interact with the market in more ways than the POE1 model.

The Problem with Nightmare Dungeons

POE2 end-game has some serious good bones, and some serious risks to resolve. The team needs to step back and realize they fell into some of the same traps that the Blizzard developers did - Nightmare Dungeons suck. Don’t emulate Diablo4. Realize that the POE1 end-game is good because it afford both flexibility, randomness, and encourages people to interact with different content layers. In the current form of end game mapping - the single most unenjoyable aspect of Nightmare Dungeons is the entire success loop of mapping – namely quest objective clear-conditions.

There is not a single more annoying Nightmare Dungeon than “kill all enemies” and sadly with the injection of clear-conditions around Kill all Elites, POE2 has fallen into the single most unenjoyable model of play in all of Diablo 4. At a minimum, this style needs to rapidly emulate Diablo 4 where - not just signals for the residual elites, but a concept allowing us to Collapse the Chaos and teleport the final sets of enemies to us / us to them is mandatory for making play at all more enjoyable. Nothing more disheartening in POE2 mapping than trudging towards 1 elite missed not near any of the teleportation nodes.

This must be fixed as a highest priority and the changes made still don’t cut it. Consider comparable options of multi-variable clear conditions “Boss killed + League Content mechanic cleared” or “Monster density reduced to xyz %”. Give us ways to play our way - that’s your strength. POE is a game of playing for variables - make the win conditions to move us along follow the same logic.

Obviously you’ve spoken of the challenge in this space about people avoiding select content types - totally agree on that thought concept and challenge in the design. But you cannot also damage your end game by emulating the worst mechanics in your competition’s game, while not emulating some enabler systems that make theirs barely even tolerable to play.
