[Ben Cafardo] Hubie Brown’s Final NBA Broadcast: Colleagues Pay Tribute to Legend Ahead of Feb. 9 Farewell

Final Broadcast Details

“Hubie Brown’s final NBA broadcast will take place on February 9, 2025, during the Milwaukee Bucks vs. Philadelphia 76ers game airing at 2 p.m. ET on ABC… The game will be held in Milwaukee, where Brown began his NBA career as an assistant coach for the Bucks in 1972. He will reunite with Hall of Fame play-by-play commentator Mike Breen, his partner during the 2006 NBA Finals.”

Colleagues’ Tributes

Mike Breen: “Hubie’s preparation is unmatched. He studies every team’s late-game plays, their defensive rotations, their injury reports. He doesn’t just show up. He makes every broadcast better. When we worked the 2006 Finals together, he elevated the entire production with his insights.”

Dave Pasch (ESPN): “Hubie treats me like family. There’s no ego, no pretense. He shares his knowledge generously. When I started, he’d leave handwritten notes in my hotel room with tips. He’s the reason I’m still here.”

Burke Magnus (ESPN President): “It’s remarkable the level he still calls games at age 90-plus. He’s a Hall of Famer, a two-time Coach of the Year, and yet he’s as humble as they come. We’re sending him off in style because his impact is timeless.”

Career Highlights

“Brown won NBA Coach of the Year honors twice (1978 with the Atlanta Hawks, 2004 with the Memphis Grizzlies)… He led the Grizzlies to their first playoff appearance in franchise history in 2004. As a broadcaster, Brown worked for ESPN, TNT, CBS, and ABC, covering multiple NBA Finals. His ability to explain complex plays in simple terms made him a fan favorite.”

Personal Resilience

“Brown’s final year has been marked by profound loss. His wife of 63 years, Claire, passed away in June 2024. Four months later, his son Brendan, a former Knicks broadcaster, died suddenly… Despite his grief, Brown insisted on honoring his commitments. ‘This final game is humbling,’ he said. ‘It’s the culmination of my life’s work.’”

Hubie Brown, 91, concludes his 50-year NBA journey with a symbolic return to Milwaukee, where his career began. Colleagues universally praise his mentorship, encyclopedic knowledge, and unwavering dedication. His farewell broadcast will honor a legacy that shaped basketball analysis for generations, blending coaching brilliance with storytelling that educated fans worldwide.
