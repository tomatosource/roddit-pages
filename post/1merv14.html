28 years ago today (Aug 1st), Cleveland signed a 7’3” Lithuanian with broken feet who turned into a Cavs legend

August 1, 1996. Cavs signed some lanky 7’3” dude from Lithuania who looked like he hadn’t smiled since the Cold War. Zydrunas Ilgauskas. Most fans had no clue who he was and even less reason to care. He immediately wrecked his foot during a preseason workout before his rookie year even started. It’s one of the worst bones an NBA big man can injure. Low blood flow, hard to heal, high chance of re-injury. Big Z didn’t play a single game his rookie year. Then kept getting hurt. The guy was basically made of drywall.

It looked like one of those classic “tall foreign project” signings that just disappear quietly. Big Z stuck around. And then some.

He didn’t move fast. Never jumped high. Kinda ran like a deer stuck in mud. That jumper was pure. Just a soft, perfect 15-footer every time. No arc, no flair, just splash. He didn’t talk trash, didn’t flex. Just hit shots, grabbed boards, and did his job.

He became a real part of that team. Made two All-Star teams. Had a run of seasons where he was putting up 16 and 8 like it was nothing. Wasn’t doing it for the camera. Just hooping.

One night he dropped 44 on the Kings. No hype, no headline. Just quietly cooked them with that slow-motion hook and jumper combo. If you weren’t watching back then, you’d never guess how important he was to those early LeBron Cavs teams.

He ended up playing 12 seasons for Cleveland. Through all the bad years, the rebuilds, the first LeBron era. Then when Lebron left for Miami, Z followed him. Not even for clout, just one last run with his guy. When the Cavs retired Z’s jersey in 2014, LeBron flew back just to be there.

Some random stuff people forget:

  • Size 20 shoes
  • Like 3 or 4 major foot surgeries
  • Still played over 800 NBA games
  • Took 19 threes his whole career, made 4
  • Once beat Shaq on a jump ball somehow
  • Was on the cover of NBA ShootOut 2001 on PS1

Big Z was never flashy, but everybody trusted him. Every teammate respected him. Fans loved him because he showed up, kept his mouth shut, and played hard. Big Z was a skyscraper with a jump shot and a busted foot, and he gave Cleveland everything he had.

Not enough people talk about him. So here it is. One of the most underappreciated bigs of the 2000s.
