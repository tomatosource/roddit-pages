Game Design Theorizing - The original “vision” and health regen as a mechanic

This post is just me dumping general thoughts and ramblings I have for discussions sake, so take that for what it is.

Seeing POE2 adopting some of POE1’s characteristics after listening to player feedback has caused me to be curious about what could have been if the vision would have played out. Originally the vision as I understood it was more methodical “meaningful” combat while keeping the POE systems, creating a hybrid of the souls-like genre with the “diablo-like” arpg genre. I am glad GGG listens to players, and the games success so far in EA shows the player base likes what they are doing, I am just left curious.

The largest step away from this vision that I can remember happened before EA released, when streamers were invited to play the game and complained about flasks not regenerating enough (only from killing rares if I remember correctly) so they continuously had to town portal back and click the well to heal. After this GGG changed the behavior to be more in line with what POE1 has. The next play-test everyone was much happier, but I feel this is when the souls-like combat tension was probably lost.

I am wondering what would have happened if they went the opposite direction, and made it so that town portals “save” the health you had when you left the map so you would always return with the same amount - essentially making it so that each map had finite healing in it, meaning you essentially had a finite health pool. In that case the annoyance the streamers had of “why do I need to town portal all the time” wouldn’t exist since doing so wouldn’t benifit them, and they would be presented a new take on the genre that would have them need to treat it in a new way.

This of course would only work if flask charges were limited like they originally were, but another thing this would require is removing passive life regeneration from the game altogether, as it serves as another way of getting “infinite” health. This might also require limiting, reworking or outright removing of energy shield.

Doing this will cause every mob interaction to demand attention, since if you get hit, even a little, it matters for the rest of the map. This would also enable making mobs hit for a lot less. When you have a lot of life regeneration, via recharging flasks or passive skills, hits have to be big otherwise they just get healed quickly like nothing ever happened, and consequently death usually happens as a quick 100 to 0 in the span of a few seconds. When healing is limited combat can be more gradual. This way your health bar is more frequently somewhere in the middle, causing developing increased tension within the current fight and the map as a whole. Currently there is the same tension throughout a fight for fear of being quickly killed but not map-wide tension as each mob pack is separate and you can often heal between them.

This gradual tension type of combat is more prevalent with bosses in current POE2 since you are far more limited in life flask charges, depending on if the boss summons additional monsters, so even though you still get hit for a lot at a time and heal up you can feel increased tension as you are left with less life flasks. This, with addition of great boss design in general, may be part of why these fights are so revered.

All this is not to say I think GGG needs to change the current game to do this now. At this point the gene is out of the bottle and GGG will never be able to make such a change that adds so many limitations to the player even if they wanted to. It also seems the player base in general likes the direction GGG has chosen so I don’t think a change like this be made anyways. Still I feel like we ended up with POE2 being a perfection of and existing genre and missed out on seeing a new hybrid genre develop. It would be very interesting if one day, after full release and everything. GGG released a “vision mode” with these, or other, changes made to create this hybrid, similar to how ruthless mode was added in PEO1,
