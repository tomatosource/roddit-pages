Esmerlyn Valdez and the 14 HR Club

Today, Esmerlyn Valdez (PIT) became the 7th player all time to reach 14 home runs in 37 career games.

Here’s a brief look at each of the 6 other members of this club, and how their careers turned out.

  1. Rhys Hoskins (18 HR, in 2017). A very promising career was derailed in 2023 when he tore his ACL chasing a foul ball in spring training. Before the injury, he had 148 home runs and a 125 OPS+ in 667 games. Though he never made an all star team or received MVP votes, his hot start was certainly not a fluke.
  2. Aristedes Aquino (15 HR, in 2019). The nightmare scenario for Esmerlyn Valdez’s outlook. Aquino was looking to be among the most feared sluggers in baseball during that first month, but the league evidently figured him out and he was never the same. After his rookie year, he only hit 22 home runs with a 71 OPS+.
  3. Wally Joyner (15 HR, in 1986). An All-Star and Rookie of the Year runner up, Joyner hit 22 HR in ‘86 and 34 in ‘87, but seemingly lost his power stroke after that. He had a very successful career spanning 16 years with 204 HR and a 117 OPS+, but would only hit 20 or more HR once more.
  4. Wally Berger (14 HR, in 1930). This was meant to be an Esmerlyn Valdez post, but upon learning about this guy I’m now declaring it a Wally Berger post. Berger would retire after only 11 seasons with 242 career HR, good for 12th all time at the time of his retirement, and a 138 OPS+. A member of baseball’s first generation of bona fide sluggers, Berger is largely forgotten by baseball history for a few reasons: first, his career was derailed by injuries and he was forced into retirement at 34. Second, he played on the hapless Boston Braves, who had only 2 winning seasons in Berger’s 7 full seasons on the team (including a 38-115 finish in 1935, a season in which Berger hit a National League-leading 34 home runs and compiled 5.7 WAR. Berger finished 6th in MVP voting). Third, he was overshadowed by his contemporaries, most notably Jimmie Foxx and Lou Gehrig. Regardless, his hot start was decidedly not a fluke, as he sustained an average of 27 HR per season over his first 7 campaigns, before injuries began to slow him down.
  5. Munetaka Murakami (14 HR, in 2026). Not much can be said about Murakami yet in terms of career outcome, considering that he’s still a rookie. He hit 2 home runs today, though, so it’s looking like his brief injured list stint hasn’t slowed him down.
  6. Albert Pujols (14 HR, in 2001). 703 career home runs. 3 MVPs. We all know about Albert Pujols. He should be a unanimously inducted Hall of Famer when he’s first eligible. If Aquino is the nightmare, Pujols is the dream. My favorite Pujols factoid is that he very nearly started his career with 10 straight seasons of 30+ home runs, 100+ RBIs, and .300+ average, except that in the 10th season he *only* had 99 RBIs and batted .299. He would have had 10 straight seasons of 100+ runs scored as well, if not for a mere 99 runs in 2007.

While historical data isn’t necessarily a good predictor of future outcomes, I think it is encouraging that of the 5 members of this club whose careers can be properly analyzed, 3 sustained their power for several seasons, and all but Aquino turned in long careers.

As for Valdez’s performance metrics, I’m encouraged by his elite hard hit rate and chase rate. Power alone is important, but power combined with patience and plate discipline is a dangerous combination. His strikeout rate is very high at 38%, but there is certainly precedent to a player finding success as a power hitter with elite hard hit rate and chase rate, but awful K%. Among players with 100 or more PA in 2026, the players with 30%+ K%, 50%+ hard hit rate, and &lt;30% chase rate are: Munetaka Murakami, Nick Kurtz, Esmerlyn Valdez, Kyle Schwarber, MJ Melendez, and Ryan McMahon. There are a couple of rough names in there, showing definite reason for concern regarding Valdez’s high strikeout rate, but being in company with Kurtz, Schwarber and Murakami shows that it’s possible be successful in spite of strikeouts.

There have been 393 players to reach 200 career HR. Ignoring Valdez and Murakami because they are rookies, and assuming that Hoskins and Sanchez each get 3 more HR, at least 10 out of 16 players (62.5%) to hit 12 or more HR in their first 37 games went on to hit 200 or more home runs. That’s very good company for both Murakami and Valdez.

What Esmerlyn Valdez has been accomplishing over the past month has been incredible, and it’s been exciting to watch. Hopefully he can sustain success and become a household name. If history is to be trusted, he’s in good company.
