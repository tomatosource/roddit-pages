Classes and skills won’t solve build diversity.

The reality is that any and all support for any kind of builds on gear and the passive skill tree is about 80-90% for direct hit abilities, and most of it attacks. No matter how many classes and skills they release, the problem isn’t that there’s no build diversity due to a lack of build options, there’s no build diversity cus most of those options aren’t supported.

Let’s say you wanna build a meta trigger build like cast on crit, or an aurabot or cursebot for party play, or an auto bomber of some sort using an interesting unique. At first glance it may seem like the game very much allows and encourage that, but as soon as you start build theorycrafting you’ll realize there’s only a scant few passive points you can take and uniques you can use, most of them occupying slots like weapon that normally would give you most of your damage on other builds, but because there’s not many other options you’re force to used them, at the cost of the build being terrible due to a lack of scaling. Then you go play the build and realize… almost all of the stuff you can get on gear is defences… with the exception of gem level, you cannot get any way to scale these unorthodox builds on non-unique gear, there’s no cooldown recovery, energy gain increase, aura/presence range increase (no, the ONE talisman on boots isn’t enough), etc.

Minion players probably have noticed this. As soon as you discount gem level, the only way you can scale minion builds on gear is either more spirit or just a better scepter. And when you realize how mandatory gem level and spirit are on gear, you end up in a situation where any and all upgrades become almost impossible to source cus they HAVE to have those mods (not to mention the loot and crafting issues).

Here’s the kick tho: as it’s pretty obvious with the wisp mechanic, the gradual increment of these inclusions that happens on normal lagues is nowhere near enough. Small increases of a new socketable or a new unique here or there won’t make enough over the course of 4-8 leagues to solve this issue. The problem is that the gear in general is incredibly unimaginative in its mod pools (meaning rare items).

Now there’s no doubt in my mind this will get solved at SOME point in the future: systems like eldritch currency or veiled mods in poe1 kinda did this sort of thing for that game. But the problem is that with the development of classes and acts, that’s not gonna come in early acces, but I also believe it’s not gonna come for about a year after even, considering all the other things they’ll have to address.

You could easily argue that this is how poe 1 started and it’s fine since the game has to last for say another 10 years, so it will change with time. But that’s a common misconception that I’ve seen a lot, including amongst the devs: the geenie cannot be put back in the bottle. When poe 1 came out, the closes thing we knew was diablo 2, and that game was so old many players didn’t have it present for comparison (not to mention poe 1 is, IMO, way more open ended than d2). Poe2 can’t have that luxury cus we have and constantly play poe 1. How’s it gonna feel when you play a poe 1 league for a month or two, get hyped for the new poe 2 league on 1.1.0, and then realize as soon as you start playing that the only powerful or interesting builds are elemental attack builds… again… or maybe some random manual caster if even?

We NEED more interesting mod pools on item right now, and especially more support for unorthodox builds on them. No single item slot should EVER have a modpool that only includes defensive and attack/self-cast mods. Where are the minion rings? Where are the CDR boots or belts? Where are the quirky unique yellow mods like getting a charge every 20 seconds?

We need more of those things and NOT just on socketables, if you put everything on socketables, the point is missed. Socketables can’t bear the load of all mechanics, especially when traditional builds don’t have that constraint. Socketables are a supporting system, not the star of the show.
