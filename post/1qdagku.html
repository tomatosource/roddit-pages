A somewhat controversial Hall of Fame-related stat that makes me feel a little confused

Forgive the baity title; the other title I thought of felt too much like actual bait.

I was looking at the Johan Santana thread in the fantastic HOF Snub Team series. Pretty well known that Johan fell off the ballot on his first appearance in 2018.

So, I was looking at who was on the ballot that year, why it was so cluttered, all that jazz. As it turns out 11 future Hall of Famers were taking up some votes, not including Bonds (steroids), Clemens (steroids), and Schilling (probably in the cabinet now?).

And, if you look at who got elected that year, probably an easy case for Chipper Jones (85 WAR), Vlad Guerrero (inducted at age 18 apparently, incredible), JI Jim Thome (612 home runs), and of course, on his third ballot, the great Tre—

Huh. Hmm.

Di–did Trevor Hoffman accumulate all of 28 career WAR?

Okay, I was around for all his seasons, and like sentient for them too. And, we all agreed he was great. And there are lies, damned lies, and statistics, and we know that relief pitchers obviously aren’t going to accumulate a ton of WAR, and he still had 600 goddamn saves, and he’s the second greatest Padre of all time, all of it.

So, I did a little digging, just, again, WAR, not be-all end-all, but I figured I’d look at that WAR-7 number. He pitched 18 years, made a bunch of all-star teams, must be pretty good I’d assume.

His top seven years? 19.4 WAR. Again, we all agreed he was, after Mo, the damn best there was. Hells Bells, etc. And from 1996-1998, when the Padres made the playoffs twice and won a pennant with help from stars like Ken Caminiti and Ken Caminiti’s dealer, Hoffman was unimpeachable – 242 IP, .93 WHIP, 308 K, 132 saves. 11.1 WAR too.

So…what gives with the rest? Now, for obvious reasons, guys like Eckersley are going to have much higher seven-year peaks, so you can wiggle the next stat, but amongst listed “Relief Pitchers” per Baseball Reference, Hoffman’s top-seven peak ranks 39th. Even tossing out the starters, that’s below Papelbon. That’s below Keith Foulke and Joe Nathan. That’s below Mark Eichhorn, which is not fair to Hoffman because Eichhorn had a marvelous season as a fireman before moving to a modern low-inning relief role, but still, Mark Eichhorn. He sits close to Kent Tekulve, Sparky Lyle, Doug Jones.

One last time – I was there. We all agreed Trevor Hoffman was incredible. We all knew he was a Hall of Famer, and even third ballot felt too late (he entered at high 60s). Some stats are flawed, absolutely.

I feel like I’m taking crazy pills. I need to lie down. Help me understand this. And get Johan Santana to the Veterans Committee stat.
