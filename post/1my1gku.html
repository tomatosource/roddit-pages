Bots, price fixers and server/hardware speed differences - a potential solution

The automated trade improvement certainly helps a lot and I’m a big fan already, but it also opens the gates for bots, price fixers* and those with the fastest server connection/hardware** even more since they are the ones who most profit from the automation.

This is especially true in the case of bots and speedsters because those are the ones who can get the fastest through the process of seeing the item offer on the trade side to the final purchase. I’ve thought of a potential solution that doesn’t change anything for regular players (and wouldn’t even be noticeable despite sounding confusing when written out below) but it removes the advantage of those with uneven advantages.

To level the playing field for everyone, it needs a randomized delay of when new items are shown on the trade side. Sounds counterintuitive and confusing at first, but please hear me out.

Instead of new item offers being shown to everyone at the exact same time on the trade side, it would show the item in randomized waves (so a few seconds delay per wave). With each wave, more and more players can see the item offer, say 5% of the total player base per wave, until everyone sees it. It is random in which wave a player is in - might be the first, might be the last, might be somewhere inbetween. This is also randomized per item offer, so you may be in one of the middle waves for one item offer and in one of the first waves of another item offer.

The odds of all bots, price fixers and speedsters being in the first few waves are next to zero. Assuming that the majority of players in the community are regular players and not bots/fixers/speedsters, the majority of players in the first waves will be regular players. They are the ones who see the item offer first, giving them enough time to trade before bots and speedsters get the chance. This removes the advantage they would otherwise have.

“But doesn’t this also disadvantage regular players who are randomly selected for later waves?” - For a singular item offer, yes. They wouldn’t notice the disadvantage since they wouldn’t even know that the offer existed if it’s traded (removed) before being shown to them, but on a single item offer, that’s a side effect.

However, since the wave selection where an individual player is in is randomized per item offer, this side effect evens out across a league. For some items, an individual may be the first to grab it, for others they may come last. On average, across all of the whatever million item offers in total, everyone has the exact same chance.

And especially those with otherwise speed/bot advantage wouldn’t have that anymore. A fair playing field for everyone.

* price fixers = purchasing all lower cost items to dictate a new higher base price where they are now the main supplier, which isn’t inherently problematic but still a costly disadvantage to literally every other player on the market for that item type. If that happens to multiple item types, this raises the general price niveau of the entire market, leading to artificial inflation that only benefits the price fixers.

** for simplicity, let’s call them speedsters: the automation also catalyses the “first come, first serve” principle because items aren’t locked in for just one buyer upon clicking on the “Secure” button. Multiple buyers can do it simultaneously and it’s then a question of who finishes the hideout teleportation first and then clicks the fastest on the buy button (also something bots are inhumanely fast in). This is obviously benefitial for those with great server connections (so definitely not EU players) and those with more powerful computer hardware to speed up the hideout teleportation loading screen. For high demand items, these seconds make a major difference.
