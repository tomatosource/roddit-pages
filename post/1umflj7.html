Wyc wasn’t a crazy spender either

There’s this idea going around that Wyc was willing to spend like crazy and that Chisholm isn’t, and that’s what’s driving all of these transactions. Of course I’m grateful for Wyc’s stewardship on the team and the championships under his watch, but the reality is that Wyc always dodged the repeater and never paid the biggest bills.

Since Wyc took over the team, the Celtics have paid \(231M in luxury tax payments. This is 8th in the league (Warriors \~\)758M, Clippers ~\(410M, Nets \~\)370M, Knicks ~\(330M, Suns \~\)285M, Cavs ~\(248M, Lakers \~\)245M, Celtics $231M).

We dodged the repeater tax by going under the tax in 20-21 (dumping Theis and trading the Desmond Bane pick for second rounders to avoid paying the first round pick’s guaranteed salary) and staying under in 21-22 (signing Schroder as the offseason acquistion and not using the Fournier TPE). Then, with the repeater cleared, we went back over for the championship run, where we ran up most of that tax bill over three seasons including the championship run (which was quite profitable for the team and financially justified the tax bills).

At the start of the 2025-26 season, we had a looming $250M tax bill, which he (or more likely his dad) didn’t want to pay, and they sell the team and leave the bill for the next owner to deal with (and take the blame for dodging). But there’s nothing in Wyc’s ownership history to suggest that he would have done anything different than instructing Brad to duck the tax, just like Chisholm did. He did the exact same thing in 20-21 - reset the repeater tax by staying under for two years, even though we made the Eastern Conference finals the year before.

If Chisholm refuses to go over the tax and run up the bill to support Tatum after the repeater is reset this year (and we’re actually over the tax right now so this remains to be seen, but I expect they’ll do it) - then we’ll know he really is a cheap owner. Until then, he hasn’t done anything that Wyc wouldn’t have.
