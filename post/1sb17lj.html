Once Jackie Robinson won the NL MVP in 1949 just 2 years after breaking the color barrier, black players went on to win the NL MVP in 15 of the next 20 years. A black player didn’t win the AL MVP until Elston Howard in 1963, 14 years after the first black NL MVP

There were 11 black NL MVPs before there was one in the AL. Including 7 in a row from 1953-59.

That difference is crazy. 14 years is a whole era of baseball and then some. And it’s not like AL had no black players in even the 50s. One of the craziest baseball, and even history stats, is that Larry Doby became the second black player and first black AL player just 11 weeks after Jackie. He deserves so much credit too. Not only was he an All Star for the next 7 straight years, but he essentially had to endure and excel under the same circumstances as Jackie.

Black players hit the league very fast, but almost entirely in the NL. Their dominance in the National League was instant.

Jackie, Mays, Aaron, Campanella, Gibson. Clemente, Frank Robinson, McCovey, Newcombe, etc. made up an iconic first wave of all time great black players. All in the NL. The only black AL player in the 50s-60s on the level of the best of their NL counterparts was Frank Robinson, who left the NL in 1966.

Those black players that became superstars in the AL came more in the 1970s into the 80s with Reggie Jackson, Rod Carew, Jim Rice, Dave Winfield, and eventually Ricky Henderson.
