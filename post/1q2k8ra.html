Japanese media have predicted the results of WBC Pool C, forecasting that Japan will finish in first place.

Pool C (March 5–10, Tokyo Dome): ◎ Japan, Australia; ▲ South Korea, Czech Republic; ○ Taiwan

In this tournament, only one pool will be hosted in Asia, and for the first time since the second edition in 2009, Asian powerhouses Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan have been placed in the same group. Defending champion Japan stands out as a dominant force, while South Korea and Taiwan are evenly matched in terms of talent, making the battle for second place likely to be fierce.

Japan

Defending champion Japan saw Shohei Ohtani quickly commit to participating in the tournament. It is also a major boost that Japan has a clear path to calling up a certain number of MLB players, including Yoshinobu Yamamoto and Munetaka Murakami, whose new team, the White Sox, has approved his participation despite this being his first year in the majors.

In the past, Japan’s failures to win the title were closely tied to the lack of MLB players: in 2013 the team was composed entirely of NPB players, and in 2017 the only MLB player was Norichika Aoki, then with the Astros.

This time, as in the previous tournament, Ohtani was once again the first to announce his participation, and his decision has triggered a similar wave of MLB players joining the team. Even if Ohtani serves solely as a designated hitter, if Japan can assemble a rotation featuring Yamamoto along with Shota Imanaga (32, Cubs), Yusei Kikuchi (34, Angels), and Tomoyuki Sugano (36, Orioles FA), the starting pitching staff would be among the best in the tournament. Japan can reasonably be considered one of the title favorites alongside the United States and the Dominican Republic. Ideally, Japan would cruise through the first round with four straight wins, leaving South Korea, Taiwan, and Australia to battle it out for second place.

Taiwan

The opponent Japan should be most wary of is Taiwan, the champion of the 2024 Premier12. Taiwan’s position players include infielder Yu Chang (30, Fubon), a former Red Sox player who hit a grand slam against the Netherlands in the previous WBC; outfielder Chen Chieh-hsien (31, Uni-Lions), who was named MVP of the Premier12; and power-hitting catcher Giljegiljaw Kungkuan (31, Wei Chuan), whose name pronunciation became a topic of discussion in Japan during the Premier12. All of them have been named to the provisional roster.

The key question is how Taiwan will deploy its ace left-hander, Lin Yu-Min (22), who pitches for the Diamondbacks’ Triple-A affiliate. In the Premier12 final against Japan, he started and shut down Samurai Japan, allowing just one hit and no runs over four innings. Taiwan opens the first round against Australia on March 5, with the Japan game on March 6 as its second contest. For Japan, however, the game against Taiwan will be its tournament opener. Given that many of Japan’s core hitters—Ohtani, Murakami, Teruaki Sato, Yoshida, and Kondo—are left-handed, facing Lin Yu-Min right away could pose significant challenges.

That said, the first round has a strict pitch limit of 65 pitches, and even if Taiwan starts Lin Yu-Min, there is no guarantee they can secure a win against Japan. If Taiwan prioritizes advancing safely from the first round, it may be more effective to save its ace for a direct showdown with South Korea, the team it is likely to be battling for second place, rather than using him against Japan.

Korea

If there is a team determined to prioritize a win against Japan, it is South Korea. Their strong results—reaching the semifinals in 2006 and finishing as runners-up in 2009—are now a thing of the past. South Korea has failed to advance beyond the first round in each of the past three tournaments. That said, baseball enthusiasm in the KBO is on the rise, highlighted by a record-breaking attendance of over 12 million in 2025, the first time in league history.

Building on the roster that faced Japan in November, South Korea is in a position to call up MLB position players such as outfielder Jung-hoo Lee (27, Giants) and infielder Hyeseong Kim (26, Dodgers). While there are no MLB pitchers on the staff, veteran left-hander Hyun-jin Ryu (38, Hanwha), a former Dodger with a proven track record, has joined the pre-tournament training camp in Saipan.

In the previous tournament, South Korea finished 2–2 and was eliminated in the first round after losses to Japan and Australia. One could argue that the outcome might have been different had they effectively sacrificed the Japan game and focused entirely on beating Australia. After that tournament, I once discussed the WBC with a Korean journalist and asked, “If they had gone all-in on the Australia game by essentially giving up on Japan, could they have beaten Cuba in the round of 16 and made it to Miami?” The response was, “That may well be correct, but in Korea, a manager who adopts a strategy that assumes defeat against Japan from the outset would never be accepted.”

“The idea that ‘you must not lose the Korea–Japan game, even if it’s rock-paper-scissors,’ is not a joke—it’s real. Even if advancing in second place is the realistic goal, you can’t be that blatant,” he explained.

South Korea opens against the Czech Republic on March 5, then faces Japan, Taiwan, and Australia in three consecutive games starting on March 7. Given the schedule, aggressively targeting a win against Japan may not be the most pragmatic approach, but the likelihood of treating it as a “throwaway game” is low. As a left-hander, Hyun-jin Ryu is a strong candidate to start that game.

While it is an unpleasant scenario to contemplate, the worst-case outcome for Japan would be if both Taiwan and South Korea choose to challenge Japan head-on, forcing Japan to deal with the ace left-handers of both teams. If Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan were all to finish 3–1, advancement could come down to run differential, raising the unlikely but not impossible risk of a shock first-round exit for Japan.

Australia

Australia, which reached the quarterfinals in the previous tournament, will once again be led by head coach Dave Nilsson (56), who played in Japan’s NPB for the Chunichi Dragons under the registered name “Dingo.” Participation has already been confirmed for pitcher Liam Hendriks (36, Red Sox FA) and infielder Travis Bazzana (23, Guardians organization).

Australia is scheduled to face Taiwan in its opening game and South Korea in its final game, and the results of Australia’s games are likely to have a major impact on how this pool plays out. Having defeated South Korea in the previous tournament, Australia has enough strength to prevent the battle for second place from being a contest solely between Taiwan and South Korea.

Czech

The Czech Republic, which earned a historic first victory over China in its WBC debut at the previous tournament, will once again feature players who parlayed their performances last time into opportunities to play in Japan. These include former Yomiuri Giants infielder Marek Chlup (26) and former BC League Kanagawa infielder Milan Prokop (22). Pitcher Ondřej Satoria (28), who struck out Shohei Ohtani with a 126 km/h changeup, is also expected to participate and should attract plenty of attention. However, having been placed in a tougher pool than last time, a single win may be hard to come by.

While it is relatively easy to predict Japan finishing first and the Czech Republic fifth, the three teams—Taiwan, South Korea, and Australia—are likely to be locked in a fierce battle. Based on their Premier12 championship pedigree, Taiwan is projected to finish second, followed by South Korea in third due to an unfavorable game schedule, with Australia predicted to place fourth.
