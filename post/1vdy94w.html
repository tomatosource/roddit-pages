Lost in the Kawhi/Clippers Scandal™ punishment determination is that the Clippers were already previously found to have engaged in dubious endorsement dealings.

You’re probably familiar with the…let’s go with passive aggressive approach the Clippers went with which to prevent then-free agent and NBA 3rd Team DeAndre Jordan from penning his agreed upon deal with the Dallas Mavericks back in 2015. What you might be less familiar with? Per Brad Turner of the L.A. Times, the Clippers offered Jordan a $200,000 endorsement deal with Lexus that the league eventually ruled violated their anti-circumvention rules.

The violation involved a presentation made by the Clippers to free agent DeAndre Jordan on July 2 that improperly included a potential third-party endorsement opportunity for the player. While the NBA’s investigation ultimately concluded that the presentation of this opportunity had no impact on Jordan’s decision to re-sign with the Clippers, the team’s conduct nevertheless violated the league’s anti-circumvention rules.

The NBA’s anti-circumvention rules prohibit teams from, among other things, providing or arranging for others to provide any form of compensation to a player unless such compensation is included in a player contract or otherwise expressly permitted under the CBA.

The team was fined about $250,000 and that was the end of it besides the bitter grumbling of Dallas fans who were the team that ultimately got screwed in the whole proceedings. Wait, why does that sound…familiar?
