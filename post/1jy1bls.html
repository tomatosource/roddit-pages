Feedback from an SSF player – crafting and progression feel too luck-based, not skill-based

Hey folks,

I wanted to share some thoughts as a long-time Path of Exile player who’s been diving into PoE2, mostly in SSF. I’ve played a lot of SSF HC in PoE1, with around 1.5–2k hours logged there. I’ve killed most of the bosses, and I typically play about a month per league before taking a break until the next one. I’m currently around 150–200 hours into PoE2.

My favorite part of the game — in both PoE1 and PoE2 — is the problem-solving. I enjoy hitting a wall and figuring out how to push past it through smart decisions, not just brute force. I don’t mind if content is hard — in fact, I want it to be. But only if I have the tools to work toward solving it in a deterministic way.

And that’s where PoE2 is currently falling flat for me.

Crafting doesn’t feel like crafting

Right now, crafting is basically just item slamming. There’s no meaningful way to chase specific outcomes, which makes progression feel random and unearned. As an SSF player, this is especially frustrating. I don’t have trade to fall back on. My only path forward is drops and crafting — but the crafting system doesn’t give me a clear way to make incremental progress. I’m just rolling the dice over and over.

And on top of that, the admin overhead is worse than ever. Loot filters are now mandatory — not just any filter, but a finely tuned one that highlights specific white bases, because you need to pick up 5–6 white items per map just to have a shot at crafting something usable later. This adds a huge amount of time spent in hideout — easily the most boring part of the game.

It becomes a full-time job:

  • Checking stats on every item.
  • Trying to figure out if Tier 6 is a good roll or not.
  • Sorting and stashing items across multiple tabs.
  • Hoarding potential bases.
  • Waiting until you’ve got a full stash tab before doing a 10% recombine… And maybe — just maybe — getting something useful out of it.

It’s not engaging. It’s not fun. It’s just a time sink that doesn’t spark any joy. I get that SSF will always involve more micromanagement, but this doesn’t feel like meaningful decision-making — it just feels like busywork.

Progression is gated by luck, not skill

I’m running into issues I simply can’t solve unless I get lucky. For example, I need mana leech. There’s no deterministic way to get it without completely gimping my build. I can’t solve it through skill, smart gearing, or creative build tweaks — just RNG.

And that’s a trend I’m noticing more and more. My ability to succeed in a map isn’t really tied to how I play. It’s tied to whether I’ve hit the right number thresholds. If I do, the content melts. If I don’t, it walls me. No amount of skillful play changes that. It’s a pure numbers check.

“What did I gain from this map?”

This is what really kills my motivation. Every time I run a map, I ask myself: What did I gain from this? How did this help me move closer to killing the end bosses? And more often than not, the answer is: Nothing concrete. Maybe I got lucky. Maybe I didn’t. But I didn’t feel like I was working toward anything — just spinning a slot machine and hoping it eventually pays out.

Frustrating damage scaling – why copy the worst part of D4?

It feels like PoE2 has borrowed the worst kind of modern ARPG damage scaling — where your actual skill damage barely matters unless you trigger the “correct” set of conditions.

For example, I’m playing Huntress with Lightning Spear, and I hate how all my damage is locked behind electrocute and shock. The skill itself does pretty mediocre base damage, but suddenly I’m doing 50x more damage just because the boss is shocked, staggered, and I crit at the right time. That’s not satisfying — it’s not my play that made that happen, it’s the system finally allowing the damage multiplier to stack up.

This kind of design feels ripped straight from Diablo 4 — where numbers explode only when the stars align and a bunch of backend multipliers kick in. Why lean into that? It feels like it undermines the entire fantasy of building and scaling a skill you enjoy.

I’m not trying to be negative for the sake of it. I love this franchise. I’ve spent years in it. But PoE2, as it stands, feels like it’s stripping away the parts I loved — the challenge, the planning, the problem-solving — and replacing them with luck-gated progression and stat checks I can’t work around. Even worse, it’s leaning into modern ARPG design trends that sacrifice gameplay depth in favor of momentary dopamine spikes.

Curious if other SSF players feel the same. Am I missing something? Or are others running into the same walls?
