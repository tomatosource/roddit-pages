[OC] Vultures’ Meat Menu: which team may swoop in for Brandon Ingram?

With the calendar year winding down, it’s about time for several teams to officially throw in the towel on the 2024-25 season. They’ll stop fighting, stop trying, and fall down dead in the middle of the road.

And when that happens, it’s time for the vultures to swoop in and pick on their remains.

We’ll look at several menu items across the league, starting with some tasty entrees like:

THE BRANDON INGRAM BURGER

WHY HE’S ON THE MENU

The New Orleans Pelicans entered 2024-25 with high hopes and a Vegas over/under of 46.5 wins. I’m no Kreskin, but I suspect that won’t hit the “over.” In fact, the Pelicans currently have the worst record in the entire NBA at 5-27.

At full strength the roster is surely better than that, but it’s too late to salvage this season. It’s time for the Pelicans to look to 2025 and beyond. And because of that, any player who may not be on the roster for the long haul should be on the market. That includes veteran C.J. McCollum and our featured item Brandon Ingram, who is on an expiring contract.

INGREDIENTS

Almost all lanky scorers get compared to Kevin Durant, but Brandon Ingram does a better job with that imitation than most. The former # 2 pick has mostly lived up to his pedigree, averaging over 20 PPG the last six seasons. He made the All-Star in 2020, but has been a tick below that since then.

You can describe Ingram as a poor man’s/homeless man’s Durant in other ways as well. He’s a good – but not great shooter. He has a similar shot diet to Durant as well, taking double the amount of 2’s to 3’s. If he split that ratio more evenly, he may be a more efficient player.

However, Ingram is not going to be a stand in the corner and shoot 3’s player. And if he was, it may not be the best use of his talent. He’s used to having the ball – and he’s pretty good at all. He’s an underrated ball mover, averaging 5+ assists over the past four seasons. He’s also improved as a free-throw shooter. He shot only 66.0% from the line in his first three years in L.A., but has boosted that to 84.7% in New Orleans. If you take away the ball from him, you’re taking away his most efficient offense. That makes him a good player, but also one that’s harder to integrate on teams that already have their offensive system set.

Defensively, Ingram has disappointed. He’s got a long frame (6’8” with a 7’3” wingspan), which should give him positional versatility. But instead, he plays more like a “tweener” in a negative way. He’s not strong enough to bang with top PFs, and he’s not explosive or engaged enough to slow down top perimeter wings. His lack of rim protection (0.6 blocks per 36) is also a frustration. Overall, advanced stats have graded him as a mild negative (-0.7 BPM) on the defensive end.

Through his career so far, Ingram has been treated as a “star” player and top scoring option. We’ve seen the ceiling of that, and it’s limited. In an ideal world, you’d treat him as either your 2nd or 3rd option. In today’s NBA, that should merit $30-35M in value. He should maintain that level for a while. Although he’s already in season 9, Ingram is still only 27 years old. His durability has become a growing question mark, but that could be said for most NBA players these days.

ITEM COST

Of course, a player’s trade value depends on several other factors.

For Brandon Ingram, the primary one is his impending free agency. The Pelicans are paying Ingram $36M this season, but he’ll enter unrestricted free agency this offseason.

That makes any potential trade a delicate business. Any team that trades for Ingram should do so with the intention (or even a handshake agreement) to extend him on a hefty contract. But can you commit to a big extension without seeing how he gels with your team first? And will Ingram agree to an extension without testing the market?

If you’re only assured a half season rental from Ingram, that deflates his value significantly. With no optimism that he’d stay around, he may only fetch a mid R1 pick in a deal. But if you can agree on a favorable extension ahead of time then that would likely double the price. Of course, you’d also have to match the salary to make the math work.

POTENTIAL VULTURES

Cleveland

For the last few years, the narrative is that Cleveland has a hole at SF. In theory, they can plug Brandon Ingram right in there and have them an awesome starting 5. The Cavs also have some decent pieces and mid-level salaries to throw back New Orleans’ way, including Isaac Okoro (\(10M + \)11M + $12M). But for Cleveland, the question may be: do they even need Ingram? Or are they (at 27-4) good enough as is? If they want to make a move like this, it’d be with the presumption that Ingram can become a very efficient player if he had stars around him.

Detroit

Detroit got tired of losing, so they spent their offseason bringing in veteran shooters to help out Cade Cunningham and company. The early returns have been promising, so perhaps they’d double down and get a secondary star in Brandon Ingram to make a playoff (or play-in) push. They also have a fairly clean cap sheet going forward, so they’d be more open to giving Ingram a big extension than most others. Of course, to trade for him now, they’d still need to match salaries in the meantime. For that to happen, they may need to convince New Orleans that “Beef Stew” Isaiah Stewart has an appetizing value on his contract (\(15M + \)15M + \(15M + \)15M).

Golden State

The Golden State Warriors already brought in Dennis Schroder, but that can’t be their end game. Trading Jonathan Kuminga (\(8M) would be the big swing, but they could also dangle Moses Moody as well. Moody isn't a huge carrot, but his skill set and salary (\)6M + \(12M + \)13M + \(13M) is an easy piece for a team like New Orleans to integrate. If they like him, perhaps they'd even take him and some contracts like Kyle Anderson (\)9M + \(9M + \)10M) to make the math work.

Memphis

Now that they’re back to their familiar place in the playoff field, Memphis is apparently in the market for an upgrade at the wings. They’d be able to offer a R1 pick or two and matching salaries (perhaps Marcus Smart at \(20M + \)22M and Brandon Clarke at \(13M + \)13M + $13M).

Milwaukee

The Bucks have surged back up the standings in concert with Khris Middleton’s return, although the two may not be as related as they appear. Middleton still doesn’t look 100%, and at age 33, he may never be again. Would the franchise and the fan base be okay flipping him and his remaining salary (\(32M + \)34M) in order to get a younger player like Ingram? Middleton’s value may be negative right now, so Minnesota would need to include picks or recent picks like A.J. Johnson to make it happen.

MY FAVORITE MUNCH / MATCH

Looking through the list of vultures, I would personally lean to the L.A. Lakers as the most logical match if a trade happens. The Lakers already made one “vulture” move, acquiring Dorian Finney-Smith from the Brooklyn Nets. Adding DFS could help at the margins. Losing D’Angelo Russell may help at the margins. But all in all, I don’t think that trade will significantly change their fate as the 15th-ranked offense in the league.

Living in L.A. and watching the team night in and night out, I just don’t have much optimism for them in their current iteration. They’re asking too much of LeBron James – who is now 40 years old on the dot (happy birthday!). At this age, the King simply can’t dominate in the half court and can’t be counted on to run the engine for 70+ games a year.

Another issue with the team is a continued lack of three-point shooting. Coach J.J. Redick has made it a point of emphasis, but the team is still only 29th in 3PA. As mentioned in the body of the post, Ingram could stand to shoot more threes, but he’s more of a threat from there than Rui Hachimura. Hachimura’s percentages are actually fine, but he’s still a reluctant three-point shooter and not one that scares defenses.

Adding Ingram’s shooting and playmaking would help the team’s stars in the short term. He’d also form a decent yin/yang with Anthony Davis going forward, especially given AD’s defensive protection. It’d also be a funny, full circle moment given the fact that Ingram was a central piece of the trade that brought AD to L.A. in the first place.

To acquire Ingram, the Lakers may not need to include draft picks. The math would work if they include Rui Hachimura (\(17M + \)18M), Max Christie (\(7M + \)8M + \(8M + \)9M player option), and salary filler (likely Gabe Vincent or Jarred Vanderbilt). That may not jump out as a great return on paper, but Hachimura is a playable rotational player and Christie (still only 21) has been quietly getting better and may be the most appealing part of the package. It’d amount to a mild positive return – but that’s better than nothing if you’ve already convinced yourself that you’re not re-signing Ingram. And with a deal like this, you could officially send up the white flag and make a mad dash for the lottery.
