[San Antonio Express-News] NBA rules 77 games from Gregg Popovich’s record go to Mitch Johnson

In a bit of coaching record housekeeping, the league office confirmed Wednesday the 77 games Johnson coached last season in the wake of Gregg Popovich’s stroke will go toward Johnson’s career ledger.

Of course that means Johnson — formally named the team’s permanent head coach in May — will begin his coaching career this fall with a losing tally (32-45).

Popovich officially finishes his Hall of Fame career at 1,390-824. He is still the winningest coach in NBA history by a fairly healthy margin, with 55 more than Don Nelson.

…

Generally, when a coach is forced to cede the reins to an acting head coach for a short amount of time, due to illness or other emergency, the wins and losses remain with the original coach.

Considering Popovich did not return at all, and Johnson was eventually named his full-time successor, the NBA believes the final 77 games of the season belong to Johnson.

In that, there is precedent. In November 1979, the Los Angeles Lakers lost coach Jack McKinney to a near-fatal bicycle accident. He was replaced by assistant Paul Westhead for the final 68 games. Westhead led the Lakers to a 50-18 finish, as well as that season’s NBA title, all of which were added to his coaching record.
