Fernando Tatis Jr.’s stance angle jumped from 29° to 38-45° open in 2025 — and his barrel rate fell off a cliff. His WBC swing suggests he’s fixed it.

One of the more interesting mechanical stories heading into 2026 is what happened to Fernando Tatis Jr. last season and what appears to be happening now.

In 2025 Tatis put up a solid-on-paper .268/.368/.446 with 25 HR, 32 SB and 5.9 fWAR. But the Statcast numbers told a different story under the surface. His barrel rate fell from 14.5% (92nd percentile in 2024) down to 10.9% (66th percentile). His bat speed dropped from 74.7 to 73.6 mph. His attack angle flattened to 7-8 degrees.

The mechanical culprit, per Statcast stance tracking: his front foot opened from 29° in 2024 to somewhere between 38-45° in 2025, one of the largest stance changes of any hitter in the league that year. An excessively open front foot bleeds hip rotation and creates timing problems — exactly what showed up in his barrel numbers.

Watching him in the WBC, the stance looks noticeably more closed. Hands are lower and tighter. The load is quieter. He hit a grand slam today against Israel — the first in DR WBC history — with the kind of hip-to-hands sequence that generates elite exit velocity. His plate discipline from 2025 (career-high 12.9 BB%, career-low 18.7 K%) isn’t going anywhere either.

He’s also moving down from leadoff to third or fourth in the order this year, which he’s said he prefers: “I feel like I create more chaos when I have situations.”
