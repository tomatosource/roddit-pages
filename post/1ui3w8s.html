The 2nd apron is basically killing the star trade market

The only trades that go thru now are ones that significantly undervalue the star player: Giannis got traded for a package centered around Tyler Herro, the Blazers are reportedly turning down Jaylen Brown trades involving Donavon Clingan. Luka got traded for a first round pick and a couple pieces of lint.

The 2nd apron is basically making star trades so restrictive that moving stars is not worth it. Star players​ typically demand large trade packages, however their contracts now functionally constitute even ​more of a teams cap space than ever, due to the penalties imposed by the 2nd apron, and most teams seem hesitant to take on more than one of these contracts at a time. Giannis and Luka only got moved because their​ relationships with their teams broke down in the first place, with their trade value becoming diminished enough for a team to be willing to make a deal.

Jaylen Brown would have been the perfect star target 5 years ago - healthy, playoff performer, coming off the best season of his career - yet it seems like few if any teams are going to pay fair value for Jaylen Brown, due to the inherent risk of having so much financial value in one player. Even if the Celtics fully wanted to move him, its hard to imagine a team paying full price for a player of Brown’s quality given many of the offers that have been reported.

The 2nd apron has basically twisted the trade value calculus to the point where its increasingly difficult to trade stars players, with many teams seeing them as not worth their true on-court value due to the the restrictive nature of the salary cap.

Personally I think this is pretty lame - star trades are one of the best and most fun ways to rebalance the league - for instance, Brown being traded to Den of Atl would reinvigorate the Nuggets or vault the Hawks into contention. It also feels slightly disrespectful to the great players of the league to see them moved for trade packages that clearly don’t match their value. However given the present risks involved with giving up large amount of assets for a large value contract, I believe we’ll be seeing less and less of these types of deals.
