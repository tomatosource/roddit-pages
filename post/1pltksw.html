The Sabermetric Hall of Fame Case for Jimmy Rollins

Yesterday I posted an analysis of this year’s BBWAA ballot, and the most interesting part of it all to me was how Jimmy Rollins’s candidacy started to look.

Rollins usually doesn’t find much support among the “advanced stats” crowd, considering his sub-50 WAR and below-average bat at shortstop. Instead, his candidacy usually rests on more traditional metrics of greatness, like his MVP, his captaincy of a World Series winner, and just overall vibes.

But there is an advanced stats angle for his candidacy, and it revolves around two things: era adjustment, and Win Probability Added (WPA).

From an isolated value perspective, Rollins falls short of the standard: 48-49 WAR with a not-quite HOF-level peak are what most see. But if we adjust his contributions for era—like considering the talent level available in MLB during his time—his eWAR is more in the 56-57 range. A substantial increase, but not unique to him; contemporary players are bound to have more favorable eWAR totals by nature of being contemporary players.

What’s more unique to Rollins is when we bring WPA into the picture. In isolation, Rollins was a below-average hitter, with a career Batting Wins total around -5 or -6. But his career WPA is around 9. Not -9. +9.

Batting Wins and WPA both measure only hitting and are both set to an average of 0, so we’re not dealing with apples and oranges here. What we are dealing with is a timing discrepancy: Rollins may have been below average at the plate, but he timed that production in an above average manner. Rollins’s hitting profile thus looks a lot better if we introduce context like base-out state, game situation, and clutch.

If we blend WPA into the Batting component of WAR, Rollins benefits from yet another boost. If we “leverage” and “era-adjust” his WAR, we’re looking at a 66-67 eLWAR. That’s a lot closer to Hall of Fame territory, and puts him firmly in the mix with other candidates on the ballot rather than lagging behind like he normally does sabermetrically.

This isn’t to say Rollins is therefore a surefire Hall of Famer. This is just to say that there is an angle to paint his candidacy in a better light that I think could be worthwhile to explore. In isolation he falls short, but with era and context in the frame, he has a real case that isn’t just based on vibes.

Thanks for reading! I’d be curious to hear others’ thoughts on Rollins.
