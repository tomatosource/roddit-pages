Tadahito Iguchi emerges as the leading candidate to become Samurai Japan’s next manager. If hired, he’d be the first skipper with MLB playing experience, and would team up with Shohei Ohtani in pursuit of Olympic gold at the 2028 Los Angeles Games.

Article in Japanese: https://news.yahoo.co.jp/articles/bb7548a03111589430ff5fb9aa485c4986518553

Former Chiba Lotte Marines manager Tadahito Iguchi has emerged as the leading candidate to become the next manager of Samurai Japan, sources revealed Wednesday.

If officially appointed, Iguchi would become the first manager in Samurai Japan history with Major League playing experience. His first tournament in charge would be the 2026 Asian Professional Baseball Championship this November, with the long-term goal of leading Japan to a gold medal at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, where active MLB players are expected to participate for the first time.

The 2028 Olympic roster is expected to be built around MLB stars, including Shohei Ohtani, making Iguchi’s major league experience and relationships throughout the game a significant asset. Following Japan’s quarterfinal exit at the 2026 World Baseball Classic, the Samurai Japan committee has been searching for a manager capable of returning the program to the top of the baseball world.

Iguchi would replace Hirokazu Ibata, whose contract recently expired. NPB Executive Director Katsuhiko Nakamura previously indicated the federation hoped to have a new manager in place before player selection begins in October.

A three-time Japan Series champion as a player with the Hawks and Marines, Iguchi also won the 2005 World Series with the Chicago White Sox. He finished his career with 2,254 hits, 295 home runs, and 224 stolen bases across NPB and MLB.

After retiring, Iguchi managed the Marines from 2018-22, where he helped develop pitchers including Roki Sasaki, now with the Los Angeles Dodgers. He currently works as a baseball analyst covering MLB and remains well connected throughout both Japanese and American baseball.

Beyond managing on the field, the Samurai Japan skipper also plays a major role in roster construction and coordinating with MLB clubs regarding player availability. During this year’s World Baseball Classic, communication with tournament officials and MLB organizations regarding the availability of major leaguers proved difficult, complicating Japan’s roster planning.

Iguchi’s relationships in the United States remain strong. Earlier this month, he threw out the ceremonial first pitch before a Red Sox–Angels game, another example of his continued ties to Major League Baseball.
The Olympics also carry personal significance for Iguchi. He represented Japan at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics, where the team won the silver medal after falling to Cuba in the gold medal game. Thirty-two years later, with the Summer Games returning to the United States, Iguchi could have the opportunity to chase the Olympic gold medal that eluded him as a player.

Before Los Angeles, Japan must first qualify through the 2027 Premier12, which will also serve as an Olympic qualifier. Samurai Japan is expected to rely primarily on NPB players in that tournament, with the highest-finishing Asian nation earning an Olympic berth.

Japan’s MLB contingent has continued to grow, with a record eight major leaguers appearing in the 2026 World Baseball Classic. That number could increase even further for the Los Angeles Olympics, making a manager with firsthand MLB experience and established relationships across both leagues an increasingly valuable asset.
