WH/ER: An interesting unofficial pitching stat

While ERA and WHIP are two of the most common stats used to gauge pitching performance, I wondered what would happen if there was one statistic combining the two metrics. By dividing a pitcher’s WHIP by their ERA/9 (earned runs per inning), the “per-innings” part of the math cancels out and you are left with a simple statistic measuring on average how many walks and hits does it take for an offense to earn a run on a given pitcher.

After taking this stat for the top 50 qualified MLB starting pitchers by ERA, I found that there was a slight correlation with pitchers having a lower WH/ER giving up more homeruns, and that there was a noticeable correlation between WH/ER and LOB%. Below will be a graph of WH/ER vs LOB%

This stat alone is not a useful stat to directly gauge the value of a pitcher. If you were to attempt to compare pitcher’s value by this stat alone, you would come to the false conclusion that a pitcher with a 1.35 WHIP and a 4.50 ERA is having a season as good as a pitcher with a .90 whip and a 3.00 ERA.

I’m still trying to wrap my head around the full usefulness of this stat and its applications but wanted to share it to potentially get some input and see what other people thought of it and ideas of how to interpret this stat.
