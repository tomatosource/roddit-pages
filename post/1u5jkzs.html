PoE2 thoughts as a HCSSF player

This season I played for now:
• 2 characters to 90+ in HCSSF and 3 characters 80+ in HC

After 1800 hours, I’ve realized how I enjoy PoE2 the most. It is the challenge and progression up until around level 90, when I’ve killed Arbiter, unlocked most of the Atlas, and upgrades become very small while farming 6 to 8 mod maps.

HCSSF forces you to slow down and lengthen this journey. Decision making matters more. When do you take bosses? How do you balance defense and speed? Those choices are what make the game exciting to me. This meaningful pace is what differentiates PoE2 from PoE1 for me, and I’m very happy it’s still part of the game.

I also understand the challenge GGG faces. You have speedrunners, casual players, giga juicers, and players like me. Keeping all of those groups happy is difficult, and I think GGG has done a great job overall.

With that in mind, here’s my feedback from a HCSSF perspective excluding class balance:

Good

• Endgame feels great right and immersive now. There are so many things to unlock and work towards.

• Atlas progression feels rewarding because you slowly build it out over time.

• Campaign tweaks are noticeable. Waterways, Freythorn, and Vasiri Outskirts with the added checkpoint are great examples of how small changes can improve pacing.

• League mechanics have clear identities and feel good for target farming. Breach for rings, amulets, and belts. Delirium for annoints and jewels. Ritual for uniques. etc. Each mechanic has its own purpose.

• The new Expedition feels much smoother now. The “What do I want to blow up?” decision-making is fun and gives players more clear cut rewards.

• Build diversity is in a great spot. I can play almost any build I enjoy and still reach endgame and kill Arbiter. The question has shifted from “What clears content?” to “What looks fun to play?”

• The midgame crafting changes feel good. It takes longer before characters truly ramp up, which makes progression more meaningful.

• Act 1 and most of Act 2 truly have a special feeling to them. Mapping eventually captures that feeling again through questlines, increasing monster density, and Atlas progression gradually adding difficulty.

• The defensive changes feel good. Energy Shield is still in an exceptional spot, and reaching the 40k Armour breakpoint for near-immortality has become more achievable.

• There are so many exciting “wow” moments. 8 to 9 rune Expedition encounters, more unexpected Divine drops, and 6-boss Rituals immediately come to mind. These are the moments players love, and there are plenty of them.

• I love that we still have really tough boss mechanics which make you sweat crazy like the Arbiters, I think my heartbeat was around 170 during the fight. They should feel something to build towards and practice for (more in HC).

Improvements

• The start of endgame can feel overwhelming with so many quests and progression paths available. The dilemma between investing in Atlas progression or league mechanics is noticeable.

• Abyss feels mandatory in SSF because target crafting options are limited.

• Ward on monsters feels strange because it’s difficult to judge how dangerous they are once their life is depleted. Potions on Rogue Exiles also feel unnecessary. Larger health pools with perhaps one emergency potion use would probably achieve the same goal.

• Ward stacking on players feels weak unless heavily optimized or more exploited because it sacrifices too many other defensive layers.

• Wisps have received recent changes, but overall they still feel underwhelming.

• Lootboxes have a lot of potential, but in practice most players only look for Researcher boxes and skip the rest. I feel there is a lot of room to improve them through small adjustments.

• Loathsome Mire feels underwhelming compared to the expectations it creates. While it offers the possibility of a one-in-a-thousand jackpot amulet, the encounter itself could be more engaging and rewarding on average.

• Recombinators are missed, especially for rare affixes such as Spirit and +level modifiers in endgame crafting. The current version feels too broad and doesn’t add much value for the average player. I still think there is room for them if they were redesigned in a more controlled way, such as selecting one affix from each item with a success chance.

• I like not immediately knowing where every objective is during mapping. Exploring for the boss and mechanics is fun. However, searching for the final Abyss pit after you’ve already completed everything else happens too often and could use a small tweak.

• There is something about Bahlak, the Sky Seer, (read: Tornado Bird) both in the Trial and on the Cenotaph map, that still feels overtuned. Even after the recent nerfs to bosses and Abyss, he remains extremely deadly, and it doesn’t seem to be the hurricane phase.

• Uniques are still mostly a niche product even with ward upgrading. Nightfall should be a ground target skill.

Campaign and map sizes (own section because it needs more love)

• First playthroughs feel amazing. Replayability starts to suffer from late Act 2 through Act 4, which discourages me from creating new characters. Bigger maps should be more linear in general, while smaller maps can afford to be more complex. Mawdun Quarry and new Dreadnought are great examples. Even Drowned City works well if players just follow the road.

• Valley of the Titans portals feel like a band-aid solution. The original concept of circling around and returning to the center felt more immersive. I think the world should challenge the player rather than assist them with portals.

• Jungle Ruins and Venom Pits move in the right direction, but now players skip most of Jungle Ruins, which is a shame because it’s a really cool map.

• I think one of the biggest questions for PoE2 moving forward is map size. The Atlas encourages exploration through unique maps and surprises, and that’s one of its biggest strengths.

What makes the open world exciting is the sense of adventure and discovery. However, if a map takes around six minutes on average, excluding giga-endgame juicers, players can realistically complete only around ten maps per hour before accounting for looting, selling, and stash management.

The problem is that I often hit my personal breakpoint before that excitement can fully develop. There seems to be a mismatch between map size and complexity, player speed, and light radius that still needs tweaking. I don’t think the answer is simply giving players more movement speed or traversal skills.

I could go over every map with specific feedback, but I don’t know if GGG actually reads posts like these.

The one immediate wish:

• Since the endgame has become so immersive, I would love to see a true solo mode where characters share no stash and no Atlas progression. I want to experience that journey and hardship from scratch multiple times in a season.

 Overall, the game has been an absolute blast. Most of what I’ve mentioned are small, incremental improvements to systems that are already in a very good place. I’m only this detailed because I’m passionate about the game and want to see it continue to succeed and I’m excited to see GGG continue building on top of it!
