Old Folks Home: where should the old free agents land?

We’re in the last stages of free agency, so it’s fitting to focus on some free agents who may be in the last stage of their career. Here’s a look at the landscape for some old timers (and likely Hall of Famers) and suggestions for where they may spend their golden years.

PG Chris Paul, turned 40 years old in May

current value: Quietly, Chris Paul played in all 82 games for the Spurs last year, an impressive feat for someone who hadn’t played over 70 games in any season since 2015-16. In terms of his production and workload while he’s on the court, he’s definitely taken a step back. He’s still a steady field general (7.4 assists, 1.6 TO), but he’s not a major threat as a scorer. He only took 7.1 FGA and 1.1 FTA per game. In some ways, he’s similar to a steady backup point guard like a Tre/Tyus Jones right now.

best fits

Buddies LeBron James and Chris Paul have never pushed to play with each other, perhaps with the knowledge that both like the ball in their hands. Now that CP3 is essentially a backup PG, could this be the right time? I’m still not convinced. The Lakers will stagger James and Luka Doncic’s minutes so there’s always one lead playmaker on the court. The team also has Gabe Vincent and his $11M salary on the books. Given all that, there’s not much room for Paul on the team right now. The same may be true for the L.A. Clippers after signing Bradley Beal; both Beal and James Harden can work as a playmaking combo guard.

But if we’re saying Chris Paul is best served as a Tyus Jones type right now, how about replacing Jones (and reuniting with his old team) in Phoenix? The Suns will use Devin Booker as a primary playmaker, but there’s not much behind him aside from plucky young Collin Gillespie. I don’t think the Suns want to embrace a full rebuild (since they don’t own their pick anyway), so maybe they want a veteran backup instead.

C Al Horford, turned 39 years old in June

current value: As he nears the end, Al Hoford’s skill set is more en vogue than ever. Over the last three seasons, he’s taken over half his field goal attempts from beyond the arc, hitting 41%. It’s an impressive evolution for a big man who hit a grand total of 10 three pointers over the first 7 seasons in the NBA combined. Defensively, he still grades as a positive defender (+1.1 BPM), although he’s only average at rebounding and rim protection. On a 1 year deal, he’d still be capable of starting and playing 25 minutes for most teams.

best fits

Celtics fans like Bill Simmons have speculated that Al Horford would want to play another season for Boston on another team-friendly deal. That may happen. But selfishly, why should he do that? He’s already made over $280M in salary in his career. He doesn’t need the money. He doesn’t need a ring. And if he stays in Boston, it’s unlikely that he’d even have a chance for that anyway without Jayson Tatum around.

With cap space drying up, it’d be reasonable for Horford to ride off into the sunset. If he wants to stick around, teams like Golden State, Boston, Charlotte, and Indiana would be his best chance at starting. The Warriors are apparently the frontrunners for him – and they do make sense.

But if Al Horford has one foot in, one foot out (towards retirement), then my mind drifts to Miami. The Heat have started to give Bam Adebayo more minutes at the 4, with young Kel’el Ware teaming up with him down low. But as Erik Spoelstra mentioned, Ware (still 21) may need some growing up to do. Horford can be a stopgap and a mentor, without an unreasonable expectation for his workload. More than anything, Miami wins this battle based on quality of lifestyle and post-career lifestyle. If he’s got an eye to retirement, Horford may want to settle in Miami, nearer to his family in the Dominican, rather than live with his suitcase half packed.

PF Kevin Love, turning 37 years old in September

current value: Unlike the prior two oldies, Kevin Love’s days as a useful rotation player may be over. He still has the shooting of a “stretch big,” but the erosion to the rest of his game has caused his MPG to drift from 20 to 17 to 11.9 last season. If he wants to stick around, his primary value may be as a mentor.

best fits

When you look for prior ties for Kevin Love, you mostly come up empty in terms of “fit.” LeBron James and Lakers have their centers (including a returning Maxi Kleber as their stretch big). Cleveland signed Larry Nance Jr. to be their smallball backup big man. Minnesota has good frontcourt depth as well.

But would Kevin Love play anywhere? Maybe Boston. Maybe Charlotte. Maybe some minutes on Philly. But if his value is as a mentor, then I like him in Dallas most of all. He can be a guide for Cooper Flagg, and a sounding board and bench-mate for former teammate Kyrie Irving as he recovers from injury. There’s some value to him as a depth play (the Mavs’ frontcourt is stacked but not exactly filled with iron men), but it’d mostly be for his veteran locker room presence. And like Horford, Love would be landing somewhere without a state income tax.

PG Russell Westbrook, turning 37 in November

current value: Russell Westbrook’s body hasn’t aged as poorly as his skill set. He can still get up and down the court and provide some energy and athleticism, but his adjustment to a new role hasn’t been seamless. He hit some big open threes in the playoffs, but was still logging just 31.7% from three in the postseason (not much different from his 30.6% in the last two regular seasons). He’s also mistake-prone, averaging 4.2 TO per 36 last year. That said, a team may look at the raw numbers (13.3 PPG, 6.1 APG) and consider him a good backup PG.

best fits

Russell Westbrook’s camp can make some calls to teams that may want backup PGs: like Golden State, Dallas, Milwaukee. But the question is: would any of those teams see him as an actual upgrade? I don’t know.

A return to Denver feels most likely, but I’m not bowled over by it either. The Nuggets brought back Bruce Brown to help out the bench. Brown is not a pure point guard playmaker in the same way that Westbrook is, but there’s still some duplication there. I don’t see the two of them playing well on the court together.

Given that, I’m leaning to a reunion with his other former team with the L.A. Clippers instead. Westbrook played pretty well for the Clippers two years ago. And notably, he had a nice stint with Bradley Beal and the Wizards a few years back. As mentioned in the Chris Paul section, the Clippers don’t need a lot of playmaking help, so Westbrook would have to accept this job with the knowledge that his minutes would be limited. At this stage in his career, settling back home in L.A. with a title contender (or close to it) may be the best scenario.
