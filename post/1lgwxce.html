[OC] 99 Cent Store: Guards

The NBA offseason is always filled with exciting storylines like star free agents and blockbuster trades.

But rather than dwell on the obvious, this series intends to do the opposite: focus on the lower-profile free agents who may have some value to teams. No NBA player is actually “99 Cents,” of course, but these are all players who may be bargains based on their perceived market. We try to organize the listing in terms of tiers. “Featured items” may get $10M a year, while “clearance rack” listings tend to be players on the fringes of NBA rosters who may take minimum deals.

This “99 Cent Store” series has been open for business since 2018. In the past, we’ve highlighted reserves like Fred VanVleet and Isaiah Hartenstein among others prior to their breakouts. Not all of the items turn out to be gems, but the returns have been mostly positive so far. Let’s see if we can keep that legacy going this season.

######featured items

PG/SG Quentin Grimes, Philadelphia, RFA, 25 years old

If you’re looking for a traditional point guard, there are a plethora of affordable options in this class: including 99 Store alums like Tyus Jones, his brother Tre Jones, and their illegitimate brother Monte Morris. All three are “steady eddies” who will pass first, pass second, pass third, and avoid turnovers like an L.A. preschool avoids peanuts.

If you’re looking for a traditional, pass-first point guard, Quentin Grimes is NOT your guy. A bluechip high school prospect, Grimes went to Kansas with some lottery buzz and expectations as a star lead guard. He didn’t deliver on that, as he didn’t show any passing aptitude in his three years in college. In fact, he only averaged 2.2 assists per game. As a result, Grimes developed into a more traditional shooting guard instead.

Grimes has been something of a forgotten man since then, but he had GMs’ ears perking up after he got traded to Philadelphia in midseason. Across 28 games, he averaged 21.9 points, 1.5 steals, and a respectable 4.5 assists. There’s always a danger of judging based on a small sample size (in a lost season), but it’s enough to wonder: is this legit? Is there more upside here than we’ve seen before?

One reason that I’m optimistic about Grimes’ upside is the “eye test.” He’s a savvy player who can shoot well (37.5% from three in the NBA). Plenty of guards can shoot 37% on wide-open shots these days, but Grimes has the quick release and sound mechanics to suggest he can sustain that on a larger volume. Among all the “no name” free agents, he has the most potential to be a 20 PPG scorer in the right situation. And if he turns out to be just an ordinary SG, he at least has the size (6’5”, 6’8” wingspan) to be a rotational player in that role as well.

Advertising Grimes in the window of the 99 Cent Store may be misleading, because he may get a fat contract this summer. But then again, he may not. Philadelphia owns his restricted free agency rights, but they’re constrained by what they can pay him on their own. Moreover, they already have some scoring guards around in Tyrese Maxey and Jared McCain and possibly another one coming in the draft. Given that, Grimes may be more “free” than “restricted” if a team can find the room to spend.

potential fits

BKN. The Nets also have scoring guards around (D’Angelo Russell, Cam Thomas, both free agents as well) – but they also have cap space to burn. They can afford to overpay on some upside swings.

CHA. Swingman Josh Green didn’t have a great debut year for the Hornets, so maybe Quentin Grimes can give them more reliable play at the SG spot.

DET. We’ve seen that Cade Cunningham thrives with spacing around him, which makes Grimes a potential fit. At age 25, he also fits their timeline more than current vets like Dennis Schroder (31) and Tim Hardaway Jr. (33).

PG/SG Nickeil Alexander-Walker, Minnesota, UFA, 27 years old

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander had to wait his turn for a chance to be “the guy,” and he took off like a rocket ship.

His cousin, Nickeil Alexander-Walker, had a blastoff that looked more like the work of Space-X. He had an opportunity to seize a starring role in New Orleans back in 2021 (after the team let Lonzo Ball walk), but he didn’t deliver, and he ended up getting tossed away to Utah and then Minnesota.

Over the years since then, NAW has had the time to lick his wounds and rebrand himself as a defensive specialist instead, utilizing his size (6’5”, 6’9” wingspan) to his advantage. What’s even more exciting is that he’s started to develop into a more reliable shooter as well, hitting over 38% from three in each of the last three seasons. If you couple the two talents together – good defense, decent shooting – then you have the makings of a starting guard on certain teams. Ideally, you’d play him as a de facto point guard on a team that has a playmaking star at another position.

Is Minnesota one of those teams? It’ll be interesting to see what the T-Wolves think. Right now, their PG spot is a question mark. Mike Conley looks too old – and Rob Dillingham may be too young. In theory, Alexander-Walker can help bridge that gap. But at the same time, can you afford to pay him $10-15M a year to be a bridge? If a team swoops in with a longer-term contract offer, it may test the Wolves’ banking limit.

potential fits

MIN. Keeping Nickeil Alexander-Walker seems like the most likely outcome, presuming the Wolves can figure out their cap situation. Julius Randle and Naz Reid both have player options and will be difficult to keep as well.

DAL. The Mavericks may bounce back into playoff contention with the combo of Anthony Davis and Cooper Flagg, but PG Kyrie Irving will miss most of the year with an ACL injury. Someone like Nickeil Alexander-Walker could hold down the fort at PG and then play minutes alongside Irving when he returns.

DEN. The money situation is tricky here, but Alexander-Walker could help soak up some minutes for the thin Nuggets and support his fellow Canadian Jamal Murray.

######99 cent store

SG Luke Kennard, Memphis, UFA, 28 years old

It’s strange – shooting is at a premium in the NBA, but the league may be devaluing (and undervaluing?) players who can only offer shooting. SG Buddy Hield is a good example, as his limited size and defense forced him to take a below-market contract for under \(9M last year. **Luke Kennard** is a similar model, given his limited size and wingspan. He may struggle to match his \)9M salary from last year.

But make no mistake, Kennard (and Buddy Hield) are both elite at what they do. Kennard may be a top 5 pure shooter in the NBA, knocking down 43.8% from three and 88.1% from the FT line over the course of his career.

Kennard’s physical limitations (and injuries) have kept him as a matchup player for Memphis, but circumstances were not ideal. He had been buried behind a better version of himself in Desmond Bane. Now that Bane has been (shockingly!) traded, does that open up room for Kennard here? Or do the Grizz simply not like him much? Either way, it’s possible Kennard can be a surprise factor for some team this year, a la Malik Beasley for Detroit this past season.

######clearance rack

SG Antonio Reeves, New Orleans, non-guaranteed contract, 25 years old

We’re going back to the well with another shoot-first, pass-never guard in Antonio Reeves. Reeves has been seen as more of a “college player” after 5 years on campus (3 at Illinois State, 2 at Kentucky). In his last season with the Wildcats, he averaged 20.2 points but only 1.6 assists per game. As an undersized and over-aged SG, he was a 2nd round pick and NBA afterthought.

But again, this kid can shoot it. He hit 44.7% from three as a senior, and 39.5% from three as a rookie in the NBA. There’s some value there. The Pelicans can retain him (for a reasonable $2M) but they can also waive him and send him into the free agency pool. They should keep him, but you never know what that franchise will do.

SG Mason Jones, Sacramento, RFA (two-way), 26 years old

I’ve always been intrigued by Mason Jones, another undersized scoring 2-guard. He’s always been productive in the G-League (21.6 points per game on good 50/43/83 splits for his career), but he’s a stocky 6’4” guard who presumably wouldn’t be able to translate that to the “majors.” That said, he has been able to draw 6.8 free throws per 36 in the NBA (albeit in 458 total minutes). Could he be a poor man’s Cam Thomas? Possibly.

Wait, did you want something more than a “poor man’s Cam Thomas”? Then get out of the clearance rack of our 99 Cent Store, you snob.
