Bench players deserve more love

I feel like bench and role players almost never get the respect they deserve. Every highlight reel, debate show, or post on social media is always about the stars, but the guys who don’t get a lot of minutes or attention are just as valuable for keeping a team together and ready for anything. The Celtics might be the best example of what it means to “stay ready.” It’s not just the starters. It’s guys like Payton Pritchard, Sam Hauser, Luke Kornet, and Neemias Queta who bring energy every single day even when their minutes are unpredictable. They keep practices competitive, lift the starters’ energy, and when their name gets called, they’re ready to go. So many games are changed because of those “in-between” players. Someone dives on the floor, hits a corner three, or grabs a rebound that keeps a play alive. You don’t see those plays on highlight reels, but they can flip the game completely. Without that kind of depth and readiness, no team can handle a long season or a playoff run. What makes this Celtics group special is that everyone seems bought in. No one is complaining about minutes or worried about stats. It’s all about the team and the bigger goal. That kind of culture doesn’t appear overnight. It’s built by the bench guys who show up ready every single day. I just wish more fans and media gave them credit. Not every player has to be a star to matter. Being “ready,” both mentally and physically, is a skill too. These guys deserve more respect for keeping the team sharp, the locker room tight, and the season steady when injuries or lineup changes happen. Next time Pritchard comes in and heats up after barely playing, or Hauser spaces the floor perfectly for Tatum to drive, remember that it is not luck. It is preparation. And that’s what separates good teams from great ones.
