Despite being 30th in the league in FTAs/game, 21st in the league in TS%, #1 in Mid-Range FGs made per game and 0 games played from their 5x All NBA Superstar, the Celtics rank 4th in offense in the NBA. How Joe Mazzulla and Jaylen Brown are keeping the Celtics offense afloat.

A lot of people rightfully assumed that after losing Jrue Holiday, Kristaps, Horford, Kornet in the offseason and being without Tatum that the Celtics would struggle mightily to score, while it hasn’t been pretty all the time they are smartly finding ways to generate offense.

Mazzulla is taking full advantage of the possession game this season:

The Celtics are #1 in TOV%, #10 in OREB%, and #5 in AST/TOV.

A staple of the Mazzulla era has been an emphasis on taking care of the ball. They simply do not turn the ball over ever. How they do it is pretty simple: They space the floor extremely well constantly, don’t throw over-ambitious passes, and they generally like to take the first available good shot. So while people may think the 3s are settling, they are also part of maintaining one of the elite features of his offense. They try to max out offensive possessions and don’t ask their non-creators to drive into traffic. Despite some inconsistencies scoring the ball, Pritchard and White have been key to the Celtics maintaining this identity as a team that takes care of the ball despite losing their main ISO hub and the best passer on the team. They are averaging a combined 10 assists and just 2 turnovers per game, despite an increased usage load.

What is new in regards to their possession battle is the increased emphasis on generating turnovers as an easy source of points. For the last 3 seasons the Celtics have been an extremely conservative defense, often just switching actions and relying on man to man defense with an elite rim protector behind to clean up anyone who gets beat. After losing 3 rim protectors the Celtics still rank #2 in the league in Opponent Points in the Paint and #6 in the league in DFG% at the rim. You can see Mazzulla has taken a page from Coach Dags in OKC and has amped up the pressure. After 3 straight seasons of ranking 22nd/24th/25th in PTS off TOs, the Celtics are 11th generating about 19 points in that area per game while only giving up 14 PTS of TOs. It’s a +5 gap ranking 3rd only to OKC and Detroit in the league. They swarm to the paint en masse to protect the rim, a style that has weaponized Derrick White on defense. In the previous scheme White could sometimes be a liability in 1on1 situations vs teams that could engineer matchups vs much bigger opponents, in this one he is a STOCK(steals + blocks) generating machine. On the season White has 32 steals and 25 blocks, ranking among the few players in the league with 25 in both stats. Another way to look at White and why despite some poor shooting he has been awesome is he has 32 steals, 25 blocks, and just 26 turnovers.

Another new wrinkle is an increased emphasis on Offensive rebounding and 2nd chance opportunities. A big weakness coming in this season was going to be defensive rebounding given they lost their entire starting frontcourt plus a few elite backups. While the Defensive rebounding is absolutely still a weakness, Mazzulla and the Celtics have almost completely neutralized it by just going after O-Board chances with a newfound fervor. The Celtics are 30th in DREB% and are 27th in opponent 2nd chance pts per game at 17.2 but it’s getting completely neutralized because they have gone after their own 2nd chance pts with reckless abandon and as a result are outscoring teams by +.5 in 2nd chance pts. It’s a small margin but given they are literally the worst defensive rebounding team in basketball just playing teams even in this area is HUGE. And the difference is when the Celtics get 2nd chance opps, they turn them into wide open 3s. Another big help is they have been able to vastly improve their offensive rebounding without killing their transition defense as they are #5 in Opponent Fast Break points. 3s lead to long rebounds, so you don’t have to destroy your floor balance to get 2nd opportunities.

Finally its not like Mazzulla doesn’t have a superstar helping him. Jaylen Brown has proved anyone who thought he wasn’t capable of leading a team efficiently wrong with the best season of his career. He leads the league in makes/attempts from Mid-Range(3.0/5.8) and in efficiency among the high volume shooters(51.7%). While it is a career best mark by far and could be looked at as unsustainable, I think last year’s meniscus injury forced Jaylen Brown to imagine a world where he is no longer a top 1% level athlete in the NBA. While ultimately it hurt him and his team it also forced him to learn how to score at a higher level with craft instead of burst. A guy who used to brute force his way into the paint at top speed at all times, now operates with patience and change of speed. If you watch the vast majority of his makes from this area, you’ll see a consistent theme of creating major amounts of space, he is not taking contested 2s from mid-range they are basically practice shots by the time he gets free from his defender. Any defender giving him an ounce of room in order to protect against his driving threat is given a bump off balance, his handle has taken another leap with a consistent use of a killer snatchback dribble, bigs that get switched on JB get put between a rock and a hard place as they decide whether they will meet him at the rim or try to contend with his ability to take a hard pound dribble stepback. He has taken from guys like Kawhi, SGA, and even late career DWade as he scores inside the arc with a cool mix of patience, upper tier strength, and superb change of speed. He’s driving 16 times per game and shooting 69% inside the restricted area despite the Celtics losing multiple spacing 5s and Jayson Tatum to draw help away at the rim, He just cannot be denied this season. The Celtics have a 121.5 offensive rating with Jaylen Brown on the court and a 109.7 offensive rating with him on the bench, proving himself as a legitimate 1 man offensive engine.
