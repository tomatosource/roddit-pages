[SBJ] All signs point to NBA launching streaming service in 27-28, with up to 20 teams

The NBA’s potential national streaming RSN, which Commissioner Adam Silver has intimated is a prerequisite for league expansion, might have to wait until the 2027-28 season when as many as 20 teams could be free to opt in.

According to multiple club execs, there has been “dead silence” up until now on the proposed national platform that will hypothetically solve declining local rights fees and appease a younger league fan base that prefers streamed and customized game broadcasts. Sources indicate that the league could have created the streaming RSN last summer while finalizing its $77B national media rights deal, but considered local TV a prohibitive revenue source – and preferred to wait and negotiate a higher-end deal with entities such as Amazon, YouTube, Apple, Roku, ESPN or Peacock.

But while the league has taken a measured approach, individual teams have had to lock in their own local TV deals in the short term, which is why all signs point to a 2027-28 national RSN launch date.

According to this article, these teams would not be under contract with a local RSN at the end of the 26-27 season, and would thus be eligible to jump on a national NBA streaming service:

  • Bucks
  • Bulls
  • Cavaliers
  • Clippers
  • Grizzlies
  • Hawks
  • Heat
  • Hornets
  • Jazz
  • Magic
  • Mavericks
  • Nets
  • Nuggets
  • Pacers
  • Pelicans
  • Spurs
  • Suns
  • Thunder
  • Timberwolves
  • Trailblazers

This doesn’t guarantee that all those teams would jump on. For example, the Nuggets and Bulls own their own RSNs and could hold out for whatever reason, but you’d think the NBA league office would be highly motivated to get as many teams as possible on this national streaming service.

The NBA is going to negotiate with ‘entities such as Amazon, YouTube, Apple, Roku, ESPN or Peacock’ to host the streaming service.

Of interest, here are the rights fees that some teams are getting for local streaming for 25-26:

  • Bucks: $24 million
  • Cavaliers: $35 million
  • Hawks: $32 million
  • Heat: $55.5 million

The future expansion teams will also join the national streaming service. For the teams not on the list, their media contracts go beyond 27-28. When those deals expire, they can conceivably hop on the streaming service after that:

The Celtics have a deal with NBC Sports Boston through 2031; the Kings with NBC Sports California through 2034; the Knicks with MSG through 2029; the Lakers with Spectrum SportsNet through 2032; the Pistons with FanDuel Sports Network through 2028; the Rockets with Space City Home Network through 2032; the Sixers with NBC Sports Philadelphia through 2029; the Warriors with NBC Sports Bay Area through 2030; and the Wizards with Monumental Sports & Entertainment through 2031.

The article does hypothesize that the NBC/Comcast teams will probably be relatively easy to add to the streaming service, as NBC/Comcast is now an NBA partner.

The contract for the Raptors with Rogers Sportsnet is also reportedly locked in for two decades, but Rogers has a majority stake in the team and could have flexibility to join the NBA’s RSN. The Rockets are co-owners of their Space City network and could likewise have maneuverability. The NBC Sports RSNs are owned by Comcast, which has a piece of the Sixers and is now a league media partner. That, too, could create an avenue for their teams (Sixers, Warriors, Kings, Celtics) to eventually opt in. In other words, the league’s national RSN will likely grow in increments.

As anyone who follows Dolan and his beef with the NBA knows, I’d expect the Knicks to be the very last time to join the national streaming service, and they might never join it, at least until Dolan croaks or sells the team.

EDIT: A funny part in this article, apparently the teams that have gone OTA have been disappointed in the level of reach they are getting. They blame it on people not understanding how to use or get antennas:

One reason for the consternation is that the over-the-air/DTC combination may or may not be a worthwhile option going forward. One league governor even said, “Some of those over-the-air teams have been disappointed in the level of reach they’ve been able to attain.” The belief is that some fans are disinterested or confused by digital antennas.
