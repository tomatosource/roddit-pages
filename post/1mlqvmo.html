[OC] The bizarre lore behind the Utah Jazz’s failed rebrand

Got bored and decided to type this up. Did my best to double check everything but my apologies if there are any inaccuracies.

So what even WERE the old Utah Jazz colors?

Let’s go back to 2021… The color palette for the Utah Jazz is an absolute mess. In the 2010’s the Jazz went through several variations of white, navy, green, and yellow jerseys. The jerseys themselves have looked solid, especially the orange gradient City Editions which were a HUGE hit amongst the fanbase in 2017, and were later updated with a “Dark Mode” in 2020. But for whatever reason, the Jazz cannot seem to make up their mind when it comes to team colors. This was made clear by Jazz owner Ryan Smith, who said this at the Silicon Slopes Conference in October of 2021 to part-time Jazz owner Dwyane Wade:

I’ll never forget when you called me and you were like, “Hey, my mom wants to know what the Jazz’s colors are, and neither of us can figure it out.”

This quick quip by Ryan was the first hint the Jazz fanbase would receive that a rebrand was imminent.

Ryan Smith had only been the owner of the Utah Jazz for one season at this point. He probably wanted to leave his mark on the franchise’s history, and decided to accomplish that by choosing (what he thought would be) the team’s new, permanent color scheme for the forseeable future. At first, a simple black and white look is what Ryan wanted. The problem was that this color scheme is already used predominantly by two teams: the Nets and Spurs. The idea was quickly vetoed by the NBA, but Utah was offered a compromise: simply add a third primary color and you’re good to go.

As far as I’m aware, no fan input was taken during this design process whatsoever. For reasons known only to Ryan, rather than picking a color that the Jazz had already used in the past, Ryan decided to go with something unique: an almost neon, highlighter yellow.

As Jazz reporter Andy Larsen would later point out, “It’s a unique color because other professional sports teams are smart enough not to wear it.”

Regardless, the color scheme was approved by the league, and the Jazz began preparing for a massive reveal. Remember, at this point in time, the Jazz still had the mindset that they had finally found a permanent identity and color scheme for the indefinite future. This reveal needed to be huge; it was meant to signify a new era of Jazz basketball for the fans to rally around. They expected fans to be thrilled and embrace the new identity. The Jazz planned to reveal their new jerseys to the public in July of 2022, and a massive reveal party was planned at the City Creek Mall to announce the full rebrand to the public.

But a viral tweet in February would instantly transform all of the excitement the Jazz front office was feeling into concern.

The leaks

On February 22, 2022, Twitter user JazzNationNews posted an Instagram message they had received that showed close-up images of two new Jazz jerseys, both showcasing the new colors chosen by Ryan. A later leak by Craig Miller in April would give fans a much better view of the jerseys. Reactions from basketball fans of all teams quickly began appearing on social media, which were mixed but mostly negative. What was a lot more concerning though, was the nearly unanimous response that the Jazz received from their own fanbase:

“These are GARBAGE.”

There was no denying it: Jazz fans HATED the leaked jerseys. They had only seen a glimpse of what the new jerseys would look like, but they had seen enough. Many fans simply kept their fingers crossed that the leaks were fake, others made their voices heard and demanded that the Jazz go back to the drawing board. Sure fans had their problem with the color choice, but what was debatably even worse was the overall layout of the jerseys. They were just… simple. Plain. Boring. Lazy. Minimalist. They looked like they were designed in Microsoft Paint in 10 minutes. The Jazz had plenty of different jerseys from their franchise’s history to take design elements from, but for whatever reason, they took none from any. The new jerseys JUST had big letters and big numbers, with zero historical ties to any other era of Jazz basketball besides a plain black J-note logo. The meaning the team would eventually give to the colors arguably made them even worse:

Owner Ryan Smith and the Jazz want it to be known that the organization will not shy away from the bright lights. In fact, Utah’s new uniforms reflect its proud past and prove that this is a team that will continue to loom for the spotlight.

The white and black uniforms symbolize the white and black keys on a piano, a significant element in the history of Jazz music. The yellow jersey represents the spotlight designated to the greatest performers in history, a nod to the fact that Utah embraces being in the spotlight with all eyes watching.

Piano keys and a spotlight… that was what the new team identity was being based around.

For nearly the entire 2022 offseason, virtually any post on social media that the Utah Jazz made was relentlessly attacked with comments mocking and memeing the leaked jerseys, with some fans begging the team to either trash them or confirm that they were fake. Although the Jazz refrained from ever confirming whether or not the pictures were genuine, I can tell you all from experience that during this time it was difficult to find even a single Jazz fan who expressed any excitement at all about the rebrand. There were a few fans who maybe liked one of the jerseys, but for the most part, the response from Jazz fans was universally negative. The majority of the fanbase was not happy, and many fans called for the rebrand to be shelved.

And things got so bad that it almost was.

According to Andy Larsen’s article eviscerating the new jerseys, the Jazz heard the negative feedback from fans loud and clear. Apparently, the Jazz did make some sort of attempt to pull the plug on the whole thing after the leaks:

Purple wasn’t the plan for the franchise’s rebrand, but became the plan after initial public reaction to the black/white/yellow scheme was exceptionally critical. But because they locked in that latter scheme with Nike, the purple jerseys are either the “City” jerseys or the “Classic” set, expected to change every year.

So at some point after the images were leaked, it’s confirmed that the Jazz did try to cancel this rebrand entirely. But by that time, t’s had been crossed, i’s had been dotted, and the team was locked into contractual obligations with Nike. Pulling out of the rebrand entirely simply wasn’t an option anymore. They were now committed to wearing these jerseys whether fans liked them or not.

Eventually, the Jazz had to concede that it was time to hit the panic button. Their new team identity had become the biggest laughing stock in sports, months before it was even scheduled to be revealed to the public. The big reveal in June went from something the Jazz couldn’t wait to show to something they were dreading. The concept of disappointing their entire fanbase graduated from being a possibility to an inevitability. That huge reveal party they were planning on throwing at the City Creek Mall was shelved entirely. But what were they to do, now that they were locked into a multi-season contract with Nike? They couldn’t cancel the rebrand, but perhaps they could ADD something to it in order to soften the blow for fans.

Purple is (eventually) back!

Purple. If there was one color the Jazz could count on on their fanbase embracing, it was purple.

As Larsen pointed out in his article, purple was not only a color that Jazz fans were demanding, but also “ties to nearly every era of the team, both with the Mardi Gras colors from New Orleans and the mountain uniforms from the team’s NBA Finals heyday.” The Jazz had released a few purple throwback jerseys during the Gobert-Mitchell era, such as these and these,but they never stuck around for more than 1-2 seasons. But now, the Jazz were ready to make the full transition. So just a few months after finalizing their rebrand for the 2022-23 season, the Jazz began working on their NEXT rebrand.

By now the Jazz had seemingly accepted the fact that their rebrand was a disaster waiting to happen. This was made apparent by the reveal video, which was finally posted on June 17.

The “reveal”

The rebrand was finally revealed to the public with this video that was posted on all Jazz social media pages, with no massive mall party like the franchise had originally planned. The headline of the rebrand was “PURPLE IS BACK!”, and the video mosty featured Jordan Clarkson wearing the resurrected Purple mountain jerseys that would be worn as classics in 2022-23, before showcasing two other purple jerseys: one a throwback to the Jazz’s New Orleans days, the other one being a brand new city edition. These two jerseys were to be worn in the following 2023-24 season.

The reveal video that the Jazz had originally hoped would establish the permanent identity of their team was essentially a trailer for ANOTHER rebrand that was coming in the near future, that also happened to include a quick montage showing the yellow rebranded jerseys towards the end. Of course Jazz fans weren’t happy when the leaked jerseys were confirmed to be genuine, but the promise that purple was coming back and it was here to stay was enough to get people excited for the future. Did it completely alleviate the pain that they’d be stuck with highlighter jerseys for a few seasons? No, but it at least assured the fanbase that their voices were being heard, and that the highlighter jerseys would not be a permanent look. At this point nobody knew when the team would be ready to fully commit to purple, but at least the fans had something to look forward to.

The Jazz would wear their three new jerseys starting in the 2022-23 season until the 2024-25 season. On October 21, 2024, the Jazz’s jersey schedule for the following season was released, and fans immediately noticed that the yellow icon jersey (arguably the one most hated by Jazz fans) was no longer in rotation. Although the white and black jerseys were still active, it was clear that highlighter yellow was being ditched as quickly as possible. The Jazz knew their rebuild had crashed and burned, it was time to transition to purple full time and forget that these jerseys ever existed.

The new look

The Jazz uniforms that are planned to be worn in the 2025-26 season have been well-received by the fanbase. They finally nailed it. The design for the jerseys and court seemingly take design elements from all of the Jazz’s jerseys from their history, which, considering just how many there are, is quite impressive.

A much needed improvement over the last batch of jerseys, which we have hopefully seen the last of. Rest in piss 2022-2025
