Grading the 2022 Draft 3 years into their career (Long)

It’s difficult to properly evaluate a guy until he’s about 3 years into his career, and luckily this class has just wrapped up their third season, so today I’ll grade all of the first round picks. This list will be lengthy, but it’s in chronological order, so it shouldn’t be too difficult to find a guy that you want to see in particular. Some of these guys are on different teams, so I’ll be grading based on the decision the original team made. If you’re already on a different team though, you probably weren’t that good to begin with.

Paolo Banchero

With the top pick, the expectation is an all-star player. And, Paolo Banchero has proven to be that. He’s averaged 26/7/5 this year, performed extremely well in the playoffs last year, and has improved every year in his career.

There’s some questions about his efficiency and his overall shot selection but Paolo was not a miss at the top pick. It would surprise nobody if he’s a top ten player sooner than later.

Grade: A

Chet Holmgen

Another great player. Chet is an extremely versatile player for OKC’s offense with his efficient scoring in and away from the basket, has unlimited defensive potential and is a key factor of why OKC has had a historically great season.

There’s one issue though. Chet missed all of year 1 and a majority of this season, even though OKC was largely unharmed by his absence. He did every play game in year 2 and in the playoffs, but questions about staying consistently healthy are valid.

Grade: A-

Jabari Smith

Jabari is a few tiers below the first 2 draft picks, but he’s a good role player. He’s a good defender, a solid shooter and a good rebounder, and could fit on any team as a starter at the 3⁄4 position.

However, he’s not really much more than that, and despite the fact he’s only in his third year, he’s probably gonna be the same player for the rest of his career. He didn’t even start for Houston in the playoffs. That is disappointing for the #3 pick three years in, but he’s definitely a solid player that fills his role well.

Grade: B-

Keegan Murray

Funny enough, you can almost say the exact same thing for this guy as I said for Jabari. Decent shooter, has missed only about ten games in his entire career, good defender, good rebounder, not amazing at creating his own shot. Unfortunately, Sacramento hasn’t particularly had the year Houston has had, but it’s not because of Keegan.

One issue though. Considering most of his threes are open catch-and-shoot looks, you want your starting forward to hit more than 34% of them. If he gets back to his rookie level (41%), the grade may increase.

Grade: C+

Jaden Ivey

This is an interesting case. Ivey is a solid combo guard who can score at the basket and at three, has shown playmaking chops, and has improved every year despite having 3 different coaches in Detroit (with the second one being particularly incompetent).

However, he’s not a great defender, Cade Cunningham and Detroit got better after his injury, and of course, the fibula injury is a concern. I expect him to recover, but as Detroit has unexpectedly went from the bottom of the league to a good team very quickly, is Ivey in the blueprint moving forward? We’ll see.

Grade: B

Benedict Mathurin

Another weird player to rate. Mathurin is a good player who can score at all 3 levels, but he’s essentially had the same role for 3 consecutive years. He improved defensively this year, but he isn’t good on that end.

He started more this year, but his offensive production was very similar to his first 2 years. I know he’s played 3 seasons, but maybe there’s something there with Mathurin we haven’t quite seen yet, and I’m not sure we’ll see it with Indiana.

However, as I’m posting this, Indiana IS in the NBA Finals. His minutes and scoring has dropped in the playoffs but he’s still had his moments, particularly 2 good games in the ECF. However, with the recent developments with the team, I’m seriously doubtful he’ll start for Indiana next season.

Grade: B-

Shaedon Sharpe

Sharpe is yet another interesting player. He didn’t play in college so he essentially was thrown on a NBA court from high school on a declining Blazers team that eventually traded Dame. After only playing 32 games in his second season, he’s been good this year. He has amazing scoring potential, has a great 2⁄3 build, and in the last 2 months of the season he put up an efficient 20 on a Blazers team who showed signs of progress go end off the year.

I’ll have to give him a B for now but I think this is a guy who can possibly improve to all-star levels next year if Portland reshuffles the roster and he can take that next step.

Grade: B

Dyson Daniels

Dyson Daniels is an exceptional defender, an intelligent offensive player, and has been a great fit in Atlanta alongside Trae this season as he won the MIP award and nearly won the DPOY award.

HOWEVER, I mentioned that I’m grading based on the team that drafted him, not on the team he plays for. The team that drafted him, the Pelicans, traded him for Dejounte Murray who suffered 2 long-term injuries that may impact his availability for next year, and the win-move completely backfired as they plummeted to a 21-61 record. So….

Grade: F (for trading him)

Jeremy Sochan

Sochan is a solid player, but he may have the most unique archetype in the league. He’s a solid defender, but offensively he’s a good passer for his size and is solid around the basket. However, he’s a bad shooter and I don’t see that changing anytime soon.

His counting stats have almost stayed the same from year 1 to 3, although he’s improved from his rookie year. As the Spurs move forward, Sochan’s role on the team is a question. Can they start a playmaking wing who’s a liability from 3? He’s a solid player but I feel like he’ll be someone whose value will wildly vary from team to team, and I’m not even sure what a good contract would be for him.

Grade: B-

Johnny Davis

Much like Dyson, Johnny Davis is no longer on the team they drafted him. However, unlike Dyson who was traded in a win-now move that horrendously failed, Johnny Davis was just awful. He couldn’t get consistent minutes on a rubbish team, and when he did he failed to prove himself worthy of being a NBA player despite his high draft selection. This is the first outright MISS in the draft.

Grade: F

Ousmane Dieng

This guy is an outright mystery. He was always expected to be a raw player that’d take time to develop, but he’s on a team that’s so good it’s been impossible for him to actually see the nightly rotation as guys like Aaron Wiggins, Isaiah Joe, Alex Caruso (from a trade), and even the guy we’ll talk about later outshined him.

We won’t really see if he’s really a NBA player unlike he leaves the completely stacked Thunder team he’s on. I won’t call him a bust, moreso a guy whose circumstances has left him in an awkward situation.

Grade: D

Jalen Williams

Needless to say, I do not have to say that about the next guy the Thunder drafted. Jalen Williams is an exceptional two-way player who’s gotten better every year and is great at everything you’d want your second option to be great at. He’s also been very healthy throughout his career. This was an absolute home run of a pick and adds to Presti’s impressive resume over the years.

Grade: A+

Jalen Duren

Jalen Duren is a very solid center and a key reason why Detroit had one of the most impressive bounce-backs in NBA history. He’s good around the basket, was 4th in the league in offensive rebounds, and played started every game for a playoff team. He also played extremely well in the playoffs with extended minutes, and he’s just turning 21.

One issue though is that his defense was very weak for his position, which is concerning considering you can’t really hide a weak defensive center. Detroit as a team played well defensively, but as they head towards that next step, it’s important for Duren to improve defensively and become a strong rim protector for Detroit. He has the physical tools and he’s still extremely young, so I have hope.

Grade: B+

Ochai Agbaji

Probably the most forgettable double digit scorer in the league, Agbaji is currently on his second team, being the third guy here who’s already left his original team. While not as good as Dyson, and not as bad as Johnny Davis, Agbaji was relatively unspectacular for Utah.

For a standard 3-and-D player, he was good at neither his first 2 seasons. His third season, however, he improved on both ends. He shot relatively well from 3, and was solid defensively. It’s probably safe to say he’s of the more generic players in basketball, but if he can play solid defense and shoot well from 3 consistently, he should turn in a solid career. This would be a C, but since his original team gave him up in a minor deal, that blows the grade a bit.

Grade: D+

Mark Williams

A lot of the things we said about Jalen Duren earlier, we can say about Mark Williams. Solid at the basket, good rebounder, pretty bad defender. However, there’s some huge issues with Mark Williams.

He’s only played about 35 games a season for a team that’s been struggling with injuries his entire career, his defense is even worse than Duren’s, and Charlotte hasn’t progressed at all throughout his time there. They attempted to trade him at the deadline, and I supported the decision. He’s not someone Charlotte can trust to be a starting center if they want to be a serious team again. I don’t know what his value is, but I wouldn’t pay him.

Grade: C

AJ Griffin: I (Incomplete)

Ummm….pretty solid rookie season. It was then followed by peculiar actions throughout the offseason, a second season that was ruined by poor play on the floor and personal issues off the floor, and he retired by the 2024 offseason.. I would say this is a F, but the circumstances in this situation are so weird I’ll just give the Hawks grace and mark it as incomplete.

Grade: I

Tari Eason

For the 17th pick, Eason is a pretty good role player. He’s great defensively (and can guard multiple positions), he’s a good rebounder, and generally does what you want role players to do.

Yes, he only played about 1⁄2 of the last 2 seasons, and his shooting could be better. However, one can argue he’s more valuable to Houston than the guy we mentioned much earlier. While I’m not sure he has all star potential, he can likely become an Anunoby-type player.

Grade: B

Jake LaRavia

Another weird grade here, as LaRavia had his best year as a role player for a good Memphis team, yet the Grizzlies decided to trade him. It’s kinda confusing they decided to get rid of a young player in such an inconsequential trade (they also were much worse after he left), but he’s currently on Sacramento right now.

He’s a solid role player. Plays hard defensively, decent from 3, plays his role well much like Tari Eason. However, there’s gonna be a grade drop considering he was traded.

Grade: C-

Christian Braun

Amazing pick by Denver. Braun is everything you’d want in a role player. Takes smart shots, very durable, good at the basket, good defender, and stepped in admirably as Denver’s starting shooting guard after KCP’s departure. Denver was much better when he was on the floor and one can argue he was perhaps Denver’s 2nd most consistent player this season.

There’s very little downsides to Braun other than the fact he’s not an amazing shooter, but he’s grown in that area too, and considering he’s improved immensely in all 3 seasons of his career, it wouldn’t be shocking to see him upgrade in that area too. Braun is on his way to becoming a fringe all star.

Grade: A

Walker Kessler.

While Utah has been one of the worst teams in basketball since drafting Kessler, he’s certainly been great for them when they’re not resting him for fake injuries. He’s an elite rim protector and he’s also great at not fouling, which is very impressive for a young center. He’s solid around the basket, and led the league in offensive rebounds, so he’s pretty much good at everything you want a rim running center to be good at.

Does he have all star potential? Time will tell. However, he’s definitely Utah’s most valuable player and if Utah improves next year, Kessler improving will be a big reason why.

Grade: A-

David Roddy

I never understood drafting a 6’4 non-shooting guard who averages 8 rebounds, but it wasn’t that bad at first. He was pretty solid for a rookie his first season. Then, he massively regressed the following year on an injured Grizzlies team, got traded in his second season, and has played on 4 different teams trying to figure himself out. How long does he have left in the league? I don’t know.

Grade: D

Marjon Beauchamp

I understand Milwaukee has been a good team when he’s been there. However, with the Bucks having rotation problems this year, Beauchamp SHOULD HAVE been playing. He wasn’t due to his poor shooting, and now his career is on borrowed time. This was a miss, but the Bucks atleast got a solid player for him in Kevin Porter.

Grade: D-

Blake Wesley and Malaki Branham

Branham was drafted slightly earlier, but I’ll kill 2 spurs with one stone here. They’ve both been bad for San Antonio and can’t even crack the rotation for a team that isn’t particularly popping with talent.

Branham was in the rotation in his first 2 seasons (pretty awful both years) and Wesley DID improve from year 2 to 3, but these guys will likely have to get out of San Antonio to be quality players.

Grade: D-

Wendell Moore: D-

It’s blunt, but it may be fair to say this guy is the worst player we’ll talk about here. In 2 seasons with Minnesota, Wendell Moore never really did much of anything. He was given away to Detroit in a minor trade, and never really did much there either.

He finally got an opportunity to get some run from a rudderless Charlotte team, and averaged 5 points in 18 minutes and had an on-off of -26.5. He’s not a F considering he was the 26th pick, but it would be shocking if he plays in the NBA for the foreseeable future.

Nikola Jovic

In a sea of waste between Kessler and Watson, Nikola Jokic Jovic is probably the best player. Does that mean he’s a great player? No, but he was adequate last year.

He’s a good playmaker for his size just like the Nikola J., but unlike that guy he’s not spectacular at anything else. He’s an average scorer, shooter, and defender. He didn’t really play much his first season so maybe next year we can see really see how much potential he really has considering he’s about to turn 22 soon, but nothing special so far.

Grade: C+

Patrick Baldwin Jr

A very raw prospect, Baldwin was definitely a guy that needed some time on the bench before seriously becoming a rotation player. Unfortunately, he was traded away from Golden State as a minor piece in the Poole-CP3 trade, and spent 2 seasons at Washington. Despite Washington not being….great, he never was a mainstay in the rotation and is currently a reserve on the Clippers.

He did have a solid stretch of games at the end of his second season, but considering it was April basketball on a 15 win team, it’s hard to determine if that’s a good indicator of a lasting NBA career.

Grade: D

Ty-Ty Washington Jr

It’s difficult to give out a F on the second-to-last pick of the first round, but this was certainly a miss. Washington was probably a prospect that needed a bit more time to become a solid NBA player, but the Rockets shipped him out after one season as they shifted away from rebuild mode. He’s still in the NBA and played sparingly with the Suns last year, but going into year 4, it’s unclear what his NBA future holds.

Grade: D

Peyton Watson

For the very last pick in the draft, Denver struck a solid rotation player in Peyton Watson. For a team that has struggled with depth the past seasons, his contributions (especially on the defensive end) has been integral, as he’s actually the leading shot blocker in the league who’s not a center or center-adjacent.

His offense was pretty bad in college, but he’s improved while at Denver. While he’s not going to light it up from three, he’s good at the basket and a good fit alongside Jokic, which is important for anyone playing on the Nuggets playing alongside the league’s premier player. He hasn’t been great in the playoffs, but he’s still a very young player so we can give him time with that.

Grade: A-

Dalen Terry

Dalen Terry was not the last pick of the first round, but he’s so obscure I literally forgot to list him. With that being said, I don’t think the guy averaging 4 points a night on a mediocre team in his 3rd season is going to amount to much. He had the worst on-off on the team, and although he’s solid defensively, that does not make up for being one of the worst offensive players in basketball. He was also drafted #18, so Chicago wasn’t drafting THAT late, thus….

Grade: F

Andrew Nembhard

Nembhard was actually the first pick of the second round, but he’s been so solid that he gets a honorary mention here. Needless to say, starting on a Finals team and being their 3rd/4th most consistent player is huge. He’s shot well this playoffs, he provides a second playmaker in Indiana’s starting lineup, and he’s an elite guard defender. You couldn’t have found a better player with pick 31, and he’ll likely be Indiana’s 2 guard for years to come.

Grade: A+

We’re done. Since these players are due to get paid this offseason via extensions, I’ll give a list of guys I think will get a big second contract, a solid one, guys I’m unsure of, and guys who most certainly will not get a second contract.

Surefire extensions

Paolo, Chet, Daniels, Jalen Williams, Braun, Kessler, Nembhard

Solid extensions

Ivey, Sharpe, Duren, Eason, Peyton Watson

Unsure

Jabari Smith, Keegan Murray, Mathurin, Sochan, Agbaji, Mark Williams, LaRavia, Jovic

No

Johnny Davis, Ousmane, AJ Griffin, Terry, Branham, Roddy, Beauchamp, Wesley, Wendell Moore, Pat Baldwin, TyTy
