[OC] Bad Postseason Hitters with High WPA

By now, I trust that most of you are familiar with Win Probability Added. It’s the most context-dependent stat out there, based solely on the game situation. A 2-out, walk-off HR is worth far more than a 1st inning leadoff single, or a HR trailing by 10, etc. There is a similar stat, cWPA, which focuses on winning the championship rather than a single game.

That means you can struggle mightily at the plate, save all of your hits for crunch time, and end up with a positive WPA and cWPA in the postseason. Here are some hitters who fit this criteria. I hope this write-up brings back some memories of clutch postseason moments.

Austin Riley: 36 G, 150 PA, .232/.273/.373, .647 OPS, 1.16 WPA, 22.6% cWPA

Riley has been a pillar of the Braves contenders. While he was very good in the 2021 WS (.760 OPS), the 2021 NLCS stands out. He slashed .200/.259/.400, but his game winning RBI in G1 and G2 spurred him to a .66 WPA and a 10.6% cWPA for the series.

Cody Bellinger: 76 G, 298 PA, .211/.282/.378, .660 OPS, 0.82 WPA, 24.7% cWPA

Bellinger’s rollercoaster regular season career is mirrored in the postseason. He has 5 series with an OPS of .875 or better and 5 series with an OPS below .550. In G5 of the 2017 WS, he hit a 3-run tiebreaking HR and a tiebreaking RBI triple in the infamous 13-12 loss to HOU, netting .412 WPA and 22.17 % cWPA in that game alone. He hit poorly otherwise, and ended with a .565 OPS, .25 WPA and 8.0% cWPA.

His 2018 NLCS is notable as well. He won series MVP with a .591 OPS thanks to a 2-out walk-off single in G4 (0.40 WPA, 7.25% cWPA) and his critical G7 HR (0.17 WPA, 8.6% cWPA). He ended up with 0.20 WPA and 5.5% cWPA for the series.

Jose Ramirez: 45 GP, 190 PA .234/.316/.365, .681 OPS, 0.86 WPA, -1.7% cWPA

Many fans know J-Ram had a rough start to his postseason career. Since 2020, though, he has been his normal self, OPSing .869 with 1.57 WPA and 15.7 cWPA% in 22 games. These are incredible figures for someone who hasn’t reached a WS in that timespan, and they’ve been oddly consistent throughout each series.

Overall, his cWPA is dragged down by the 2016 WS (-11.5%). Weirdly enough, he hit a respectable .310/.333/.448 in that series.

Joe Morgan: 50 GP, 222 PA, .182/.323/.348, .671 OPS, 0.14 WPA, 31.3% cWPA

Arguably the greatest 2B of all time, the late Morgan was the inspiration for this post. In the 1972 WS loss to OAK, he hit a poor .125/.300/.208. However, his final PA was a no-out double in the bottom of the 8th in G7, moving Pete Rose to 3rd and giving CIN a golden opportunity to overcome a 2 run deficit (CIN ultimately plated 1 run). This was worth a massive 22.9% cWPA, boosting him to 10.7% for the series.

His magnum opus was the 1975 WS. The soon-to-be MVP was pedestrian at the plate (.259/.364/.296). However, in the top of the 9th of G7, in a 3-3 game with 2 outs, Morgan singled in the tiebreaking, game-winning run. He notched 0.34 WPA and 33.8% cWPA on that play, resulting in 0.16 WPA and 28.9% cWPA for the series. The Big Red Machine captured a long-overdue championship.

Sal Bando* (WS only): 19 G, 79 PA, .206/.295/.265, .560 OPS, 0.09 WPA, 10.4% cWPA

A swashbuckling 3rd baseman, the late Bando was a lynchpin on the Swingin A’s, who three-peated in 72, 73, and 74. His overall postseason numbers are good (.737 OPS, 0.24 WPA, 11.7% cWPA), but his WS numbers are bizarre.

Bando had average figures in the first two WS (.655 OPS, -0.07 WPA, 4.2% cWPA). In 74, however, he hit a shocking (.063/.200/.063). His 1 hit in 20 PA was a game-tying single in the 6th inning of G4 (0.14 WPA, 5.61 cWPA%). In G1, however, he reached on an error in the top of the 8th, scoring Bert Campaneris and decisively breaking a 1-1 tie (0.12 WPA). In the closeout G5, he drove in the first run with a sacrifice fly. Overall, his ghastly slash line netted him 0.15 WPA and 6.25% cWPA.
