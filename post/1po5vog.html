The flaws of the temple are pretty much in line with the fact they took Loop Hero as a base.

Let’s put the abysmal rewards on the side and talk about the design of the temple mechanic itself.

I have nothing against loop hero, I played a bit of it and enjoyed it a lot, and I agree that the hidden room upgrades in that game are fun to discover and work around.

But loop hero has 2 things that PoE2 doesn’t.

First of all, placing rooms in Loop Hero is the WHOLE game. You basically only do that.

Secondly, a run of loop hero takes 20 minutes. And once you’re done, you go again directly.

This means it’s okay that the room placement upgrade is hidden, because you’re placing rooms constantly, it’s all you really do, so placing a room at the wrong spot is actually almost inconsequential, because you will place 100 others soon that will eventually work and improve your knowledge of room combos.

In PoE 2 though, it takes you 6 maps (depending on your efficiency, that can take up to a whole hour) to place what, 3 room? This means that making a mistake in placement has HUGE consequences, meaning the lack of knowledge punishes you AND you’re learning the combos at a very slow pace.

For me this is a very poor port of a game mechanic into another, like it wasn’t thought through to make sense in the new game.
