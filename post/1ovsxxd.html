[OC] An analysis of ABS Challenges in AAA, part 1

In anticipation for the ABS Challenge system’s Major League debut next year, I will study how the challenge system test went in AAA in 2025. To recap how the challenge system is configured for AAA, each team receives two challenges to start the game. If the challenger is correct, their team retains the challenge; otherwise, they lose one. That means, in a nine-inning game, teams are allowed to be wrong at most twice on ABS challenges. This will be the same for MLB in 2026 (with one small addition, highlighted later).

Overall stats

In 2025, the overall umpire accuracy in AAA was 93.5% (ranging from 92.2% to 95.3%). Here, accuracy is determined solely by which pitches could be overturned by a challenge, not including a margin of error or stance adjustment (I analyzed the latter in my last post). That meant that each game had an average of 10.6 pitches that could be overturned by a challenge.

The overall success rate on challenges (i.e. pitch result overturned) was:

  • overall: 49.5%
  • offense: 45.1%
  • defense: 53.5%

Unfortunately, the pitch-by-pitch data does not discriminate between pitcher and catcher, only the battery. But I would assume that pitchers have a much lower success rate than catchers.

This confusion matrix breaks down of the 335,881 pitches that were tracked in scheduled 9-inning AAA games from 2025:

AAA, 2025 Not challenged Challenged
Umpire correct 309,804 4,385
Umpire incorrect 17,397 4,295

Players were only able to successfully challenge 19.8% of all missed calls, leading to an overall post-challenge accuracy of 94.8%.

The distribution of challenges per game

In terms of pitch counts, 2.6% of all taken pitches were challenged, working out to about 1 in 39 taken pitches. The average number of challenges per game was 4.25 and the median was 4, with the following distribution:

The average number of successful challenges was 2.1 per game, with the following distribution:

Notably, about one in six games had no overturned calls, and only 1 in 100 games had more than 6 overturned calls.

Challenges based on situation

Let’s start breaking down how teams are using (and not using) their challenges based on the situation. First, we look at if teams are more apprehensive about risking their second challenge after losing the first.

Challenges remaining Challenge rate Pitches per challenge Success rate
1 2.73% 1 in 36.7 50.1%
2 2.93% 1 in 34.2 49.2%
any (including 0) 2.58% 1 in 38.7 49.5%

The answer is yes, but overall the drop in challenge rate is not particularly great (the challenge rate drops by only 7%). We’ll qualify this by considering the inning. As one might expect, the challenge frequency increases as the game progresses, peaking at the 9th inning:

ABS Challenge rate by inning in AAA games in 2025.

The challenge success rate also drops inning by inning, meaning that teams are trading confidence for aggression:

1st 2nd 3rd 4th 5th 6th 7th 8th 9th 10th
54.4% 53.2% 50.9% 51.4% 51.7% 50.9% 46.7% 47.1% 42.2% 35.7%

In extra innings, there is a significant drop in challenge rate. There are reasons for this in AAA, but which probably won’t translate to MLB for a couple reasons:

  1. AAA frequently uses position players to pitch (even in close games as early as the 10th inning) if the pitchers scheduled to appear that game have already gotten their work in. At that point, players don’t feel inclined to challenge to keep the game moving.
  2. Next year in MLB, teams will get an extra challenge for every extra inning if they are out of challenges. This was not the case in AAA, so many teams were simply out of challenges entering extras.

Putting everything together, we plot inning based on number of challenges remaining:

ABS Challenge rate by inning and number of challenges remaining in AAA games in 2025.

With one or two challenges remaining, the challenge rate spikes in the 9th inning. Note the extremely low challenge percentage in the first inning with one challenge remaining. It looks like teams who lose their first challenge very early on in the game are extremely cautious in the first couple innings as to not risk their one remaining challenge. In particular, the challenge success rate in the first inning with one challenge remaining is 63.6% (compared to 54.1% with having both challenges).

In general, hitters are very aggressive late in the count at challenging called strikes, especially when it’s strike three. On a full count, 22.6% of strike three calls are challenged. In fact, hitters are just as aggressive with one challenge remaining versus two (25.3% vs. 25.1%). Despite this aggression, hitters only have a 33.4% challenge success rate on 3-2, the lowest of any count, while they are more than 60% successful challenging on 2-0 and 3-0.

Conclusion

As ABS goes from being experimental in the minor leagues to critical in the majors, we’ll see how these factors translate to much more important games. I imagine teams will be much more conservative than identified here early in a game, and also make sure that they get all the challenges in they possibly can by the end. In part 2, I will analyze the quality of challenges (and maybe other suggestions in the comments). In other words, are teams effective at challenging the big misses? (Spoiler: &gt;!no.!&lt;)
