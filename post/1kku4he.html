INTRO

Hello everyone, since I am a crazy person, and I wasn’t satisfied with the armour-breaking test from yesterday, and given that the post from the other day sparked way more interest than I expected (over 120k people saw the post), I decided to repeat the test, with a larger dataset (more runs), better metodology, better tools, and a better presentation. The previous test turned out to be misleading due to either a bug I stumbled-on, or the much-worse setup I had, and I wouldn’t want to have mislead people. So here we are, after a day spent doing tests and rewatching them, a new version of the test from the other day.

PURPOSE

The main objective of the test is to figure out how armour-breaking works against bosses. The physical damage mitigation formula is well known, but the web is full of contradictory statements about the general behavior of armour-breaking:

  • Does armour-breaking have a progressive effect on the target? Meaning, if I break 250 points out of a 1000, am I reducing that boss’ mitigation?
  • If not, hence only the fully-armour-broken debuff has an effect (and nothing before that) then what such effect even is? Equivalent to the boss having actually 0 armour rating? A flat percentage bonus?
  • Can armour go negative with something like Vulnerability being applied to a target that already has been stripped of all its armour?

This stuff is kind of important because while there’s a clear design around consuming the fully-armour–broken debuff for additional damage or effects, some builds might benefit more from simply keeping the debuff on all the time instead of consuming it; but that depends on answering the questions above, and getting some numbers.

LIMITATIONS

Ideally I would want to setup a testing environment with as few uncontrollable variables as possible. I took care of as much of that as I could, but the real issue is that I don’t really have a good way to remove critical hits on attacks (for spells I could have used the “Controlled Destruction” support gem). An additional optimization for the testing environment would be to equip items that flatten out damage roll variability, but again I have no way to do so with my character. See, I normally only play on SSF Hardcore, and I literally insta-delete characters that I die with, so I don’t have many characters with lots of money and access to trade markets; these tests have been performed on the only character I have on softcore, so no crazy setup with uniques that would make the data much less noisy, unfortunately, just a relatively close-to-normal setup.

SETUP

The test is really simple: it’s performed on Act 3’s optional boss Mektul The Forgemaster; I’m using a monk with a very simple passives-tree in order to not have any conditional buffs (eg: “damage bonus if you’ve hit with a projectile recently”), only deals purely physical damage, and I only use a single skill from the Spear weapon, over and over again: Thrown Spear. I spend the entire fight repeatedly hitting the boss with it, but in four distinct phases:

  • Normal hits while the boss has all of its armour rating.
  • During the fight, I add the Splinter support gem in order to start breaking it’s armour.
  • After the boss gets the fully-armour-broken debuff I keep hitting it.
  • In 3 out of 4 tests I also added a fourth phase where I apply Vulnerability while the boss still has the aforementioned debuff.

I record the gameplay, then watch it back and with the help of a properly setup spreadsheet, I end up with a complete sequence of every hit’s damage, divided by type, in chronological order. This allows me to make averages (and more) and should be able to find out the answers to the questions above, hopefully. I’ve ran the test four times for a total of over 700 recorded hits; note that given the extreme variability of conditions (crits, rolls) this is still not a very big sample size; I am not a statistician (and it shows) but a cursory look at the charts below suggests that a hanful of lucky (or unlucky in this case) crits proccing on upper-bounds damage rolls can completely skew the data, so take all of this with a huge grain of salt: the point is not to identify precise magnitudes of damage at the different states, again: I just wanted to see whether there’s a measurable effect for the actual debuff, the ramp-up to it, and for vulnerability while armour is fully broken.

WHY THE PREVIOUS TEST FAILED

The test I perfomed a few days ago failed spectacularly because the results didn’t make any sense: the data showed no significant effect from armour breaking, while still showing signs that Vulnerability was working; there was also no sign of the damage reduction the boss is supposed to get while standing in molten gold and throwing waves at the player, which was certainly weird. This strange result is what sparked me to actually post the data and see what you people would think of it. Why did it fail?

I don’t really have a perfect answer, it’s still possible that I simply stumbled on a rare bug. It’s also possible I somehow messed up the test: at that time I was not using a super-rigorous way of doing things, and I later realized I did have some uncontrollable variables dirtying up the data (a couple conditional passives turning on and off, and a tad of chaos damage which I didn’t remove).

I’ve now normalized every variable I could think of: the character only deals physical damage, has no conditional passives, no added damage as extra, nothing; as previosuly stated I am also solely using Thrown Spear and nothing else, over and over, with the exception of Vulnerability for the 4th stage of the test, applied directly as a curse instead of via Hand of Chayula, for a stronger more visible effect and less stuff to remove from the data (the damage from the HoC itself).

SUMMARY THE RESULTS

Here’s the part most people will skip to, so before showing you the tables and the charts, let me summarize the findings:

  • Partial armour breaking appears to have a progressive effect, so the number shown on the user interface seems to be a measure of actually stripped bits of armour rating, having. How big of an impact this has? Depends on the nature of the hits, the amount of armour on the boss, and other factors. In my case, the average damage over the ramp-up from 0 (full armour) to 1500 (fully armour broken) was in the order of 109%~112% of the baseline. Finding generalized numbers is not the purpose of the test, the purpose was to just see if there’s any measurable effect of partial armour breaking or not, and apparently there is. Also, the partial breaking state doesn’t even matter much as it’s unlikely to last very long (if you’re taking that much to strip armor, you have a bigger problem).
  • Fully armour broken clearly works to reduce the target’s mitigation. Again, the actual numbers would vary enormously depending on a vast array of variables that would be active on a real character, as well as which boss is taken in consideration, but in this particular case the average damage of hits on the boss while it had a fully broken armour was 134%-156% of baseline, which seems quite significant. This tells me that it can be non-trivial, so for characters that are very fast but light-hitting, and based mostly on physical damage, breaking armour and keeping the debuff on rather then consuming it might very well be extremely worth it (remember that just one armour-breaking-hit every once in a while will be enough to constantly keep the debuff’s duration refreshed).
  • Vulnerabily appears to not be able to make armour go negative, which makes sense given the wording on the skill (“ignore” armour) and was my initial expectation; so it can’t be used provide additional damage when applied on a boss that has its armour already fully-broken. Note that it’s not just your hits ignoring the armour, because the ignored part actually participates in the count for reaching full breakage. In other words, with an example: if you need 1000 armour-breakage points in order to reach full-breakage, you can use Vulnerability first (let’s say for 500) and then quickly deal the rest of the breakage normally (another 500). But again: applying Vulnerability after the target’s armour is fully broken does not appear to make it go negative.
  • This particular boss does get a damage reduction while it does it’s thing in the molten gold, throwing waves at the player: according to my data I would guess that it’s probably a ~25% damage reduction.

Additionally, there’s a bit of a mystery around the actual armour rating on this boss: PoE2DB mentions 3046, and frankly that seems to roughly match my data: the average of my post-mitigation hits on the boss, after having fully broken its armour, is around 349~364; assuming it actually has 3046 armour rating, my average hit before even beginning to break its armour should theoretically be around 235, and guess what, the final average over my runs was around 240~259, which is quite close considering how much variability there is due to crits and rolls. Yet, this 3046 number does not match up with the amount of armour breakage required to reach the fully-armour-broken debuff, which instead seems to be around ~1500. It surely is interesting that this value happens to be precisely half of the previously mentioned armour rating. Does this mean armour breakage has an internal 2x multiplier (or the threshold is halved, which is the same thing). Possible. Who knows? Remains a bit of a mystery.

TABLE AND CHARTS

Final table with overall averages for the raw-data plus some totally arbitrary manipulation (read for context):

You might be thinking: “wait a second, why did this dude conclude Vulnerability isn’t having any effect on the target when fully-broken-armour? The average damage goes from 156% to163%! And WTF is that filtered data?!”. Well, this is where the absolute inadequacy of my setup becomes evident: have a look at the following hit-sequence chart of a specific run (the second in this round of tests):

https://preview.redd.it/g0o9r3w2sc0f1.png?width=860&format=png&auto=webp&s=be6cc7878372f2e545ac5b1efbf04e74739c1633

Look at the red part of the line (last segment), which is where I’m testing Vulnerability on the fully-broken-armour boss. See the spikes? Those are big lucky crits that rolled on large damage-rolls, and on this particular run, as you can see, there have been not one… not two… but three! Plus a smaller but still decently-lucky one at the end… That is enough to completely skews the data with this sample size. Which is why I decided to add that “Filtered Data” part: those are averages computed ignoring every hit that is above the maximum expected non-critical hit; even a mathematically ignorant individual like me realizes this is not appropriate: I am arbitrarily cutting some data points, which can’t be good. Also, we can still have all sorts of crits within the valid range, so again, I know this is not particulary great. With that said, given that I am unable to make a better setup that prevents this from happening, and I certainly won’t repeat the tests 50 more times in a desperate attempt to minimize noise, I decided that my arbitrary cutting-out of the highest peaks is good enough as a last-resort measure to not have wasted an entire day of my life, given that those very lucky rolls are clearly the biggest factor in messing up the data. Result: in the filtered version of the averages, there’s basically no appreciable difference between w/Vulnerability vs w/o Vulnerability, while the differences between the other boss-states is still quite evident, so by this very arbitrary manipulation I managed to somwhat normalize the data against that insane steak of luck from that particular run. I therefore choose to believe Vulnerability is not actually having any effect, other than bringing insane luck maybe (lol). If you want to compare that sequence chart with another one, here it is:

https://preview.redd.it/oulkhkddtc0f1.png?width=853&format=png&auto=webp&s=3c8a4dc8f015d3b94c11e795319df36a8193e2ae

Just like with the previous hit-sequence chart, even without proper trend-lines or moving-average, I can clearly see the ramp-up of the damage bettween blue &gt; green &gt; orange (fully armoured &gt; breaking &gt; broken), but I see no appreciable difference between orange and red (broken vs broken + vulnerability).

CONCLUSIONS

I think this post is waaaaay too long already and it wouldn’t make much sense to share every table and every chart for every run. I think this is enough to conclude that:

  • This test is largely dumb, while I actually had fun doing this, I won’t be doing any more of it: without the additional setup which would require special items and such, the variability inherent to the weapon and the nature of damage calculation (especially crits) makes the data so noisy that an absurd amount of runs would be needed to get solid data.
  • Depsite being dumb, it still makes it quite evident that armour breakage DOES work to reduce the target’s physical damage mitigation, and it’s not SOLELEY designed to be consumed for additional effects on a big-hitter. That might still be the best use for it of course, especially for hard-hitting and slower-attacking characters which would not benefit much from armour-breakage in the first place, but it might be totally worth to keep the debuff up for a very fast-but-light attacker.
  • In the post from the other day, I had even more variability due to the worse setup, but given how close to baseline every boss-state was, I strongly suspect I had stumbled on a rare bug with the boss… it’s very strange that the damage reduction while in molten-gold did not show up in that data, at all, so it was the right call to delete that part of the other post.
