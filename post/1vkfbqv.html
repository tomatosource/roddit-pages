The Statistically Curious 1889 Season of John Clarkson

John Clarksons 1889 season for the Boston Beaneaters has many statistical curiosities that fascinate me.

For starters Clarksons campaign was a Triple Crown winning effort. He lead the league in Wins (49) ERA (2.73) and Strikeouts (284). While any triple crown season is impressive, that’s not what caught my eye. What did, is the fact that despite leading the league in ERA, he also gave up more runs than any other pitcher in the National League that year. That’s right, his league leading 2.73 ERA came while giving up a league leading 188 runs.

How did he do that, why leading the league in Innings Pitched of course! Clarkson hurled 620 innings that year, more than anyone else in baseball. He threw over 100 more innings than second place Mark Baldwin of the American Associations Columbus Solons who threw 513.2 innings.

The curiosities don’t stop there, Clarkson also had a league leading WHIP of 1.277, and get this, he not only gave up more hits than anyone else, but more walks too! Clarkson gave up 589 hits, also over 100 more than any other pitcher in baseball gave up that year (481), and he walked 203 batters (3rd in baseball but more than the NLs 2nd place at 167).

So to recap, he lead the league in ERA while giving up the most ERs and lead the league in WHIP while giving up more hits AND walks than anyone else. Truly a unique season.

Clarkson also faced 2,641 batters that season, almost as many as Cristopher Sánchez has faced in his entire 6 year career. (2819)

While not necessarily a curiosity Clarkson also pitched the first known immaculate inning on June 4th of that season.

While that’s the extent of his 1889 season the rest of his story is notable as well.

- He threw a no-hitter on July 27th, 1885.

- He lead the league in wins three times, 1885, 1887, and 1889 and also lead the league in strikeouts each of those same three years. Falling 0.22 runs shy of the triple crown in ‘87 and 0.27 in ‘85.

- He along with his two major leaguer brothers Dad Clarkson and Walter Clarkson are 3rd in wins by brothers, behind Gaylord and Jim Perry and Phil and Joe Niekro. John did most of the heavy lifting in the trio, he had a career 328 wins while Dad had 39 and Walter could get no more than 18. John had 85% of the threes total wins.

- At the time of his retirement his 328 wins was the most in National League history and is still 12th all time. His 53 wins in 1885 is second most in a season in MLB history. His 49 in 1889 is 4th most.

- Clarkson also slugged 24 home runs over his career which is still 7th most by a pitcher and was the most from a pitcher at the time of his retirement.

John Clarkson was elected to the BBHoF in 1963, 52 years after his death. He died in 1909, aged 47, three years after he was declared insane and committed to an insane asylum.
