A Wild Mark Buehrle Stat

Was exploring Mark Buehrle’s Bref page recently, since his name has been coming up in HOF voting season, and learned a stat that blew my mind:

Buehrle’s rookie season was 2000. He pitched in 28 games, almost entirely relief appearances (3 starts), and threw 51.1 innings.

From the next season (2001) onward, he threw at least 200 innings in 14 straight seasons. This streak spanned the entire rest of his career, other than his final season (2015), in which he threw… 198 and 2⁄3 innings.

This discovery blew my mind. Obviously the 200 inning mark didn’t used to be nearly as impenetrable as it is now, and Buerhle’s career fell right at the very tail end of the era when it was very normal for a starting pitcher to rack up 200 innings in a season. But I still think this streak was crazy for the time period.

Here are the longest 200 innings streaks from some other notable big-innings guys from the same era:

Roy Halladay: 6 seasons (2006-2011)

CC Sabathia: 7 seasons (2007-2013)

Justin Verlander: 8 seasons (2007-2014)

Max Scherzer: 6 seasons (2013-2018)

Felix Hernandez: 8 seasons (2008-2015)

James Shields: 9 seasons (2007-2015)

Cole Hamels: 6 seasons (2010-2015)

Obviously not a comprehensive list, but I’m confident I’m not going to find anyone from the 21st century who surpasses Buehrle. Like… pitchers get injured sometimes. Or at least these days they do.
