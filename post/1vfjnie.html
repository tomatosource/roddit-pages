[Beer] Five second-round picks from the 2026 NBA Draft who have not yet signed professional contracts now have the option to return to college this fall as domestic draft-and-stash prospect

Source: https://tommybeer.substack.com/p/knicks-news-rumor-roundup

A federal court in Colorado issued a consequential ruling on Friday, declaring that all athletes who entered college as members of the high school Class of 2022 and exhausted their fourth season of NCAA eligibility this past spring are eligible to return for a fifth season in 2026-27.

The class certification applies to:

“All persons in the United States who began to play in collegiate sports in the 2022-2023 season, competed in NCAA Division I sports, and completed four years of eligibility as defined by the NCAA’s prior rules by the conclusion of the 2025-2026 season, and are therefore barred from playing a fifth season due to the NCAA’s adoption and immediate implementation of the Five-Year Eligibility Rule.”

In granting the preliminary injunction, U.S. District Judge Charlotte Sweeney ruled that the affected athletes “will suffer irreparable harm“ if the injunction is denied. She also rejected the NCAA’s argument that applying the decision retroactively would create widespread “roster chaos.”

The ruling will have massive ripple effects across college athletics. Yet, how does this impact the NBA, you may be asking? Well, five second-round picks from the 2026 NBA Draft who have not yet signed professional contracts now have the option to return to college this fall as domestic draft-and-stash prospects.

The five draft picks are: Braden Smith (38th, Pacers), Richie Saunders (32nd, Grizzlies), Tyler Nickel (47th, Knicks), Dillon Mitchell (40th, Celtics), and Lajae Jones (54th, Warriors)
