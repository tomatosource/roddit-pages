[OC, Long] Why does baseball not have more World Series MVPs from the losing team? Examining all 70 World Series MVPs to determine how much bias towards winners has historically affected the award

Introduction

Across the three biggest sports leagues in the United States (NFL, NBA, and MLB), there have been 187 championship games/series MVP awards given out (60 Super Bowls, 57 NBA Finals, and 70 World Series). Of those, only three (one in each league) have gone to a player from the losing team.

  • In 1971, Chuck Howley recorded two tackles and two interceptions to win Super Bowl MVP despite the Cowboys losing 16-13
  • In 1969, Jerry West averaged 37.9 points, 4.7 rebounds, and 7.4 assists per game to win Finals MVP despite the Lakers losing in 7 games
  • In 1960, Bobby Richardson set a World Series record with 12 RBI on 11 hits to win World Series MVP despite the Yankees losing in 7 games

In basketball and football, an individual player’s great performance is likely to influence the team’s success. 34 of the 60 Super Bowl MVPs have been quarterbacks, easily the most important individual position in football. NBA teams can run their offense through their best player, giving him the ball when it matters most.

But baseball is the sport where an individual player has the least ability to carry a team to a series win. Pitchers can only pitch a few times in a series, and batters can only take one of every 9 ABs. We’ve seen plenty of heroic individual performances come in lost games and series.

So why has there only been one World Series MVP from a losing team? Has it just coincidentally been true that every time a WS loser put up an incredible performance, someone from the winning team was better? Is is possible that there is a consistent bias towards the winning team resulting in the best player not receiving the award due to the rest of his team? If so, how often?

Judging the Candidates

The primary statistics to be considered are batting lines (AVG/OBP/SLG), HRs and RBI for hitters; IP and ERA for pitchers; and Championship Win Probability Added (cWPA) for everyone. However, statistics do not always paint the full picture of the games - narratives and big moments can inherently change the voting results. With that in mind, along with stats, particularly important moments and narratives will be taken into account when evaluating MVP cases.

Players will not be directly compared to their teammates, as no winner’s bias would be present when comparing two players from the same team.

Each MVP will be sorted into five categories:

  • Right - Should be no debate, no one on the other team was better
  • CBNC - “Close, but no Cigar” - It’s hard to tell who might have won MVP if the winning team was ignored
  • Winner’s Bias - Losing team’s player was clearly better, but many voters will still vote for a winner
  • Robbed - The voters definitely should have picked the losing player, no matter how much they value winning
  • What? - The losing team’s player was better, but winning team also had multiple better players than the MVP

Aggregated Results/Conclusion

TL;DR: Of the 70 years where WS MVP was awarded, 44 were definitely Right, 15 were “Close but no Cigar”, 7 were awarded due to Winner’s Bias, three were complicated beyond winning/losing, and one was awarded to a player on the losing team.

Somewhat surprisingly, despite there being only one losing MVP, there were no instances of a player being truly robbed. While there were 7 players who clearly outplayed the MVP but their team lost, it only takes a moderate amount of winner’s bias to not vote for them.

It’s interesting that in more than 70 years of the sport where one player is least likely to have their individual accomplishments lead to wins, there has only been one instance of a player who undeniably deserved WS MVP more than anyone on the winning team.

How long will it take for a losing player to win WS MVP again? What kind of stats would he need to put up? Surely something like 8 homers in a 7-game series would be enough to override the winner’s bias. We’ll probably never see that, but I do think there will be a performance in the near future that is good enough to win WS MVP as a losing player.
