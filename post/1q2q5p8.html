The Warriors exemplified the worst part of the NBA

I don’t expect anyone to read this. I am writing this mostly as a cathartic exercise.

This week, I spent $400 to watch the Warriors play the Thunder. This was a game that saw (or, in some cases, didn’t see) Stephen Curry, Jimmy Butler, Draymond Green, and others on the bench out with their various ailments. Curry was out with an ankle sprain, Butler couldn’t play due to an illness, and Green came down with a wicked case of rest. Maybe I’m just a sucker, but this is now the third NBA game I’ve taken my brother to where a star player we wanted to see was out (Lakers vs Hawks: LeBron out, Heat vs Hawks: Jimmy Butler out)

My brother had been asking to see the Warriors play this season (which is surprising since the Warriors are as middle-of-the-pack as you can get, but he’s a dedicated fan). He’s pretty busy with school, so with him being on holiday, I thought a nice Christmas gift would be tickets to see his favorite team play the reigning champs. I thought that since the game was nationally televised, at least some of the team’s stars would play. Instead, it was announced the day of the game that none of the players we wanted to see were playing. Even Jonathan Kuminga, who was apparently planned to get significant minutes, was deemed inactive shortly before the game.

Though I don’t believe it was done with this intent, this felt sleazy. It came across as the team waiting for everyone to buy their tickets before dropping this news, because they knew people wouldn’t come otherwise. It was hard not to feel like I was being scammed, especially after I had to pay $10 for an empty cup at the arena.

I understand the NBA schedule is grueling and that the Warriors play back-to-back games, with a game against the Jazz tomorrow. I can see how one can look at the Thunder, then look at their less-than-100-percent healthy roster, and decide that the best long-term strategy would be to rest those unhealthy players so they are available for the more winnable game. This, however, feels like the antithesis of competition and playing professional sports. It feels, from my perspective, that this team decided they were incapable of giving the Thunder their 6th loss, so they chose to punt the game and not even try. It’s a logical strategy, but now you have professional athletes actively avoiding competition in their sport. The fact that it is a strategy at all proves how faulty the NBA schedule is.

I don’t think there’s a professional sport that has more things outside of the game influencing its strategy than professional basketball (the fact that fouls are such a fundamental aspect of the game when it’s ultimately just a penalty is absurd, but that’s a conversation for a different day). How have we reached a place where it makes more sense to not play a game than to try to win every game?

The central problem is the 82-game season. It’s an insane expectation for players to be healthy every game, especially with how frequent these games are. Resting seems like a natural solution, but it’s also an admission that not every game matters, and I think that contributes to the general apathy surrounding the NBA lately. You know what makes the NFL so great? Scarcity. Every game feels like it matters because every game does matter. Teams can’t selectively throw away games in favor of maintaining an aggregate. Players do whatever they can to play, and will even rush back from injury because there’s no concept of “affording” a loss. If you want your sport to be a readily-available, pastime sport like baseball, don’t gouge your fans with these crazy ticket prices (but maybe this is just a Bay Area thing).

Before we left for the game, my brother walked out of his room wearing one of those big Warriors chain necklaces, his face grinning with an excitement that you don’t often see from a teenager. What broke my heart wasn’t having to tell him that his favorite players weren’t playing, but seeing him act like it didn’t bother him out of an obligation of gratitude for getting to go to a Warriors game at all. The Warriors lost by almost 40 points, and we left in the 3rd quarter to beat the traffic on our 1+ hour drive home. At least we got this magazine that thanks all of the Warriors’ sponsors and executives.
