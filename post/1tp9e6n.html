[Fischer] Multiple teams are “working hard” to gauge the LA Clippers’ willingness to trade out of their No. 5 overall selection. Rival teams continue to mention the OKC Thunder as a strong potential trade-up candidate.

Source: https://open.substack.com/pub/marcstein/p/the-latest-chatter-from-the-nba-draft?r=nuq3a&utm_medium=ios

When it comes to next month’s draft, I’m already hearing considerable curiosity about the Clippers, who currently hold the No. 5 selection via Indiana thanks to the teams’ Ivica Zubac trade in February.

How likely are the Clippers to trade that pick? Rival teams are certainly working hard to gauge that, fueled in part by the fact that the pick falls one turn too low to choose from what is a near-consensus foursome of top prospects: AJ Dybantsa, Darryn Peterson, Cam Boozer and Caleb Wilson.

Will the Clippers keep the pick to keep injecting top-tier youth into their program after the recent acquisitions of Darius Garland and Bennedict Mathurin? Or are they more likely to try to use it to acquire a player closer in win-now readiness to Kawhi Leonard when the decision at No. 5 likely means choosing between a slew of guards?

______________________

Yet another nagging question: Which team out there might be eager to trade up to pilfer No. 5 from the Clippers?

It’s unclear whether this particular franchise feels the need to climb that high up the draft board, but rival clubs continue to mention a certain team in Oklahoma City as a strong potential trade-up candidate.

______________________

There is an undeniably growing anticipation leaguewide that the Thunder are going to do something. Sam Presti’s front office is clearly facing some type of roster crunch if it holds on to No. 12 and 17 — plus No. 37 in the second round — while all 15 players on the Thunder’s playoff roster are likewise under contract for next season. And that’s before even considering the team option decisions Oklahoma City will soon face on Isaiah Hartenstein and Lu Dort, plus one of its Game 5 heroes: Kenrich Williams.

The deductive reasoning from there ain’t difficult. And if the Thunder ultimately can’t or don’t trade up and decide to keep No. 12, league sources say they have already communicated to some agents and opposing front offices that they’re very much open to moving pick No. 17.
