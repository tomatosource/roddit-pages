A tanking solution you haven’t heard before: Hot Dog Quarantine

While I think we all know addressing the structural causes of tanking is hard (perhaps impossible), what I haven’t seen proposed are methods to address the symptoms. To my mind, the major negative impacts of tanking are plethora of non-competitive games at the end of the season and the poor in-stadium experience for fans. Unlike the structural issues, these are fixable.

The Format

The premise is simple: the season begins with an initial schedule of 58 games—a home-and-home with every team—to be played before the All-Star break. At that point, the bottom five teams from each conference are eliminated from playoff contention and moved to Hot Dog Quarantine.

The league then schedules the remainder of the season (likely releasing the schedule in one- or two-week tranches to minimize logistical headaches). Hot dog teams only play hot dog teams, and contenders only play contenders. This ensures that the “good” games are high-stakes previews of the playoffs, while the hot dog teams can be terrible in whatever way seems best to them.

The “Hot Dog” Teams

In addition to only playing each other, Hot Dog teams are required to install Costco Food Court booths in their stadiums. These booths must use the same pricing as a traditional Costco. (For those unfamiliar, Costco Food Courts sell a selection of items that are surprisingly high-quality and famously cheap, with their flagship hot dog and soda combo costing only $1.50). Team ownership is required to pay the expense and subsidize other food vendors in the stadium for lost revenue. So the game might be terrible but at least you have your chicken hot dogs.
