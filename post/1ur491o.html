Real routes to use the TPE while staying under the 1st apron

I have to give credit to /u/aceking555 because his recent post covered a lot of the same ground, but after going back and forth with him in the comments, it got me exploring different scenarios and I want to expand on what he’s saying with more detail and depth.

Luxury tax problem: First point of emphasis for those who still aren’t clear on it, we are currently over the luxury tax line. That’s a problem. Boston’s years above the luxury-tax line made it a “repeater” taxpayer. That triggers significantly higher, escalating penalties for every dollar spent over the line. Instead of paying the standard \(1.00 per dollar in the first tax bracket, a repeater starts at \)3.00 per dollar, with the rates growing increasingly brutal as payroll rises. Boston got under the tax in 2025–26, and staying under again in 2026–27 would complete its reset, allowing the team to return to the tax in 2027–28 without paying repeater rates. Teams below the line also avoid the tax entirely and can receive a share of the money collected from taxpaying teams. Boston is about 1 million over this line right now.

Luxury tax solution: Dalano Banton’s 2.8 mil contract is entirely unguaranteed so they can waive him and remove his money from the books. This seems to be the most likely outcome for the season. It would get us down to the minimum 14 players and $198,722,406 under contract.

To be clear, that’s probably the most likely scenario here. Edit: Banton has now been waived

Brad and Bill made it clear in their press conference that if they saw a move that made us a better team, they aren’t completely against the idea of going over the luxury tax this season. My guess is, it would have to be a significant opportunity and player. More likely, we just stay under the tax, but if they genuinely thought “hey people are underrating how good Paul George will be, this is a 60 win team as-is, this one last piece makes us a legit contender”, maybe they consider a move.

Let’s explore it.

TPE offers some flexibility: Because of the Simons trade, we have a $27,678,000 Traded player exception that would theoretically allow us to trade picks for a player and use that TPE to absorb their salary. We have February to use it.

1st Apron Hard Cap Problem: Naturally, fans wonder if that means we could trade 2-3 picks for Trey Murphy (27 mil contract). Problem is, even if we waive Banton we’re at 198,722,406 mil under contract and after signing Mitchell Robinson using the Full Non-Taxpayer Mid-Level Exception, we are hard-capped at the 1st apron (\(209,015,000) meaning that even with Banton waived, we only have \)10,292,594 of room. Meaning, we could theoretically waive Banton and trade picks for a guy making 10.2 mil using a portion of the TPE to absorb his salary, but it would be impossible to take on more without sending out additional salary.

Sending out salary can help: From here you can then start to brainstorm ideas similar to what u/aceking555 outlined in his post where Boston sends out additional salary to give them the opportunity to use more of the TPE. Obviously, if Boston were to move George, White or Pritchard, the math becomes a lot easier here, but if we’re assuming Boston keeps those 3, here’s how I’d think about it…

- Scenario 1 - Just waive Banton = $10,292,594 of room we could use of the TPE so you can start to look at free agents (via sign-and-trade) or current players that fit.

- Scenario 2 - Waive Banton and send out Hauser (10.8) in a deal: Now you’re looking at roughly $21,140,809 of the TPE you could utilize. Current players making less or S&T targets willing to take that as a starting salary are now in play.

- Scenario 3 - Waive Banton and send out Hauser (10.8) + Garza (2.8): Here it becomes interesting, because in theory that would get you to \(23,942,155 to target an individual, but there's a catch... getting rid of 3 roster spots to add 1 leaves us with only 13 players under contract. We'd need at least 14 under contract and the absolute minimum we could probably give someone would be a rookie with 0 years of service at around \)1,358,084. So even if you shave 2.8 of Garza, you have to replace that roster spot with someone making 1.35 meaning we’re at $22,584,071 of the TPE you can use.

- Scenario 4 - Waive Banton and send out Hauser (10.8) + Garza (2.8) + Walsh (2.4): Like Banton, Walsh is non-guaranteed and we could just waive him to remove his salary. For the purpose of this exercise let’s say we send him out (either via trade or waive). Similar rule applies above where you still need to replace that salary with a 0 years of service rookie making around 1.35 mil. So you’re now at $23,632,192.

Just a side note, that with Scenario 4, some are going to push back on the idea of dropping Walsh. It should be noted that mid season these options will remain in play and by then Mike Conley (also around 2.4 mil) is a possible sub-in for Walsh in the concept.

Denver as an example: You can expand these concepts to any team, but for fun let’s just use Denver for all of them. As aceking555 pointed out, their salary situation makes them an interesting trade partner to consider hypotheticals. As-is, the Nuggets are currently sitting at 211 mil on the books with only 9 players under contract. That puts him over the 1st apron and a stone’s throw away from the 2nd apron. In theory, they are going to be looking to alleviate salary.

  • Scenario 1 example: Say we just waived Banton, Boston could in theory make a deal with Denver and absorb the salary of Jonas Valančiūnas (10 mil). Not that Boston would do this, but it’s one example. Is there a guy in the league making less than 10.2 that this would be worth doing for? Edit: Denver just waived him since only 2 mil of his contract was guaranteed, but I was mostly using it as an illustration of the salary range of talent
  • Scenario 2 example: Peyton Watson allegedly wants 25 mil per year. It sounds like Denver is more interested in giving him 20 mil per year. Denver also is open to doing a sign-and-trade. With a sign-and-trade, a player is limited to 4 year deals at 5% annual increase. Let’s say Boston waives Banton and sends out Hauser and picks in the deal. The max we could make work would be giving Watson a first year salary of \(21,140,809. Next 3 years would be \)22,197,849, \(23,254,889, \)24,311,929 for a total payout of 4 years 90.9 million. Slight hiccup here is that doing this deal has base year compensation restrictions on Denver trading Watson where for the purpose of matching salary on the Denver side, Watson only counts as 50% of his new contract (10.5 mil) and can’t take back more than they send out in a trade because they are a 1st apron team. Meaning, either Boston would need to find a 3rd team for Hauser or if Denver wanted Hauser they’d also have to send out a small contract to a 3rd team.
  • Scenario 3 example: As a variation on above, if Boston were to waive Banton and also send out Hauser + Garza, they could offer Peyton Watson a starting salary of \(22,584,071. His next 3 years would be \)23,7113,275, \(24,842,479, \)25,971,683. Total contract value 4 years 97.1 mil. Annual average of 24.2 mil (close to his reported ask of 25 mil/year). Watson reportedly wants more than Christian Braun’s 5 year 125 mil deal which would be difficult given the financial situation. But fwiw, if you just look at the first 4 years of Braun’s contract, it actually works out to 4 years 96.5 mil (first year salary of \(21,551,726) so technically it WOULD pay him more especially if the contract has a player option where he could opt out after his 3rd year and get a larger deal. One last point here is that at a starting salary of \)22,584,071, due to base year compensation, Denver can take back $11,292,035 without issue. So they COULD take Hauser (10.8) back directly. However, Boston would probably have to find a third team to offload Garza.
  • Scenario 4 example: Alternatively if you were to waive Banton and send out Hauser, Garza and Walsh, Boston could trade for Cameron Johnson (\(23,062,500 expiring contract) using the \)23,632,192 in available TPE space. For what it’s worth, I saw a random thread on the Nuggets sub where a fan had suggested trading Johnson for Hauser and two second rounders. Technically this would work. Would Walsh be a suitable replacement for the two 2nd rounders? Even if Denver took on Hauser + Walsh, it cuts 10.4 mil from their books

Quick Note on Murphy: Fwiw, I just don’t see any mathematical route we could trade picks for Murphy and use that full 27.6 mil TPE unless we also traded out one of our larger contracts like George, White or Pritchard. You can play with hypotheticals like George (54 mi) + Hauser (10.8) + filler + picks for Zion (42.1) and Murphy (27 mil) which we could probably make work financially and would intrigue of people, but I’m basing this on the idea Boston keeping George. George (54) + a buttload of picks for Murphy (27 mil absorbed with the TPE, Herb Jones (14.8 mil) + Saddiq Bey (6.5 mil) works and generates a new 35 mil TPE, but again I’m basing the idea George stays. You could trade White for Murphy and use the TPE to absorb Murphy while generating a new 30.3 mil TPE for sending out White, but obviously nobody wants to trade out White. If Pritchard is part of the outgoing salary picture, you can do a lot more, but clearly nobody wants to do that.

Big picture why consider any of this?: I’ll repeat that it would seem the most likely route is just staying under the luxury line. However, I do wonder if how the Paul George thing is being marketed (we’re going to use his big fat expiring contract + picks to land a star) is a bit of a pipe dream. It’s certainly a possibility, but part of me wonders if instead it’s just as likely that George ends up fitting in really well in Boston and being a valuable player. What if adding a guy like Watson is the final piece to push us over the top and George contributes to a deep playoff run? Remember, Horford was 37 when we won a title with him. Can 36 year old George stay healthy and make a similar impact? And if so, is there a route here where next Summer, with no real superstar trade market developing, we convince George to opt out of the final 56.5 mil year of his deal and sign a long-term deal with Boston? For instance 3 years 75 mil descending salary where he gets paid 26.3 mil, 25 mil, 23.6 mil from ages 37-39. Or maybe even 3 years 90 mil descending salary paying him 31.5, 30, 28.4 mil. I’d think Paul would take the guaranteed money rather than take 56.5 mil and risk making up the 18.5 to 33.5 mil difference as a 38 year old free agent in 2028-29.

At that point, assuming we just hang onto George with him on the books for 26-31 mil in 2027-28, maybe he still ends up a tradeable contract, but if Boston also had a guy like Watson under contract at 22-23 mil and White (32.5 mil expiring at that point unless we’ve extended him) and Pritchard (8.3 mil expiring at that point unless we’ve extended him), there would still be lots of options for including players and picks in trades. Or alternatively, we just hang onto the cost-controlled team and run it back if we’re having success.
