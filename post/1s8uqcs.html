[Cowley] Jaden Ivey reportedly asked reporters if they “fornicated before marriage”

It needed a “Godfather I” ending.

Instead, it felt like the final episode of “The Sopranos” – incomplete and fade to black.

That was the disappointment to come out of Monday evening. Bulls ownership had an opening to take care of all family business at once but stalled out after dismissing guard Jaden Ivey for conduct detrimental to the team.

It should have played out like this: Ivey sent packing, followed by the front office that did no homework on him before acquiring the one-man distraction from Detroit. Then at the same time a deal worked out with Billy Donovan in which he can continue coaching if he wants, choosing the general manager/executive he wants in place, and once he feels like his coaching bucket is full, he takes the Brad Stevens express lane to a front office job to help with personnel decisions.

An ending still in play but now major long shot more than reality.

What is reality for the organization is that executive vice president of basketball operations Arturas Karnisovas continues proving that the job is too big for him. The Ivey acquisition itself is not grounds for Karnisovas being let go. What it was, however, was the latest flammable excrement dropped on a dumpster pile that was already in flames.

Before Ivey even stepped foot on Chicago soil there were rumblings from Detroit that the shooting guard was a “preacher.” They undersold it. His interviews became sermons in which he was asking reporters if they’ve been “saved” and if they “fornicated before marriage.”

My bad, brah, I was just checking in on how your knee felt.

It’s great that he has religion, but the relationship between reporter and player isn’t built like that after just few interactions. While reporters were left feeling like they didn’t need to be preached to from a 20-something kid a few years removed from walking around with a silver spoon in his mouth on the AAU circuit, his Bulls teammates were left shaking their heads at just the latest distraction provided by their front office.

Not one Bulls player was shedding a tear for Ivey’s removal from the roster.

And not one Bulls player – former or current – has believed in the Karnisovas plan since the start of the season.

It is over.

The trades have been a disaster, the draft picks a joke, and the idea of chasing “competitive integrity” over increasing draft odds a complete misguided exercise in organizational malpractice.

The Ivey fiasco shouldn’t be the straw that broke the camel’s back as much as a reminder that the camel had damaged legs, one working eye, and a bizarre-looking hump for years.

If Karnisovas stays, there’s a growing chance that Donovan walks at the end of the season. Then the Reinsdorfs will leave Karnisovas with a coaching search, a lottery draft choice, and free agent decisions with money to spend.

This front office can’t even get trade dumps at the deadline right, let alone being trusted to map out the immediate future of an organization that has gone from bad to national embarrassment.

In all fairness to team president Michael Reinsdorf, not one NBA person thought Karnisovas was a bad hire six-plus years ago. In fact, the team was widely applauded for the decision. Billion-dollar companies need to pivot when decisions go bad, however, and that’s where the Bulls are at.

Ivey’s social media rants showed the make-up of the man and why he didn’t fit with the Bulls locker room. More importantly, they put the spotlight directly on the failed front office that brought him to Chicago in the first place.
