Wilt Chamberlain never won an NBA Finals as the leading scorer on his team. Bill Russell did twice.

NBA fans often speak about Wilt Chamberlain as one of the greatest scorers in NBA history based on his unprecedented per-game averages. He averaged 50 PPG for a season, after all. But this overlooks the fact that Chamberlain’s teams were most successful by far when his scoring numbers were dramatically lower. He wasn’t even the number-one scoring option on any of his best teams.

You can see this in his two championship series wins. In the 1967 NBA Finals, he was the 76ers’ fifth leading scorer. He was tied to be their second leading scorer in the playoffs overall. Likewise, in the 1972 NBA Finals, he was the Lakers’ third leading scorer. He was their fourth leading scorer in the playoffs overall.

This wasn’t restricted to his championship years either. Wilt Chamberlain had three other seasons in which he won multiple playoff series: 1969, 1970, and 1973. In the 1969 playoffs, he was the Lakers’ fourth leading scorer (and fourth in the Finals). In the 1970 playoffs, he was the Lakers’ second leading scorer (and second in the Finals). And in the 1973 playoffs, he was the Lakers’ fourth leading scorer (and fourth in the Finals).

This is even more surprising when you draw a comparison to Bill Russell, who is universally seen as a far inferior scorer to Chamberlain. Of course he is; he shot 44% from the field for his career. And yet, most people don’t know that Bill Russell won two of his NBA Finals as the leading scorer on his team. In the 1962 NBA Finals, he was the Celtics’ leading scorer with 22.9 PPG. He was also their most efficient scorer in that series, shooting 54% from the field. And he led his team in PPG for the entire playoffs, so Russell won multiple playoff series in a single year as his team’s leading scorer, which Chamberlain never did. Later on in the 1966 NBA Finals, he was once again the Celtics’ leading scorer with 23.6 PPG. And again he was their most efficient scorer in that series, shooting 54% from the field.

So Chamberlain, who many think of as one of, if not the, the greatest scorers of all time, never had a season in which he won a championship series as the first or second leading scorer on his team. While Bill Russell, who many think of as an awful scorer, won two championship series as the leading scorer on his team.

Between these two players, only one of them won multiple series in a single season as their team’s leading scorer in the playoffs. If you said that to a group of NBA fans, almost all of them would guess that Chamberlain was the one to do that. But it was Bill Russell in 1962. Scoring was easily the weakest part of Russell’s game, but he was still capable of being the most impactful scorer on a championship team.

Chamberlain had unbelievable scoring averages in his early years, but that scoring never translated to success. He never won multiple playoff series in a single year until he allowed other players to shoot more than himself while he focused on defense, rebounding, and playmaking. In other words, Chamberlain’s teams weren’t successful until he started playing more like Bill Russell. In light of these facts, NBA fans should rethink how they compare Chamberlain and Russell as offensive players.
