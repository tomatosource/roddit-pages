Why Brad said “Optionality” so often

I thought I’d nerd out and explain to some who may not understand why Brad is valuing optionality under the current CBA.

Unlike ANY prior time in NBA History the new CBA has very strict rules around building a roster. Arguably the most limiting is that there is now a hard cap. If a team executes a Sign and Trade, uses the MLE, or executes ANY trade in which they take back more money than they are sending out they become hard capped at the first apron. This means they cannot for any reason go over the limit of the first apron and any attempt to do so will be rejected by league rules.

Now why does this matter with the Celtics, because they are the ONLY team in the NBA with two supermax contracts on the books. The current number that a team facing a First Apron hard cap is limited to is \(209M, Tatum and Brown combined cost \)107M in other words about half of what you’re allowed to spend is tied up in two guys. Remember an NBA roster is 14 spots so that leaves \(102M for TWELVE other players or roughly \)8M per player.

This is why Brad wanted options. PG has two years left and won’t be expecting an extension, and with the two picks that were also part of the trade it means the Celtics have 7 First round picks and 8 second round picks. So in 2027 they can either trade Paul George with only a year left as an expiring deal to a team that is also facing CBA restrictions and wants to move off of long terms money or they can salary dump PG and have \(53M to play with along with the picks for trades. OR they keep PG for both years of his contract and go into 2028 with \)53M in cap space and all those picks. It’s a way to essentially multiple avenues to very quickly retool around Tatum.
