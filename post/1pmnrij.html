In 1920, the New York Times named two stand-out athletes powering a “history-making year” for sports in the wake of WWI. One was the racehorse Man o’ War, who was named “the outstanding figure of the two”. The other was Babe Ruth.

From the article(behind paywall):

A superman and a superhorse - these were sport’s greatest contributions to the history of the year about to close. Some might rate the superhorse, Man o’ War, as the outstanding figure of the two, for he has passed on from the field of competition and has left a story of achievement which may never be surpassed. The superman of the sport world, Babe Ruth, remains to strive for greater records than those made by him or evolving around him during the year. At any rate, the anomaly of baseball’s greatest hitter astride the turf’s leading performer for all time provides fitting leadership for the sport parade of 1920 as it passes in review.

1920 was a huge year for Babe Ruth. It was his first season as a Yankee and he obliterated his own league record by driving an unprecedented 54 home runs. It was one of the greatest individual seasons in baseball history.

That same year, Man o’ War was setting his own records, obliterating numerous track and distance time records. To give you a sense of just how absurdly dominant Man o’ War was, at the 1920 Lawrence Realization Stakes, every other horse was pulled from the race because rival horse owners refused to race against Man o’ War. The only other horse was a last-minute entry named Hoodwink. Man o’ War won the race by an absurd 100 lengths, with some contemporary sports writers claiming the real margin was closer to 200 lengths. Later in the year he would win the Belmont Stakes by 20 lengths, a record at the time and is still the 3rd largest margin of victory to this day. In his career, Man o’ War won 20 out of 21 races, with his only loss coming in 1919 at the hands hooves of the appropriately named Upset.
