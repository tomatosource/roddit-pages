Lore analysis of the second Teaser “Overcome the Insanity”

Alright, ive done this pre-abyss already, but this is another Ted Talk about the PoE lore and what that might mean for the upcoming content. I will be explaining the lore sources i use and will conclude what it might mean for the content at the end. If you dont care about the lore, skip ahead to the last section.

First of all to clarify, i dont think this will be the actual league content, but a rework of the base content as part of the expansion.

The Lore:

The second teaser shows Elder Madox, who we have met in the interlude III. He is an Elder of the Azmeri tribe with a deep connection to the Azmeri Owl spirit, also known as the Winter Owl, the spirit of wisdom.

Previously his hesitation has brought misfortune to the Azmeri, wich is why he dedicates himself to observing the land from the mountain range. He has now decided to fight the “insanity” that has befallen Wraeclast.

He is being guided by the owl spirit to a growth in the woods, wich is shrouded by a fog similar to the fog we see in simulacrums and in this growth, a mirror not unlike the Delirium mirror is shown. Inside the mirror is the image of a child that is asking to be saved.

Now who might this child be:
I suspect the child might be the original mind of Tangmazu, also known as “The Raven Trickster”, to the Azmeri; “Tekaru, the Deceiver” to the Karui; “the Black Crow” to the Ezomytes and “The Strange Voice” to the Player.

In poe2, when we receive Delirium atlas points, it unlocks new dialogues from Alva, where she describes having strange dreams. These dreams seem to have alternatives in the files wich im also gonna include. These seem to be older versions of the dream, wich i dont know about how cannon they are. There are 4 Dreams in total and they go:

First Dream:

“As I’ve studied these grey splinters you found, I’ve come to believe thatthey have a kind of… knowledge imbued in them.

It’s fragmented, of course. Not enough to glean a conscious thought. But I keep having dreams…

Nothing coherent yet… but I’ll let you know…”

Second Dream:

“I had a more coherent dream after working with a larger number of splinters… I saw a feast. A peaceful, tranquil place. I felt… smiles. I felt calm.

I didn’t get the impression that this was part of a vast empire, or a grand civilisation, or anything like that. Just a rare haven from the world’s ills.

I really wish I could have written it down… but it’s gone. I can’t hold on to the sensations…”

Third Dream:

“I had another dream… well, more like a nightmare. I was running through a glade, chasing a rabbit, but no matter what,

I couldn’t catch it. It fled into a stump, and disappeared into darkness… while I was peering down, something loomed above me. A horned creature. Emaciated. I could see its shadow fall over me but… it seemed impossible. It was like the shadow came from… below.

 When I finally met its gaze, I expected horror, but instead, I was blinded by a void. My eyes were open, yet I saw only… eternity. A creature reached out its claw and cut deeply into my forehead.

Countless screaming voices poured into my mind. Then… I woke. Disturbed, but… still myself. What do you think it means?”

alternative:

“I had another dream… more like a nightmare. There was so much anger, so much hate. They were all dead.

The haven… bloodied. Bodies everywhere, mutilated, all because of a harmless little lie I told, the kind any child might. They made me watch.

I was alone. Traumatized. I vowed to make them suffer. I vowed to darken the places they felt safest. To turn their very homes into prisons of paranoia and fear. I did it, too.

Countless faces twisted in agony and misery. I kept hurting them, but it never made me feel better… it just made the void bigger. When I woke up, I felt… empty…”

Fourth Dream:

“I believe I had the final dream. I think I felt… divinity, as crazy as that sounds.

 In the dream, I was hollow. Completely empty. I was a liar. A torturer. A deceiver. 

I’d perfected the art of contorting reality to my will, purely to bring untold misery upon the world. Perhaps strangest of all, I had a… portal of sorts… in my forehead. 

A gateway from my mind to the world. Where all the horrors I imagined could be made real… I think I’m done with those splinters. I don’t want to know anymore.

The completed object… when the splinters are reassembled… it’s a fruit. A stone carving of a fruit we used to gather from the shore. Sweet and juicy, like summer, now lost forever… Sorry, what they used to gather…”

alternative:

“Once you put all those splinters together, I believe I had the final dream. I think I felt… Divinity, as crazy as that sounds.

In the dream, I was hollow. Completely empty. I was a liar. A torturer. A deceiver. I’d perfected the art of shaping reality with words, purely to enact misery.

I was so good at deception, I lied to myself, told myself that I was a god… I was so convincing, even I believed it. And you know what? It became true…

But I knew that if I ever stopped being alone, if I ever let go of my agony, if I ever let myself care… it would all unravel… and I woke up feeling hopeless.

Divinity isn’t wonderful. It’s isolating.

I think I’m done with those splinters. I don’t want to know anymore. The completed object… it’s a fruit.

A stone carving of a fruit we used to gather from the shore. Sweet and juicy, like summer, now lost forever… Sorry, what they used to gather…”

These Dreams describe the Story of a child. A child that has grown up in a “safe haven” not associated with any of the cultures we know. Somehow, an innocent lie of this child has caused the demise of his whole community, and thus, the child was consumed by anger, revenge and hate. This child achieved divinity, being able to turn lies into reality, similar to Tangmazu himself.

Conclusion:

The teaser points towards a narrative driven rework of the current Delirium mechanic, with Elder Madox being our guide and the end goal might be to confront Tangmazu himself (probably in a direct Boss fight), either to free his mind from his trauma or to destroy him and give his Child-like mind a rest.
