0.5 Campaign & Endgame Feedback

Act 1

1a. You should not need to talk to Una in town to summon Una to the Grim Tangle if you’ve broken the runes on the tree.

1b. The dialogue at the end of the Act 1 boss’ dialogue is a long time to wait for loot to drop. I end up tp’ing out early a lot of the time. This slow paced moment halts all momentum. A skip button feels necessary if slow story beats are here to stay (akin to the placement of the end delirium button).

-————–

Act 2

2a. The Path of Mourning has nothing of interest. I often end up running through the zone without bothering to clear.

-————–

Act 3

3a. The Drowned City feels vestigial. The Molten Vault is a loading screen to grab the waypoint, and lacks any incentive to actually complete during campaign.

3b. Walking all that way out and waiting for Sin to drop his hood at the end of the act weirdly sucks. I feel like 1b, 3b, and 4e are all sources of fatigue for why people always whine about wanting to skip campaign, and they’re still worth pointing out - however small.

-————-

Act 4

4a. Singing Caverns is a huge drop in my fun. Whole chunks consistently had zero monster density on multiple playthroughs, and the boss has more mechanics/invuln than end of Act bosses. This might be the only boss that I feel demands tweaking to give me more agency - something like needing to break five crystals in the singing phase to immediately end it.

4b. I liked that the Torn Map Pieces weren’t mandatory. The expedition reward + Farrow questline feel like they require far more steps than navigating to the Molten Vault or the Lightless Passage to unlock their respective content early. I think this area is tricky to navigate, when considering adopting to core, as Kingsmarch + Torn Map Pieces remains the perfect spot to introduce and incorporate Farrow. I would hope it remains skippable, and that routing to Kingsmarch in endgame would unlock everything you skipped in campaign.

4c. The Eye of Hinekora is so incredibly pretty, which is a shame because this ends up feeling like the worst zone in the whole game. Enemy design lacks density, is slow to spawn, and not engaging to fight. I end up running past most. The need to wrap around this area twice (once linear w/ zero reward, another non-linear w/ rewards) feels unnecessary. A single wrap around w/ rewards + Navali’s Rest, followed by the the Yama Fight would be more than enough content. Currently, it feels unnecessarily padded.

4d. It is clear that a lot of effort went into the Act 4 final boss. It is also my least favorite Act boss. Each stage of the fight lacks readability, specifically in the color and intensity of VFX. The result is a cluttered fight, but instead of feeling intentional, it just feels… Garish, and messy.

4e. The scenes after The Excavation (beating Benedictus, First Herald) halts all momentum. This needs a skip button. Not only does this feel like the moment where the Act should end, its glacial pace to get through these cutscenes does not leave me excited to actually finish the Act.

4f. Parry is in a much better spot in the game, now. I think it feels great through campaign, and has some great new flavors (ie Kalguur gem). The glaring exception to this rule is when campaign boss design absolutely saps the viability of your build - mainly: the boss of Volcanic Warrens boss, who you cannot parry reliable, if not ever.

-————–

Atlas Mapping

5a. The Arbiter of Ash’s design does not take multiplayer into account. The line phase should take the average of each player’s positioning before placing down another line. The tight windows and circles made body-blocking a major concern. It was, ultimately, way way easier to take down this content alone. If that is the intended case, I’d rather have been forced to start the fight alone.

5b. The Arbiter of Ash’s circle phase is too far spaced out to allow for comfortable readability. Watching a friend play through the fight on their monitor vs my ultrawide made this issue all the more apparent.

5c. The Arbiter of Ash /still/ feels like the hardest fight, yet it’s located incredibly early into the endgame experience (and blocks off so much of the atlas). I ended up focusing on clearing the fight before investing in any other content - which in turn, made all other pinnacle bosses pale in comparison. I would rather see a lot of the more unforgiving qualities of the fight removed from the first Arbiter of Ash. Focusing on readability and minimizing some of the challenge could allow those details to return in, say, an Uber version of the fight. The Arbiter of Divinity, in comparison, still feels easier to me than Ash.

5d. I have never used a Widowhail. I still pick up every Widowhail I see, identify it - crank them to 20% quality, socket, vaal, or simply collect to reforge for a perfect value range. There’s something incredibly satisfying about uniques like this, that make them worth paying attention to - and I find myself doing the same with uniques like Ventor’s Gamble - they’re /worth/ my time to look at their stats and evaluate how good they are. I wish this feeling existed for more uniques - to give them higher ceilings, that made reforging and vaal’ing more exciting - especially on low level uniques like Widowhail. Why don’t a ton of unique boots have a chance at reaching higher ranges on their movement speed? Why cant Painter’s Servant, or Erian’s Cobble, have even more extreme ranges? I just feel like there’s still so much room, here, to make me excited to see a unique on the ground.

5e. Atlas Passive Choice Node || +Select a monster modifier to remove [Mana Siphon] (or) +[Small % chance] to add additional monster modifier to rares. //// Yes, the obvious answer is to try and balance monster modifiers - but this is such an easy way to collect data on which monster modifier players/builds overwhelmingly dislike. Keeping it as an atlas passive also makes sure they deal with the modifier throughout campaign, and while climbing endgame.

5f. I did everything 0.5 wanted me to do, and I could have run through even more alts, but there was only one singular reason I stopped playing - Crashing. Most of the times I’ve stepped away from the game were not when I chose to stop, and it was the top reason why I’ve stopped playing 0.5. I had a lot of fun, and could arguably spend a ton of more time making new or old builds stronger - but I can’t theorycraft my way out of losing 1 portal maps due to hitching and crashes.
