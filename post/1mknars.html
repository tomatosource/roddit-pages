How the Cipher Works

This puzzle uses a Pigpen Cipher, also known as a Masonic or Freemason’s Cipher. The alphabet is placed into a series of grids, and the shape of the cell containing a letter becomes the symbol for that letter.

For this specific puzzle, a slightly simplified version is used. The letters are encoded based on their position within two tic-tac-toe grids:

  • Grid 1 (Letters A-I): The symbol is the shape of the lines surrounding the letter.
  • Grid 2 (Letters J-R): The symbols are the same shapes as the first grid, but with a dot in the middle.

Here is the key for the letters used in the puzzle:

  • A = └
  • B = ┴
  • C = ┘
  • D = ├
  • E = ┼ (though in the game, it’s shown as a full square □)
  • F = ┤
  • G = ┌
  • H = ┬
  • I = ┐
  • K = └ with a dot
  • L = ┴ with a dot
  • N = ┘ with a dot
  • O = ├ with a dot (though in the game, it’s often a diamond shape &lt;&gt;)
  • R = ┌ with a dot

The game takes some artistic liberties with the symbols, but the underlying grid logic remains the same. The handwritten notes like “Well?”, “Winter?”, and “King?” are clues pointing to keywords in the final message.
