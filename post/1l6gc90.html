[Discussion] Suns Teardown Hypothetical: Booker to Detroit

Proposal: Devin Booker to Detroit — Ivey Heads to Phoenix as the Suns Rebuild Around Beal (Tank Commander) + KD to San Antonio

The Trade

Detroit Pistons receive:

  • Devin Booker
  • Royce O’Neale
  • Jordan Goodwin

Phoenix Suns receive:

  • Jaden Ivey
  • Isaiah Stewart
  • Tim Hardaway Jr. (expiring)
  • 2026 1st-round pick (top-5 protected)
  • 2030 1st-round pick (unprotected)

How It Works

Let’s be real — Mat Ishbia is running the Suns like it’s NBA 2K, and if he trades Booker, he’s going to want a Big Ten guy like Ivey in return. In this scenario, Phoenix trades Kevin Durant to San Antonio (KD literally said “San Antonio” when asked), keeps Bradley Beal as the franchise’s tank commander (NTC), and begins a soft rebuild.

For Detroit:

  • Booker gives Cade a legitimate co-star without overlapping skill sets.
  • Royce O’Neale brings veteran playoff experience, 3-and-D versatility.
  • Jordan Goodwin adds backcourt depth and defensive energy off the bench.
  • Giving up Ivey hurts, but the Pistons keep Cade, Ausar, Duren, and both their forward rotation options.
  • This elevates Detroit from “promising” to “dangerous.”

For Phoenix:

  • Ivey becomes the new face of a youth-centered rebuild.
  • Stewart adds toughness and cost-controlled size.
  • THJ gives short-term floor spacing and an expiring contract.
  • Two first-round picks staggered across windows give Phoenix the ability to trade or draft for real value.
  • Beal sticks around as Vice Admiral of the Tank Fleet. They get bad, but in a structured way.

The Salary Cap

Detroit Incoming Salary:

  • Booker ~ $53M
  • O’Neale ~ $9M
  • Goodwin ~ $2M
  • Total: ~$64M

Detroit Outgoing Salary:

  • Ivey ~ $9M
  • Stewart ~ $15M
  • THJ ~ $17M
  • Total: ~$41M

Net cap increase: ~$23M, but Detroit still stays below the luxury tax line due to rookie-scale contracts, expiring deals, and the flexibility built into their books.

Phoenix sheds long-term salary, resets their roster, and stays under the tax while developing a young core.

Conclusion

This version of the trade reflects where both teams are headed:

  • Phoenix goes full youth movement post-KD, with Ivey/Stewart/headliner picks, while Beal logs minutes as a high-usage tank commander.
  • Detroit builds a core of Cade-Booker-Ausar-Duren that balances skill, athleticism, and youth — with playoff-level spacing and shot creation.
  • The Pistons still have flexibility to find a backup big via free agency or trade.
  • Phoenix gets what Ishbia would want: a young guard, grit, and future capital.
