Multiverse of Reddit Madness: How would your team do if they signed LeBron James?

According to reports, LeBron James is relying on Rich Paul and voice memos to pick his next destination. However, there’s another angle he can consult: the Multiverse. Here’s a look through 32 different timelines to see how James did with each respective decision.

ATLANTA HAWKS

The Hawks already handed the franchise over to their own playmaking forward in Jalen Johnson, who will now have to share the spotlight with LeBron James. Of course, in terms of “duplication,” having two big forwards who can pass is a better problem to have than others. Can the rest of the supporting cast step up? C.J. McCollum, Nickeil Alexander-Walker, and Dyson Daniels make up for a “good enough” backcourt that helps propel the team to the 4th seed in the regular season.

In the playoffs, the Hawks match up with a team the King snubbed: the Cavs. And here, the matchups work against them. The Cavs’ big men duo of Jarrett Allen + Evan Mobley outclass the Hawks frontcourt and grind out a 4-2 series win.

BOSTON CELTICS

Like Atlanta, Boston already made their choice between their stars, swearing allegiance to Jayson Tatum.

Having a 41-year-old LeBron James around doesn’t change that math much. James and Paul George act as complementary pieces as Tatum works his way back to 100% strength and back into the All-NBA conversation.

The Celtics enter the playoff as the # 2 seed out East, with the flexibility to throw out a super-sized lineup (Derrick White, George, James, Tatum, and a big) that motivates many pundits to pick them to win the conference. At first, the Celts look capable of living up to that with two series wins. In the Conference Finals, they match up with rival New York for an epic clash of would-be titans. Unfortunately, the Celtics shooting runs cold (perhaps a product of the older James and George getting worn out), as the Knicks beat them 4-2 and return to the Finals instead.

BROOKLYN NETS

Brooklyn’s most decorated players are also big fowards in Michael Porter Jr. and Julius Randle, leading to a questionable fit with LeBron James. The Nets’ young guards (Egor Demin, Mikel Brown, and Nolan Traore) also look too inexperienced for a playoff run as well. Fans are unsure why James signed there at all. Did he get off at the wrong subway stop?

The team flirts with .500 but looks destined for the play-in tournament instead. Around midseason, the Nets brass have a heart-to-heart with James, debating whether this is truly the best place for him to be. James agrees to a buy-out, and the Nets agree to pay for his cab fare to a real contender for the playoffs.

CHARLOTTE HORNETS

Earlier in his career, LeBron James resisted the idea of mentoring younger players (like would-be Cav Andrew Wiggins), but he’s accepting the challenge here in Charlotte. In his mind, Kon Knueppel and Brandon Miller are advanced enough to be great spacers for him.

The plan looks good at the start as James averages 9 assists a game in his de facto PG role. There’s even some early “MVP!” buzz from talking heads looking to fill air time. Eventually, the momentum slows down and the team settles into a 6th seed. The Hornets will happily take their playoff berth, but they don’t appear to be serious contenders for the crown. And sure enough, they’re handily beaten 4-1 by Philadelphia in the first round, as Tyrese Maxey and Jaylen Brown crash the paint and averaged a combined 50 PPG in the series.

CHICAGO BULLS

In another dimension, LeBron James mentors two other young prospects in Matas Buzelis and Caleb Wilson. But in this case, there’s more duplication involved. Neither Buzelis nor Wilson work as reliable spacers, leading to some clogged courts and some side-eye glares from James. He doesn’t appear to trust these young kids yet. And he doesn’t appear to trust his new PG Josh Giddey around his teenage daughter either.

At the end of the day, it’s a sour fit that leads to a play-in appearance but no playoff ticket.

CLEVELAND CAVALIERS

LeBron James’ return to Ohio leads to overwhelming media coverage and a potentially overwhelming wealth of talent. The Cavs immediately become one of the betting favorites to win the title, with two bigs and three All-Star perimeter players.

At first, there’s some unexpected bumpiness as the team figures out their proper play style. Do they run through Donovan Mitchell? James Harden? LBJ? The Cavs start out a modest 18-12, a record that leads to some panic in the social media space. Skip Bayless claims James made a fatal mistake hitching his wagon to a bum like Harden and will thus “never be in the GOAT conversation again!”

But the Cavs eventually find their footing and James finds his way right back into that GOAT conversation. The team finishes as the # 2 seed behind the Knicks. And there, in a ballyhooed Eastern Conference Finals, Donovan Mitchell steps up as the alpha dog and scores 35 points in a decisive Game 7. It appears that the King is set for a storybook ending. However, the OKC Thunder have something else to say in the Finals. The Cavs have a difficult time bottling up Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and a cohesive Thunder attack, as OKC wins 4-2 and leaves all of Cleveland broken hearted.

DALLAS MAVERICKS

LeBron James makes the unexpected decision to go to Dallas, reuniting with his old buddy Kyrie Irving in the process. But where does that leave new Mavs star Cooper Flagg? Nice guy Flagg plays the role of good soldier, saying that he’s unconcerned with his stats as long as the team make a playoff run.

Unfortunately, that’s no guarantee. The old Dallas perimeter (with Irving, Klay Thompson, and James) leads to some poor defensive effort at times. There’s also grumbling that the old heads haven’t fully embraced a “college” coach in Dusty May. The media claims there’s a virtual “civil war!” in the locker room between the old dogs and the new guard (Flagg, Dusty May, Morez Johnson, etc). But in truth, there’s simply not enough talent on the team to do much better than their 8th seed. The team loses 4-1 to OKC in the first round.

DENVER NUGGETS

The Nuggets proudly announce the upset as LeBron James joins forces with Nikola Jokic in Denver. It’ll unite two of the smartest basketball players of all time on a roster that can support them with some sharpshooters like Jamal Murray and Cam Johnson. The team is among the early title favorites.

Even still, the Nuggets need to survive the regular season first. Their depth is a question mark, particularly at backup center and particularly if Christian Braun can’t bounce back to form. More than anything, adding to that depth is James’ biggest assignment on the team. He handles enough of the load and absorbs enough minutes for the Nuggets to keep Jokic (and Aaron Gordon) more refreshed and rested than they’ve been in the last few playoffs.

The team wins two series (including an epic win over OKC), but faces another tough matchup in the WCF against the younger and friskier San Antonio Spurs. Here, the Nuggets’ lack of a reliable backup big hurts them, as Jokic looks worn down. The Spurs win the clash, 4-3, sending the team home disappointed. It’s doubly bitter because the Spurs won partly because of their depth – with rookie big Tarris Reed Jr. playing solid minutes. And as it happens, he’s the same player the Nuggets could have drafted if they didn’t trade down for cap relief.

DETROIT PISTONS

LeBron James joins Detroit, giving the Pistons two giant-sized playmakers to run their operation through. James willingly takes a backseat to Cade Cunningham during the regular season. In fact, James’ presence helps open the game up even more for Cunningham, whose efficiency spikes and lands him at # 3 in MVP voting.

But come playoff time, a familiar issue emerges for the team. Even with two stars, there isn’t enough shooting in the supporting cast to score consistently in the halfcourt. It’s difficult for J.B. Bickerstaff to play Jalen Duren and Ausar Thompson together, leading to some scrambled lineups and an upset loss in R2 of the playoffs.

GOLDEN STATE WARRIORS

The “Expendables” dream gets greenlit in Golden State, as LeBron James teams up with his former rivals turned friends in Stephen Curry and Draymond Green. While their collective age is certainly an issue, James swears that the respect and chemistry between the group will lead to smooth sailing.

One month in, it’s not so smooth. The Warriors start out decently, but don’t look like true contenders. There are leaks from James’ camp that the team should consider benching Green in favor of Yaxel Lendeborg who may have more life in his legs. Various minor injuries also creep up here and there, leading the team to barely slip into the playoffs as a 7th seed (after a play-in win).

Still, the fans and media hope that the team may be able to “flip the switch” come playoff time. But unfortunately, that lower seed means a matchup against one of the Conference powers: in this case, San Antonio. The old Warriors play nobly, but lose 4-2 in the first round.

HOUSTON ROCKETS

In the neighboring universe, LeBron James teams up with another former rival in Kevin Durant. Here, he’ll be a welcome addition to a team that struggled to find its playmaking last season.

The Rockets play well for the majority of the year and even have the # 3 seed for a bit, but familiar problems emerge. There are reports that Durant and James are bristling at the hard-charging coaching style of Ime Udoka. And there are reports that Udoka doesn’t still doesn’t trust Reed Sheppard as a potential spacer for his stars. Overall, the bad vibes swallow up the season and lead to the team’s premature dismissal in R1. Udoka is fired after the season.

INDIANA PACERS

Although LeBron James may have laughed off the idea of joining Tyrese Haliburton in Indiana, he has a change of heart and decides to team up with the likable young man after all.

It’s a good combination right away, as James’ presence takes some of the pressure off Haliburton in his return from injury. There’s a natural chemistry to the club – as they climb as high as # 3 in the Eastern Conference standings. That leads to a tough second-round matchup against defending champion New York (who had been coasting to a 2-seed). The IND/NYK rivalry is alive and well, heating up the TV ratings. Unfortunately, the Pacers’ defense isn’t quite title caliber, leading to an epic 4-3 loss.

LOS ANGELES CLIPPERS

Clippers owner Steve Ballmer manages to force his Kawhi Leonard trade through and follow it up with another rabbit out of his hat with a vet-minimum deal for LeBron James. The NBA immediately launches an investigation to see whether Ballmer secretly paid James more under the table, but the league office’s head inspector Clouseau finds no evidence.

Adam Silver drops the case as soon as he realizes it won’t matter much. The Clips have some scorers in Darius Garland, Brandon Ingram, and LeBron James, but their defensive limitations prevent the team from looking like a viable contender. The Clippers struggle to make the playoffs and get bounced out in R1.

LOS ANGELES LAKERS

After surveying his options elsewhere, LeBron James pulls a George Constanze and simply shows up at his old office like nothing happened.

At first, it appears to be a genius move. The 2025-26 Lakers may have been doomed by their roster construction, but now that the team has a true defensive anchor in Walker Kessler it’s a different story altogether. James willingly plays the role of third banana behind Luka Doncic and Austin Reaves given the team’s success, as the club climbs to a # 3 overall seed. In a rematch against Houston, they thump the Rockets again. And this time, they may even venture further than that.

However, in R2, the defensive issues pop their ugly head up again as Kessler can’t cover for all the mediocre perimeter defense around him. Denver rolls past them, 4-1.

MEMPHIS GRIZZLIES

As it turns out, LeBron James felt so bad about his comments about Memphis that he wants to make it up to the city and play there in 2026-27.

Expectations are low, but that only creates some goodwill when the team starts out decently well (with a 8-9 record). James goes out of his way to praise rookie teammate Cam Boozer, calling him one of the smartest teammates that he’s ever had. Along with big Zach Edey, the trio may be able to push this team into an unexpected playoff berth.

Unfortunately, Edey misses time here and there, leading to some losses. It doesn’t help that James is a natural PF right now, and many of the Grizzlies’ top players could be there as well (Boozer, Jerami Grant, and former LBJ’s former MMA opponent Isaiah Stewart). The good vibes and good intentions of the Memphis move can’t lead to immediate success, as the team has a respectable 38 wins but misses the playoffs. Regardless, it’s a heartfelt effort that ends with an emotional 20-minute standing ovation at the end of his last game. James addresses the crowd, saying how much he appreciates the Memphis fans and how Memphis will feel like a second home to him from now on.

(After the speech, he never returns to Memphis again.)

MIAMI HEAT

Pat Riley and Miami land their newest big three: agreeing to terms with LeBron James to join Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo in Miami. And while James may not be at the top of his game defensively, the switchability of the trio leads to one of the best frontcourts in the league. The “King” also allows the prince Antetokounmpo to handle most of the scoring load, as Antetokounmpo averages 28 PPG and lands in the top 3 in MVP voting.

If there’s a concern with Miami as they head toward playoffs it’s a matter of spacing. PG Davion Mitchell’s shooting has slumped down below 35% from 3 (after two solid years) and made the team consider an upgrade in that area. Pat Riley tries to figure out a solution – even half-tempted to re-acquire Tyler Herro from Milwaukee. However, Herro doesn’t appear interested in rejoining Adebayo and the Heat, so talks stall. The Heat can’t seem to find the missing piece that they need, and end up losing in R2.

MILWAUKEE BUCKS

In a surprise move, LeBron James will fill the void left by Giannis Antetokounmpo and lead the Bucks back into the playoffs. In terms of roster balance, it’s a double upgrade, as James takes over the PF mantle and pushes Kyle Kuzma down to be a second-unit scorer (a role which he may be better suited for). Between Ryan Rollins, Tyler Herro, Jaime Jaquez and Myles Turner, there’s enough talent for the team to flirt with the playoffs. But at the end of the day, the team’s lack of “high end” star power leads them to land in the play-in instead. A big game from Herro gets the Bucks into the playoffs as the 8th seed, but they’re blasted 4-1 in that first round.

MINNESOTA TIMBERWOLVES

LeBron James stocks up on winter jackets as he heads up north to Minnesota in pursuit of another title.

In terms of roster construction, it’s a great fit for both sides. The T-Wolves had lost both Julius Randle and Naz Reid, so James fills in the space at PF perfectly. His skill set also aligns with LaMelo Ball and Anthony Edwards, deferring to them when needed and performing the role of a “glue guy” perfectly well. The Wolves head into the playoffs as one of the top favorites along with OKC and San Antonio.

Of course, slicing through the gauntlet of the Western Conference is no easy task, and will take Minnesota’s best effort. They have to play disciplined, mistake-free basketball. And unfortunately for them, the inconsistency of LaMelo Ball gets the best of them, as Ball struggles against San Antonio’s backcourt (33% FG, 4.2 TO per game) and allows the team to lose 4-2 in R2.

NEW ORLEANS PELICANS

In an unexpected move, LeBron James signs with the New Orleans Pelicans. According to him, there’s some untapped potential with the club and a reason that they won 49 games a few years ago. He vows to get them back to that level and vows to get the most out of Zion Williamson. Under James’ workout plan, Williamson will get in phenomenal shape and fulfill his vast potential.

A few weeks later, James decides to give up on Williamson and take Derik Queen under his wing instead. He’ll get him into phenomenal shape if it’s the last thing he does.

A few hours later, James decides to give up on that dream, too. He asks the team if they could try to include him in any Trey Murphy trade packages. Unfortunately, a trade doesn’t come together, and the Pelicans miss out on the playoffs.

NEW YORK KNICKS

LeBron James’ decision to join the defending champions is met with mixed reactions. There’s immediate brushback in the New York media about whether or not he’ll spoil the team’s magical chemistry. Will he take the ball out of Jalen Brunson’s hands? Will he push one of the starters to the bench? Will he clash with Mike Brown (whom he indirectly got fired twice in Cleveland)?

Of course, when James signed with the Knicks, he didn’t do it with the intention of taking over the team. He willingly fits in well in a marginalized role early on as the team rolls for most of the season. James’ worth is best felt when O.G. Anunoby misses 15 games, allowing James to step up and remind the league that he’s still a near All-Star caliber player when refreshed and rested.

The Knicks enter the playoffs as the favorites in the East, but it’s tougher sledding than expected. The team can’t carry over their red-hot shooting from the 2026 playoffs as easily, and actually take 7 games to win in the first round. They settle down and find their rhythm, advancing to the Finals against OKC in a series that most consider a 50⁄50 proposition. Some clutch play (and some good whistles) lead to a second title for these Knickerbockers, although James doesn’t get as much credit as usual for this particular ring.

OKLAHOMA CITY THUNDER

In a nearby universe, LeBron James joins up with the former champion Oklahoma City Thunder. And as with the NYK version of LeBron James, this one isn’t going to rock the boat on a team with proven chemistry and results. In fact, his best contribution to the regular season is allowing the team to manage the minutes for him and Jalen Williams to the point where both can enter the playoffs at 100%.

And with that, it’s a wrap. Even at this age, James proves a solid addition to the starting lineup and gives a team that didn’t need much help more than enough to cruise. 4-0, 4-1, 4-2, 4-2. It’s another ring for the Thunder and the “King,” although not one that he led the way on.

ORLANDO MAGIC

In theory, a good starter like LeBron James should be able to push a “good” team up to “great”. But unfortuantely for LeBron James and his new Orlando squad, the fit isn’t perfect together. Their key players Franz Wagner and Paolo Banchero have some duplication with James, who in turn doesn’t provide the elite shooting that the club needs to take the next step.

The same problems that plagued Orlando over the last few years (namely shooting outside of Desmond Bane) return once again, leading to a disappointing R1 exit.

PHILADELPHIA 76ers

Ohio’s own Mike Gansey pulls his second major coup, convincing his high school rival LeBron James to join Jaylen Brown on a star-studded Philadelphia team. On paper, the team looks stacked, especially given James’ perfect fit at the PF position. With All-Stars like Tyrese Maxey, Jaylen Brown, and Joel Embiid around, James doesn’t have to do too much, filling in the gaps and providing leadership as the team flirts with the # 1 seed all season long. At long last, it may finally be the final step to the “Process.”

As the playoffs approach, there are some minor causes of concern. Youngsters V.J. Edgecombe and Labaron Philon have some “brain fart” momentums, leading to doubts about their title readiness. The team’s center depth behind Joel Embiid is also a major question. When Embiid misses a few weeks late in the year, the team stumbles with a 1-3 stretch. James volunteers to play center in the meantime, earning laudatory praise from the media and even earning him a few MVP votes for “teaching the Sixers how to win.”

Still, those underlying questions remain all the way through the playoffs and into the inevitable ECF matchup against the Knicks. There, Embiid’s durability plagues them again. He doesn’t look 100%, and shoots 6⁄17 from the field in a devastating Game 6 loss to put the nail in their coffin. After the game, Embiid suggests that the Sixers fans should be happy with the deep run because “The Conference Finals is pretty good, right?” The Philly fans respond by pelting him with soggy cheesesteaks.

PHOENIX SUNS

LeBron James signs with Phoenix for… some reason. No one is sure why. It’s neither here nor there. In fact, it’s so strange that it motivates a lonely redditor by the user name of BloodyPigDick to investigate why. He spends all day and night in his room, coming up with conspiracy theories.

Meanwhile, the James-led Suns are… OK. There’s enough talent and enough shooting to keep them in the playoff conversation, but enough question marks to keep them from the top of the field. They head into the playoffs as a 6th seed.

During Game 5, the game flow is interrupted as BloodyPigDick bursts into the arena. He’s uncovered why LeBron James is on the team. He’s discovered that the “multiverse” is real and has broken open ground-breaking new discoveries about our planet. Unfortunately, NBC has to cut to pre-paid commercials for Kalshi instead. When the broadcast returns, BloodyPigDick has been ushered away and the Suns have gone on to lose, 4-1.

PORTLAND TRAIL BLAZERS

New Portland owner Tom Dundon is thrilled to announce the signing of LeBron James, and even more ecstatic that it comes on a cheap vet minimum deal.

Immediately, the Blazers go from fringe playoff team to intriguing contender. The frontcourt is stacked with talent, from Deni Avdija to LeBron James to Donovan Clingan. That said, new coach Micah Nori still struggles to find the right combination for the backcourt. Vet Jrue Holiday happily accepts any assignment, but Ja Morant and Damian Lillard have high expectations as well. Overall, some chemistry issues and some shooting from Morant lead to a disappointing # 5 seed and a loss to Denver in R1.

SACRAMENTO KINGS

LeBron James takes on the biggest challenge of his NBA career: reviving the Sacramento Kings.

But alas, he’s not a miracle worker. The limited defense of the roster and the limited acumen of the coaching staff leads to a predictably disappointing season. James plays the role of the good soldier in an attempt to lead the team to a playoff berth, but even that’s out of his reach at this age. The team loses badly in the play-in tournament.

Fans remain baffled by why LeBron James ever made this decision in the first place. That is: until the end-of-the-year press conference where it’s announced that James will retire and be part of an ownership group that will buy the franchise from Vivek Ranadive and move it to Las Vegas. James regrets taking away the team from Sacramento, but he explains that the idea of a LeBron James-owned Las Vegas “Kings” team was too good to pass up.

SAN ANTONIO SPURS

Although most young teams may be worried about disrupting their chemistry with an aging star, the Spurs eagerly sign LeBron James. Victor Wembanyama gave them the illusion of size, but the team was “small” at the forward spot and could have used a competent vet at the 3 or 4. Tobias Harris helps to some degree, and LeBron James adds another level from there. The Spurs roll through the regular season, winning 66 games and earning the # 1 seed.

In the playoffs, the inevitable suddenly looks shaken! Wembanyama’s knee bends awkwardly and has to leave the game. There’s panic throughout social media. An Achilles? An ACL? Maybe relying on a 7’5” alien wasn’t sustainable after all. But as it turns out, it’s a minor injury that will allow him to return in a couple of weeks. The Spurs manage to slug their way through the first round without Wemby, and by the time they get him back they’re rolling. They win the title with ease.

TORONTO RAPTORS

Suddenly, the Toronto Raptors become a prestige franchise after landing Kawhi Leonard and LeBron James in the same offseason. Combined with Scottie Barnes, they’re considered the best forward platoon in the entire NBA. Better yet, with Barnes’ fresh legs buffering them, both James and Leonard can load manage during the regular season and enter the playoffs close to 100%. The team enters the bracket as a frisky # 2 seed.

Can the team get the job done from there? Round one and round two go well, leading to a ballyhooed matchup with the New York Knicks in R3. The Raptors go up 2-1, as fans start dreaming of a second title for their team. But unfortunately, the wear and tear of the playoff run starts to lead to some decline from Leonard and James. Shots are falling short. Immanuel Quickley and R.J. Barrett aren’t shooting well either. The Knicks lock in and roar back when it matters most, winning the series in 6.

UTAH JAZZ

In signing with Utah, LeBron James shows great faith in the readiness of # 2 pick Darryn Peterson (and Keyonte George). If the young kids can step up, this frontcourt may be able to make a deep run on the backs of James, Lauri Markkanen, and Jaren Jackson Jr.

Unfortunately, the early returns from the kids are mixed – with some high highs and some low lows. There’s also concern about whether or not Jusuf Nurkic can hold up for the long haul as well. After sputtering around in the 7-8 range of the standings, James’ camp pushes the Ainges to make a move. They want the team to trade some of their young talent (like Ace Bailey) for a proven veteran like Anthony Davis. The Ainges resist, prioritizing the long-term plan. And with that, the Jazz stumble, losing in the last play-in game.

WASHINGTON WIZARDS

It’s an entirely new era in Washington, as the Wizards have gone to laughingstock to the home of three stars now in LeBron James, Trae Young, and Anthony Davis. Their new veteran star power relegates top pick A.J. Dybantsa to a complementary piece and kills off his Rookie of the Year hype, but it’s well worth it if the team can make a deep playoff run.

Early on, it looks like that may indeed happen. Young, James, and AD all play effectively with each other. Better yet, the young prospects like Dybantsa, Alex Sarr, and Kyshawn George fill in the gaps well. The Wizards rise as high as the # 2 seed in January, leading some to talk about whether or not the team can win its first title since Wes Unseld.

Unfortunately, we didn’t turn off injuries in this simulation. Davis hyperextends his knee, which stretches the depth and leads to some porous defense during a 7-10 stretch of games. The Wiz stumble all the way down to the # 5 seed by the time the playoffs start and can’t regain their momentum from there. They lose in the first round.
