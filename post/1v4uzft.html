“Call it what it is: a hard cap. It’s pretty clear what the objective here was,” a long-time agent of NBA players said. “The owners wanted to set an upward boundary on spending. Mission accomplished.”

“It’s tough, but not as much about the aprons, but by how many things can cause you to have a hard cap at the first or second apron,” an Eastern Conference executive opined. “We’ve rarely gone deep into the tax like you have to for the second apron. But so many things can hard cap you at the first apron now, that you have be really cautious. Twice over the past few years, we put an extra player in a trade, just to make sure we didn’t trip a first-apron hard cap. That’s not a great place to be.”

“We’ve made it harder for fans - not even casual ones, but regular fans - to follow the league now,” an Eastern Conference executive said. “You know the old joke? ‘I was told there would be no math.’ Well, I’m hearing fans complain about having to do so much math to follow the league now. That can’t be a good thing.”
