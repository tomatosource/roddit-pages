Pacers’ legacy isn’t making the NBA Finals. It’s how hard they played.

Fred Katz June 24, 2025

https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6446825/2025/06/24/pacers-nba-finals-legacy-conditioning-pace/

Their season ended Sunday, when the Oklahoma City Thunder bested them in a heartbreaking Game 7 of the NBA Finals. Indiana lost its leader in the process, watching All-NBA point guard Tyrese Haliburton, who was toughing out a painful calf injury, rupture his right Achilles tendon midway through the first quarter. To that moment, Indiana had produced one of the league’s freshest rosters, a team of ultra-physical try-hards who zipped around basketball courts for 105 games as if they were putting the rest of the NBA through a beep test.

Pace — whether on offense, where Indiana dominated on the break, or on defense, where a full-court press engulfed even the league’s elite offenses — may have been this squad’s 2024-25 signifier. But its insistence on constant movement was its identity, one that formed before training camp even commenced.

Back in the autumn, and with the Pacers off to an uninspiring start to the season, Johnson drove into the parking lot at the team’s facility. Carlisle had given the players an optional day. No practice. Come in if you need it. If not, rest.

Johnson required treatment between games. But when he pulled into the lot, there was nowhere to park. All the spots were already filled, his teammates’ cars in each one.

“I’m like, ‘Oh, s—!’” Johnson said. “I had to double look at my phone to make sure it said optional.”

It did. Johnson wasn’t wrong. But his and his teammates’ actions said something different.

Every Pacers player went to the facility that day. Johnson has played for 10 organizations over 16 NBA seasons. This, an optional day indistinguishable from an actual practice, was not the norm, he thought.

“A lot of teams I’ve been on, you go there and there’s one, two, three guys that show up early, leave late. (Then you) go two months, three months and they start showing up late,” Johnson said. “This s— never wavers for these guys.”

If the Pacers did not care to slow down during games, they could not rest outside of them.

Until now.

After a 10-15 start turned into a surprise run to win the Eastern Conference, the greatest season in Pacers history is over.

This team was resilient. In each playoff series, including the one they lost, the Pacers pulled off a remarkable comeback. They drove a dagger into the Milwaukee Bucks’ chests in Game 5 of the first round, roaring back from down seven with under 40 seconds to go to clinch the series on a Haliburton layup. The Pacers completed nearly the same feat, down seven with under 50 seconds to go, two games later, this time going up 2-0 on the No. 1 seed Cleveland Cavaliers, thanks to a game-winning 3 from Haliburton.

In Game 1 of the Eastern Conference finals, they trailed the New York Knicks by 14 with under three minutes remaining, forced overtime on a ceiling-scraping Haliburton jumper and then won again. The Pacers began the finals with another miracle. Naturally, that one ended with Haliburton nailing a jumper at the buzzer.

Each playoff opponent appeared, at different moments, gassed against Indiana. There was no more Pacers-ish image than the one of Cavs star Donovan Mitchell hunched over at the free-throw line, hands on his knees and breathing with enough force to spit his lungs inside out, during Indiana’s clincher over Cleveland. With his season on the line, a worn-down Mitchell missed three consecutive free throws. Meanwhile, the Pacers still sped around the court with the same vigor they showed in the first quarter.

The flip side of teams depleting their energy against Indiana was that Indiana rarely looked tired.

“We encourage our guys to be in top shape when they arrive at camp. … We have to train to play this way,” Carlisle said.

The Pacers leaned on their depth. They deployed 10 players regularly. They had no other choice. Even the best-conditioned athletes need a break from running the break.

None of the other 29 teams played with the Pacers’ verve this season. They traveled more miles per game on offense than anyone else in the playoffs, and traveled more miles per game on defense than anyone else in the playoffs, according to Second Spectrum.

Indiana’s players combined to run 444 total miles over their 23 playoff games, shattering the previous postseason record, which belonged to the 2019 Toronto Raptors. Second Spectrum has tracked the statistic since 2013.

“We play hard for 82 games straight at a higher pace than anyone else,” starting guard Andrew Nembhard said. “I mean, that’s what it is. You only get in shape from the games. (Most teams) just don’t play as hard as us every game. It’s just will.”

Over the final four months of this regular season, they emerged as a top-10 defense. They stifled four top-10 offenses during this playoff run, which included more defensive gusto than this group had ever shown.

They spent a season scrambling the league. Had Haliburton stayed healthy for three and a half more quarters, maybe he would have uncovered one more ounce of magic. Either way, the beauty of a team that nearly pulled off one of the most improbable titles in league history wasn’t that it won; it was how.

“You can’t just choose to play this way,” McConnell said. “To play this way is a lifestyle.”
