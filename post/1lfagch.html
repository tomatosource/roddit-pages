Based on weighted minutes in the Finals, this Thunder team is even younger than the 2012 Thunder with KD/Russ/Harden

The last time the Oklahoma City Thunder made the Finals (in 2012), the team appeared to be a future dynasty – or at least, a team that would return for multiple Finals appearances down the road. After all, their core was incredibly young – with Kevin Durant (age 23), Russell Westbrook (23), James Harden (22), and Serge Ibaka (22). Unfortunately, they may have been too young, as they lost to the veteran Miami Heat and never returned to the Finals…

Until now, with this new variation of a young OKC powerhouse. Similarly, the team is anchored by young studs like Shai Gilgeous-Alexander (age 26), Jalen Williams (23), and Chet Holmgren (22).

In fact, if you average each team’s roster based on minutes played in the Finals, this team is even younger (at 24.91) than the original OKC Thunder Finals team (at 25.65). (When we factor in and weigh minutes, that means that a player who logs 30 minutes counts 3X as much as a player who plays 10 minutes.)

The primary reason that original OKC team skewed older, on average, is mostly because of PG Derek Fisher, who played 128 minutes in the Finals at age 37. The new OKC Thunder don’t have anyone close to that age, as “salty ol’ vet” Alex Caruso is only age 30.

(Now, you may have noticed that Alex Caruso is actually 31 right now, but I counted him as ‘age 30’ there. That’s not an intentional fib. For “age,” I’m being lazy and using the ages listed on basketball-reference.com. That site lists a players’ age as their age during the midpoint of the NBA season. For this stat to be a precise number, I should have factored in their exact age during the Finals and even included months as fractions. Still, even when rounding like we’re doing here, it’s still safe to presume that the 2012 OKC team would still be older than the 2025 team.)
