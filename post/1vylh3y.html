Q&A: NBA executives break down the second apron, competitive balance, dynasties and how Lebron affects chedule making

SRC: https://www.nba.com/news/qa-nba-executives-break-down-the-second-apron-competitive-balance-and-more

what’s that message you want to share about the second apron?

DR: Certainly, the primary message is the one that we’ve been talking about, which is that it’s there to create a more level field, create more balanced competitive opportunity, create the fairest and strongest league-wide competition that’s going to be the best for all teams, players, and fans collectively. That’s the single most important thing at the highest level to understand about it. There’s of course a lot of detail. Embedded within some of that detail is what I would say is one of the biggest misconceptions about it that we continue to hear, which is that it is there to save owners money or will cause players to get less money. We have a revenue-sharing system every year. Players get a percentage of revenue, 51%. We call it BRI (basketball-related income). And that means that when we have a system that creates more level field by limiting the amount that some teams spend at the high end, then that necessarily means that we’re going to have other teams that are increasing their spend, who are collectively those other teams that are not the highest spenders.

Ultimately, we and the union together spent a lot of time – hours and hours in the last CBA negotiations – talking about how to create a win-win system. It wasn’t about a system that would be good for teams versus a system that would be good for players. It was about a system that collectively would be better for both and distribute opportunity more fairly and more broadly. And those are really the messages that I’d want to get out there.
