Questionable Assumptions Underlying the Support for the Brown Trade

As I’ve listened to the arguments in support of the Jaylen Brown trade, I haven’t been able to adopt them and feel good about the trade because they seem to rest on some assumptions that, at a minimum, are not supported by much evidence.

**Assumption One: If we didn’t give Jaylen the supermax extension this offseason, we had to trade him**

This assumption relies on the idea that JB would feel so slighted at not getting the supermax extension that it would make his continued presence on the team untenable. But where is the evidence for that? There are no reports or public statements suggesting he would sit out if he weren’t traded, and he’s never sat out before. There is no evidence JB would ever phone it in on the court - he always plays hard and with a chip on his shoulder. And there’s no evidence he’s ever been a locker room cancer or would be right now.

**Assumption Two: The trade return would only get worse if we waited**

The fact is that we don’t know what would have happened in the trade market if we waited. Trade markets change as teams’ need change. While people say JB’s value was at its highest now, there are reasons to think otherwise:

  1. We made the trade after most teams made their big moves this offseason. By the time we made their trade, the appetite for big trades was low.
  2. The perception was that the Celtics were looking to offload Brown no matter what. The Celtics were trading from a position of desperation and little to no leverage. They were ripe to be taken advantage of.
  3. There were no more top flight players available when we made the trade. As time passes, teams that thought they might compete could learn that they are not competitive and are more willing to blow it up. That sometimes includes a player like Giannis becoming available. Conversely, if teams that weren’t sure they’d compete become competitive, they become more willing to part with assets to get a win-now player.
  4. JB just came off the lowest PER of his career. There is every reason to think it goes back up a bit now that JT has returned.

There are certainly people who think the trade was right because they think JB was straight up bad for this team and had to go because his presence, whatever his contract, was hurting the team. For those people, it wouldn’t matter what would happen if he wasn’t offered the supermax and wouldn’t matter if trade return would get better if we waited, because they want him off the team ASAP. Not a ton of people are making that argument and I don’t think it needs much response.

EDIT: I want to clarify since I see it come up a lot in the comments. I completely agree that JB would be seeking the max extension. No doubt about it. I take issue with the assumptions people make about what would happen if he didn’t get that max extension.
