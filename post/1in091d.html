I’ve watched a lot of games in VR this year and one of the cameras is at the scorer’s table where you can sometimes overhear the huddles and team interactions. One thing that has stood out to me: The best teams communicate. A lot.

I’ve really gotten hooked on watching VR games and one of the things I love is that they don’t cut to commercials. During timeouts or stoppages they switch to the VR camera at the scorers table where you can sometimes overhear the huddles, player interactions, and players talking to refs (also, on Saturday, I could hear JJ Redick scream “YOU ARE HIM!! YOU ARE FUCKING HIM!!” to Austin Reaves every time he scored a basket)

Anyway, one of the things that has stood out to me is how much the better teams communicate. It sounds simple but take a team like the Thunder. During timeouts they don’t all sit there and listen to the coaches. They all have multiple conversations with each other. SGA talks to Hertenstein, then Hartenstein talks to Williams, then they call Chet over and they all talk, then one of the assistants joins and gives them some stats and some tips. They fill almost the entire timeout with multiple conversations about the game.

Then you look at a team like the Timberwolves (I’m sorry to say) and it’s like Golbert is trying to talk but hardly anyone listens. Anthony says nothing to anyone during the entire timeout. The assistant coaches just talk to eachother and not the players.

I’m not saying the Timberwolves are a bad team but I’m just using them as an example as an “average” communicating team.

Anyway, I know it sounds simple but it really opened my eyes. I feel like I could just listen to a timeout of a team and be like “Yeah, that team has their shit figured out”
