Elvin Hayes had INCREDIBLE longevity for his era, topping all-time lists. 50,000 minutes played during his 16 year career, #3 played 14,168 minutes less. Since 2010, the gap between #1 and #20 is 14,329. Hayes was extremely controversial at the time, I looked into his career and numbers a bit.

I wanted to actually look a bit closer into how Hayes got to #3 on the scoring list after I saw a post by u/gridironk “Elvin Hayes retired 3rd all time in career points. At the end of next season he could drop to as low as 18th all time in career points”. It’s easy to make a conclusion right there, but you can dig a little deeper.

Elvin Hayes was drafted #1, debuted at 23. According to Bill Simmons’ book, he intentionally retired at exactly 50,000 minutes. Apparently the first MVP chants started because of him. He was notably poor in the clutch. And he was the ultimate “my stats” guy.

A 1974 quote: “During his 4 years with the Rockets, Hayes was variously considered a ball hog, a rotten apple, a dumbbell, and a guaranteed loser.” Sportswriters seemed to hate this guy.

Washington made the Finals before getting him in 1971, and made 3 more Finals with him as the lead scorer in the regular season, they won one. Most in the 70s.

He retired all-time #1 in minutes with 50,000 and #3 in points (27,313) and #3 rebounds (16279). Now #7, #13 and #4 respectively. While players during his era tended to burn hot and get washed earlier, he bucked the trend and accumulated a ton of stats.

During both 68-84 and 09-26, exactly 38 players average 20PPG. Here are some total stats so we can see how the gaps look.

Westbrook and DeRozan are similar scorers in some ways. Both were very consistent scorers, but inefficient relative to the era. At the end of their careers they had incredible career numbers, but some of the clutch playoff stuff is very suspect. The biggest difference is Hayes started his career with the craziest greenlight. 26 FGA/G across his first 3 years, and a scoring title.

Four seasons averaging 44+ minutes per game, Kareem had ONE. Lower the threshold to 40+ MPG, and IVERSON HAS 11 seasons. This is the craziest stat I found. Every player above 5 debuted in the 1960s. Payton (5), Kobe (5), Sprewell (4), LeBron (4) are the next closest, all in the 2000s deadball era.

The gaps in the stats by era aren’t actually different, except for the massive minute gaps. 14.2k and 23.6k minute gap between Hayes and #3 or #20. That durability is really impressive for that era, and he played a ton of playoff minutes too. Only Kareem matches him.

The biggest difference is who fills up these all-time lists - today it’ll go from the best to the 20th best player of the era, generally. It was all over the place in Hayes’ lists.

It’s hard to come up with a modern comparison. LeBron is the only 30K/10K guy, but Harden is at 29K/8K and Westbrook at 27K/10K. Both get viewed negatively as ball hogs who dropped in the playoffs like Hayes. But at the time they were loved, people understood they were high usage for a reason, and were exceptional at a time.

Hayes place in history might say more about his era and the media than anything. The 70s are a weird era in NBA history. A parity era before the 2020s. College legends who were NBA superstars and flamed out are more fondly remembered than the consistent stars of the era, like Bill Walton or Pete Maravich. His Finals MVP teammate Wes Unseld, who won MVP next year as a rookie, only averaged 10 PPG. Kareem is a mile ahead of the rest, while Elvin Hayes is the only one who measures up statistically.

Elvin Hayes shoots less today. Ball-hogging or inefficient shots might get coached out of him. He doesn’t play five 44-minute seasons. At the same time, he immediately took on the heaviest workload outside of Wilt.

28⁄17 his first three years, no MVP votes. At 33, he was Top-3 in MVP averaging 22⁄12. LeBron (2x), Malone (3x), Jordan (2x), Kareem, Hayes, Wilt, West, Baylor. Hayes is among those inner-circle HoFers to be recognized as that good at 33+.

There’s probably a lot of truth to the negative perceptions about Hayes, but he just kept producing. He justified outlier numbers again and again. His coach played him into the ground and he kept going longer than all of his peers. He got love after his statistical peak, not during it.

After reading a ton about him, thinking about today’s controversial players: Apply nuance to these guys. Some players are drastically different from one moment to the next, even if the play-style doesn’t really change.

A couple quotes can become their legacy. Here are three:

On the Rockets, 1969-72, averaging 27⁄16, coach Alex Hannum called him “the most despicable person I’ve ever met in sports.” This one is the most famous, but it might not even be true, there’s no good source. He beefed with a lot of his teammates though.

After winning a ring, he said: “No player in 10 years has done more for his team than I have and yet I’ve been criticized,” said Hayes. “No one has been more consistent — not even Kareem [Abdul-Jabbar].”

After leaving the Bullets, he said: “I’ve been the most-consistent player in the league over the last 14 years. One important key has been no major injuries. I’ve gone past guys [in career statistics] who people say were the greatest players ever to play the game.”

On the Rockets again, his coach Bill Fitch telling young Ralph Sampson, who Hayes said he was going to mentor - “You stay away from that no-good fucking prick.”

His coach in the Bullets, decades later: “Elvin, in my opinion, was probably the best power forward that we’ve ever had [in the NBA]. I know most people want to say Karl Malone. But Elvin Hayes… there really wasn’t anything that he couldn’t do.”

There’s a lot going on there. Very few people have got this invested in Elvin Hayes in some time probably. 10 PPG Wes Unseld was an MVP. Earl Monroe was on that team. Bob Dandridge won rings as a 20 PPG guy behind both Kareem and Hayes, no one talks about him. A lot of interesting guys on that team.
