In the Clippers’ front office, the September storm has disrupted business as usual. According to team executives, who spoke with The Washington Post on the condition of anonymity because they are not authorized to speak publicly, Ballmer and his front office are steadfast in their belief that they will be exonerated by the NBA’s investigation, which is being conducted by the Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz law firm.

The recent firestorm has been “exhausting” and “infuriating,” said one executive, who added that he was so certain that the Clippers had abided by the rules that he was “not afraid” of significant punishment from the NBA, which could include the stripping of draft picks, a fine of up to $7.5 million, suspensions of personnel and even the voiding of Leonard’s contract, which runs through the 2026-27 season.

The gist of the Clippers’ defense goes like this: They understand and respect the salary cap; they were not involved illegally in Leonard’s deal with Aspiration; and they were victims of fraud perpetrated by Aspiration co-founder Joe Sanberg, who last month agreed to plead guilty to two counts of wire fraud following a Justice Department investigation. Moreover, Ballmer, the former Microsoft CEO and one of the world’s richest people with an estimated net worth of more than $150 billion, invested tens of millions into Aspiration because he believed in its environmental mission, which included a carbon offset program of planting trees.

“We appreciate that there will be a clear-eyed look at these allegations, and we’re eager for the truth to come out,” Clippers team president Lawrence Frank said Monday. “The assumptions and conclusions that have been made are disappointing and upsetting. We expect the investigation will show these allegations are wrong. I’m hurt for Steve. He’s one of the best and most honorable people I’ve ever met. He does things the right way for the right reasons. He constantly reminds us to stay on the right side of the rules. I’m also hurt for our players, staff and fans. … We’re very confident in what [the investigation] will show.”

Frank’s strident defense of Ballmer, who did not speak with reporters Monday, and the Clippers had a few holes. The Clippers were fined $250,000 in 2015 for offering free agent center DeAndre Jordan an endorsement deal with a car company, which was against NBA rules. And after the Clippers signed Leonard in 2019, executives from rival teams said Dennis Robertson, Leonard’s uncle, had sought impermissible benefits during their own negotiations. Ballmer did not directly answer when asked in an ESPN interview this month whether Robertson had made similar requests of the Clippers; Frank dodged when asked the same question Monday.

“Dennis knows the rules, Kawhi knows the rules, [Leonard’s agent] Mitch Frankel knows the rules, and we know the rules,” Frank said. “Yes, we all know the rules.”

The size and scope of Leonard’s deal with Aspiration have received significant scrutiny. Leonard apparently never touted the company in public, even though the contract terms trumped his endorsement with New Balance and vastly exceeded Aspiration’s endorsements with Hollywood stars. Pressed on his agreement with Aspiration, Leonard dismissed the reports as “clickbait” and “old [news]” because “the company went bankrupt a while ago.”

The Clippers also have yet to explain the timeline of payments made by Ballmer and Wong, his close friend and college roommate. “Pablo Torre Finds Out” reported Wong made a \(1.99 million payment to Aspiration in December 2022, shortly before Leonard received a delayed \)1.75 million payment from Aspiration. Meanwhile, Ballmer reportedly made a $1.875 million donation to one of Sanberg’s charities in December 2024, well after the Clippers had ceased their business partnership with Aspiration following the 2022-23 season because of the environmental company’s inability to fulfill its contractual obligations.
