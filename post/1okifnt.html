Congressman Paul Tonko letter to Adam Silver: ‘You told the public that “nothing is more important than the integrity of the competition.” That claim rings hollow when the league sold its credibility to gambling operators, pushed betting content into broadcasts, normalized wagering for teenagers…’

The Honorable Adam Silver

Commissioner

National Basketball Association

645 Fifth Avenue, New York, NY 10022

Dear Commissioner Silver:

You said you were “deeply disturbed” by the indictments of a National Basketball Association (NBA) head coach, an active player, and those to organized crime.1 What should disturb you more is that this corruption is the foreseeable consequence of the NBA’s decision to entangle the sport with a rapidly expanding, barely regulated gambling marketplace.

You told the public that “nothing is more important than the integrity of the competition.” That claim rings hollow when the league sold its credibility to gambling operators, pushed betting content into broadcasts, normalized wagering for teenagers, glorified it in advertising, and then feigned surprise when criminal conduct erupted inside your own league.

And the damage here is not confined to the sanctity of competition. The NBA’s actions have fueled a public health crisis. We now have:

• A dramatic surge in sports betting and gambling addiction help-seeking2

• Increasing bankruptcy, family collapse, and suicide linked to gambling losses3

• Constant, high-pressure marketing aimed at vulnerable populations

• A rapidly widening pipeline of new gamblers driven by league-authorized promotion

The league partnered with an industry that profits from addiction and loss, then placed that product in the pocket of every fan in America. To pretend the only stake is “integrity of the game” ignores the human wreckage accumulating off the court.

You also asserted that the league could not act because only federal prosecutors have subpoena power. That is not a defense. It is evidence that voluntary self-policing has failed, and the status quo is fundamentally broken.

That is precisely why we need federal regulation, as you yourself called for more than a decade ago in your New York Times op-ed. My SAFE Bet Act would impose minimum federal standards including marketing limits, data and privacy protections, public health safeguards, and transparency measures that no league has been willing to adopt on its own. While reasonable people can debate the specifics of federal standards on the sports betting industry, it is abundantly clear that the exclusively state-based regulatory approach is fundamentally flawed and will continue to jeopardize the integrity of our sports and the public health of our citizens.

The choice before you is now explicit. Either engage directly with Congress to establish mandatory federal guardrails that restore integrity and protect the public or stand in opposition and accept responsibility when the next scandal breaks and more families and lives are destroyed.

Sincerely,

PAUL D. TONKO

Member of Congress

Source: https://tonko.house.gov/uploadedfiles/letter_to_national_basketball_association.pdf

Congressman Paul Tonko represents New York’s 20th Congressional District, including the communities of Albany, Amsterdam, Schenectady, Troy, and Saratoga Springs. He represents all of Albany and Schenectady Counties and parts of Rensselaer, Saratoga, and Montgomery Counties.

He is serving his ninth term, after first being sworn into Congress in 2009.

Paul serves on the Energy and Commerce Committee, the oldest standing committee in the House, created in December of 1795. He is the Ranking Member of the Energy and Commerce Subcommittee on Environment and also serves on the Energy and Commerce Subcommittee on Energy, and Subcommittee on Oversight & Investigations.

This 119th Congress, he was selected to serve on the House Committee on the Budget. He also serves as Democratic Deputy Whip for Policy that works on the coordination and planning of the House Democratic Caucus’s policy.

Along with his committee duties, Tonko co-chairs the Sustainable Energy and Environment Coalition, and the Addiction, Treatment, and Recovery Caucus.

He has previously served on the Natural Resources Committee, the Education and Labor Committee, and the Committee on Science, Space, and Technology.
