2026 has seen more PAs by left-handed hitters since records began (2002). LHH also out-produced RHH for the first time on record this May, producing 150 more runs of value over righties in 760 fewer PAs. As of now, LHH vs RHP is the only hitter-friendly platoon split.

courtesy of FanGraph’s Leo Mortgenstern

it’s not just that there are more lefty batters on major league rosters, but that those lefties are seeing more regular playing time. Teams are, in fact, loading their lineups with lefties. Of the 542 batters who have taken at least one plate appearance this season, 54.1% are listed as right-handed and 35.4% as left-handed. That would be the second-lowest percentage of righty batters and the highest percentage of lefty batters in a season dating back to 2002 (pitchers excluded).

…More than two months into the season, qualified lefty batters outnumber qualified righties. It’s close, and it will probably stay close until the end, but there’s a chance we’re about to witness the first season with more qualified left-handed hitters than right-handed hitters in 126 years.

…this isn’t simply about an increased availability of lefties. It’s about managers choosing to put their lefty options in the lineup more often. Batters listed as left-handed have accounted for 41% of all players in starting lineups this season, their highest rate in that area since, again, 1900.
