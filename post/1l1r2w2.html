Celtics assistant general manager Austin Ainge has been named president of basketball operations of the Utah Jazz.

Ainge, 43, held a variety of roles over his 17 years in the Celtics’ front office. He had stints as the head coach and general manager of the team’s G League affiliate in Maine, he worked as Boston’s director of scouting and player personnel, and he spent the last six years as assistant general manager, helping build the roster that won the NBA championship in 2024.

“You’re always trying to build the next one,” Ainge told the Globe by telephone on Monday. “You win [in 2024], and if you remember, we did a draft workout the next day. It’s kind of always there. But I am really glad that we got that done, and it was an amazing group and an amazing time. But I think about it more as the next challenge and progression in my career.”

A league source said Ainge’s departure transpired quickly in recent days, and that the hiring of a replacement is not imminent.

Ainge, who was a two-time captain at Brigham Young University as a player, will be reunited in Utah with his father, former Celtics president of basketball operations Danny Ainge, who retired from his post with the Celtics after the 2020-21 season and was named CEO and alternate governor of the Jazz later in 2021.

“I talk to my dad three times a week either way,” Austin Ainge said with a chuckle. “But look, we worked for 14 years together in Boston and had a lot of fun and success. It’ll be fun to do it again.”

In Boston, Austin Ainge was part of a small, close-knit front office that built an NBA power with an unusual level of consistency. President of basketball operations Brad Stevens has been with the team since being hired as coach in 2013, vice president of basketball operations Mike Zarren has worked for the Celtics since 2005, and assistant general manager Dave Lewin joined the organization in 2012.

“They’re family to me,” Austin Ainge said. “The amount of hours we spent, I’ll miss everything. But I’ll still talk to them all the time, just like I do with other people that have left in the past. Nobody’s dying, but I’ll definitely miss working with them every day because they’re friends and also because they’re good. They’re really good. The Celtics are in good hands.”

Still, Ainge said the opportunity to step into a lead role in a front office that remains in the early stages of a rebuild was too good to pass up. The Jazz went just 17-65 this past season and hold four picks in this month’s draft, including the Nos. 5 and 21 selections.

In addition to rejoining with his father, Ainge will work alongside general manager Justin Zanik and former Celtics assistant coach Will Hardy, who is entering his fourth season as head coach.

“Ownership is awesome and it’s just kind of the next progression of my career,” Ainge said. “Kind of done a lot in Boston and we’ve had a lot of success over the years, and the chance to run my own team is a big part of it. And the Jazz have a lot of potential, man. It’s a lot of work, but a lot of potential.”

The Celtics’ rebuild following the end of the Paul Pierce and Kevin Garnett era was remarkably swift, accelerated by the massive 2013 trade with the Nets in which Boston acquired draft picks that were used to select Jaylen Brown (2016) and Jayson Tatum (2017).

The Celtics reached the Eastern Conference finals in six of the last nine seasons, with two NBA Finals appearances and the one championship.

“I learned everything here from so many people,” Ainge said. “But yeah, we built how many different teams through the years? And you make a million mistakes and have some successes and work with a lot of great people and coaches and you learn and grow. I spent 17 years there, so it’s been fun.”
