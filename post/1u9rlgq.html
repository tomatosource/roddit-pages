New Grip of Kulemak - 1 Rage / Volatility on kill, and not hit? We have a bit of a disconnect when we talk about adding new ‘powerful’ stat lines to items or nodes versus what is actually deployed.

There’s been a few times this patch where GGG media presentation has stated a numeric issue with stat lines on items or passive tree nodes, and said they have taken direct action to fix those numeric disappointments, and then in the same fix, fallen completely short.

I’ll provide two examples:

1. Armour / ES passives on templar were supposed to solve a noob trap and created a much larger pitfall.

During the Q&A, Mark had mentioned armour / ES as one of the defensive packages that players struggle with the most. A lot of people in this subreddit conflate that to mean it is weak, when in reality, what he is trying to represent is that it is complicated and players click it thinking it is as straight forward as pure energy shield, thus leading to more deaths. How many posts do we see with soemone saying “I’m armour / ES!” and they provide their character POB or phone screenshot and they’re at like 1.8k ES and 3k armour.

Mark mentioned that they would be fixing that with stronger nodes on the tree to help guide players into appropriate choices for Armour / ES. This all sounds nice on paper, but when you look at the nodes added, you see the horror of what was actually added. The vast majority of those nodes are 6% armour applies to elemental damage and 3% faster start of energy shield. This is a massive problem and I know a player like Mark would look at this node and shake his head because them attempting to solve noobtraps created far bigger ones. Gaining armour applies to elemental damage without having a large enough base armour (itemization + tree armour %) means the node is beyond worthless. So now the player mentioned before, still has 1.8k ES, 3K armour, and slightly more armour applies to elemental damage, which is meaingless because their base numbers are insufficient.

This was explicitly supposed to be added as a way to help shore up players, both new and old, and have stronger point-for-point efficiency on the tree for defense. These armour / ES nodes completely missed the mark (pun intended).

This hurts even more because a large portion of players don’t understand defensive layers yet, and still conflate energy shield with life, and this compounds that issue for a litany of reasons that I won’t bore people with in this post.

2. In today’s video, we were told Grip of Kulemak should be stronger, but added 1 rage / volatility on monster death, not hit.

Very often media artifacts are voice recorded without identifying the b roll that we see in the end product. This has to be an example of that tonight. In the video, we start with Jon saying: “Some of the stats just didn’t hit the mark” and watch as Grip of Kulemak loses 40% increased armour with a red strikethrough, a stat line that does not exist on rings (rings only roll flat evasion), and in the next panel, Jon’s voice trails over the jubilant “Added some new ones!” with a highlight over: “Gain 1 Rage when you kill a monster affected by Abyssal Wasting”. now I’m going to give the benefit of the doubt here and assume that if you kill multiple monsters within the same instance of an attack that you gain multiple rage, AND I’m also going to assume that if you apply abyssal wasting in the same instance as the monster’s death, you also gain the rage, but to anyone that has ever played with Rage, you understand that 1 rage per monster death is insufficient for being a desirable statline. This absolutely should have read as ‘Gain 1 rage on HITTING a monster affected by Abyssal Wasting’ The same goes for Volatility on monster death.

I’m a massive fan of this project, and have been a backer for over 15 years, it’s one of the reasons I followed my career as a product manager, and I really appreciate when GGG cooks and even go as far as to say that GGG is unironically correct far more often than players, or than players give them credit for, however, it feels like there’s a small gap in the pipeline behend the team that manages changes to balance, whether that is passive tree, or items, and what is represented and vocalized from these patch iterations. If you’re going to SAY that the goal is to make something more usable by making it more desirable, replacing / adding new stat lines that are going to be equally, if not less, chosen, defeats the entire initiative behind that iteration. A lot of the time these changes are a ‘wait and see’ sort of moment, but there is no denying that mechanically, looking at the two examples I provided, that Mark would sit and nod his head with a “Good change, these are sufficient and achieve our north star for solving the issue we addressed.”
