5 More Guys Who Aren’t Rasheer Flemming For Brad Stevens to draft at #32

Well boys night 1 of the draft has come and gone and the Celtics are left mixed. While a lot of us are watching Gonzales hype videos, many others are looking at the draft boards amazed that Rasheer Flemming was still on the board as pick #28 came and went.

Luckily, the Celtics are picking 2nd tonight, and there is a good chance Flemming will be available.

So, with that being said, here are 5 guys the Celtics could draft that aren’t Rasheer Flemming.

&nbsp;

Maxime Raynaud 7’1’’ C

Raynaud has a great combination of size and skill, and the C’s could really use both. His big enough to bother and swat shots, has already proven he’s a productive shooter from the 5 position. He’s been called one of the most offensively talented bigs of this draft, and is a defensive presence from his size alone.

He is known to tunnel a bit on offense and turns the ball over more than he needs to, instead of fitting into an offense. He’s also not the fastest guy.

&nbsp;

Johni Broome 6’10’’ PF/C - confirmed Celtics worked out

Broome is 6’10’’ and was considered one of the most impactful 2 way college players last season. He has a great mind for the game and plays with great energy. The dude is just always in the right place. On offense Broome has great hands, can catch the ball, and has a variety of moves around the basket to score with. Defensively he averaged 2.1 blocks per game using his great positioning and timing. Broome can reliably predict offenses and guard 3-5.

Broome is considered a limited athlete despite his insane 2 way reputation. He also does not have a consistent jumper, and honestly it’s unlikely he develops into a reliable 3 point hooter.

&nbsp;

Noah Penda 6’8’’ G/F

Penda is a hustle beast and projects to be a 2/3/4 depending on the need on any given night. His energy and tenacity make up a lot of ground. Penda is a team offensive player, scoring off of cuts to the basket and tenacious offensive rebounds. His defense is his strong suit where scouts think he could guard 2-3 regularly while also not being completely overwhelmed on PG’s or C’s. He’s also a very switchable defender, and can have his assignment changed mid game.

Penda’s biggest knock is his shooting. He’s already not a strong creator, so offensively they’d expect him to be a spot up guy. Scouts are optimistic he can develop that shot, his free throw shooting says there is something workable there, but right now he hasn’t proven it.

&nbsp;

Micah Peavy 6’7’’ G/F

Micah Peavy is a sleeper in this draft for me. He’s an older prospect who came on strong in his 5th college year. He’s a versatile wing who can defend the perimeter, is crafty around the rim, and can shoot the 3. He also has a wide frame and can use his strength to score through contact. He’s an absolute ball hawk on D, averaging 2.3spg and causing chaos.

Some scouts are concerned that his massive jump from year 4-5 was more about his age and experience, than a real jump in ability. There is some speculation that his 4 previous college years are a more accurate account of his abilities.

&nbsp;

Tyrese Proctor 6’5’’ G

Proctor is just solid everywhere on the court. He’s become an excellent spot up shooter and that’s probably his best on court ability. He can also function as a secondary ball handler for stretches, or if a play breaks down and he needs to run a quick pick and roll to get something. Defensively he’s again, solid. He has decent length for a combo guard and can affect shooters. This is a super safe pick for a team looking for a guy ready to eat minutes.

Proctor is limited as a ball handler and I wouldn’t expect him to be able to run point for longer stretches. He would definitely be most effective playing next to a more ball dominant guy. His contributions outside of shooting and solid defense are also limited. He’s not going to rack up a bunch of rebounds or assists.
