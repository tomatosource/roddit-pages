Why does nobody seem to remember Andruw Jones’ domestic violence arrest?

As more and more ballots are made public, I’m surprised by just how many writers seem comfortable declaring their support for Andruw Jones and his Hall of Fame inclusion. I guess I shouldn’t be, since most of these writers voted for him in previous years, and it’s not like this is some sort of recent development. However, with this year’s ballot being a weird mix of one-and-dones, guys with strong sabermetric profiles but underwhelming traditional statistics, and guys who “cheated the game” in some way or another, Jones looks like he might have a shot to float to the top of this algae-infested pool and clear the 75% hurdle that he’s been trending towards over the past eight years (despite only receiving 7.3% of votes on his first ballot). I know no one can agree on the utility of the character clause (other than to punish known or suspected PED users), but having read the details of the arrest … it’s grim. There isn’t a ton of ambiguity or nuance that might allow for some alternative interpretation of Jones’ off-field character. For me, choking and threatening to kill your wife (whether he actually intended to or not frankly doesn’t fucking matter) is sort of a  “one strike and you’e out” policy when it comes to your hall of fame prospects. He plead guilty, was placed on probation, and hasn’t reoffended (he and his wife attempted to reconcile but divorced in 2015), so I can see the argument that he’s paid his “debt to society”, whatever that means in this context. However, the MLB Hall of Fame election process is literally a court of public opinion, and I’m discouraged that nearly all of the controversy surrounding Jones’ case is related to his precipitous decline in performance as opposed to his Christmas morning attempted homicide. I don’t understand how so many voters exclude A-Rod and Manny Ramírez on moral grounds, yet see no issue with including Jones on their ballots. For writers like Jay Jaffe that explain their voting rationale, the arrest usually merits only a paragraph or two near the end of the piece (if it’s addressed at all). No one in the comments section objects. No one seems to be taking it seriously at all.

There’s a decent chance Jones gets in this year, and I think his statistical case is a pretty strong one. I’m not saying he’d be the worst human being to ever be inducted, or that you can’t be happy about it if you have fond memories of watching him play. I’m honestly just looking for validation that I’m not missing something about this situation—after all, Omar Vizquel’s momentum completely evaporated in light of his off-field transgressions. Was that just because there were more allegations after his first year of eligibility, whereas with Jones, voters came in with their eyes wide open? (Am I taking crazy pills?)

Ultimately, the HOF is about honoring a real person, and I’d like that person to actually be honorable. That’s where all of this is coming from. (There’s also the larger societal issue of certain privileged people seemingly being immune from consequence, which is probably feeding into this sentiment/rant.) If you’re a statistical purist and only care about on-field production, fine, whatever—but using that criteria, A-Rod and Manny should be slam-dunk picks. I’d like for everyone to be judged by the same standards, and I feel like that just isn’t happening here. I’m probably screaming into the void at this point, but maybe (hopefully), this prompts a few people to examine their priors and/or starts a (respectful) dialogue about the serious issue of domestic violence and other societal harms’ relevance to the platform(s) bestowed upon the perpetrators. I’ll be satisfied with that.
