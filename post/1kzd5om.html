No-numbers draft prospect all-star projections.

Posted this already in a Jazz thread, as I’m a Utah Jazz fan, but figured it had wider relevance to the whole league. A few years ago I built a draft prospect all star probability projection model based entirely off of written draft prospect analysis scraped from the web - no numbers as inputs. The idea being that human analysis of player skills and deficiencies may be even more informative for predicting outcomes if aggregated and considered together. Last year it picked Jared McCain (37%) as the most likely all star out of the bunch. I couldn’t post the image, so here it is in text:

As expected, Cooper Flagg comes out as a near sure thing at 72%. The big surprise is Thomas Sorber who is not far behind at 71% probability for an all star outcome. It’s rare that prospects come out with probabilities this high, so I would really consider him as a potential value pick in the 7-12 range). I’ve seen him mocked in the late lottery/late teens.

Other interesting results are Dylan Harper landing at 8th at 20% and Tre Johnson at less than 10%. I’ve been a big fan of Tre Johnson for the Jazz at 5 up to this point. I now might be hoping Bailey ends up dropping to 5 for the Jazz.

For reference, last year nobody had greater than 40% probabilities for all star outcome with Jared McCain coming in first at 37%. We already know this, but this demonstrates how much stronger this draft class is than last year’s.

I don’t claim this model to be perfect. It was mostly a hobby project as a data scientist to learn about Natural Language Processing. But the accuracy is around 85% (not hard to do given how infrequent all star outcomes are) and the false alarm rate is &lt;10%, giving strong confidence in more positive outlooks for players like Flagg, Sorber, and Bailey in this case.
