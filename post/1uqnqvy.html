Why ‘just keep Brown for now’ may have been Boston’s riskiest option.

I keep seeing, “If PG’s contract is basically the same, why not just keep Brown and wait for a better offer?”

That assumes a better offer was guaranteed to materialize. If the front office had already decided that Brown’s supermax wasn’t a long-term fit under the new CBA, then waiting may have only reduced their leverage while delaying the roster changes they ultimately wanted to make.

The closest recent example I can think of is Jimmy Butler in Minnesota. Once it became clear that the partnership wasn’t going to last, the Wolves’ leverage only declined with time. I’m not saying Brown would have demanded a trade, but if you already know you’re eventually moving on, there’s a real case for acting while you still control the timeline.

The other thing people overlook is that not every max contract is viewed the same. Teams were willing to wait on players like Harden or AD because they were universally seen as franchise-level, MVP-caliber players whose contracts were unquestioned assets. Brown is an elite player and a Finals MVP, but a supermax under the new CBA is a much more complicated calculation.

Keeping Brown also likely would have made it harder for Boston to regain flexibility under the new CBA. Even if they could have eventually gotten below the second apron, doing so probably would have required other significant moves.

There’s also the opportunity cost. Making the move now gives Boston a full season to reshape the roster and invest in the development of Baylor Scheierman, Jordan Walsh, Hugo González, and Ron Harper Jr., instead of spending another year on a core they may have already decided wasn’t their long-term future.

You can absolutely argue the Celtics should have gotten more. But I don’t think “just wait” is the obvious answer once you’ve internally decided your 1B star isn’t part of the next phase of the team.
