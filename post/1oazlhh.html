Stats’ Incredible: Dodgers Dominance Defined

Here’s a look at just how dominant the Los Angeles Dodgers have been since the assumption of the current ownership group (Guggenheim Baseball Ownership) fronted by Baseball Basketball Hall of Famer, Magic Johnson in 2012. (cue the background music)

Since 2012, the Dodgers have …

  1. Never finished worse than 2nd in the division, and that only twice (in 2012 and 2021).
  2. Never finished with fewer than 86 wins in a full season, and that was way back in 2012.
  3. Never missed the postseason since 2012.
  4. Won a record eight NL West division titles in a row (2013-20), second only to the Braves’ run of division title dominance in the NL from 1991 to 2005. They’ve now won their division the last four times in a row, the longest active streak in MLB.
  5. A postseason record of 73-57, and a postseason series record of 18-10. The franchise’s postseason game and series record before 2012 were 67-95 and 14-20, respectively.
  6. 919 wins and a .567 winning percentage throughout the 2010s (2011-2020), the most wins and the third highest win percentage by the franchise in any decade of its existence. And the most wins of any team in that span, by miles.
  7. Won 100 or more games in a season five times during this stretch, including three in a row (2021-23), making them the 7th team in MLB history to accomplish that! For perspective, no MLB team has ever won 100+ games in a season, four times in a row.
  8. Amassed the following hardware:
    1. Nine Silver Slugger Awards;
    2. Six Golden Gloves;
    3. Seven Individual Single-Season ERA Titles;
    4. Two Reliever of the Year awards
    5. Three MVP Awards, to Clayton Kershaw, Cody Bellinger, and Shohei Ohtani, respectively;
    6. Two Rookie of the Year trophies, to Corey Seager and Cody Bellinger, respectively;
    7. Two Cy Young Plaques, to (checks file) Clayton Kershaw, and;
    8. One Manager of the Year award, to Dave Roberts,
    9. One League DH Award (Ohtani);
  9. Oh yes, and five National League pennants and two World Series trophies, perhaps three.

And then there’s the comparisons, with the one team with this amount of historic Postseason pedigree, and the only contemporary team with the same amount of recent success.
