Cognitive complexity: build complexity and action complexity are not the same thing

I think one important thing often gets mixed together in ARPG discussions: complexity in building a character and complexity in playing that character are not the same thing.

In a game like Dota 2, most of the complexity happens inside the match itself. You constantly make decisions: positioning, timing skills, reacting to enemies, coordinating with teammates, reading cooldowns, and so on. That makes sense because the game is built around short, intense sessions.

But an ARPG like Path of Exile / Path of Exile 2 is different. A huge part of the game is already in the theorycrafting. You think about passive trees, gems, support gems, sockets, items, defenses, damage scaling, spirit reservation, minions, triggers, and many other systems. Even a “simple” build can require a lot of planning, farming, testing, and optimization.

So after spending all that effort creating a working build, many players expect the build to feel smooth and comfortable to play.

That does not mean the build is brainless. It means the complexity already happened somewhere else.

This is why many people enjoy one-button or low-button builds. A one-button build does not mean the build is simple, cheap, or easy to create. Often it is the opposite. These builds can be very complex under the surface. They use automated interactions, triggers, clever item choices, support gems, and mechanics that support each other.

For many players, that is the most enjoyable kind of theorycrafting: building a system that works smoothly by itself.

For example, people often enjoy builds like Cast on Crit-style setups, trigger builds, automated buffs, or other mechanics where many systems interact together. The fun is not in pressing six different buttons every few seconds. The fun is in designing a machine where everything supports everything else.

Minion builds are a good example of where this can feel wrong.

If I spend spirit on many sniper minions, and those snipers have a skill like gas arrow, why do I have to manually command them one by one? If I have ten archers, I cannot realistically command all of them individually. If each command has a use time, then it starts to feel like I am just casting gas arrow myself ten times, but with extra steps.

That does not feel like a minion build.

A minion build should feel like I created an army with its own behavior. If my archers have gas arrows, they should be able to use them automatically. Or at least there should be support gems that allow me to command all of them at once. Even better, there could be support gems or behavior options that make minions use their special skills intelligently.

For example:

- Archers could use gas arrow on ignited enemies.
- Minions could focus enemies on low life.
- Defensive minions could retreat or reposition when low on health.
- Certain supports could make minions prioritize rare monsters, bosses, or enemies affected by specific ailments.
- Players could build minion “logic” through support gems, instead of manually playing piano with every minion skill.

That would be much more interesting than simply adding more buttons.

Modern games can support smarter minion behavior. And in an ARPG, this kind of automation would actually increase build depth. The player would still make important choices, but those choices would happen in the build design: which minions to use, which skills they have, which supports control their behavior, and how they interact with the player’s own spells.

Then the player can focus on positioning, survival, buffs, curses, movement, and key decisions, while the minions actually behave like minions.

This is the core issue: ARPGs are usually farming games. Players run maps, zones, bosses, and mechanics for many hours. If every rare monster requires five or six precise actions, constant timing, watching tiny icons, rotating skills, and manually commanding every part of the build, the game becomes mentally exhausting very quickly.

That kind of gameplay can work in games designed around short, intense fights. But in a loot-based farming ARPG, if the baseline gameplay requires that level of concentration for hours, it stops feeling deep and starts feeling stressful.

There should absolutely be room for skill expression. Better players should be rewarded for better movement, positioning, timing, flask usage, cooldown usage, and boss knowledge. But high cognitive load should be optional or situational, not required for every normal farming encounter.

Many people come to ARPGs after work or after a stressful day. They want progression, loot, power fantasy, and the feeling that their build works. They do not always want every map to feel like a competitive match.

For me, the best ARPG design separates these layers:

Build complexity: deep, flexible, rewarding, full of discovery.
Gameplay comfort: smooth, readable, sustainable for long sessions.
Automation: a valid reward for good theorycrafting, not something to be treated as bad design.
Skill expression: available for players who want to push harder content, but not mandatory every second.

A build that took a lot of thought to create should feel powerful and efficient to play. If every build becomes mechanically exhausting no matter how much planning went into it, then theorycrafting loses part of its reward.

Complexity is good. But where that complexity lives matters a lot.

It’s not about forcing 1-button builds like COC, but make many other builds worth to play including minion builds, many valuable unique items with skills, buffs, effects. Enrich the game and make many other ascendancies/builds worth to play.
