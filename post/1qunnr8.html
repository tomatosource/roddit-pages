Rod Carew is just a sliver behind Tony Gwynn as a pure contact hitter, if that.

Hear me out, I know the .338 lifetime average vs. .328 (both godly for modern players) seems too much, especially with Gwynn’s incredibly low strikeout rate. Maybe this is common knowledge to most, and I think it is, but I’d assumed Gwynn was miles ahead of Carew, and everyone else, including Wade Boggs. Growing up in the 2000s and 2010s, Gwynn was heralded as the best contact hitter to ever play - And he still is (minus Ty Cobb and maybe Rogers Hornsby if you’re a real historian, who you really just can’t compare because of the difference in era). And I don’t take anything away from those early 20th century legends.

Better than Ichiro without a doubt, that was never even a real question in the media even when Ichiro averaged 224 hits over 10 years and batted .330 something. But as I look into the stats more I’m shocked at just how good Rod Carew was, and even with being a whole era behind Tony, how much praise and remembrance he gets today.

In Carew’s prime he hit .338 (1969-83) against a league average of .257. In Gwynn’s prime (1984-97) he hit .341 against a league average of .261. Makes their neutralized averages just about the same.

Gwynn had a longer prime and slightly more hits, with 8 batting titles to Rod’s 7. But Carew grounded into less double plays, got on base more, was good at not swinging at bad pitches (still whiffed much more than Gwynn, but that’s not saying much lol. Gwynn also hit the bar a bit farther with slightly more pop and extra base hits. Even as a contact hitter, your ability to make more meaningful contact matters, so I won’t not mention that on Tony’s behalf. But make no mistake Carew was great at grinding out triples (which is more base-running than contact hitting) but Gwynn has more doubles and generally made contact to better parts of the field.

Interestingly, Carew’s .388 season he played in all but 6 games with 239 hits. Tony played in just 110 games with 165 hits in his brilliant .394 season cut short by the strike. Who knows maybe he could’ve reached .400 with 50 more games to play. But that still makes Carew the closest to Ted Williams .400 year in a full season.

The Beastie Boys weren’t joking when they said “I got mad hits like Rod Carew.”
