This is part 1 of 30 in a series of calculating the WARs of each Backyard Baseball character. In each post I will be revealing the inputs and WAR total of one character, and walking through one step of the process or explaining the fundamentals of WAR.

Backyard Baseball. For all you Zoomer whippersnappers out there who never had the privilege of playing this game (it’s currently on Steam and mobile, by the way), the best way I can describe it is imagine if you and all the friends you grew up with got cartoonized and put into a baseball video game. In addition to spawning versions of soccer, football, basketball, hockey and skateboarding (wait, that last one can’t be right…), there were also 2001 and 2003 versions of the game, featuring Major League players. The Backyard Sports wiki claims Backyard Baseball 2005 and 2007 also existed, but I have no memory of this being real, other than the vague recollection of some kids at a family friend’s summer house playing an unfamiliar version of the game, so I’m going to assume that’s all just Russian misinformation and ignore those last two.

When I was about eight, my friend got Backyard Football on PC. Playing the game (well, mostly watching him play) had such an impact on me, when I discovered they released Backyard Baseball 2001 with players from each MLB team, I knew had to get it. I have such strong memories of excitement just holding the box in my hand, dicussing the perfect lineup, and studying every inch of the artwork. The game was pretty good, too. Fast forward over 20 years, I had my own eight year old. Hoping to give him a similar experience to the one I had, I downloaded Backyard Baseball 2003 (I wasn’t able to find BB 2001) and eventually Backyard Football, Backyard Basketball, even Backyard Soccer. We played for a couple of years, but eventually we kinda forgot about them. That is, until I found something that rocked my world.

What was it? Find out in part 2!
