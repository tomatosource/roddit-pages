Thank you, Baseball

Dear Baseball,

Thank you. Thank you for always being there and welcoming me back with open arms. Unfortunate personal circumstances pulled me away from baseball for over 10 years. When I came back, you looked a little different with new technology, but who doesn’t change a little as they age? I picked up my scorebook, dusted the cobwebs from the baseball corner of my brain, and we picked up as though nothing had ever changed. 

I’m fortunate enough to live somewhere now that I can go to a baseball game almost every day of the week if I wanted to. This weekend, I am seeing the Yankees play the Nationals three days in a row! Just a year ago, I never could have imagined I would be seeing so much baseball. Earlier this week, I went to a baseball game on a Tuesday night, just because I felt like it.

Let me tell you, it’s a strange and interesting experience to grow up watching the Derek Jeter years of the New York Yankees and all of those World Series wins, and then adopt a far less successful team. It has been so fun and so heartbreaking to watch the Nationals attempt to rebuild their club this season. 

But the amazing thing about baseball? These two clubs with polar opposite histories are playing each other this weekend, and everyone is really quite unsure who will win. The Yankees have been in a slump, and the Nationals lead the Major Leagues in runs scored! The Yankees may very well take advantage of the Nats less than stellar bullpen and sweep the series. However, all of the statistics in the world can’t provide the final score, and that’s what we love about baseball.

Since returning to baseball, I decided to read Doris Kearns Goodwin’s 1997 memoir “Wait Till Next Year” and I am so glad I did. Her description of her first visit to Ebbets Field made me jealous of her, for having seen such an amazing time in the history of baseball. How amazing to have met Jackie Robinson! Even Ebbets Field itself: “The floor tiles embellished with baseball stitches, and in the center of the domed ceiling hung a chandelier composed of a dozen baseball bats.” Her experience reminded me of my first visit to Yankee Stadium as a young kid who trekked from upstate NY. I also connected with Kearns Goodwin’s experience as a young girl who learned how to score baseball, a skill that I cherish and boast about still today. 

Later, Kearns Goodwin tells the reader that after her mother’s death, she disconnected from baseball for many years. “For years I had managed to stay away. […] But I could not get away. Addiction or obsession, love or need, I was born a baseball fan and a baseball fan I was fated to remain”. 

So, thank you Baseball. I am fated to remain a fan for the rest of my life, and I am so glad I could come right back to this wonderful game.

StevenMC19 | Baltimore Orioles
