The Snowy Mets Road trip to Denver,or When We Were Left in Charge of the National Ballpark Museum

Went to Denver this week for the Mets/Rockies series with my brother Joe and honestly it turned into one of those trips I know I’ll never forget.

The weather was absolutely ridiculous. Snow, game-time changes, flight changes, delays, and constant schedule changes left everything feeling completely unsettled. One day it looked like Denver was having all four seasons at the same time.

Somehow the trip ended up being about way more than just the games.

We spent one snowy afternoon at the National Ballpark Museum near Coors Field and the proprietor Bruce actually had us “mind the store” while he ran out for a few minutes. Sat on this old leather couch the color of a baseball mitt flipping through baseball books and memorabilia for hours.

At one point Bruce had me sign an autographed celebrity bat because of my Uncle Bill Kane, who spent decades with the New York Yankees organization as traveling secretary and briefly assistant GM “for a cup of coffee,” as baseball people would say. We ended up telling Bruce stories about Uncle Billy’s legendary fights with George Steinbrenner, road-trip bar nights with Mickey Mantle and Whitey Ford, and all the insane old Yankees stories that sound made up but somehow probably aren’t.

So on the bat I wrote:
“Baseball is life.”

Then signed my name underneath it.

One of the books in the museum even mentioned the March 30, 2000 Giants/Brewers preseason game at PacBell Park that Joe and I went to with our friend Jim. Such a random thing to find in a museum in Denver.

Then later we ended up sitting in a pub for hours watching baseball, talking to the bartender and just having one of those great conversations you don’t really expect to have.

Coors Field itself was awesome. Rockies fans and staff could not have been nicer to us even though we were very obviously New York Mets fans. Also: the brisket sandwiches there were unreal.

And somehow in the middle of all this, Juan Soto hit the first leadoff home run of his MLB career.

Just one of those baseball trips where everything goes wrong and somehow it still turns into something really special.
