Can a Baseball Team Actually go 162-0? I Ran Every Combination to Find Out.

Edit: Spelling and formatting

I wanted to know what it would take to produce a 162-0 team. Is it even mathematically possible for a team to go 162-0? If not, what would it take for a team to go 162-0?

TLDR: Used the Pythagorean Formula to find the combination required to produce a perfect 162-0 season. The winning team requires 2004 Bonds hitting and 2000 Pedro pitching. The opponent is the 2024 Chicago White Sox. The winning combination produces a win probability of 99.85%. A split scenario using the 1930 Philadelphia Phillies pitching staff against Bonds while Pedro faces the White Sox offense pushes win probability even higher to 99.93%.

Assumptions

This exercise is purely mathematical and makes the following assumptions:

  1. No injuries occur at any point during the 162-game season.
  2. All nine Bonds and all five Pedros perform identically and consistently — there are no hot or cold streaks, slumps, or outlier performances.
  3. All projected stats represent a flat average distributed evenly across all 162 games.
  4. Pedro Martinez pitches all 9 innings in every start. No bullpen is used.
  5. Defense is considered negligible. Given the combination of a .140 opponent batting average and Pedro’s historically dominant strikeout rate, the impact of fielding on outcomes is treated as statistically insignificant.
  6. No park factors, weather conditions, or travel fatigue are applied.
  7. The opposing lineup and pitching staff perform at their seasonal averages for all 162 games. No adjustments for home/away splits.

In short: this is a controlled mathematical model, not a simulation. Every result is derived from seasonal averages applied uniformly. Real baseball is messier. That messiness is precisely why 99.85% still isn’t 100%.

The Method

Pythagorean Win Expectation: Win% = RS² ÷ (RS² + RA²)

This is the same formula MLB teams use to evaluate team quality. I projected runs scored and allowed for every hypothetical matchup, and then computed.

What It Takes To Reach 162-0

The mathematically achievable 162-0 season using one real opponent:

  • 2004 Barry Bonds (offense)
  • 2000 Pedro Martinez (pitching)
  • vs. 2024 Chicago White Sox
  • Win probability: 99.85% → 162-0

Even at 99.85%, one game in every ~666 (4.1 seasons) would be lost to pure chance. This is as close to a perfect season as the math allows when limiting to one opponent.

Bonus: The 1930 Phillies Wrinkle

I went looking for a way to push the win probability even higher. Could I get 99.99%?

I found the 1930 Philadelphia Phillies pitching staff - the worst in modern MLB history with a 6.71 ERA. I re-ran 2004 Bonds and 2000 Pedro against the 1930 Phillies and came back with a worse result (159-3 , 98.11% win probability) because the Phillies hit .315, one of the greatest hitting lineups in history. Pedro would surrender ~2.50 runs per game against them.

Key takeaway: A great-hitting opponent hurts you more than a terrible-pitching opponent helps you.

Isolating just their pitching staff against Bonds while Pedro faces the White Sox produces the highest result, a win probability of 99.93%. At 99.93%, one game in every 1,429 (8.8 seasons) would be lost to pure chance.

Conclusion

A 162-0 season is mathematically achievable but it’s absurd. It requires the greatest offensive season, the greatest pitching season, and the worst offensive team in modern MLB history to exist simultaneously. Thankfully, the universe only had to produce Barry Bonds, and Pedro Martinez. The White Sox donated the third.
