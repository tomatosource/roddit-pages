[Fischer] The Sacramento Kings are a team that is already “open for business” on the trade front. The Kings are willing to listen to offers on the veterans up and down their roster. Including, but not limited to: Zach LaVine, DeMar DeRozan, Domantas Sabonis, and Keon Ellis.

Source: https://open.substack.com/pub/marcstein/p/sunday-best-the-pieces-of-this-seasons?r=nuq3a&utm_medium=ios

One team that is definitively open for business: Sacramento.

Maybe this was inevitable after a 41-point loss to the Morant-less Grizzlies, but league sources say that the Kings are willing to listen to offers for players up and down their roster … particularly for Sacramento’s veterans.

Word is, furthermore, that new general manager Scott Perry realistically understands that he has to undertake a multiyear rebuild as opposed to trying to cobble together a plucky playoff-worthy squad from the ashes of Sacramento’s Beam Team. The hope is that Murray and Clifford are going to be a part of that.

From there? It’s anyone’s guess … particularly because, as the Kings surely understand, they are unlikely to bring back a significant haul by making former All-Stars Zach LaVine, DeMar DeRozan or the injured Domantas Sabonis available.

Sabonis has nearly $95 million left on his contract across the two seasons after this one and is viewed as a defensive liability around the league.

Establishing a market for LaVine also figures to be difficult. Interestingly, though, I’ve heard multiple teams say that a LaVine market could indeed take shape if he were willing to bypass his $48.9 million player player option for the 2026-27 season and replace it with a longer-term deal at a lower annual salary.

There was an offseason push from LaVine’s side, sources say, to try to interest Atlanta in a swap that would have landed Young with the Kings. Sources also say that, when Monte McNair was running Sacramento’s front office, there was some exploration — at least internally — of De’Aaron Fox-for-Young trade structures.

The King who appears to hold the most immediate trade value: Keon Ellis.

“More than half the league has called on him,” one source with knowledge of the situation told The Stein Line.

He could certainly factor into the Kings’ future alongside Murray, Clifford and the Kings’ incoming first-round draft choice … but he could also fetch a first-round pick, sources say, if Sacramento opts to move him before the deadline. He’s likewise a player teams would almost certainly ask for from the Kings as part of any trade discussions about absorbing the contracts of LaVine or DeRozan.
