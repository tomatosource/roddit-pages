The design decision no one is talking about

tl;dr- The wording of certain skills shows an overemphasis on “combo” gameplay which may end up stifling emergent gameplay. —- One of the things that I and many others love about POE1 is the sandbox nature of its design. Most main skills don’t have caveats on how you’re supposed to play them, or if they do, there are enough tools to get around them to play the skill how you want.

Whereas in POE 2, it seems like most skills are being designed with a specific gameplay pattern in mind and we don’t have the tools to subvert the “combo setup -&gt; combo payoff” gameplay pattern created by the wording of the skills.

My favorite example I’ve been playing recently in POE1 is Blood Sacrament. There was clearly an intention for how the skill should be used as a part of its wording. However, the long channeling time can be completely subverted with a few skill gems, and one unique item so that the skill functions as a spammable semi-instant skill instead of a channeling skill.

Compare this to the recent hexblast change: “Hexblast can now only detonate Curses for which at least 50% of the duration has expired. This means it can never explode Curses that have an infinite duration…The Explosion radius of Hexblast has been increased to 2 metres (previously 1.6 metres), and it now has a maximum of 3 Explosions per cast (previously 9).”

Paired with the slower activation time of curses themselves, this is clearly a design change that creates an explicit expectation for how you are meant to play the skill. Hexblast, as it now functions, is supposed to be a payoff for using a curse build simply to finish off enemies that have a small amount of health remaining. And there are currently no options in the game (as far as we know) to get around the “combo” restrictions created by this new wording.

If you are planning on responding to this with typical Redditor equivocation about all the exceptions to this statement, don’t. I understand that we don’t have the full patch notes essentially.

However, this speaks to a larger design decision by the devs. Go back and watch any Class reveal video and you will essentially find the same wording being used by Jonathan over and over: “this skill can function in this way and when paired with this other skill, it will do a large amount of damage.”

Almost every skill highlighted in every class reveal video has some statement by the devs in this regard. This essentially highlights their main skill design vision. Spam a setup skill -&gt; press a pay off skill. This is a Meta design that restricts gameplay and creative freedom by the players. Diablo was criticized for basically being a resource generator -&gt; resource spender meta design that was boring and restricted freedom. And I worry that POE2 is falling into a similar trap.

All of this would be negated, of course, with a support gem that would essentially read “skills that require a certain number of previous combo skills require 3 less“ or something along those lines. But I don’t think that the devs would include something like this because it would basically break every skill in the game since they are all designed to be paired with a different skill.

Essentially, this just boils down to the devs trying to be too prescriptive with how they imagine skills should be played. And I think I and many other players would prefer it if they simply put out hundreds of skills with very little consideration on how the skills interact with one another and let the chips fall where they may.

Yes, this leads to often broken interactions, but I feel that broken interactions will happen no matter how hands-on the devs try to be. So I would prefer them giving the players freedom over trying to micromanage how we play the game.

I think about when Jonathan was asked about whether or not one-button builds could be created in POE2 and his answer essentially was that yes it’s possible, but I think now that I’ve seen enough of their design decisions that his answer should have been no. Because while it is technically possible, the skills are designed in such a way that you are actively hurting yourself by trying to play the game in this manner.

A lot of this just comes down to personal preference at the end of the day. And if you like having a more prescriptive experience laid out for you by the developers then that’s great. I don’t want to yuck your yum. I would just prefer an experience that I get to tailor to my own interests within the game. This freedom is part of the reason that I have stuck with POE 1 for thousands of hours.
