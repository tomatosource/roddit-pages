LOS ANGELES – Sam Gilbert, a UCLA basketball booster for 15 years, allegedly arranged and paid for abortions for the players’ girlfriends and helped athletes get discounts on cars, stereos and airline tickets.

Gilbert, a 69-year-old multimillionaire contractor, vehemently denied the allegations published Sunday by the Los Angeles Times.

Lucius Allen, former star on the UCLA Bruins’ basketball team in the late 1960s, told the Times that Gilbert arranged abortions when he and other players got girls pregnant.

‘It happened all the time,’ Allen told the Times. ‘If a ball player impregnated someone there was always a hospital available. I never paid for it and it was my case.’

Gilbert, however, said he never paid or arranged for any of the abortions.

‘I think the comment on the abortions is outrageous,’ Gilbert said Sunday. ‘It’s dreadful, he said. ‘I think this kind of journalism is the pits. It’s rubbish. I don’t know what’s happening to our society. That’s not journalism. There’s another name for it.’

The UCLA basketball program was placed on two years probation by the NCAA Dec. 8 after an investigation revealed recruiting violations. Gilbert was also cited by the NCAA for co-signing a promissory note so a UCLA basketball player could buy a car.

The Times said it interviewed more than 45 people connected with UCLA basketball, many of them former Bruin players and coaches, and found that many infractions were not uncovered by the NCAA investigation.

Players who led UCLA to seven of its NCAA-record 10 championships - a dominance unmatched in college basketball history – said Gilbert helped them scalp their season tickets and then helped them get a good deal on cars, stereo equipment and airline tickets.

Under NCAA rules, scalping tickets and helping students get discounts on goods is a violation.

The newspaper said Gilbert negotiated professional contracts for players before their college eligibility had expired – also an NCAA violation. One condition of the NCAA’s sanctions against UCLA was that Gilbert cease all recruiting activities.

During the past 15 years, UCLA players became the favored sons of Gilbert and his wife, Rose, the Times said. On weekends and holidays, many players would go to Gilbert’s home where they got good food, free advice and warmth.

Gilbert’s relationship with the players was such that they often asked him to negotiate their pro contracts.

He charged Lew Alcindor, now Kareem Abdul-Jabbar and the center for the Los Angeles Lakers, $1.00 for negotiating his inititial pro contract. He later accepted an autographed picture of the star player in place of his fee.

Gilbert said his motive for helping the players – many of them black and from poor families – was to provide needed services the university could not give, the newspaper said.

‘People can say what they will,’ Allen said. ‘As far as I’m concerned, he’s still my daddy.’
