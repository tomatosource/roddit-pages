Some of the top Pre Agents (not free agents) to keep an eye on this summer

Sadly, we may never see the true open-mindedness and true madness of a pure NBA free agency again – the good ol’ chaos of Mark Cuban chasing down DeAndre Jordan and RDAmbition setting the internet on fire. These days, most free agent decisions are made well in advance of the actual offseason.

And oftentimes, they’re made exactly one year ahead of that particular free agency. Pundits (I believe Bill Simmons was the first?) have coined the term “PRE-agency.” That is: the finangling involved when a player has only 1 year left on their contract. Rather than play out the season, the team and player tend to figure out their future ahead of time. Do we sign an extension? Or do we test the trade market instead?

It’s not a coincidence that many of the big names that have been dealt this offseason (Kevin Durant, Kristaps Porzingis, C.J. McCollum, Anfernee Simons) all fit this bill as “pre agents” and expiring contracts. We should expect more movement to come in the following months.

There are some star “pre agents” that I’d expect to be locks to re-sign (like Luka Doncic and Jaren Jackson Jr.), but here are some other PRE AGENTS to keep an eye on.

(1) PG De’Aaron Fox, San Antonio Spurs

After firing Mike Brown, the Sacramento Kings and De’Aaron Fox’s representatives decided to jump the gun and engineer a trade with 1.5 years left on his contract. The Spurs won the bidding war for the speedster, with the natural presumption that they’d sign him to a fat extension after.

However, the Spurs landing the # 2 pick and Rutgers PG Dylan Harper may have complicated that plan. The team and fan base are talking themselves into the idea that the two can play well together, but that’s a pricey gamble to take when it comes to signing Fox to a $200M extension. And if Harper (and Stephon Castle) start to eat away at De’Aaron Fox’s stats, then that would eat away at his trade value too. It’d be better to make this decision as soon as possible.

(2) SF Mikal Bridges, New York Knicks

Like the Spurs, the Knicks already made their bold decision to add a star (actually, two) last season, trading a boatload of picks for Mikal Bridges. It worked – for the most part. The Knicks didn’t make the Finals, but they should have every opportunity to do that next year in a depleted East.

Mikal Bridges’ value is a dicier proposition. Playing as the 3rd/4th banana, his individual stats went down to 17.6 points, 3.2 rebounds, and 3.7 assists per game. That doesn’t jump out to you as a $40M player. I have to figure the Knicks will double down and re-sign Bridges to a big extension anyway. He’s versatile, good on both ends, and exceedingly durable (having played 82+ games the last four years). However, if they end up with cold feet, the contract issue may be a reason why. (Note: Mitchell Robinson is also in the same boat, which will test the team’s bank account next summer.)

(3) C Deandre Ayton, Portland Trail Blazers

Two years ago, Portland managed to flip an aging Jusuf Nurkic for Deandre Ayton, with Ayton’s big salary ($37M) being a key reason why. It’s debatable whether the Blazers will want to re-sign Ayton to that – or any – number as he approaches free agency. He’s been solid over the course of his career (averaging a double/double), but nothing extraordinary.

Last draft, the team selected another center in Donovan Clingan with more of a traditional defensive anchor. This draft, they selected another center in Yang Hansen. I’m sure the team would love that combination to seize the mantle at center so they don’t have to worry about their pre-agency vets (both Ayton and Robert Williams III). If they find value, they may trade one or both. However, I doubt that decision will happen overnight. There’s still some doubt about Yang Hansen’s NBA viability, and there’s some doubt about whether big Donovan Clingan and his feet can handle 25⁄30 minutes a night.

(4) SG Norman Powell, Los Angeles Clippers

Early last season, the Clippers needed to rely on James Harden and Norman Powell to do the heavy lifting for the offense while Kawhi Leonard was out. When Leonard came back, Powell’s role took the bigger hit. His averages dropped from 24.2 PPG to 14.4 PPG after the All-Star break.

Now Harden and Powell are entering “pre agency” at the same time, with Powell being the less likely of the two to stick around. At age 32, he won’t get a huge contract extension, but he can still get a healthy number as a starting caliber player. I just don’t know if that’ll be for the Clippers.

(5) PF P.J. Washington, Dallas Mavericks

With Kyrie Irving out for a while, the Mavericks are going to be so desperate for PG help that I’m expecting a try-out any day now. But as for the frontcourt? That’s a different story. They may have the most stacked depth chart in the entire NBA. They have two legit centers in Daniel Gafford and Dereck Lively II capable of combining together for 48 minutes at least. At PF, P.J. Washington will team up with SF/PFs like Cooper Flagg and Naji Marshall for at least 48 minutes there. On its own, that’d be enough: but they also happen to have a Hall of Famer in Anthony Davis who can play both PF and C.

Barring injury, there simply aren’t enough minutes to go around here – especially not enough to keep P.J. Washington happy as he enters his contract year. The Mavs could still deal Gafford (even after extending him), but Washington appears to be the odd man out. He’s nothing spectacular, but he’s about “average” in every facet and could start for plenty of teams.

(6) PG Collin Sexton, Utah Jazz

The Utah Jazz finally embraced the dark side, descending into a tank that netted them Ace Bailey in the draft. Most likely, they’ll be in that top 5 mix again next year. As a result, their solid veterans (like Lauri Markkanen and Collin Sexton) may be shipped out.

On a reasonable $19M expiring salary, Sexton would be a simpler move to make. His best role may be as a scoring combo guard off the bench. In that spot, he’d be a contender for Sixth Man of the Year. Quietly, he averaged 18.4 PPG in only 27.9 minutes per game on a good 59% true shooting.

(7) SG Jaden Ivey, Detroit Pistons

On paper, Cade Cunningham and Jaden Ivey should have been a great complementary fit. In real life, it hasn’t been as seamless. It’s probably no accident that Cunningham had his breakout season with Ivey hurt and better shooters surrounding him instead.

Ivey’s best role still remains a mystery as he heads into restricted free agency next offseason. He can put up numbers as a starter (16.1 PPG in his career), but he may actually be better off the bench as well. But will he accept that role? Or will he want more? And if so, will he want a new team? The Pistons can see how it meshes together for a couple of months this season, but the clock is ticking on his contract.
