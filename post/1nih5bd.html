The Kuminga Impasse ✍️

The offseason’s most protracted staredown between Jonathan Kuminga and the Golden State Warriors shows no signs of blinking. The Warriors have intensified their efforts, recently upping their offer from a two-year, \(45 million deal to a **three-year, \)75.2 million package**. The catch? It still includes a team option on the final year—a non-starter for Kuminga’s camp.

The negotiations have laid bare a fundamental lack of trust. In a mid-August meeting in Miami, owner Joe Lacob reportedly asked Kuminga point-blank, “Do you want to be here?” Kuminga, feeling the team-friendly contract structures are designed to make him an easily movable trade asset, reportedly shot back, “Do you even want me here?”

Kuminga’s side clearly views his future elsewhere, having spent the summer gauging interest. The Warriors reportedly shut down sign-and-trade frameworks from Sacramento (centered on Malik Monk and a future first) and Phoenix. Both destinations would have offered Kuminga the starting power forward role he craves—something that seems unlikely in Golden State behind Draymond Green and Jimmy Butler.

The ultimate leverage remains the $8 million qualifying offer, which Kuminga has until October 1st to sign. Doing so would grant him a no-trade clause and unrestricted free agency next summer, but would also represent an organizational “burning of the long-term relationship,” per sources. For now, both sides are stuck, with the Warriors’ other free agency plans—targeting veterans like Al Horford—on hold until this domino falls.

Nets Land a Flyer on Bufkin ✈️

The Brooklyn Nets are making the kind of low-risk, high-upside move that defined their first rebuild under Sean Marks. The Nets have acquired guard Kobe Bufkin from the Atlanta Hawks for cash considerations.

For the Hawks, the move is about roster and financial flexibility. They create a $5.4 million trade exception and open a roster spot, signaling a clear priority to build around Trae Young with more established talent. Bufkin, the No. 15 pick in the 2023 draft, was blocked in the backcourt and limited by injuries to just 27 appearances over two seasons.

For Brooklyn, it’s a chance to see if a change of scenery can unlock Bufkin’s potential. He was a standout in this year’s Las Vegas Summer League, averaging 19.5 points and showing promise running the pick-and-roll. While the Nets are crowded with young guards after an eventful draft, they have the cap space and developmental timeline to see what they have in the 21-year-old.

The Clippers Conundrum

As the NBA’s investigation into the LA Clippers and potential salary cap circumvention continues, the potential penalties are coming into sharper focus. The measures Commissioner Adam Silver could take if the league finds the team guilty of funneling money to Kawhi Leonard through a team sponsor are significant.

While fines for owner Steve Ballmer and the loss of up to five first-round draft picks seem like a given, the real chaos agent is the “biggest, ugliest, most confounding part” of the CBA’s Article XIII: voiding Leonard’s contract.

This penalty is a double-edged sword. On one hand, it’s a direct punishment for the violation. On the other, would it be a favor to the Clippers? Leonard is due $50 million next season, and clearing that off the books would give them max cap space in 2026. More importantly, can you imagine the mayhem if Kawhi Leonard suddenly became an unrestricted free agent mid-season? The potential for him to sign for the minimum with a contender like, say, the Lakers would send 28 other owners into a frenzy and completely distort the competitive balance of the league.

The league’s investigation is months from a conclusion, but the whispers around the league are deafening. This isn’t your standard tampering fine; it’s a situation that could fundamentally alter the landscape of the league.

EuroBasket Echoes 🌍

The buzz from a thrilling EuroBasket tournament continues to resonate. Germany’s championship run was capped by Dennis Schröder earning MVP honors, cementing his legacy in German basketball history. Not to be forgotten, Giannis Antetokounmpo delivered an emotional performance for Greece, calling their bronze medal win “probably the greatest accomplishment so far in my life” after dropping 30 points and 17 rebounds in the final game.

But the name generating the most chatter in NBA front offices is German wing Isaac Bonga. The former Laker was a revelation, winning the inaugural Best Defensive Player award and showcasing a vastly improved three-point shot. His 20-point explosion in the gold medal game put the league on notice.

There was already NBA interest this summer, but his contract situation makes an immediate return impossible. He recently extended his deal with Partizan Belgrade, and the NBA exit clause for this year has expired. The consensus is that teams will be lining up to make a serious run at the 3-and-D wing when he becomes available in the 2026 offseason.

Around the League 🏀

  • Knicks Workouts: The Knicks are bringing in a familiar face, working out former lottery pick Dennis Smith Jr. After a year in Spain, Smith Jr., now 27, is eyeing an NBA comeback, having reinvented himself as a defensive specialist in his last few stints. The Knicks have also reportedly offered Ben Simmons a minimum deal, which he rejected, while Simmons himself shut down any retirement rumors.
  • Spurs Add Depth: San Antonio is bringing back veteran center Bismack Biyombo on a one-year deal. Biyombo will provide experienced depth behind Victor Wembanyama and Luke Kornet.
  • Cuban Complains: In what might be the least surprising news of the offseason, Mark Cuban is already complaining about the officiating. On a recent podcast, he warned the league about Anthony Edwards, claiming the Wolves star “steps over the line” on every single free throw. The mind games have officially begun.
  • Jazz Still Waiting on Love: Don’t expect a Kevin Love buyout from the Utah Jazz just yet. Reports suggest that serious discussions haven’t begun, as it’s customary for the process to advance only after a post-buyout landing spot is secured for the veteran forward.
