Expected Wins Added by MVP Candidates - Inspired by Bill Simmons Estimates

Disclaimer: This is not a post trying to make the case for or against anyone for MVP. All four of the primary candidates have had historic seasons. My vote would be for Shai but I am certainly not going to fault anyone for arguing for Wemby or Jokic. This is purely an exploratory exercise.

Background: Over the weekend Bill Simmons and Tim Legler had a hypothetical conversation over how many wins the MVP candidates’ teams would win if replacing their star with a league average player - swap SGA with Podziemski, Wemby/Jokic with Okongwu. They assume everything else about the season remains unchanged (injuries, etc). The estimates were as follows:

OKC: 53-29 (Simmons) 55-27 (Legler)
SA: 47-35 (Simmons) 50-32 (Legler)
DEN: 35-47 (Simmons) 43-39 (Legler)

I think we all intuitively feel this is somewhat accurate, but I wanted to put some numbers to it. So I looked into the On/Off Impacts under the Team Efficiency and Four Factors model. This is the most direct way to see a single player’s impact on team performance. 

A quick primer on the Four Factors model: 
The basis of this model is that there are four ways a given possession can end - a made FG, a turnover, a defensive rebound, or free throws. The model logically then measures, Pts/100 Possessions, eFG%, TOV%, ORB%, and FTr for both offense and defense. Based on these characteristics a regression can be applied to estimate how many wins teams with these characteristics should be expected to win. Comparing these outputs for when a player is on the floor vs off the floor allows for rough expected wins added to be calculated. Below are the values (and percentiles) for each of the four MVP candidates. Sourced from cleaningtheglass.com. (Note: garbage time has been filtered from the data to not skew the impacts)

To estimate what the win totals would like without the star players on the floor:

Player Exp W On Exp W Added Exp Win Off
Shai Gilgeous-Alexander 72-9 19 53-29
Victor Wembanyama 73-8 28 45-37
Nikola Jokic 65-17 35 30-52
Luka Doncic 51-31 12 39-43

Limitations:
Teams would likely gameplan differently throughout the year without their star player. You would have more minutes of role/bench players against other teams starters, etc. In relation to the Simmons conversation, this obviously cannot account for the effect of adding a new player, I assume it would add a couple of wins to each team compared to just slotting in the backup. Also most of these teams would have significantly more cap space to spend on shoring up the rest of the roster.

Conclusion:
The Thunder and the Spurs are historically good teams with their stars on the floor, and would likely still be playoff teams without them. Denver suffers the biggest net effect from losing Jokic. This makes sense considering how much he touches everything on that team - however, that roster is constructed specifically to maximize him. Luka has the least impact, but probably also the most flawed roster of the bunch. In totality, Simmons estimates are probably pretty accurate, but the impact of taking Jokic away from Denver is probably still even understated. In the end, none of this is really surprising. We all see it with our eyes when we watch the games. But it does give validity to our intuitions.

EDIT: This assumes 48 mpg. Obviously, not reality. Adjust the numbers down to some degree.
