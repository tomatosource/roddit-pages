Is this the best comp for this year’s Celtic team?

When I look at a team of Brown, White, and Pritchard, my first instinct is to think of the early 2000s Celtics built around Pierce, Walker and Kenny Anderson.

Define what these guys actually are.

  • Brown: Borderline all-nba All-star
  • White: Borderline all-star
  • Pritchard: Borderline starter

The 99-00 Celtics won 35 games. You could actually argue that Pierce (star), Walker (star), Anderson (former star) was a greater trio.

You can find other comps on that roster like Eric Williams being the “solid rotation player” comparable for Hauser.

24 year old Vitaly Potapenko being the “serviceable 4th string big thrust into a starting role on a team with no decent bigs” comparable for 26 year old Neemias Queta.

There’s no real comp on that team for “borderline 20 point scoring non-star” Anfernee Simons off the bench. A couple years later in 2001-02, Boston sort of had that in rookie Joe Johnson, but they flipped him for veteran Rodney Rogers and had a fluke 49 win season.

Another team that keeps crossing my mind, partially because i live in Seattle, is the Ray Allen (star), Rashard Lewis (star), Luke Ridnour (borderline starter) Sonics that won 31 games in 2006-07.

But honestly, the best comp, imo, happens to be a Laker team from 20 years ago

Just like Boston and Jayson Tatum, The Lakers had a superstar (Shaq) carrying them to contenders year after year after year. In 2004-05, Shaq had been traded and what was leftover is strikingly similar to what this Boston roster looks like.

Kobe Bryant (the All-NBA star): Most here would reluctantly admit Kobe is a greater talent than Jaylen Brown

Lamar Odom (the borderline all-star): Much like Derrick White, Odom was a legitimate talent who threatened to make all-star teams. His year prior to this in Miami he had averaged 17 points, 10 rebounds, 4 assists, 1 steal and he continued that play in LA.

Caron Butler (the scorer): Like Simons, this was a pure scorer who actually did end up making a couple all-star teams averaging 20+. In LA that season, he was a 15.5 point, 5.8 rebound, 1.9 assist, 1.4 steal player.

Chucky Atkins (the borderline starter): Is it doing 6th man of the year Payton Pritchard a disservice to compare him to fellow borderline starter Chucky Atkins? LIke Pritchard, Atkins was a solid small guard who bounced between being a key reserve and starter. On LA that season, he started all 82 games and averaged 13.6 points while shooting 39% from three.

Chris Mihm (Deep bench center starting by default): Mihm had a 9 year career, most of which was getting rotation minutes, but much like 26 year old Neemias Queta stepping in for a team that lost star bigs, the 7 foot 24 year old Mihm was thrust into a starting role that season where he put up 10 points, 6.7 rebounds and 1.4 blocks per game.

Sasha Vujacic (lone intriguing prospect): The Lakers found themselves in a similar position where years of contending left them with a roster devoid of any blue chip prospects. The closest thing that roster had to a guy worth developing was a 20 year old from Slovenia named Sasha Vujacic they selected with the 27th pick. He showed some flashes that season and eventually capped out at his rotation player ceiling. Pretty similar situation to the 19 year old Hugo Gonzalez we selected 28th from Spain. Here’s hoping Hugo can end up being a better long term player than Sasha ended up, but the circumstances remain where he’s literally the only guy on the roster who seems to have any semblence of “potential” worth developing.

That Laker team won 34 games. Right in line with expectations for this Boston team. Is there a better comp? For their troubles, that Laker team ended up missing the playoffs and picking 10th in the lotto where they selected Andrew Bynum. Bynum went on to become an all-star big who contributed to two title teams. My concern, however, remains that a 34 win team in 2025-26 could easily sneak in to playoffs via the play-in tourney, get swept in round 1, and get stuck picking 15th at the lowest.

The difference in trade value between the 15th pick and one in the top 5 is astronomical. If Tatum comes back close to 100% next season, this is our only real shot to land something significant via the draft. If we are projected to cap out as a 35 win team, I think Brad needs to strongly consider making additional moves to ensure we’ll be a sub 30 win team while at the same time picking up more prospects so this season isn’t exclusively about developing Hugo.
