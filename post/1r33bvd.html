I was curious, bored, and wanted to see how many players have managed to have an OPS of over 1000 while having a slugging below .550. Mostly a curiosity, you usually have to be a great power hitter to get to an OPS of 1000, while being a good walker. I was curious to see what hitters achieved this mark while being less impressive power hitters.

I expected the results to skew heavily toward the dead-ball era, and they did. What I didn’t expect was just how rare this becomes in modern baseball. Only two seasons on this list have occurred in the modern era (1900–present), and only one has happened in the live-ball era (1920–present).

And that one season belongs to Joey Votto.

Votto’s 2015 season is basically a statistical unicorn: the only player in the last 115 years to thread this needle, largely because of his absolutely unhinged 20.6% walk rate, which led all of MLB. His second-half patience that year has become the stuff of legend.

But it raises a fun question:

If he was that locked in, why wasn’t the slugging higher?

1. He wasn’t an absolute home run slugger. He hit good but didn’t have gaudy power.

Votto hit 29 homers. That’s a great season! But it’s not the typical profile of a 1.000 OPS hitter, where you’re expecting 35–45 bombs and a pile of extra-base hits. And the 33 doubles isn’t exactly eyepopping either.

2. Much of his value that year came from getting on base and quality contact rather than simply pounding the ball over fences.

He had more than 140 walks that year, an impressive amount. But this also matters because it keeps his total number of bases does, which in turn boosts his slugging percentage despite the total bases not even hitting 300. He had 695 PA but only 545 AB! This is just sort of a quirk inherent with OPS and how the two different numbers are calculated.

All in all, this shows why Joey Votto’s 2015 season wasn’t just impressive, but structurally weird in a very cool way!

Now, in the grand scheme of things, is this actually all that crazy? Probably not, there are probably a lot of hitters in the .950-.995 OPS range that have similar stories.

But Votto is the only guy in the last century-plus to hit that exact sweet spot: 1.000 OPS with sub-.550 slugging. And I think that’s really neat!

Not exactly world shattering analysis here, I know. But I just wanted to share because I thought it was really cool!
