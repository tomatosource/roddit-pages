How much of GM skill actually measurable? A research question turned website

This started as a question I’ve always wondered: when a GM gets credit for a great run, how much of it is skill vs. inheriting a stacked farm system, a top-5 payroll, or plain luck?

To answer that you have to strip out the stuff GMs don’t control. So I controlled for payroll, split every roster into how it was built (draft / international / trade / free agency), and then looked for things that actually repeat year over year, because a skill that doesn’t persist is just noise with a good story.

One of the few that holds up is a stat I’ve been calling ‘Acquisition Leverage’: this simply measures how much your team’s playoff odds increase for each WAR you acquire. It’s not about just acquiring wins cheaply, but acquiring them at the right time, buying when you’re a contender and the marginal win matters, not when you’re 12 games back. A perennial seller can look “efficient” on a cost-per-win basis while creating zero actual leverage in terms of their playoff odds. Separating those two is where GMs can distinguish themselves.

But to truly credit (or blame) a GM for a trade, I also needed to grade each trade by what it was reasonably expected to return at the time, not with hindsight. So I built a Bayesian valuation for every player in every deal: pedigree (draft slot / prospect rank) + recent performance + the years of team control acquired, producing an expected WAR with an honest uncertainty band.

Then I ran it on all ~4,500 trades since 1985 and checked it against what actually happened, here’s what I found:

Trades are basically an efficient market. The model is well-calibrated, its expected values land within about 2 WAR of reality roughly 75% of the time, and yet which side actually comes out ahead is close to a coin flip. Most trade outcomes are genuinely unforeseeable at the moment of the deal. The huge uncertainty band on a prospect is the story, not a flaw in the model.

That trade model kinda took over the project so now I have this:

  • Every trade, 1985-present, filterable/sortable, each with its own page showing how each player’s expected value was built (pedigree to recent form to control horizon) vs. what he actually produced
  • A trade calculator: build any hypothetical deal and see the model’s read on each side
  • The GM and draft analysis that kicked the whole thing off: every front office and scouting director since 1985 ranked on the stuff above (Acquisition Leverage, payroll-adjusted wins, draft value over slot)

It’s free, nothing is paywalled and there’s no signup required, genuinely just a research project that got out of hand cause I’m a psycho: rosternomics.com

Big caveats: it’s mostly descriptive, not gospel. It’s WAR-based, so it inherits any of WAR’s blind spots. Which side “wins” a trade is nearly unforeseeable at the time, and the tool says so on every page rather than pretending otherwise. I’m one person, I’m sure there are mistakes I’ve made that I haven’t noticed so feel free to poke holes.

TLDR: Tried to measure whether GM skill is real (‘Acquisition Leverage’ looks like one of the few repeatable ones). Had to grade every trade ever to do it, found that trades are a near-efficient market where even a well-calibrated model can barely beat a coin flip at calling the winner, and the trade-grader ballooned into a public database + calculator: rosternomics.com
