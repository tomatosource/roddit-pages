The last time the media and the fans said the celtics got “fleeced” we went onto have the most dominant playoffs runs and haven’t stopped losing

Go back to 2013. When Boston traded Paul Pierce and Kevin Garnett to the Nets, the immediate reaction was that the Celtics had officially entered a long rebuild. Some people thought Danny Ainge was simply cashing out on aging stars, while others believed Brooklyn had built an instant championship contender by adding two future Hall of Famers to a roster with Deron Williams, Joe Johnson, and Brook Lopez.

What happened instead completely changed the direction of both franchises.

The Nets never reached the NBA Finals. Injuries, age, and roster limitations prevented that team from becoming a true contender, and Brooklyn eventually spent years trying to recover because they had surrendered control of so many first-round picks.

Boston, on the other hand, used those draft assets to build the foundation of a championship team. Those picks eventually became players like Jaylen Brown and Jayson Tatum (through later trades involving the Nets assets), while the extra flexibility also helped the Celtics acquire key pieces over the following decade. That trade didn’t just help Boston rebuild—it helped create one of the NBA’s most successful organizations over the next 10+ years.

It’s a reminder that blockbuster trades shouldn’t be judged in the first week or even the first season. Sometimes the value of a trade isn’t fully understood until years later, once the draft picks are made, the roster evolves, and the long-term impact becomes clear.

That’s why I think it’s always interesting when everyone rushes to declare a winner and a loser immediately after a major trade. We’ve seen this movie before, and history has shown that early narratives can age very poorly.
