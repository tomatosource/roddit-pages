Ted Williams’ .482 all-time OBP record has only been matched (or exceeded) by 2 players in a single season since 1963

Barry Bonds (4x; 2001-2004) and Frank Thomas (1994).

Thomas did it in the ‘94 strike shortened year, still in 500 plate appearances. Bonds himself never had above a .480 OBP other than those 4 years, but in that time from 01-04 he posted a .559 OBP… That’s 50+ points higher than anyone was getting in a single season for half a century (since Williams/Mantle).

Here’s where it gets even crazier. The next most recent players (after Thomas in 1994) to achieve higher than a .482 OBP in a single year are as follows:

Mickey Mantle: 1962 (.487)

Norm Cash : 1961 (.486)

Ted Williams: 1957: (.526)

Mickey Mantle: 1957 (.512)

(Hilarious that Mantle didn’t lead the AL with a .515 in 1957)

So there’s only 2 players that didn’t play with him to match his career OBP mark in a single season once he set the record. And 3 decades of no one reaching it from 1963-1993.

He set the record and no one has come close since. Barry Bond’s 2001-04 run is insane, especially the .609 OBP in 03 (one of the best video game numbers ever), but his career .444 OBP was still a whole 3.8% lower than Williams. Which makes his 01-04 peak where he averaged a .559 OBP that much more insane.

No other player besides Williams, Mantle, or Cash matched the .482 mark for 45 years from 1949 to 1993. It shows how much the game has changed but at the same time how great Teddy Ballgame was. It also emphasizes Bonds statistical dominance and gets into the “how much did steroids help him” because he was playing so much better than anyone else.

Juan Soto had a .490 OBP in the 2020 COVID year. I’d be curious to know how many other players had .480+ with OBP in a somewhat significant amount of at bats (like 300+), but didn’t meat the 3.1 per team game mark, if any.
