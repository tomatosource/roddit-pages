The design of ground degen/why its current design is a plain mistake

GGG loves their ground degen, but imo it has gotten out of hand. I’m going to try to give it a fair analysis and suggest ways that they can incorporate these mechanics in ways which present the player with greater agency over whether they live or die.

TL;DR: degens bad unless you give clear telegraphing to give the player the ability to counter.

The problem

Its current implementation presents players with a catch 22 until they get to a point in their build and gear where they can simply zoom:

  • Defensive builds (especially melee) get stuck standing in the bad because they can’t clear fast enough
  • Balanced but not zoomy builds feel squishy to normal hits

Thus there is really only room for the best builds to progress. You can only be safe if you delete the screen continually.

The effect

First off, there is only one defensive play here, which is pure energy shield. These effects bypass armor and evasion. If life is intended not to be a real defensive layer, then these other layers need to have a comparable effect on mechanics.

Secondly, it feels bad to lose to a puddle. The impact here is not that you feel like you could have outplayed the enemy, it’s that the game decided that you should die for trying to play it. The player feels like they have very little agency over whether they survive an encounter, which over time leads to feelings of frustration and unfairness as the lack of agency becomes more apparent.

This has a homogenizing effect on builds. Everyone will funnel into where the offensive power is, or into energy shield, because otherwise you can’t play around these degens. They often just happen, particularly once density exceeds a threshold.

The proposed solution

I do think ground degens have a place in ARPGs, but only when they are telegraphed or the player has agency over their location. Either of these can lead to counter play, enabling builds which don’t merely zoom to beat pinnacle content. Mostly I feel this is handled well with such bosses. Even Vessel of Kulemak, which pushes this pretty far, feels more fair than a lot of random encounters.

In random encounters, however, the above problems come to light. This is bad since this is the vast majority of the game. In such scenarios, I’ll suggest the following design principles:

  • Add them for flavor: I will admit that many add to the visual experience. They should remain mostly as such, with a token dot effect.
  • Add them for minor annoyance: if you do want it to have some kind of threat, it should be supplemental to the monsters, not the core threat, such as a modest dot to take off, say, a third of on-level life potion recovery.
  • Add them when you can control the density: if you can control the density and telegraph the location of the degen, then the player can counter.

The last is tricky but could be cool. Imagine for a moment that a monster casts a spell which grabs a bunch of normal enemies and creates a massive blood pool with a degen. This differs from the current experience in several key ways:

  • It clears the encounter space to give a visual indicator that something is about to happen
  • The degen is seen as an enemy action rather than as a thing which just happens
  • It prepares players for the kind of counter play they will need to use in pinnacle bosses

Degens which do not adhere to these principles should be forced to do so or removed. They force narrow play styles which are inaccessible to a huge chunk of the player base.
