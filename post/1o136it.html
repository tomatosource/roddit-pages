Improving the end-game

I’ve been playing POE since the beta, and I still remember the key moments and updates that reignited my excitement for the game.
The first end-game map system introduction, The Master’s daily missions, Atlas of Worlds expansion, The introduction to Scarabs, The Atlas Passive Skill Tree and most recently the significant rework expanding on Scarab utility and the new system surrounding them.
Each of these updates shared one thing in common: They encouraged exploration, reinforced a sense of progression and kept me coming back to play more.

GGG has spent years refining and improving the amazing systems in POE1. Players love these mechanics, so why move in a completely new direction instead of building on what already works so well?
I’m all for innovation, but it’s important to preserve the core pillars of POE1’s endgame as the foundation for POE2.

What if the limitless Atlas became a separate, optional mode unlocked after players hit 100% progression? It could stand apart from the endgame loop and give players the choice to engage with it or not.
The idea is to have two distinct endgame systems where the 2nd system will be unlocked after hitting 100% progression together with the Atlas passive skill tree, allowing players to decide which one they prefer to play, which will make both of this systems optional.

To better illustrate my point, let me share my idea and explain how it could work:

1. Limitless Atlas system
It could work similarly to Kirac’s mission system in POE1, but to addition focused on exploration that unlocks features for the second endgame system. Within this system, players could encounter different types of nodes, each with distinct mechanics, similar to how unique nodes currently function.

Possible node types:

  • Imprisoned Nodes: Contain trapped monsters (similar to the current unique Essence node).
  • Boss Nodes: Small areas with no regular monsters, only boss encounters.
  • Pathing Nodes: Small-Medium sized areas filled with monsters but without bosses or special mechanics. It’s sole purpose is pathing from one node to another.
  • Citadels: Exclusive to the Limitless Atlas system. These would be small, monster-free areas featuring only the citadel itself. To access a citadel, players would need to defeat specific surrounding nodes to uncover the entry.
  • Biome-Based Unique Nodes: Themed around the biome they’re found in, each with its own specific objective.
  • Broken-Down Nodes: A timed zone where players must complete an objective before the node is destroyed. Failure to do so results in the player’s death.

You get the idea…

All of these nodes, except the pathing nodes, would be distinct from one another, offering a fresh and unique experience each time with goal, objective and reward. Why not make the citadels contain only the boss? Right now, they don’t feel special. They’re treated like any other map, with monsters, breaches, rituals, and so on.

Towers can serve as a way for players to clear the Fog and navigate through the Atlas with some additional functions.
There should be no juicing of the way stones and the Atlas passive skill tree wouldn’t apply here.

This structure would encourage players to explore the Atlas and complete missions or objectives for rewards exclusive to the Limitless Atlas system, such as fragments, new gem types, or other special drops unavailable elsewhere.
Each of the encounters can be made to be unique.

  1. The end-game loop system
    Once the Limitless Atlas reaches 100% completion on the main objectives, we unlock our own map device, allowing us to travel anywhere we want, just like in POE1.
    Here we can just blast away. Run our favorite map layouts, juice our maps, test different scarab strategies for weeks, griding.

How specifically are we able to run the same map over and over again you might ask?
We will connect it to 1 specific waystone node on the Limitless atlas and access it through our map device that we got from Doryani or whatever after we’ve completed a mission about collecting some fragments to build the device or something…

All jokes aside, the POE1 end-game loop is amazing, the latest significant Scarab update literally was game changer for the end-game and made it 1000% better. I’m not asking for scarabs, but give us something that function as Scarab do in POE1.

Well, that was all. Not sure if it’s good idea or not, interested to hear other opinions.
