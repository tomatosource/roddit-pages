The most confusing elements for a new player

I have put a few hundred hours into this league, which strangely remains a new player in this complex game. There have been lots of pieces of feedback on the gameplay, which I obviously think is great based on me spending hundreds of hours playing it. However, the barrier to entry in this game is often in the complexity. More specifically, I believe many aspects are not presented in an intuitive way. Here is my unofficial collection of things that confused me most this season.

  • Distractions Endgame quests during the campaign: Ritual, Temple, Expedition, etc… These things teach you in the campaign that they are a waste of time because you don’t get anything good from doing them. In early maps, that message is reinforced emphatically. They give junk and take more time. In fact, my XP shot through the roof when I realized I should ignore all of these and just rush bosses on maps. They can become very useful, but I only know that from YouTube.
  • Dying hurts XP, but that doesn’t really matter during the campaign. Then it suddenly matters very much without any communication. I stopped leveling completely until I realized that I couldn’t play recklessly with Flickerstrike. I think this approach is good and doesn’t need to be changed, but it’s not clear to new players that there is an XP penalty for death.
  • Uniques aren’t good. I find tons, and they are usually “worth” a single exalt on the market but nobody is looking for them. Too many dropping, but none of them are exciting.
  • To add to the previous issue, I don’t find any usable items. I probably found the last item I actually used in act 3 of the campaign.
  • Support gem interface. I like that they added compatibility, but it’s still so overwhelming to look at the endless circles and try to know what will work well together. I’m not suggesting anything specific because they’ll need a UX team to make this interface better.
  • The end game quests don’t show players where to go. The lack of standard UI for quest logs directing you where you need to go— especially in cases where you need to talk to an NPC— feels simply unfinished.
  • If I take off my helmet in my current build, I can’t put it back on. Not enough INT stat. I have to put on a different amulet and then put it back on, then switch to my real amulet. I had struggled with this in POE 1 years ago, and as a player it’s a frustrating and confusing experience to get a major upgrade and then be unable to use it because you didn’t even realize your gloves had +20 strength.
  • Crafting in game seems a little complicated, until you go to external sources and learn that there are crafting items I’ve never seen. The tutorials are 35-55 minutes long, operate mostly on terms that require definition for a new player audience, and then comments saying not to attempt unless you can use 900 chaos orbs on one set of gloves. Setting user expectations more clearly would be a massive step in letting people know what is reasonable to attempt and what wastes “currency.”
  • The name “currency” is confusing for new players. It feels like calling screws currency. You use these to build, but you can just buy a better house than you can build, so the screws are money, but also well known for their non-currency use.
  • It’s often not clear what I should use currency for. Should I be hoarding it? Which ones? I have sold two craniums because they were 10 divines each. Smart? Dumb? I have no way of knowing.
  • There’s an assumption that the game will scale to harder maps in the endgame simply by doing more maps because that’s how the campaign worked, and also because that’s how every game works. The concept that you’ll need to ramp things up yourself is unique enough that it simply must be shown to players.
  • Reliquary keys. This place shows the UI for adding a waystone, but then the map just fails to open and puts tiny “you need a key” in local chat. I got some keys, tried again, and it worked the same. That UI needs to say “Key required” and only allow for keys to be put in, not waystones. It only works one way but seems like it was never tested before going live.
  • The genesis tree. I had a thousand little blackberry looking wombgifts, but there was a button so tiny I couldn’t even see it to consume them. Poor design, and I thought I was progressing the quest before it had even started at all.
  • Quests that make you go back to your hideout, then travel. Just open a portal for the player. The Expedition quest had me completely lost.
  • Expedition has a thing that says “unlock on your atlas” and players have an atlas passive tree, but they mean on the map. Wild re-using of terms.
  • Trials are bad. I’ve worked in software for years, and when users hate a feature so universally, the time to tweak is long gone. They need a major pivot to something that players don’t hate. The amount of people still paying for ascendancies shows that most people don’t want to do this and will pay to avoid it.
