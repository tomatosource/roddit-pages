Opinion: Challenging Simulacrum as an Invoker Monk was the most engaging endgame content for me

Let me preface by saying this won’t apply to every build and certainly not to everyone’s preference. For me, while solving the “build puzzle” in PoE has been extremely fun, I felt that once you solve it to an extent, the “action” part of the game starts to feel idle and more like a walking simulator. Juiced T18 mobs get deleted instantly and T4 Arbiter melts.

Enter a measly T0 Simulacrum and I get my ass handed to me, forcing me to play the game again rather than watch enemies die:

  1. Illusion mobs don’t leave corpses or cause shatter explosions, rendering Herald of Ice useless. My ace card for deleting entire screens of mobs is gone and I must reconsider my options. I ended up stacking damage and AoE on Herald of Thunder and making it electrocute enemies for more crowd control and power charges.
  2. Being stunned for even a split second can mean death in the Simulacrum. This means target prioritization actually matters now. I used Storm Wave to pick off ranged enemies and Tempest Flurry + Bell when things got too up close and personal. Movement also matters because of the way enemies target your last known position.
  3. Reassigning skill points is cheap. I added a lot more stun threshold and swapped my stun charm for a freeze one. Getting stunned and letting the stun charm activate was useful while mapping, but in the Simulacrum it feels much better to not be stunned at all.

All in all, the Simulacrum experience felt like a meaningful and distinct challenge. While the “farm currency and push my dps by another 2%” game loop certainly has its place, solving these mechanic counters have felt way more rewarding for me and I wish to see more of these in the coming patches.

I do feel like, RELATIVE to the difficulty of the other endgame mechanics, Simulacrum is a bit overtuned. But I enjoyed the difficulty, and I don’t think just making it easier is the solution.

Bonus: one thing I’d like to see added is loadouts. If we allow players to save a configuration of gems and support gems (or maybe even items), it will make it more feasible to create content that forces players to tweak their builds.
