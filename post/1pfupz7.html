Mike Mussina and Justin Verlander have very similar career numbers, yet I think almost everyone would easily pick Verlander as the better pitcher. How much did Roger Clemens, Greg Maddux, Randy Johnson, and Pedro Martinez affect people’s perception of Mussina?

https://preview.redd.it/wxo5jf5v4m5g1.jpg?width=599&format=pjpg&auto=webp&s=4b95190b1333a2d5f95a0f0f17fc757f260c3fa5

For the record, I am not trying to argue Mussina was as good or better than Verlander. I think Verlander is the better pitcher, but the gap between them is much smaller than most people think. Verlander should be a lock to make the hall of fame on the first ballot (and rightfully so), while Mussina just barely cleared 75% of the vote on his 6th ballot. At the end of his career, a lot of people thought of Mussina as a very good pitcher but didn’t really consider him great. I recall the words “Hall of Very Good” being thrown around.

I am wondering if people would have thought of him as a better pitcher had he played in a different era. I think pitching at the same time as Clemens, Maddux, Johnson, and Pedro Martinez skewed people’s expectations for what a great pitcher should look like. Clemens, Maddux, and Johnson were 3 of the top 10 pitchers ever going by career numbers (ignoring the fact that Clemens’ numbers are tainted), and Pedro arguably had the best peak ever (though I think Johnson was close to him in peak value) and was definitely at least a top 20 pitcher of all time based on his career numbers. Pedro could be argued to be even higher just based on how good he was on a per start/inning basis (all the pitchers ahead of him by career WAR made more starts and are way ahead in terms of innings pitched). Do you think that Mussina would have more recognition for being a great pitcher if he pitched in a different era?
