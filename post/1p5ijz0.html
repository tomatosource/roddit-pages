At what point do you start looking at Danny Ainge’s tenure in Utah as a failure?

This is meant as more of a philosophical question - how much leniency do you get if you’re perpetually rebuilding?

Ainge took over the Jazz in 2021 with Mitchell and Gobert. They were a consistent 50 win team that didn’t have the juice to get over the hump in the playoffs.

Ainge determined (probably correctly) that it wasn’t the foundation for a core that could compete for a championship, and tore it down, trading Mitchell and Gobert mostly for future assets.

At this point, most of those have conveyed. The Jazz are in their fourth straight year of rebuilding, and will likely tank again when it comes down to it to avoid giving their pick away to OKC.

Taylor Hendricks was likely a bust with the ninth pick in 2023, Cody Williams in 2024 has an uphill climb to ever be a useful NBA player, and I think the Jazz know that.

Ace Bailey, drafted against his will, definitely has significant potential and will need more time. Keyonte George still isn’t the most efficient player but has potential, Walker Kessler is solid. Lauri Markannen is 28 and borderline all-star caliber, has done enough to stop the Jazz from having a better shot at top picks in the past while still not having a shot at the playoffs, and might never be traded.

And that’s what the Jazz have to show for blowing their team up four years ago, and for four losing seasons.

Ainge’s reputation for his moves in Boston is well-earned, but at this point he’s dragged Utah through four losing seasons in his attempted rebuild without a star to show for it, and it seems overwhelmingly likely they’ll head for a fifth.

At what point does “rebuilding” stop being an effective shield when you take a 50 win team and drag it into a half decade of losing without coming up with a better player than either of the two you traded away?
