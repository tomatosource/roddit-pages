How Brad Could Swipe Flagg (Long Shot)

TL;DR:
Celtics trade Porzingis, Jaylen Brown, Derrick White, Payton Pritchard, Jrue Holiday, and 4 first-round picks to the Nets and Mavericks in a blockbuster deal to acquire the rights to Cooper Flagg.

I’ve been thinking about what Brad’s been up to by interviewing Cooper Flagg and I believe he’s aiming at a long shot trade to nab the rights to Cooper Flagg. Here is my trade breakdown of how it goes.

Trade Breakdown

Trade 1:

Celtics send:

  • Kristaps Porzingis
  • 2025 Boston 1st-round pick

to Nets for:

  • Salary cap relief

Why the Nets do this:
They receive a 1st-round pick and a potential trade asset in Porzingis. If KP is healthy by the deadline, he could be flipped for even more picks. KP has a unique skillset, being a big that can shoot the 3. Someone like him doesn’t grow on trees and could fetch a healthy price to a team like the lakers or warriors looking to run it back once more with Lebron or Steph. Brooklyn is in rebuild mode, and trading cap space for assets aligns with their long-term strategy.

Why the Celtics do this:
This move brings Boston below the second apron, allowing them to aggregate salaries in trades — a key to unlocking bigger deals.

Trade 2:

Celtics send:

  • Jaylen Brown
  • Derrick White
  • Payton Pritchard
  • Jrue Holiday
  • 2026, 2028, and 2030 1st-round picks

to Mavericks for:

  • Rights to Cooper Flagg
  • Kyrie Irving
  • Klay Thompson
  • PJ Washington
  • Naji Marshall
  • Max Christie
  • Additional role players

Why the Mavericks do this:
Kyrie is recovering from an ACL injury and may not return until after the All-Star break — and likely won’t be the same. This trade gives Dallas efficient, defensive-minded players in peak condition who align better with AD’s timeline. Plus, they gain three valuable first-round picks — one of which is likely a guaranteed lottery pick given the Celtics are dismantling their core.

Why the Celtics do this:
They secure Cooper Flagg, the projected generational talent. To make this legal under the Stepien Rule, Boston would need to trade their 2031 first-rounder to acquire a 2029 first-rounder.
