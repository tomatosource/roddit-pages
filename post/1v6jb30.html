[OC] How often has a player won the NBA Finals and reaffirmed it by following up with an MVP season? How often has a player lost the NBA Finals and gone on a revenge season winning an MVP the next year?

People often talk about teams where a player won the championship and MVP in the season, or won MVP and didn’t win the championship that season. But I was curious how often in NBA (and ABA) history a player won the Finals and reaffirmed the title with an MVP season the next year. Or in contrast, how often did a player lose the NBA Finals and go on a revenge tour the following season to win MVP?

Does the momentum of winning or sting of losing the Finals correlate to MVP seasons? How many players have done it “backwards” going to the Finals before becoming a league MVP?

ABA Champions that Won Season MVP the next season

    • Won 1970 ABA Finals over Los Angeles Stars 4-2

  • New York Nets: 1975 MVP Julius Erving (2nd MVP)
    • Won 1974 ABA Finals over Utah Stars 4-1
    • 11 out of 30 first place votes

Players that followed up a Finals loss with their 1st MVP

  • Kentucky Colonels: 1972 Artis Gilmore (ABA)
    • Rookie Season
    • Season result: ABA Eastern Division Semifinals (first round) loss to New York Nets 2-4
      • 68-16 record upset by Nets despite 44-40 season
    • Artis Gilmore was not on Colonels team that lost in 1971
  • Philadelphia 76ers: 1981 Julius Erving
    • 10th pro season (5th NBA season)
    • Season result: Eastern Conference Finals loss to Boston Celtics 3-4
      • Blown 3-1 lead to Bird’s Celtics, who came in 2nd for MVP
      • Sixers lost Game 82 of the season 98-94 at Boston, ending the season with both teams tied 62-20. Tiebreaker gave Celtics Game 7 at home.
      • Lost Games 5 through 7 by a combined 5 points (111-109, 100-98, 91-90)
  • Dallas Mavericks: 2007 Dirk Nowitzki
    • 9th season
    • Season result: Lost First Round vs Golden State Warriors 2-4
      • First 1 seed to lose to 8 seed in 7 game format
      • Most wins by a team to fail to advance past the first round (67-15)
