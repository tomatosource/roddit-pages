NBA Stars Have Massive Pace Splits This Season — Some Guys Are Completely Different Players Depending on Game Tempo

I ran a stat deep-dive comparing how the top 12 NBA scorers perform against the 5 fastest-paced teams (MIA, UTA, ATL, DAL, CHI) vs. the 5 slowest (BOS, HOU, LAC, BKN, CHA) this season. DNP games excluded from all splits. Some of these are wild.

🔥 TRACK MEET MONSTERS — players who go off in fast-paced games

Anthony Edwards (+1.6 PPG in fast games, but the real story is team impact) Ant scores consistently no matter the pace — 28.8 PPG vs. fast teams, 27.2 vs. slow ones. The scoring gap is smaller than I expected. But his plus-minus is the real tell: +11.8 in fast games, -9.8 in slow ones. That’s a 21-point swing. When Minnesota is in a track meet, Ant lifts the whole team. When Boston or Houston slows it to a crawl and packs the paint, the Wolves struggle badly even when he’s putting up similar numbers. His FG% also drops from 48.6% to 42.3% in slugfests.

Luka Doncic (+3.4 PPG in fast games) 34.1 PPG against fast teams, 30.7 against slow ones. The scoring gap is real but not enormous — Luka scores everywhere. The bigger story is team context: +7.7 +/- in fast games, -4.3 in slow ones. The Lakers as a whole are a different animal when the pace picks up.

Tyrese Maxey (+7.1 PPG in fast games) This is one of the cleaner splits in the dataset. Maxey drops 33.1 PPG against fast teams and only 26.0 against slow ones. He’s a jet — when the game opens up he’s nearly impossible to contain in transition. Pack the paint and make him work in the half-court and the production falls off noticeably.

Lauri Markkanen (+7.3 PPG in fast games) Plays for one of the fast teams (Utah) so context matters here, but 31.3 PPG in up-tempo games vs. 24.0 in slow ones is a real gap. He’s a stretch big who thrives in space — slow it down and defenders can load up on him.

🧊 HALF-COURT ASSASSINS — players who actually prefer the grind

Jalen Brunson (+5.6 PPG in fast games, but dramatically more efficient in slow ones) 24.8 PPG in slow games vs. 30.4 in fast ones — so actually a PPG dip in his preferred environment, which sounds wrong until you look at the efficiency: 69.1% TS% in slow games vs. 51.8% in fast ones. And his +/- is +10.7 slow vs. +5.7 fast. In transition-heavy games Brunson is scoring but it’s often forced. In half-court settings he’s a surgeon. The Knicks running slow offense is not an accident.

Cade Cunningham (nearly flat PPG, mild efficiency edge in slow games) 25.6 PPG vs. slow teams, 24.5 vs. fast ones — scoring is consistent across the board. 59.1% TS% in slow games vs. 53.5% in fast ones, but it’s a subtle lean rather than a dramatic split. He’s a methodical playmaker, and the grind suits him.

Giannis Antetokounmpo (small sample but eye-opening slow numbers) Only 30 games played total this season so treat this carefully, but his +/- in slow games is +14.0 and his TS% is 77.1% — both his best splits. When teams slow it down hoping to take away his transition game, he just bulldozes them in the half-court instead.

⚖️ THE TRULY PACE-NEUTRAL GUYS

Nikola Jokic — Scores more in slow games (33.0 PPG vs. 28.0 fast) and posts a 92.9% TS% in those matchups. That number is basically a rounding error away from impossible. He was making everything and drawing fouls on top of it. The man does not care about your game plan.

SGA — Remarkably consistent: 33.8 PPG fast, 32.0 mid, 28.5 slow. His efficiency is actually highest in slow games (75.2% TS%) but the counting stats dip. He’s elite everywhere, just more explosive when the game opens up.

Kawhi Leonard — Almost perfectly flat: 27.1 / 28.2 / 28.4 PPG across fast/mid/slow. TS% barely moves either. Genuinely pace-proof.

Jaylen Brown — 28.3 PPG fast, 29.3 slow. Slight slow-game edge with better FG% (51.5% vs 44.4%). Pace doesn’t really move the needle.

🔄 THE FLIP (most surprising finding)

Donovan Mitchell — I originally wrote him up as a slow-game specialist. That was wrong — the original query included DNP games. Clean data tells the opposite story: 31.5 PPG and 78.5% TS% in fast games, dropping to 24.2 PPG and 55.8% TS% in slow ones. He’s actually the anti-grinder. His pull-up game and athleticism thrive when defenses are scrambling in transition. Slow it down and he becomes much more beatable.

Steph Curry — Fewer possessions = fewer Steph threes. 28.6 PPG in fast games, 21.2 in slow ones. His efficiency is actually fine in slow games (63.6% TS vs 57.4%) but the counting stats take a real hit. Less chaos = less Steph.

Edit: Original post included DNP games as 0-point performances, which skewed the splits. All numbers have been corrected to reflect only games played. Biggest changes were to Mitchell (flipped from slow-game to fast-game specialist) and Cade (averages were dragged down)
