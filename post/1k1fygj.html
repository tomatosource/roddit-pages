Grizzlies GM Zach Kleiman’s Terrible, Horrible, No Good, Very Bad Year

Zach Kleiman has received a ton of praise over the years as a standout GM for the Memphis Grizzlies. His draft history is undeniable. Late first hits in Clarke, Bane, and Aldama. Second round hits in Vince Williams, GG Jackson, Jaylen Wells. I don’t think there’s a better drafting GM in the league than him. His prowess has earned him Executive of the Year honors in the past and he has a hell of a career ahead of him. What can’t be ignored however, is just how bad he’s been this year.

Let’s start with the good. Drafting Edey, Wells, and Cam Spencer in this year’s draft has paid off. Thats a 9th overall pick and two second rounders, all of whom look like future solid contributors. The fact that the team got away starting TWO rookies all year and still has a chance to be a playoff team is ridiculous. I don’t know any other scenario where a playoff team had two rookies starting for them, one of them being a second rounder. I’m sure it’s happened but it’s just crazy. Both of those guys should be 1st team All-Rookie. Cam Spencer isn’t well known yet but he’s going to be a 10+ year player in the league. I’m not really sure how the second leading scorer on one of the greatest college teams ever (2024 UConn) who shot 44% from three fell all the way to the end of the draft, but he’s shown a lot of promise in his very little playtime.

Now let’s look at the missteps he’s had in the time leading up to this season.

Marcus Smart: The trade for Smart was immediately questioned when it happened going into last season. How are we going to start a guard at SF, is he even still good, Boston has always screwed us in trades. It all proved to be even worse than imagined. Two first round picks down the drain for 39 total games from Smart in two seasons. Just a total debacle all around.

Coach Drama: The Jenkins drama still has a lot of details under wraps that may come to light over the next few years. What we do know is that after last season the two were at odds. I think Kleiman wanted him gone, but it’s hard to drop him after being so successful and then having one bad injury year. So, what does Kleiman do going into the year? Makes Jenkins fire every assistant coach and replace him with Kleiman’s handpicked choices. Surely neutering your coach goes over well. I know a lot of fights happened between them throughout the year and imagine most of it was over forcing all these young guys into the roster and not having any vets or reliable pieces to contend outside of the Big 3. It all came to a climax a few weeks ago when Kleiman fired Jenkins with eight games left in the season. I think it was time to move on, but the way it was done just came off as erratic. Why even keep him in the first place if you’re just gonna put him in a position to fail. This isn’t to say getting rid of Jenkins was wrong, I think it was right. But no team that fires their coach right before the playoffs is going to find any success.

Ja Morant: Kleiman knew Morant’s availability was an issue. It always will be. I’m not sure if the new scheme implemented this season was more Jenkins or Kleiman, but whoever is responsible for it is an idiot. The new scheme basically boiled down to this. Every player on the court is a ball handler, zero screens, and less minutes for the starters to keep them fresh. This offense worked really well for guys like Jaren and Santi. The issue was it completely downgraded 2⁄5 of your starters. Edey was useless as he’s an old school rebounding big man. If that’s the scheme you want to run with, you don’t draft Edey. It’s just not a fit. It’s also not a fit for your franchise superstar. In no scenario is Ja Morant sitting in a corner hunting a three and not touching the ball at all in a possession a good use of the player. Super weird position for a ball dominant slashing playmaker who can’t shoot threes. Thanks to this scheme everyone in the world now think Ja Morant sucks at basketball and Zach Edey is unplayable. Luckily, too little too late, the new coach Iisalo came in and changed it up. Now Ja is back to being the primary ball handler, he’s getting huge Edey screens, and played at an All-NBA caliber to finish the year. Edey also looked like a ROTY to end the year. I have lots of confidence that they can build on that going into next season. The point of this story is don’t completely tank the value of your franchise superstar by putting him in a scheme that negates all of his strengths. Also don’t play your best players way less than the opposing team plays their best players. Might be a big reason you can’t beat a single good team this year.

Doubling Down on Youth: Instead of adding vets each year, Zach keeps doubling down on youth. The oldest player on the Grizzlies right now is 28 years old. We started two rookies the entirety of the year. How is that possible. How are you even supposed to feign contention when there’s not a single vet on the team. How do you fight a brutal battle against the Warriors in 2022 and then say okay time to get rid of the vets and add more young role players to our roster of young role players. It’s 2025 now and the team somehow feels younger than it was when we were contenders in 2022.

2022 Team Downgrade: Speaking of that 2022 team, after losing the Warriors series Kleiman diagnosed the issue. We have to upgrade the SF and C positions. We like our Big 3, but to win it all we gotta upgrade these two spots. Three years later and not only have we not upgraded, we downgraded. We went from Dillon Brooks and Steven Adams starting to starting two rookies. People love to blame Ja or Jaren for the team not being successful. They are not at fault whatsoever. You can’t be a true contender without a great team around your stars. Somehow, someway, we burned three years of our Big 3 since that 2022 series and have only gotten worse players around them.

Trade Deadline: This one is a tough one. Maybe the worst contending team deadline perfomance ever? I think we were the two seed at the trade deadline. Feeling great. Jaren had been playing at an All-NBA first team level. Surely at the trade deadline we add some pieces to help us, as we can’t start two rookies in the playoffs. Who does Kleiman bring in to help us? Marvin Bagley. That’s not even the worst part though. Not only did we not add a vet or any needle moving player. We gave away a solid bench player in Laravia for nothing and then gave away a first round pick just to dump Marcus Smart. That’s two firsts to acquire Smart, and one to dump him. Three first round picks burned for NOTHING. Which brings me to my next and most damning point.

Tomorrow Night’s Game: Because of Kleiman’s Smart dump we now enter tomorrow in a lose/lose situation. If we win the game, we earn a playoff series with OKC who will murder us. We also lose that first round pick I previously mentioned. If we lose tomorrow, we get to keep the lottery pick and the Smart dump turns into 2 seconds. So now there’s literally no chance to win tomorrow. You either get the draft pick which you desperately need if you want some good trade capital, or you lose the draft pick but get 4 games of playoff experience for the team. If we had kept Smart, we probably wouldn’t even be in the damn play-in scenario to begin with but also wouldn’t have to worry about losing some of the very little capital we have by advancing to the playoffs. This should be a fireable offense. A small market like Memphis will never attract a star in free agency. It’s proven to be hard to even trade for one (see KD/Butler/Bridges.) So to put your team in an unneeded scenario where you have to choose between going to the playoffs or giving up a precious lottery pick (quite literally the only way to improve your team whether through using it or trading it) all because you didn’t want Marcus Smart on the team is INSANE. He probably would’ve helped us more these past few weeks than Vince Williams anyways.

Thank God for Nico Harrison. He’s been a great meat shield for Kleiman and already took the crown for worst GM of the year. I just wanted to point out how truly bad this year was for Kleiman as a GM, and that his job went from rock solid, to a full-on hot seat entering 2025-2026.
