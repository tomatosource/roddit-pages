It’s not just making shots - two quick examples of a small adjustment Joe made to open up the Knicks defense in game three

Rewatched some of the offensive possessions in the first half of game three, and while it was a bit of “just make your shots” Joe had some really creative sets to get the Knicks defense in motion.

Our standard offense is to get a mismatch and attack it to force the defense to react and then making the right read when the defense rotates. Knicks messed with that by essentially pre-reacting to the mismatch - they collapsed on drives ahead of time to clog the paint while leaving threes wide open but with no motion.

So in the first quarter game three we came out and ran a bunch of decoy sets, where we would set it up like we were trying to make out usual read and react on mismatches but there would be a second action on the other side that was actually the main play.

Here’s one play that worked perfectly: https://www.nba.com/stats/events?CFID=&CFPARAMS=&GameEventID=68&GameID=0042400213&Season=2024-25&flag=1&title=Brown%203%27%20Driving%20Hook%20Shot%20(7%20PTS)%20(Tatum%202%20AST)

Starts off with jaylen setting a screen for white who swings it to Tatum at the top of the key. Al comes up from the other wing to set a screen, which is something we do all the time to try and get Tatum matched with a big and force the best wing defender (bridges) to be stuck in the corner guarding brown. But Tatum doesn’t care about the horford screen and brown doesn’t go to the corner - as soon as he screens for white Jaylen cuts to the paint and Tatum never even puts the ball on the floor because he’s just waiting for Jaylen to establish position. The whole defense is shaded to collapse on Tatum and he doesn’t even move and we get an easy layup.

Here’s a second one: https://www.nba.com/stats/events?CFID=&CFPARAMS=&GameEventID=112&GameID=0042400213&Season=2024-25&flag=1&title=Tatum%2025%27%203PT%20Jump%20Shot%20(6%20PTS)%20(Brown%202%20AST)

A little simpler, but same concept of using our base sets as a decoy. Clip is short but the play started with a series of screens to get Jaylen brown isolated against Brunson on the baseline. We do this a ton and tried it a bunch in games one and two, but Brunson is strong enough and pesky enough with the reach ins to not get blown by, and the Knicks would wait for brown to commit to a shot and then collapse and block the passing lanes out to the perimeter.

This time Jaylen gets back to the basket against Brunson but he’s never looking to score - he’s not putting any force into his backdown or trying to fake spin moves or anything - he’s staying in control and staring at the two help defenders - OG and Robinson. The second those two take a step in and commit to collapsing (right after Jaylens second bump on Brunson which is usually when he goes into his shooting move) Jaylen fires the pass to Tatum who has time to set his feet and then read a book before Robinson can get out to contest the three.

The key on both of these plays is that the “main” action was always a decoy because we knew the Knicks were sitting on it to stop it.

After the first quarter, now the Knicks have all these secondary actions in the back of their mind and they were way less decisive in what they wanted to take away, and that half second of indecision is the kind of thing our usual offense is built to take advantage of and we ran away with the game.
