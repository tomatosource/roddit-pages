The Jay’s Top Individual Performances (post-season)

Warning, this is a very long post. This is for those who want to or enjoy going down memory lane.

Can’t stand how divisive Celtics fans have become regarding the dynamic duo that blessed us for nearly a decade. I wish we could just celebrate them during this grieving period rather than tear them down individually. Whether JB’s your favorite player or you think JT is the best, you should still be able to celebrate all they’ve done to give Celtic’s fans something to enjoy come playoff time.

I’ve put together a list of the top/my favorite individual post-season performances over the years. Might turn this into a YT video or might put together one for the regular season, not sure. All I know is I want to celebrate the Jays Era before it turns into a real rivalry.

Let me know which moments I’m missing. Tell me your favorites. Which moments deserve more credit. I’m went off memory and reviewing box scores so I’m sure I’m missing something.

The Jays Post-Season Performances:
Ranked — Best / Biggest Individual Performances

How this is organized
This isn’t ranked by checking boxes against a rubric. It’s organized by what kind of story each moment actually is, and how it feels to remember it — not just what the box score says or how deep in the playoffs it happened. Some of these are the lore moments - the ones fans will discuss for decades. Some are lightning strikes - you had to be there to feel the impact. Some are great nights that just haven’t been talked about enough. A few are real, meaningful games that still don’t rise to myth. The tiers are built around that emotional shape, not necessarily by what was more impressive. It’s far from perfect, but it’s my best shot.

Tier 1 — Where legends are born
These are the stories that get passed down for generations. These moments will always be mentioned first when discussing top performances.

  1. Tatum — Game 6, 2022 East Semis at Milwaukee.
    A duel with a ghost in the room. Two nights earlier, Boston had blown an almost identical lead and lost the series lead because of it. Now it’s happening again — Milwaukee claws a 14-point deficit down to 4, and everyone on the bench is thinking about the Game 5 collapse. Across from Tatum, Giannis Antetokounmpo is having an MVP-level game himself: 44 points & 20 rebounds. Tatum doesn’t just dig the Celtics out of a hole. He simply refuses to let a new one open — 11 straight points, including fade-aways and step-back threes, and the door shuts for good. 46 points on 53% shooting. 9 rebounds and 4 assists. Dropping 16 points in the final 9 minutes of an elimination game on the road against the reigning NBA MVP. There’s no wonder why many consider this his greatest game of all time.

  2. Tatum — Game 7, 2023 East Semis vs. Philadelphia.
    Win-or-go-home game 7 against the soon-to-be-MVP. Following up an already legendary take over in game 6 to keep the series alive. By tip-off, he’s already found what he was searching for most of the game before — 25 points before halftime. Unfortunately for the lore, his dominance coming early and often in this game would actually turn what was supposed to be a can’t miss game 7 to a stress-free reminder of who the actual big brother in this rivalry is. This game’s not discussed enough. But look at what actually happened: the record for most points in a Game 7, outscoring Philadelphia’s top-3 scorers, including that season’s MVP, by himself - one game after putting on a similar performance in the 4th quarter just to make this game 7 a possibility - that HAS to make the top 3.

  3. Tatum — Game 6, 2023 East Semis vs. Philadelphia.
    Without this night, the record above never happens. Boston trailed 81-83 with under six minutes to go, Tatum having missed his first 13 shots has only scored just 3 points through three quarters — one loss from elimination. Then, without warning: four three-pointers in a matter of minutes, flipping the deficit into an 87-83 lead, then extending it to 92-84. He scored 16 of Boston’s 26 fourth-quarter points, personally outscoring the entire 76ers team over the final period. A resurrection turned into domination, that would ultimately lead to a game 7 for the record books.

Tier 2 — David vs. Goliath
The statement game. If you didn’t see him before this game - you did now.

  1. Tatum — Game 3, First Round 2021 vs. Brooklyn.
    Jaylen Brown is done for the season, out after wrist surgery. Tatum’s last month has been special. He’s had a 50-point game, a 60-point game, but can he do that in the playoffs? Boston is down 0-2 to a Nets team stacked with three players who will all end up in the Hall of Fame — Kevin Durant, James Harden, Kyrie Irving — and staring at an 0-3 hole that no team in NBA history has ever climbed out of. Nearly every all-time great has been swept at some point in his career — MJ, LeBron, Bird, Magic, Kobe, Duncan, Shaq, all of them have been swept more than once. On paper, this series was shaping up to be no different: a 23-year-old with key players missing from his supporting cast, completely overmatched against three Hall of Famers in their prime. Tatum doesn’t share the burden with anyone. He scores 50 points on 16-of-30 shooting, with 6 rebounds and 7 assists, closing it out with a jumper hoisted directly over Durant’s outstretched arm — the third-youngest player in NBA history, behind only Rick Barry and Michael Jordan, to score 50 in a playoff game. It didn’t save the series, and it didn’t change the Celtics’ trajectory that year. What it did is simpler and rarer: it’s the single reason Jayson Tatum, unlike the stars previously mentioned, has never been swept. One gun against three Hall of Famers, and for one night, the one gun was enough.

Tier 3 — You had to be there
These moments don’t dominate a box score — they dominate the memory. The moments that were the loudest, that got you out of your seat and undoubtedly left a mark.

  1. Brown — Game 1, 2024 Eastern Conference Finals vs. Indiana.
    Down late in the series opener, Brown drills a contested three over Pascal Siakam with 6 seconds left to force overtime. A ridiculous shot at a ridiculous moment. Arguably the sole reason for why the Celtics made it to over time that game and arguably the main reason for that series being a sweep.

  2. Tatum — Game 1, 2024 Eastern Conference Finals vs. Indiana.
    The thunder that follows Brown’s lightning. Same game, same night. Brown’s shot buys the extra five minutes, Tatum makes them count — 10 of his 36 points in overtime alone, the second-most overtime points in a playoff game in Celtics history. Two different players, two different flavors of clutch, one shared moment that will never be forgotten.

  3. Tatum — The Dunk on LeBron James, Game 7, 2018 Eastern Conference Finals
    This doesn’t need a stat line. A 20-year-old rookie takes off in the fourth quarter of a win-or-go-home game and posterizes the best player alive — a photograph, not a performance, the single image that told the league this kid wasn’t scared of anybody. It’s the exclamation point on a rookie playoff run that was already historic in its own right: only the second rookie ever, after Kareem, to score 20+ points in ten different playoff games, with a record 5 straight at just 20 years old. Boston lost this game, 87-79 — the dunk didn’t win anything. It just announced someone.

  4. Tatum — Game 1, First Round 2022 vs. Brooklyn Nets.
    With the game on the line in the final seconds, Tatum smothered Kevin Durant into a contested, off-balance fadeaway three that missed — then, moments later, capped his own night with a spinning layup off a Marcus Smart feed to win it at the buzzer, 115-114. His first career buzzer-beater, and Boston’s first in the playoffs since Paul Pierce in 2010. Easy to miss behind the highlight: this was a full two-way takeover — 31 points, 8 assists, 4 rebounds, 2 blocks (one on Kyrie Irving, one on Durant), and a steal, locked in on both ends against a stacked roster all night before delivering the finish himself.

  5. Brown — Game 4, First Round 2023 vs. Atlanta.
    Brown started the night 1-for-7, frustrated, wearing the protective mask he’d worn since a facial fracture that February. Mid-timeout, he simply took it off. What followed was instant: 11-for-15 the rest of the way, finishing with 31 points to match Tatum’s 31 in the same game. “Maybe it was all in my head,” Brown said after. “I just needed something different.” A small, strange, unmistakably his moment — and, if we’re honest, the purest example in this tier of aura outrunning substance. A good scoring night against a first-round opponent, remembered entirely because JB refused to quit.

Tier 4 — The Finals At Last

  1. Tatum — Game 7, 2022 Eastern Conference Finals vs. Miami.
    This one isn’t about personal heroics. It’s about a franchise finally exhaling. Boston hadn’t been back to the NBA Finals since 2010 — over a decade of near-misses and disappointment. The game itself was more grind than takeover: Miami sent late double-teams at Tatum for most of the second half, and rather than forcing his own shot, he adjusted into a playmaker, finishing with 26 points, 10 rebounds, and 6 assists. It doesn’t have one signature highlight. What it has is the feeling in that building when the final buzzer sounded — pure release, a decade of waiting finally over. It won Tatum the first-ever NBA Eastern Conference Finals MVP award, but the award is almost beside the point. This moment gets its own space because nothing else on this list feels quite like it.

Tier 5 — Underrated Explosions
Genuinely great nights, well short of myth — no history changed, no giant slain, no comeback from the brink. Just great players being great, on a real stage, remembered less than they deserve.

  1. Tatum — Game 3, First Round 2022 vs. Brooklyn.
    Lost in the shadow of a sweep that gets miscasted as easy: 39 points (27 in the second half), 5 rebounds, 6 assists, and a playoff career-high 6 steals — the first Celtics player with a 30/5/5/5 line in a playoff game since Dave Cowens in 1976. A genuinely rare statistical performance, that nobody talks about because the series wasn’t close.

  2. Brown — Game 2, 2024 Eastern Conference Finals vs. Indiana.
    The night after his Game 1 shot, and one day after being passed over for All-NBA, Brown answers with 40 points on 14-of-27 shooting — matching his playoff career high — to put Boston up 2-0. Combined with Game 1, this is almost certainly the two-game stretch that won him the Larry Bird Trophy as ECF MVP — a clean, satisfying, personal answer to a snub.

  3. Tatum — Game 3, 2024 Eastern Conference Finals at Indiana.
    Down 18 in the second half on the road, Tatum put together the kind of complete game that’s hard to fake: 36 points, 10 rebounds, 8 assists, and zero turnovers — the first 36/10/8/0-TO playoff game in NBA history. The signature moment was a behind-the-back feed to Al Horford for a dagger three that Brown said belonged in the Louvre — degree-of-difficulty stacked on top of degree-of-difficulty, in a series that was much closer than it is remembered as.

  4. Tatum — Play-In Game, 2021 vs. Washington.
    50 points in a win-or-go-home game that half the league still doesn’t fully count as “real” post-season — still the NBA record for most points ever scored in a play-in game, and the feat that made Tatum the only player ever to hit 50 in the regular season, the playoffs, a play-in game, and an All-Star Game. It deserves more reverence than the format it happened in usually gets.

  5. Tatum — Game 7, 2020 NBA Bubble, Round 2 vs. Toronto.
    A genuinely forgotten classic. Boston beats the defending-champion Raptors on the road in a win-or-go-home game, with a 22-year-old Tatum going for 29 points, 12 rebounds, 7 assists — the second-youngest player ever to post 25/10/5 in a Game 7, behind only Kobe Bryant. Ask most fans about this game and they won’t remember it happened. That’s the whole story here.

16 & 17. Tatum — Games 4 & 5, First Round 2025 vs. Orlando.
Two dominant, nearly matching performances, back to back, both nearly lost in the shuffle of a first-round series against an overmatched opponent. In Game 4, still playing through the wrist injury that had already cost him a game, Tatum came back more aggressive, not less — 37 points, 14 rebounds, a perfect 14-for-14 from the free-throw line, and a physical, technical-foul exchange with Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, the same player whose hard foul caused the injury in the first place. Two nights later, in the closeout Game 5, he did it again: 35 points, 8 rebounds, 10 assists, and a perfect 11-for-11 from the line. Across the two games he shot a combined 25-for-25 from the free-throw line and became only the second Celtic ever, after Larry Bird, to string together three straight 35-point playoff games (the first coming in Game 3’s loss — see Tier 9). Two technician’s masterpieces in a row that nobody outside Boston remembers happening at all.

  1. Brown — Game 2, First Round 2025 vs. Orlando.
    With Tatum sidelined by a wrist injury — the first playoff game he’d missed in eight seasons — Brown answered every question about his own ailing knee at once: 36 points, 10 rebounds, 5 assists on 12-of-19 shooting, his first career 30-10-5 playoff game. Not a must-win, but a real test “can JB do this hurt without JT” moment, answered emphatically.

Tier 6 — The Quiet Summit
Everything from the 2024 championship run. Enormous in what they built toward, strangely calm to actually relive — like reaching the top of a mountain on a clear, quiet day after a stormy climb. None of these has a stat line that would stand out on a random Tuesday in February; what makes them matter is entirely the occasion.

  1. Brown — Game 1, 2024 NBA Finals.
    22 points, 6 rebounds, 3 blocks, 3 steals — the first Celtic ever to hit 20/3/3 in a Finals game. A real rarity, but a series opener, not the game anyone remembers first from that Finals.

  2. Brown — Game 3, 2024 NBA Finals.
    30 points, 8 rebounds, a playoff career-high 8 assists — only the second Celtic ever, after Havlicek in 1968, to post 30/8/8 in a Finals game. Came in a game Boston nearly let slip — a 21-point lead cut to 1 before Dallas’s best player fouled out — closed out by a Brown jumper with just over a minute left. This is very likely the individual performance that earned Brown his Finals MVP; the closeout game (below) is comfortable and forgettable by comparison, and voters tend to remember the game that felt hardest to win.

  3. Tatum — Game 3, 2024 NBA Finals.
    31 points, 6 rebounds, 5 assists in the same nail-biter — part of a night where he and Brown became the first Celtics duo ever to each post 30/5/5 in a Finals game. Solid, unspectacular, elevated only by the circumstances around it.

  4. Tatum — Game 5, 2024 NBA Finals (title-clinching game).
    31 points, 11 assists, 8 rebounds in the game that won Boston its 18th championship — his best game of the series, but a comfortable one. Boston led by 26 and never trailed. The stakes are as high as they get; the game itself was never in doubt, heavily because of JT’s performance.

Tier 7 — One Half Was Enough

  1. Tatum — Game 4, 2023 Eastern Conference Finals vs. Miami.
    Down 0-3 in the series, one loss from a sweep by the 8 seed— the whole season came down to this single game. Tatum was quiet in the first half, then took over in the second: 14 points in the third, 12 more in the fourth, turning a 9-point deficit into a comfortable win and buying Boston one more day. This series will probably go down as the Jays biggest disappointments and biggest what-ifs, but on this night JT did what he could to try to prevent that.

  2. Brown — Game 2, 2022 East Semis vs. Milwaukee.
    25 of his 30 points came in the first half alone against a legitimately great, defending-champion Bucks team. Confident, comfortable dominance — a wire-to-wire win with no late tension at all, and no real stakes attached beyond a good regular playoff game against a rival in the Bucks and Giannis.

Tier 8 — The Legendary Night turned to a nightmare

  1. Tatum — Game 4, Second Round 2025 vs. New York.
    Forty-two points in forty minutes, some of the best basketball of his year — and then his leg simply gives out. This isn’t a good, clean game to file away. It’s the night that quietly rewrote the next fourteen months of two men’s careers, ending in the exact kind of moment nobody wants to remember and nobody can forget. But hidden behind all of that was an incredible performance by Tatum.

Tier 9 — Not the Hero of the Story
Real numbers, real stakes in some cases — but either the deciding moment belonged to somebody else, the loss swallowed the performance whole, or the player was too young for anyone to be watching that closely yet.

  1. Brown — Game 4, First Round 2018 vs. Milwaukee.
    At 21 years old, Brown set a new playoff career high with 34 points, nearly erasing a 20-point deficit — Boston lost anyway, 104-102, with the series tied at 2-2. An explosive, ahead-of-his-years performance that gets forgotten because it ended in a loss.

  2. Brown — Game 5, Second Round 2018 vs. Philadelphia.
    24 points in a game that actually came down to the wire — Boston’s lead shrank from 9 to 1 in the third quarter before a late stop and a go-ahead bucket sealed it, 114-112. A genuine classic. The final possession just wasn’t his.

  3. Brown — Game 6, 2018 Eastern Conference Finals.
    27 points on the road, Celtics down 3-2 — but LeBron simply took the game over with 46 points, and it was never really competitive in the fourth quarter. Real stakes, someone else’s night.

  4. Brown — Game 1, 2022 NBA Finals.
    Boston trails by 12 in the fourth quarter on the road against a Warriors team unbeaten at home that postseason, and steals it with a 40-16 final period. But that quarter belonged more to White & Horford who bombarded the Warriors with threes. Derrick and Al will be remembered for capturing that moment, but Brown got us there.

  5. Tatum — Game 4, First Round 2021 vs. Brooklyn.
    The night after his 50-point masterpiece, Tatum went for 40 more — but this time Brooklyn’s trio combined for 104 points of their own, and Boston lost in a 141-126 track meet. A huge number in a game that got away, as Tatum fought 1v3.

  6. Brown — Game 3, 2022 Eastern Conference Finals vs. Miami.
    A game-high 40 points on 14-of-20 shooting, 11-of-11 from inside the arc — the first player in the shot-clock era (since 1954) to score 40+ points on a perfect night from two-point range, and the first Celtic ever to do it on 70%+ shooting in a playoff game. It should be one of the most efficient monster games in franchise history. Instead it’s a footnote, because Brown also had 7 turnovers in a game where Boston coughed it up 23 times as a team and lost, 109-103. A brilliant individual number swallowed by a series slipping away.

  7. Tatum — Game 1, Second Round 2023 vs. Philadelphia.
    39 points, 11 rebounds on 14-of-25 shooting, in a game that was tied four separate times in the second half — before James Harden simply had the better night, scoring 45 and hitting the shots that mattered as Philadelphia stole home court, 119-115. A genuine giant game, in a genuinely close loss, decided by someone else’s fourth quarter.

  8. Tatum — Game 3, First Round 2025 vs. Orlando.
    Initially doubtful to play after a hard foul from Kentavious Caldwell-Pope bruised his wrist in Game 1, Tatum suited up anyway and dropped 36 points, 9 rebounds, and 4 assists on a perfect 12-for-12 from the free-throw line. Boston lost anyway — the first of three straight 35-point games he’d play in this series, and the only one that didn’t end in a win (see Tier 5 for the other two, told together as one entry).
