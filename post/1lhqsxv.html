Tracy McGrady averaged more in elimination games than Michael Jordan

Amid the chatter around T-Mac’s recent comments on Kobe and his belief that he could’ve won more if he had better teammates, here is an interesting stat.

  • McGrady averaged 31.7 PPG on 52.7TS% in 9 career elimination games.

  • Michael Jordan averaged 31.3 PPG on 53.7TS% in 13 career elimination games.

  • Kobe Bryant averaged 22.3 PPG on 50.3TS% in 19 career elimination games.

  • For some more comps to SGs from T-Mac’s low efficiency era:

  • D-Wade averaged 21.0 PPG on 51.0TS% in 21 career elimination games.

  • Iverson averaged 29.8 games on 49.3TS% in 14 career elimination games.

All numbers from StatMuse.

Of course sample sizes are different, as is difficulty as T-Mac didn’t go up against many dominant defenses. Nevertheless, he did rack up these numbers typically as the much lower seed against a far better team designed to stop him and only him.

In 35 playoff games as a first option before his body broke down, McGrady averaged 29.5⁄6.9⁄6.5 with 2.5 steals + blocks per game, and never had a playoff series with less than 25 PPG.

This is not to say McGrady would’ve threepeated with Shaq like Kobe did, but it is to say that he certainly has the statistical profile of a dominant playoff performer.

Which is what makes his career one of the strangest in NBA history - somehow a guy that never made the second round (2014 Spurs does not count) has some of the most prolific playoff stats on a per game basis.

A final fun fact: Tracy McGrady is 3rd all-time in career playoff BPM among retired players. Behind only Michael Jordan and Magic Johnson. A freakish career.
