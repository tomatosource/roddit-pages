A lot of commentary has been made about finding “a smoking gun” linking the Clippers vs. circumstantial evidence. Do people not realize that a smoking gun by definition is circumstantial?

I was listening to the muppets do The Athletic podcast and one of the hosts said something along the lines of: “Circumstantial evidence is basically a sin in legal cases, you can’t ever say that word in a courtroom.” It honestly shocked me how stupid that is.

The whole idea of a “smoking gun” is rooted in circumstantial evidence: if someone is found holding a freshly fired gun that’s still smoking, you can logically infer they pulled the trigger, even if nobody actually witnessed the shot. That’s the definition of circumstantial, and it can be just as powerful as direct evidence.

The legal system relies on both direct and circumstantial evidence. If a murder happens with no eyewitnesses, that doesn’t mean the killer walks free. Prosecutors build cases on a chain of circumstantial facts: cellphone records placing you at the scene, DNA evidence, the murder weapon showing up in your possession, etc. Each piece may be indirect, but together they create a clear, compelling picture of what happened.
