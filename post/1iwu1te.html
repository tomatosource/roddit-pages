Jalen Clark Head Injury: The NBA has a serious safety flaw. The game should stop immediately when a player has a head injury.

With 6:24 to go in the 3rd quarter of Sunday night’s Timberwolves vs Thunder game, Jalen Clark fell while driving and hit his head on the floor. He stayed down on his back clutching his head while his team gathered the rebound and scored. The official on the end line noticed him injured on the floor, but the Thunder inbounded the ball standing right next to Clark, and the official took off to chase the action, as is the custom, leaving it to the T-wolves to foul and call a time out so they could tend to Clark. 23 seconds elapsed before anyone made it to Clark (a T-wolves player from the bench reached him first). With the possibility of a brain bleed and other serious injuries, that’s an unacceptable amount of time. Adam Silver and the NBA need to change the rules to require an immediate stoppage if a head injury occurs. Any team’s medical staff should act immediately to reach and treat the injured player regardless of play. It’s more important than gameplay. It was sort of disgusting to see everyone ignore Jalen Clark clutching his head on the floor as play continued, especially when the basket was scored and the ball was inbounded right next to him, with the players, officials, and staff all ignoring his distress. This needs to be fixed.
