Tell Me Where I’m Wrong About the Celtics

I wrote about every team and every move in the chaotic offseason. Tell me where I’m wrong about the Celtics:

“It’s not terrible, but the vibes in Boston are a far cry from where they were a couple of weeks ago. Swapping out Finals MVP Jaylen Brown for Paul George as a calculated analytics play seems like a *massive misfire* on its face, and I fully expect that to manifest on the court. I love Jayson Tatum’s game, and to PG’s credit, he proved he can still operate at a high level as a premium role player during Philly’s playoff run last spring. But here is the bottom line: Tatum and Brown already proved they are a flawless, championship-winning duo. It is going to take nothing short of another banner to convince me this trade makes a shred of sense.

The cascading effect on the roster creates just as many questions. Mitchell Robinson, fresh off a championship run of his own, is a fantastic defensive anchor when healthy, bringing elite rim protection and offensive rebounding to the table. But his arrival most likely means squeezing out Neemias Queta, who won over Boston by absolutely thriving as a starter during last year’s surprisingly dominant regular season.

For the Celtics to stay afloat, they need a major offensive evolution from the supporting cast. We’ll need Derrick White to bounce back into peak form, while Payton Pritchard will have to absorb a chunk of Brown’s vacated minutes with high-efficiency spacing. If youngsters like Hugo González, Baylor Scheierman (a truly insane first name, by the way), and Sam Hauser can expand their playmaking toolkits, Boston has a chance. But given the staggering miles on the legs of Tatum, George, and the notoriously fragile Robinson, I am capping this team’s ceiling at the second round of the playoffs. If the injury bug bites, it could look worse.”
