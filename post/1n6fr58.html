Why POE2’s Balance Feels So Awkward Right Now

A lot of people are wondering why POE2 is struggling so much with balance, when GGG already has years of experience from POE1 to build on.

In my opinion, it comes down to the new design philosophy. GGG clearly doesn’t want POE2 to devolve into the same “zoomy one-button mash” gameplay that defined POE1. Instead, they’re experimenting with interacting spells and abilities — builds where multiple moving parts combine to create power.

The problem? When you balance a game around interacting cogs, there’s always a risk of creating a threshold where if the pieces don’t mesh perfectly, you end up with something more mediocre than what POE1 offered.

And that’s the puzzling part: POE2 feels more railroaded into specific builds, but even those “intended” builds can’t handle the current balance. GGG hasn’t quite figured out how to both:

  1. Prevent brainless one-button builds, and
  2. Make interaction-based builds strong (and fun) enough to feel worth it.

I get it — the game is still Early Access, and I’m fine with GGG taking the time to iterate. But for that to work, we as a community need to provide constructive criticism to help shape the best version of the game.

One possible alternative would be to make character building more open-ended. More freedom means players would naturally discover strong builds. But the trade-off is… that’s basically back to POE1 again: tons of options, but you need a PhD to create a viable builds or you end up googling it.

The current system feels way less customizable. Take the new Remnant system for “Elemental” damage (on Sorc) as an example:

  • There’s no interaction with weapon-category gems.
  • The explicit “Remnant” text makes the mechanic feel overly restrictive. In a more POE1-style approach, Remnants would interact with Projectile, orb, etc. — opening up creativity and allowing all classes to make use of the mechanic.

To be clear, I’m glad GGG is trying something new. But if they want POE2 to be truly great, they need to:

  • Keep experimenting but be more open with their intentions,
  • Open up the sandbox a bit more, and
  • Loosen the rails so players can actually find/build solutions to the balance problems.

Right now, the system feels too narrow and too restrictive — which makes the balance issues even harder to swallow.
