Happy black history month!

Happy Black History Month. Every February, we get a chance to look back at the giants upon whose shoulders today’s game stands. This year, the NBA’s theme is “Out of Many Cultures, One People,” with a special focus on the 75th anniversary of the league’s integration.

This season marks 75 years since the NBA color barrier was officially broken. We have to give monumental flowers to the trio who faced unimaginable pressure to pave the way: Chuck Cooper: The first Black player drafted by an NBA team (Boston Celtics). Nat “Sweetwater” Clifton: The first Black player to sign an NBA contract (New York Knicks), coming over from the legendary Harlem Globetrotters. Earl Lloyd: The first Black player to actually play in an NBA game (Washington Capitols) due to scheduling. Rest in peace.

have you heard of Leroy Chollet?

There’s a fascinating piece of history that suggests the league might have been integrated a full year earlier, in 1949, without anyone realizing it. Chollet was a standout player at Canisius College whose mother was white and whose father was biracial. In an era of strict segregation, Chollet largely passed as white to avoid the systemic racism of the time. He played for the Syracuse Nationals (now the 76ers) in the NBL and was on their roster when they merged into the NBA for its inaugural 1949-50 season. While Cooper, Clifton, and Lloyd are rightly celebrated as the public faces of integration, Chollet’s story is a complex reminder of the different battles fought during that era. But it looks like Leroy chose to fully assimilate as a white person so maybe that’s why the NBA doesn’t fuck With him.
