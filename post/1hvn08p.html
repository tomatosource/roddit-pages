Randomness doesn’t explain all: is age a factor?

Following our recent losses I read many comments along the lines of “Water finds its level”, “This won’t happen again”… these are sound arguments if we talk about a random process, but if systemic factors are in play they do not hold.

My take is this is the case and we are starting to show our age. We used to be the team with wings able to switch 1-5 and exert our superior athleticism. We are no more. We added a ton of skills and experience, we are overall better and we won a ring (hallelujah) but we now are at a disadvantage in terms of physical aggression.

The eye test (not sufficient but necessary) is that we have been a step behind our opponents in energy and speed. To find some more evidence I checked connections between average age and our wins/losses.

First of all, we are the fourth older team in the League considering usage (data from https://nbaage.com/), at 29.2 years.

So far we met 7 teams at or below 25 years (including the Thunder that are a whiff over 25): we swept the Wizards (who doesn’t?), we beat the Rockets (that’s kind of against my point, but let’s note they play quite slow), then we lost with all the other 5: Hawks, Magic, Grizzlies, Bulls and Thunder. We actually split with Hawks and Bulls but they are at 0.500 or below and they made with us more than expected.

The other losses came twice from Indiana (27.1 years old) that plays very athletic, Cleveland (they are just good), Golden State and the Sixers (I count that as a dud). Apart from the Sixers, those other teams are in the top 10 of the League in pace. Actually we had 7 of our 10 losses against teams that are top-10 in pace, and the Thunders are in the eleventh place.

If age is our weakness, though, the silver lining is that it won’t hurt that much in the playoffs, where the players rest more between games and experience can be a deciding factor.

Let’s raise banner 19!
