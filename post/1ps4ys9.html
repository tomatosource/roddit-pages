The Guy Who Was Always First In Line

Since it’s the offseason, I thought I’d share an old story with you guys.

I wrote a blog post the other day about a man named Charles Kierst.

You’ve probably never heard of him. I had never heard of him until I stumbled across his story the other day.

Charles Kierst was a firefighter in Auburn, New York. He was also a baseball fan. And he had this amazing ability to be the first in line.

He had his name printed in newspapers around the United States back in 1921 for being the first person in line for tickets to the first game of the World Series. According to The Geneva Daily Times, Kierst “camped under the elevated structure at the Eight Avenue entrance to the Polo Grounds” at 10 AM the day before tickets went on sale, and remained there.

You see, back in the old days newspapers would do stories on fans who would camp out for tickets before major sporting events. It’s actually a long tradition. You can find stories like this going back to the 1890s if you look hard enough.

Anyway, Kierst had his 10 minutes of fame, and that was that.

But then he came back.

For reasons I don’t quite understand, Kierst decided to start reliving his youth. He was first in line to buy a bleacher seat at Yankee Stadium before the Yankees’ first home game for the 1950 World Series. Kierst was 59 years old.

Kierst did the same thing in 1951 - also arriving first. This time he got his picture in the newspaper. However, he told The Syracuse Herald-Journal that he wasn’t going to show up for World Series baseball games anymore.

It seemed that he kept his word in 1952, since his name didn’t appear in any of the newspapers.

But he was back to his antics in 1953. And again in 1954 - though there was a bit of a scandal. Somebody else was first in line in 1954. But then the police outside the Polo Grounds ordered everybody to temporarily disperse for some reason. When they reconvened, Kierst was back to his customary place at the head of the line.

Kierst had a few belongings stolen in 1955 when he asked a stranger to save his place in line while he got something to eat at a nearby cafe. That’s how I first learned about his story: I was researching the 1955 World Series when I saw an odd story about two line campers who were robbed by some random stranger.

The robbery didn’t deter Kierst, however. He did the same thing before the first games held in New York in the World Series in 1956, 1957, 1958, 1960, 1961, 1962, and, finally, 1964.

I think Kierst gave up after that. I couldn’t find any mention of him when the New York Mets went to the 1969 World Series. And he was 83 years old when the Yankees finally returned to the World Series in 1976 - probably a little too old to camp outside the stadium to wait to buy tickets.

Kierst passed away at the age of 87 in January 1980. Unfortunately, his obituary in The Syracuse Herald-Journal says nothing about his uncanny ability to purchase tickets to postseason baseball games.

And it really is a shame. I’m sure he was a fine firefighter. But his streak of being first in line had actually gained him some national recognition in the late 1950s and early 1960s. Though I haven’t found any footage, he was apparently on television shows and radio shows from time to time, all because of his knack at being first in line.

There was this article at syracuse.com about 12 years ago that mentioned Kierst’s feat. However, there are a number of inaccuracies. For example, the author claims that Kierst died while watching the Yankees come from behind in the 9th inning of an exciting game. Unfortunately, the truth is that Kierst passed away in January 1980, during baseball’s offseason. That story was nice, but it’s clearly apocryphal.

Anyway, if you’re interested, I wrote a pretty long blog post about the event, complete with original newspaper scans. You can check it out here.

Not everything I come across or write up is quite as interesting as Kierst’s story. But I do come across some really fascinating people every once in a while…
