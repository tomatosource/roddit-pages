Munetaka Murakami on the Kumamoto earthquake: “I went through an earthquake myself, and it was honestly one of the scariest experiences of my life. So when I heard something like that had happened again, I was really worried.“

White Sox 2, Yankees 3 (July 28, Chicago — Yuki Akao)

White Sox infielder Munetaka Murakami (26) started at first base, batting second, and went 1-for-4. After the game, he spoke to reporters about the 2026 Kumamoto Earthquake, which struck his hometown of Kumamoto Prefecture at around 4:27 p.m. Japan time on July 28.

“I went through an earthquake myself, and it was honestly one of the scariest experiences of my life. So when I heard something like that had happened again, I was really worried. It had only been about ten years, and things were just starting to recover little by little. So it’s really heartbreaking, or… just incredibly unfortunate.
It’s a natural disaster, so there’s nothing anyone can really do to stop it, but I was just really worried.”

Murakami himself lived through the 2016 Kumamoto Earthquake while he was a second-year student at Kyushu Gakuin High School. After finishing baseball practice, he was riding his bicycle home when the powerful quake struck. His school’s gymnasium became an evacuation shelter, and for a time he was unable to play baseball. For Murakami, who has often said that “baseball is my life,” it was a life-changing experience.

He said he first learned about the latest earthquake through the news, and added that he had been able to confirm that his friends and acquaintances back home were safe.

When asked about if he still thinks about Kumamoto, he replied, “Of course I do. People back home have always supported me and given me strength. Times like this make me want to do whatever I can to give something back, even if it’s only a little.”

Murakami shared those thoughts after another solid game. He went 1-for-4, including a double down the right-field line off Gerrit Cole, the 2023 Cy Young Award winner, who has also led the league once in wins and twice each in ERA and strikeouts. The hit extended Murakami’s hitting streak to five games and his on-base streak to 14 games

As he continues to battle every day in a foreign country, Murakami hopes to bring a little encouragement to the people of his hometown who have been affected by the disaster.
