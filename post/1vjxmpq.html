Some interesting opinions from Chinese NBA fans (Part 2)

Continuing from my last post:

Kobe vs. LeBron: the legacy debate
By far the most heated NBA legacy debate in China is Kobe vs. LeBron. There are plenty of LeBron fans in China, but in Chinese online NBA communities, the discussion overwhelmingly tends to favor Kobe.
Beyond the arguments you commonly hear from American NBA fans, there are several points that are particularly prominent in Chinese fan rhetoric:

1. “5 &gt; 4” — championship rings as the core player
One common argument is that LeBron is inferior to Kobe because he has one fewer championship as the “core player” of a championship team. You will often see the argument simplified to “5 &gt; 4.”
Chinese fans sometimes distinguish between simply being on a championship roster and being regarded as the central player who led the team to the title.

2. “一人一城” — one man, one city
Kobe’s loyalty to the Lakers is a huge part of his legacy in China.
There is a very popular Chinese phrase, “一人一城” (yī rén yī chéng), literally meaning “one man, one city.” It is used to praise players who spend their entire careers with one franchise.
For many Chinese fans, “one man, one city” isn’t merely a nice bonus — it is considered an important component of all-time greatness. Kobe’s entire career with the Lakers therefore carries enormous symbolic value.
LeBron, by comparison, is often criticized for changing teams multiple times.

3. “总决赛胜率” — Finals winning percentage
Another argument you see constantly is “总决赛胜率”, literally “Finals winning percentage.”
The argument goes something like this: once you have already reached the Finals, how often do you actually win?
LeBron’s 4–6 Finals record is therefore frequently brought up by Kobe fans as evidence against his all-time ranking, especially when compared with other players regarded as all-time greats.
Whether this is a good way to evaluate a player’s career is obviously debatable, but the metric is extremely popular in Chinese NBA discussions.

4. The “dynasty” argument
Even if LeBron eventually matches or surpasses Kobe’s championship total, some Chinese fans would still rank Kobe higher because Kobe was part of a dynasty, while LeBron is generally not credited with building a dynasty of his own.
“Dynasty” (王朝) is a particularly highly valued concept among Chinese NBA fans.
Interestingly, Chinese fans sometimes treat it almost as a separate achievement category from individual awards. You will even see people contrast the MVP, which they may dismiss as a “media award,” with a dynasty, which they view as something more tangible and historically significant.
And this is where the perceived authority of the NBA itself becomes important…

5. “The NBA officially recognizes Kobe as second only to Jordan”
This is probably one of the more unusual arguments you’ll encounter.
There is a belief among some Chinese fans that Kobe is the only player besides Jordan to have an individual permanent exhibition/showroom at the Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame museum(名人堂独立展厅）, and this is sometimes cited as evidence that the basketball establishment itself effectively places Kobe second only to Jordan.
Whether the specific interpretation of the exhibition is accurate is another question, but the important point is the rhetoric: “Even the official basketball institutions recognize Kobe.”
Chinese NBA fans tend to place considerable weight on what they perceive as official NBA/Hall of Fame recognition.

6. The retirement tour
Kobe’s 2015–16 retirement season is also frequently used as evidence of his popularity and respect among NBA fans.
The Chinese term “退役巡演” (retirement tour) is often associated with Kobe’s farewell season. The argument is that the widespread tributes he received around the league demonstrated how much respect he had from fans of teams other than the Lakers.
This is sometimes contrasted with LeBron, whose relationship with opposing fanbases is perceived as much more polarizing.

7. “Who is your daddy?”
And finally, there is the whole “Who is your daddy?” meme.
After Denver beat the Lakers, Nuggets fans famously taunted LeBron with variations of “Who is your daddy?” This was widely circulated in Chinese NBA communities and became another piece of evidence used by Kobe fans to argue that LeBron does not command the same level of respect from opposing fans.
There is also a darker layer to the Chinese discussion: some Chinese fans interpret or deliberately reinterpret the “daddy” joke in relation to LeBron’s family background, which can lead to some pretty nasty jokes.
