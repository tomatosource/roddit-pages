Banner 18 for a Celtics family

Every family has their thing they do together. For some it’s fishing or running or fixing up an old car. For us and I’m sure many others here, it’s the Celtics. As far back as I can remember, I’d watch games with my dad. It was our thing. When I was a kid, it was just cool to see these larger than life guys fly through the air like superheroes after he got home from work and I knocked out my homework. I played basketball because of it and my dad was our coach for a few years.

As I became an adult it was the thing we’d have drinks over and catch up. My dad’s business eventually took off and he got season tickets for us that he could give away and write off as client entertainment for the games we didn’t go to and I went to a ton of games from the mid 90s on. We largely weren’t overly good pre-08 but that didn’t matter. I got to hang out with my dad. It was our thing. It was awesome.

I never thought anything could top 2008, and there are many reasons to still put that higher basketball wise with how quickly it came to be for a guy we’d watched fight here for a decade but for me, 2024 is the season I will never forget and it means the world to me for reasons so much bigger than sports.

My dad is older now, battling the kinds of things that pop up when you reach that stage and I fully expect to get “the call” any time now. I now have my own little boy who’s just old enough to get it.

The season tickets are partial now but they are mine, and I’ve been able to take my dad to games like he did for me and try to repay all he did for me. It’s wheel chair seating and a lot less bourbon but those times are everything. My son is able to come and has started to become a fan the same way I did at the same time which really started last year and that specific year ending with a banner put a cap on an experience that, even 0-82 would have meant the world to me.

I didn’t think we’d get to share another title while my dad was still here. I certainly didn’t think I’d get to do it so quickly with my son. I wept openly when Tatum picked up Deuce and yelled, “We did it!” my son passed out on my shoulder and my dad on the phone. It isn’t just sports, it is a big part of how our family bonds and sometimes something unexpected gives you the most magical gift that has nothing to do with what’s on the screen in front of you.

I love this team. No matter what happens next, banner 18 will mean the world to me forever that I could not be more thankful for, for something so much more than winning. Go Celtics.
