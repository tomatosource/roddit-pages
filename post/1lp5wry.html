Brad has a history of being the anti-tank

This roster still has talent. Jaylen Brown, Derrick White, Anfernee Simons, Payton Pritchard… that team can win some games.

Explain to me how it’s different than Kobe Bryant, Lamar Odom, Caron Butler, and Chucky Atkins. That team won 34 games.

On some level this has to look a lot like the Ray Allen, Rashard Lewis, Chris Wilcox, Luke Ridnour Sonics winning 31-35 games or the Paul Pierce, Wall Szczerbiak, Delonte West, Mark Blount Celtics winning 33 games.

Your mileage may vary depending on how you compare Jaylen Brown to Kobe/Ray/Pierce and how you compare White to Odom/Rashard/Wally.

When teams “tank”, it’s generally not the players and coach who are intentionally losing games, though Doc Rivers more or less admitted he was actively tanking games in unison with Ainge in 2007. The goal there was to land a transcendent star. Instead, the lotto balls gifted us a worst-case scenario of the 5th pick in a draft “with 2 stars”. The prize looked to be Yi Jianlian, but in spite of this, top 5 picks hold insane value - so we were able to flip it for Ray Allen and set the stage for a championship run.

But unlike that productive 2007 tank job, typically coaches don’t shift the “winning is everything” mentality for the greater good. That job usually falls to the GM to effectively set the team up to lose with the big picture in mind.

We saw this play out in 2014.

Ainge had already done his job and set the team up to fail by trading away Pierce/KG in the offseason. The net result, in addition to a haul of picks that became the foundation of our next title team, was a 25 win season. The 5th worst record in the league.

The prize for our efforts? The 6th pick. Once again considered a fairly weak draft, we were unsuccessful moving up to select Joel Embiid. Instead, we had to settle for Marcus Smart. Just one pick before eventual all-star Julius Randle. Not a terrible pick by any means as he helped us make multiple ECF runs, won DPOY, and became a key chip that was moved for one of the core players of our future title team. Ironically, one of the greatest players ever, Jokic, went 41st though this is very much the exception. The majority of all-stars/HOF level talent have been taken in the top 6 and those picks continue to hold incredible trade value.

The next year, more of the same was planned with big names like Karl Towns headlining the draft class. Ainge set the team up to tank by dealing away Rajon Rondo and Jeff Green. The roster, made of role players and youth, was often referred to by the media as “not having a single starter-caliber player”. This was a classic GM tank job roster. A starting lineup that featured the likes of Avery Bradley, Marcus Smart, Evan Turner, Brandon Bass and Tyler Zeller. Clearly set up to fail.

And yet Brad Stevens had other plans. Inexplicably, Brad Stevens like a basketball MacGyver, utilized his paper clip and duct tape roster, to go on a bit of a run.

What was funny was that at the time Danny Ainge was even on record admitting he didn’t see the point in making the playoffs and ending up with a weak draft pick if they had no real shot of competing. This was very much a case of a GM that was rooting for losses while a coach was pushing for wins.

The result of Brad’s efforts was a 40 win season, sneaking into the playoffs with the 8th seed, and promptly getting swept by a far superior Cavs team.

The prize = the 16th pick.

What’s interesting about this is that the value of that 16th pick was astronomically lower than picks in the top 5 of that draft. It was reported that Ainge had attempted to move up by offering the 16th pick along with 5 other picks (3 of them 1st rounders) just to try and move up and select Justise Winslow at 9th. Beyond that, there were reports that had they successfully moved up they were also prepared to give up even more picks with the goal of moving into the top 3 to take Jahlil Okafor.

Sometimes the best trades are the ones you don’t make.

But it remains an interesting reflection on value. We recently saw how teams valued our own talent. Allegedly the best offer were received for Derrick White was the 9th pick. Allegedly the best offer we received for Brown was the 5th pick. We saw teams giving up extremely valuable unprotected 1sts just to move up 10 spots. We’ve seen multiple examples over the years of teams utilizing top 5 picks to trade for all-star level talent.

But I wonder if Brad the GM is wired similarly to Brad the coach where he believes attempting to win in spite of the big picture is the best path forward.

Don’t get me wrong, Brad has been very successful taking a championship foundation with multiple trips to the ECF (all-nba superstar Jayson Tatum + Brown + significant assets) and augmenting it with veterans to set the team up for a short title window, but we’ve yet to see Brad face a challenge like the one facing him right now. It will be interesting to see how much he can stomach aiming for the long-term over immediate results. Finishing with the worst record guarantees you a Top 5 pick. If this team is on the path to 35 wins and a mid 1st, tough decisions need to be made about how that outcome moves us closer to competing than getting value for one or more of our key players and aiming for golden draft asset while Tatum is sidelined. Best case there is a situation similar to the Spurs when Robinson was out and they landed Duncan. Worst-case, you land a trade chip (a top 5 pick) that might be more valuable than every player on the roster.
