Improving the NBA Cup

Okay.  I’m prepared for the top four comment categories to be “Why are we still discussing the Cup,” “I have a way to improve the Cup—throw it in the garbage and don’t have an NBA Cup,” “something something human rights violations in the UAE,” and “We are playing the Pistons and Wizards while these other teams are killing themselves to win nothing. Cup Improvement Quest Achieved, Flawless Victory.”  Fair enough.  But if we do want to improve the Cup, I think I have solved it.  

Some other ideas, like “give an auto-playoff spot to the winner,” are problematic.  Not only does it mean that that team wouldn’t have to try for the rest of the season (they’re already in the playoffs), it gives a huge advantage to their first round opponent.  Everyone else gets to play a real playoff team who earned the spot through 82 games, and one team gets to play a would-be lottery team that had one good 7-game stretch over 4 weeks in November and December.

Having the tournament be 8 games instead of 4, to avoid the point-differential tiebreakers from mattering so much, would drag this out for a lot longer and people still probably wouldn’t be any more sold on it than they are now.  

Making the second tie breaker “best record” or “best record from last season” rather than point differential would lead to the best teams almost always advancing, when some of the charm now is the fact that any Cinderella team (like ATL) has a chance.  It would also take away the mad rush for points in the last few minutes, which especially on the last night can be the most exciting part. We remember the Hauser 3 with 1 second left. We remember Billy Donovan’s eyes wide in disbelief as we were intentionally fouling up 34 points last year.

Having all of the teams play their final game at the same time?  There are 5 teams in each group and 15 in each conference.  Impossible as of now to have every team playing at the same time within the same conference. Expansion would solve this, but it doesn’t yet.

But my solution is doable immediately.  Since each team plays their division rivals four times every season, twice at home and twice on the road, the winner of the Cup gets one of those road games as an extra home game in next season’s schedule.  So if we won, the Raptors would give us one of their TOR/BOS home games, and it would be played in Boston rather than Toronto, leading to 3 at home and 1 on the road.  

For the Cup-winning team, that’s 4 extra home games and 4 fewer road games.  For each other division team, it’s only 1 fewer home game, so it doesn’t hurt nearly as much as the Cup winner is helped.  The change is built into the coming year’s schedule when the schedule is designed in the summertime, so it doesn’t create any weird situations with ticket sales or travel schedules or etc., for this season.

In part, this is great because unlike just handing a team a playoff spot, they still have to earn their prize by winning those extra home games.  A team that goes 0-4 in the additional home games squanders their advantage, and it is their own fault.  A team that would have gone 1-3 in those 4 road games, but uses the home cooking to go 3-1 instead might get a significant advantage in their playoff chase.

It also helps fuel division rivalries.  If (say) the Knicks go to the Cup Finals next week, we’d be rooting hard for them to lose, so that we don’t lose a home game to them next year.  It would also give us a reason to watch the Cup final with a real rooting interest.

Lastly, a ton of people benefit, which is the missing ingredient in today’s Cup (where the Celtics have basically nothing to gain but can still unnecessarily tax themselves while grinding out the final rounds of a single-elimination tournament).  We have no good reason to care about this Cup because there are no stakes. This would add stakes. 

The players benefit because they don’t have to go on as many road trips the next season, and can hear their own fans cheering for them in more games (and hopefully can use their home court advantage).  The fans benefit because there are more chances to see their team at home (the season ticket prices might go up or down, but that’s a minor issue).  The owners of the Cup-winning team benefit because they get to sell tickets to more games.  The other owners in the division who are upset at losing a home game?  Good news—they can recoup their losses by just constructing a team that can win the NBA Cup the next season—it only takes 7 games.  We don’t feel badly when those owners aren’t able to sell tickets to playoff games because their team isn’t good enough.  It should be the same here.

The players who will be moved to another team at the trading deadline or in the offseason, who as a result of their success in winning the NBA Cup with one team could now lose a home game to that team because they’re now on a division rival?  They will not be happy.  But as Olivia Wilde says, “you can’t have everything.”
