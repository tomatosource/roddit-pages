Looking back, outside of their two stars, the 2010s OKC Thunder were not particularly well built despite making multiple WCFs.

Sure you had good pieces like Steven Adams, Ibaka, and Andre Roberson backing them up, but Roberson had zero offense to speak of aside from cuts and open layups in the open court while Adams was more of a play finisher and strong screener, you also had Enes Kanter (or Freedom, if you prefer) for a couple years adding some post big spice to a perimeter oriented team, all his glaring weaknesses notwithstanding, Kevin Martin was decent for the one year he was there, but that aside, the Thunder were not, by any means, a deep team.

The roster was built to out grind you on defense while Russ and KD carried the team offensively, with some spacing from whoever the designated shooter was in a given season, so the Big 2 very rarely had a guy they could pass to as a pressure release valve when teams sold out on guarding anyone else but them.

2014 was the first year for the Thunder where you could call them deep, Ibaka upped his scoring, Reggie Jackson had his breakout year, they had Caron Butler (who was shooting over 40% from three on 5 attempts), Jeremy Lamb could shoot as well, and old man DFish even got his shot back, but unfortunately, this was also the year when Russ was still coming back from his injury, so it was more of a KD, Reggie, and Ibaka team, at least for half the year.

And through all of this, you had Big Perk eating up cap space and slowing down a team that thrived on running whenever he took the floor, and the claim that his defense had value diminished with each year he was under contract, eventually being superseded by Adams and Ibaka (and Kanter, I guess).

All of this in mind, it really surprises me that they managed to beat the 67 win Spurs in 2016, since they were arguably less talented on paper than they were just two years ago.
