Using Player Impact Estimate to determine which stars are playoff risers and fallers

Player Impact Estimate aka PIE, is a metric to gauge a player’s all-around contribution to the game. Almost all statistical categories in the box score are involved in the PIE formula. PIE answers what % of the events in a game each player contributed.

PIE has actually been a pretty decent indicator of who wins the MVP. Shai was just 1.1 behind Giannis (who led the league), and every other year since 2016 the leader has been the MVP winner (2021 Embiid only played 51 games) so I think it’s a decent metric that gauges how impactful a player has been during the regular season. But what about during the playoffs?

What I have done here is taken every player who has finished top 5 in MVP voting the last 5 years (and some of my own choices I found interesting to round it at an even 20 players) and broken down their year by year PIE and how that compares to their playoff PIE from that season. The players with the largest plus are playoff “risers” and the players with the largest minus are playoff “droppers.” Obviously we’re only including seasons they played in the playoffs, and I left off seasons with low playoff MPG

These scores simply indicate how players “estimated impact” scores in the playoffs relative to their regular seasons. Even though Embiid has a higher playoff PIE than Anthony Edwards, they are at complete opposite ends of this list, and most fall in between +1.0 and -1.0 which I would consider a normal standard deviation. Most players fall in the negative because playoffs are harder and it’s easier to overperform in the regular season. For this reason the only true clear playoff underperformer is Joel Embiid.
