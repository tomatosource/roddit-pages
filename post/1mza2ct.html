There are No Legitimate Criticisms for Asynchronous Trading

Across Reddit, Twitch chat (lol), and even the PoE forums (lmao), I keep seeing the same tired, illogical pitfalls trotted out as “concerns” about async trading or auctionhouse–style systems. I want to go through the most common ones and explain why none of them hold up.

If you’ve got a concern that isn’t listed here, feel free to drop it in. I’m genuinely open to hearing one valid argument. I just haven’t seen it yet.

“Bots will snipe cheap listings faster than humans.”

This is nothing new, bots already do this today. They scrape the trade site, whisper instantly, and humans can’t realistically compete. Async trading doesn’t create the problem, and it doesn’t make it worse. In the current system, if Human A underprices an item, Bot B whispers first, and Human C whispers second, the seller trades with Bot B. In async trade, the same thing happens. The faster party gets it. The slower human loses either way.

“Price fixers will manipulate the market.”

This is already happening today. They list fake cheap items that never sell, then whisper-snipe undercutters instantly. Async trade removes fake listings since items actually leave the stash when listed. If you see it, it’s real and buyable. This change actually weakens price fixing.

“I could missclick and insta-sell a valuable item for too cheap.”

This is simply not true in practice. Listings in premium stash tabs are not instantaneous. In my experience, they only list after an instance change or a relog. You have time to take the item down and relist it appropriately.

And if you’re upset that you can no longer outsource your price checks to a 1d dump tab; good! The burden of checking the value of your item is on you. You’re wasting other people’s time doing this and it arguably makes you lazy and selfish. The async change preventing this practice is a good thing, not a flaw.

“Async trade devalues items due to lack of friction”

Devaluation isn’t a problem if it’s universal. If common items are worth less, then the currency you earn is also worth more in terms of buying power. If you sell for less, you also buy for less. Your relative purchasing power stays the same. If anything, async trade removes fake inflation caused by inconvenience.

“It’ll let flippers and traders make more money than people that actually play the game.”

This is already happening today. Flipping is not enabled by async trading; it’s enabled by basic supply, demand, and price variance. There’s no evidence that flippers make the game worse, no evidence that it isn’t already supported by GGG’s game design of allowing players to re-trade items multiple times, nor does it “steal” from anyone. If a player sells something underpriced, they still chose a number they were happy with at the time. The flipper just recognized the market value and moved it to someone else willing to pay closer to the fair price. Also, I’d also argue flipping and trading is just as valid of a way to “play the game” than any other form.
