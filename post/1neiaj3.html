The NBA’s standard for conflict-of-interest disclosures appear to be lower than standard white-collar employment contracts

For those of you with corporate office work experience, you’re familiar with that set of forms from HR that’s waiting for you when you join on day 1. This package generally includes forms from the compliance department who want you to disclose things like companies that you hold a controlling interest in, companies that you’ve directly invested into, etc. In many cases, you’ll need to repeat this disclosure process on an annual basis.

This brings me to question why Adam Silver so confidently declared during yesterday’s press conference that he had no prior knowledge of Aspiration’s existence. Does this infer that NBA governers, such as Steve Ballmer and Dennis Wong, have no need to report that they have partial ownership in companies that they’ve pumped \(50m and \)1.99m respectively into? They are technically equity holders in this outside entity. It’s akin to Steve Ballmer not needing to formally disclose that he has ownership in Microsoft.

The same applies at a player level. Players are employees of the NBA. Why should they not be expected to disclose (in confidence) to the NBA’s legal compliance team when they secure compensation outside of the lines of the CBA? It could very well be that this already happens, but it would directly contradict why the NBA is just finding out about Kawhi’s deal with Aspiration. It also brings up a deeper question on why a company going defunct even needs to happen for these types of things to come to light, when they should already be fairly disclosed and assessed by the league office for potential circumvention.

It’s both funny and disturbing to me that the middle class is expected to follow so many rules and go through so many checks and balances just to seek out and maintain employment. What would even happen if someone like us was an client asset manager at Wells Fargo while getting the same client who we formed a personal relationship with to pay us an extra 1-2% in a side deal?
