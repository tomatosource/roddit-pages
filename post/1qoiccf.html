Power rankings for every MLB team ahead of the 1926 season

AMERICAN LEAGUE

Projected Order of Finish

1. New York Yankees

The Yankees once again loom over the American League. The chief reason, of course, is Babe Ruth, restored from last year’s ailments and reportedly in good health. Flanking him is Lou Gehrig, no longer merely a promising understudy but a full-fledged force, capable of driving the ball with alarming regularity. The lineup is deepened by the steady bat of Bob Meusel, the sure hands of Mark Koenig, and the leadership of Wally Schang behind the plate.

On the mound, Herb Pennock, Urban Shocker, and Waite Hoyt provide a trio that inspires confidence nearly every fourth day. There are few obvious weaknesses here, and rival managers privately admit that stopping New York will require both health and fortune.

2. Cleveland Indians

Cleveland enters the season as the Yankees’ most credible challenger. Tris Speaker, though past his best days, remains the smartest center fielder in the league and a steadying influence atop the order. Joe Sewell continues to be one of the most difficult men in baseball to strike out, while George Burns and Glenn Myatt supply balance throughout the lineup.

The pitching staff, anchored by Stan Coveleski and supported by George Uhle, gives the Indians a fighting chance in nearly every series. Cleveland lacks the sheer terror of New York’s middle order, but they play tidy, disciplined baseball and figure to remain in the race deep into the summer.

3. Washington Senators

The reigning American League champions are older, perhaps a touch slower, but still dangerous. Walter Johnson, no longer the flamethrower he once was, remains a master craftsman on the mound, aided by Tom Zacharyand Dutch Ruether. The Senators’ offense is driven by the bat of Goose Goslin, the steady play of Bucky Harris, and the reliability of Sam Rice, whose glove and bat remain invaluable.

Washington’s strength lies in cohesion. They remind observers that championships are not always won by noise and spectacle, but by steadiness and teamwork.

4. Detroit Tigers

Detroit continues to orbit around the incandescent presence of Ty Cobb, still ferocious, still feared, and still capable of deciding a series by himself. Harry Heilmann provides one of the league’s purest swings, while Johnny Bassler and Lu Blue contribute professionalism if not brilliance.

Pitching remains the question. Hooks Dauss and Earl Whitehill must shoulder heavy burdens, and if they falter, even Cobb’s offensive prowess may not be enough. The Tigers look strong enough for contention, but fragile enough to slip.

5. St. Louis Browns

The Browns are no longer resigned to irrelevance, though they remain short on star attractions. George Sisler, battling back toward form, is still the heart of the club when healthy. Ken Williams and Baby Doll Jacobson give the lineup some punch, while Urban Shocker’s departure is still felt deeply.

St. Louis hopes competence and persistence can lift them above the league’s bottom tier. That alone would mark progress.

6. Chicago White Sox

The White Sox continue their long climb back to respectability. Eddie Collins still brings intelligence and grace to the infield, and Ray Schalk remains a steady hand behind the plate. Bib Falk offers offensive promise, but the lineup too often thins out after its better bats.

Pitching from Red Faber provides occasional brilliance, but not enough depth exists to mount a sustained challenge. Chicago looks improved, but not transformed.

7. Philadelphia Athletics

Connie Mack’s young Athletics are clearly being built for tomorrow. Al Simmons shows signs of becoming a major force, and Jimmie Foxx, still rough, hints at extraordinary power. Mickey Cochraneis already proving his worth as a field general.

Yet youth brings inconsistency, and the pitching staff remains uneven. The A’s will surprise on occasion, but patience is the watchword.

8. Boston Red Sox

Boston remains in hard times. Harry Hooper is gone, the pitching staff lacks teeth, and though players like Carl Reynolds and Bubbles Hargrave try gamely, the roster struggles to compete nightly. Until arms and bats arrive together, the Red Sox appear bound once more for the cellar.

NATIONAL LEAGUE

Projected Order of Finish

1. Pittsburgh Pirates

The 1925 World Series Winning Pittsburgh Pirates stands as the most complete club in the senior circuit. Paul Waner is already emerging as a hitter of rare quality, complemented beautifully by his brother Lloyd Waner. Pie Traynor anchors the infield with skill and calm, while Max Carey provides speed and intelligence.

The pitching staff, led by Ray Kremer and Vic Aldridge, is deep and adaptable. The Pirates do not overwhelm so much as they suffocate, winning by doing everything well. They are deserved favorites.

2. New York Giants

Never count out a John McGraw club. Rogers Hornsby, though now wearing New York colors, remains one of the game’s greatest bats. Frankie Frisch supplies speed and fire, while George Kellysteadies first base.

Pitching from Art Nehf and Virgil Barnes keeps the Giants competitive, and McGraw’s tactical daring can still steal games others might lose. The Giants may not dominate, but they linger menacingly.

3. Cincinnati Reds

The Reds are a well-rounded and often underestimated club. Eppa Rixey anchors the pitching staff with consistency, while Pete Donohue and Dolf Luque add reliability. Offensively, Edd Roush, Heinie Groh, and Jake Daubert supply balance and experience.

Cincinnati lacks headline glamour but possesses the tools to hang near the top if things break favorably.

4. Brooklyn Robins

Brooklyn’s fortunes rest on sound pitching and steady hands. Dazzy Vance remains one of the league’s most dominant arms, capable of single-handedly turning a series. Zack Wheat continues to be a dependable bat, while Wilbert Robinson’s guidance keeps the club focused.

Run production may be a challenge, but Brooklyn is stubborn and difficult to sweep.

5. Chicago Cubs

The Cubs remain enigmatic. Hack Wilson brings power, Kiki Cuyler speed, and Charlie Grimm steadiness. On the mound, Grover Cleveland Alexander still shows flashes of greatness, though time waits for no man.

If the pieces align, Chicago could climb; if not, they risk stagnation.

6. St. Louis Cardinals

The Cardinals are young, fast, and uneven. Rogers Hornsby’s absence is still felt, but players like Jim Bottomley and Chick Hafey offer hope. Jesse Haines provides a reliable arm, though support is thin.

Observers sense a strong future here, but the present remains unsettled.

7. Philadelphia Phillies

Philadelphia can hit, but often cannot stop opponents from doing the same. Cy Williams remains a formidable bat, and Fresco Thompson adds support, yet the pitching staff struggles to protect leads.

The Phillies will entertain, if not contend.

8. Boston Braves

The Braves face a difficult campaign. Rabbit Maranville still dazzles with his glove, but the roster lacks depth and punch. Pitching inconsistencies and a thin lineup suggest a long summer ahead.
