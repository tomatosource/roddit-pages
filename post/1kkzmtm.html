The Pacers Have Been to the Fifth Most Conference Finals Since 1994

The Pacers are seldom discussed as a model franchise and staple among the league’s elite, but making it to the conference finals isn’t easy. And they have done it consistently across eras.

They went to the conference finals fours times in the 90s, including three heartbreaking game 7 losses in 94, 95 and 98. They made it to the finals in 2000 in Reggie’s last great year, only to run into the beginning of the Kobe/Shaq dynasty.

They made it back four years later, only for Tayshaun Prince to swing the series with one of the biggest blocks in league history. They looked like the best team in the East the next season before the Malice at the Palace eliminated that entire nucleus (along with Jermaine O’Neal’s declining health).

They started over and made it back in 2013 and 2014, including taking the 2013 Heatles to seven games (and they should’ve stolen Game 1).

Then Paul George destroys his leg in the Olympics and they start over again, before surprisingly getting back to the conference finals in 2024, playing in a “competitive” sweep.

Now they’re up 3-1 against the Cavs and look as good as anyone in the East.

If they make it back this season - that will make 10 conference finals in 32 seasons. Yes, that means the Indiana Pacers were among the last four teams standing in over 31% of seasons during that period. They would have made it multiple times in every decade since the 90s, which is something the Lakers, Heat and Celtics have not done (and the Spurs have not done yet in the 2020s).

For context, the Spurs made it to 11 during the same period and the Heat/Lakers/Celtics all ten. Meaning, the Pacers will tie the Lakers and Heat for conference finals appearances over that period. And tie the Celtics if the Knicks win the series.

For greater context, if the Pacers make it, they will double the appearances by the Rockets (5), OKC/Seattle (5), and Mavs (5), They will have four more than the Pistons (6). They will have as many as the Warriors (6) and Suns (4) combined. They will make more than the Jazz (4), Bulls (4) and 76ers (1) combined. They will make more than the Knicks (3), Blazers (3) and Bucks (3) combined.

Sure, the East has had period of extreme weakness, they’ve only been to 1 finals (and lost), they have not had a Top-25 (or arguably, top 50) player, and their owner is a notorious cheapskate who has always refused to pay the tax, but this level of ongoing excellence is impressive.
