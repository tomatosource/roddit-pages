A reason to hope for Shaedon Sharpe to Boston.

Shaedon Sharpe receives a fair share of criticism, much of it valid, regarding his defensive inconsistencies and scoring efficiency. That said, some may be selling him short too soon. Standing 6’6” with a 7-foot wingspan, Sharpe possesses an athletic profile in the 99th percentile of all NBA players.

He is an elite finisher at the rim, having a record breaking vertical and elite body control. He also has developing mid-range game, excels in the pick-and-roll, and carries an outside shot hovering around 34%. And he just turned 23 years old.
The most glaring issue has been a lingering injury history over his first four seasons. The silver lining is that there has been no indication of degenerative issues; his recent setbacks can largely be chalked up to mechanics and overcompensating stress related issues.

This season, he averaged 20 points per 29 minutes. Sharpe is already a quality sixth man and Portlands style of play doesn’t do any favors for his game. If anything, his play aligns much more with the Celtics, rather, a superstar like Tatum. Running the offense through Tatum allows for creativity and spacing.
If the Celtics organization goes all-in to support his development, it is not unreasonable to expect rapid growth and results under the insulated structure the Celtics provide.

The untapped talent and skill are right there for Shaedon Sharpe. It’s a suboptimal indicator to evaluate a player using highlights but for sake of seeing the flashes of brilliance, if you haven’t, watch some of his highlights.
The Celtics lack explosive athleticism, and while it may not be strictly necessary to win, it can unquestionably serve as a great equalizer.

If nothing else, Shaedon Sharpe has the makings of an All NBA Guard; patience and a bit further development are the key.

Hopefully, Sharpe is part of whatever trade the Celtics inevitably make.
