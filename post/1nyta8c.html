[Washington Post] The Clippers are the worst. Last time we checked, there’s only one team in that city named the “Dodgers,” but the little brother Clippers should take on that moniker, after the way they’ve ducked and swerved out of questions. Clippers fans should aspire to root for another team.

Source: https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/2025/10/04/kawhi-leonard-clippers-villains/

Hate is a strong word. And yet it’s not strong enough to fully capture the feelings around the 2025-26 Los Angeles Clippers.

They are the Brussels sprouts of basketball. And not the roasted kind, glazed with sweetness that you learn to endure as an adult. But the raw variety, just dense and nasty, that are supposed to provide fiber or whatever, but are plopped on your dinner plate and become part of your childhood trauma. These Clippers are worse than force-fed vegetables.

Better yet, they are like the Tesla Cybertruck of the NBA, basically an oversize trash can but uglier, that only the misinformed and the try-hards of society would actually think is the way of the future. No one looks cool in that car; rather, the only reason people stop and stare is because they’re pitying the poor idiot who’s chosen to go into debt by leasing the equivalent of a toaster on wheels. This season, anyone who willingly walks around in a Clippers jersey, much like a Cybertruck driver, will be declaring their soulless ambitions. These Clippers are worse than Elon Musk’s rage magnet.

If fans need something to galvanize them for the upcoming NBA season, it should be this: the 2025-26 Los Angeles Clippers are the most hated team in the league.

From Bradley Beal (once the possessor of the worst contract in basketball), to James Harden (shameless stat chaser who’s now vaguely interested in winning), to Chris Paul (just plain despised any year), to Kawhi Leonard (aspiring to never actually work) and owner Steve Ballmer (accused of aspiring to pay his aspirants boatloads of money to never actually work), this team is bloated with contempt.

These particular Clippers, no matter where they play this season, should be an object of scorn. Like five Christian Laettners running up and down the court. As annoying as the guy in line having a loud conversation over FaceTime — without his earbuds. As repulsive as the family member with a visible cold sore, intent on greeting everyone at Christmas dinner with a kiss. But cold sores and cruciferous vegetables or even Cyber-monstrosities would be more appealing than rooting for that other team in L.A.

Because for however long it takes the NBA’s investigation, the Clippers will be chasing a ring while under a cloud of suspicion.

Did Ballmer provide millions to the now-defunct environmental company, Aspiration, to circumvent the salary cap? And was Leonard’s sponsorship with the company merely a no-show deal so that he could receive at least \(28 million more from the Clippers, in addition to the four-year, \)176 million extension he signed in 2021? Those questions need to be answered. Because we’re not getting much insight from the answers coming out of the Clippers.

Last time we checked, there’s only one team in that city named the “Dodgers,” but the little brother Clippers should take on that moniker, after the way they’ve ducked and swerved out of the way of direct questions. Last month, Ballmer boldly denied any circumvention of the salary cap and gave lengthy responses during an interview with ESPN reporter Ramona Shelburne. However, when Shelburne asked Ballmer if Leonard’s uncle Dennis Robertson, who serves as one of his representatives, had asked for illegal benefits since 2019, Ballmer turned succinct, and repetitive.

“They know the rules … we know the rules, and if anything’s not clear we remind ourselves what the rules are,” Ballmer said. “And we make absolutely clear we’re going to abide by those rules.”

Even Neo didn’t dodge all those bullets better than Ballmer dodged that one. Nowhere in that rule-abiding response did Ballmer answer yes or no if Leonard and his team had asked for impermissible gifts. The same fumble happened earlier this week during team president Lawrence Frank’s response to a similarly direct question from Shelburne at the team’s media day.

“Yeah, look, Dennis knows the rules. Kawhi knows the rules, Mitch Frankel [Leonard’s agent] knows the rules, and we know the rules,” Frank said, even though he had weeks to come up with a much better answer.

For his turn during media day, at least Leonard feigned confusion. It wasn’t better than parroting the company line “rules rules rules!” but at least it provided greater levels of absurdity for entertainment purposes.

“When you say ‘understanding’ what do you mean?” Leonard said in response to Shelburne’s question on what did he understand about the endorsement deal.

When Shelburne repeated the question, Leonard did give an inch and confirm that yes, he understood the full contract and the services required, but then he attempted the classic deflection of criticizing the media.

“Like I said, I don’t deal with the conspiracies or clickbait analyst or journalism going on. So, that’s what it is,” Leonard offered.

Urghh. Clippers fans should aspire to root for another team.

It’s one thing to want a nice ending for Buyout Beal — whose greatest days came when he was the likable star in Washington, before he signed that onerous max contract with the no-trade clause. We all have soft spots for those in sports who’ve done nothing morally wrong but have taken a beating in the public eye anyway. Yet even so, no self-respecting fan of the NBA, or just human being for that matter, should want this team to succeed.

Not even Clipper Darrell. He should burn that half-blue and half-red suit, and join the Lakers’ bandwagon. They might be ruled by a 40-year-old who still thinks teams should bow to his every whim, but at least the Lakers aren’t receiving aid from an authoritarian regime.

This year, the Clippers will wear “Visit Rwanda” patches on their jersey, helping the East African country sportswash its human rights violations. The country also has a sponsorship deal with the Los Angeles Rams, and several European football clubs, as a way to promote tourism inside its borders. However, after caving under pressure from fans, Bayern Munich ended its marketing deal with Rwanda. According to Amnesty International, there have been reports of torture, enforced disappearances and concerns about the rights allowed to women, refugees and the political enemies of the country’s four-term president, Paul Kagame.

Rwanda might be one of the poorest countries in the world, yet Kagame okayed the construction of a $104 million arena to host a team in the NBA-affiliated Basketball Africa League. It’s way past problematic for the NBA to be as chummy as it is with Rwanda, and yet the Clippers have taken it a step further by diving into the deep pockets of that government. Maybe Uncle Dennis needs a new Gulfstream?

Or maybe Billy Crystal, Anthony Anderson and any living die-hard Clippers fan should take up hockey this winter. Anything instead of spending money for tickets and desiring good things to happen to this roster. Even the aura around Ty Lue — one of the friendliest coaches in the NBA, who remains great at his job — can’t save this team. They are the worst. Yet they’re cast as the perfect villains.

Lovers of all that’s pure and beautiful with basketball, meet the 2025-26 Clippers. They deserve your hate.
