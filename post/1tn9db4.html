All-NBA Eligibility Confusion. Let’s clarify.

Many people are saying Jaylen got robbed of being First Team All-NBA. The argument is that both Luka Doncic and Cade Cunningham were selected even though they do not meet the 65-game eglibility threshold.

Other people respond that Luka and Cade got an exception, and since the exceptions are defined in the rulebook, then they qualify fair and square, and JB wasn’t robbed.

But there’s a problem with the second argument. Yes, the eligibility rules define exceptions to the 65-game requirement. But, even under the exceptions, Luka and Cade still do not qualify.

The CBA defines two possible criteria for award eligibility. The first is the obvious one:

(1) the player played in at least sixty-five (65) Regular Season games;

The second is where the exceptions are defined:

(2) the player (A) played in at least sixty-two (62) Regular Season games, Article XXIX 433 (B) suffered a “season-ending injury” (as defined below), and © played in at least eighty-five percent (85%) of the Regular Season games played by his Team prior to the player suffering such injury.

Note that A,B,C must all apply in order to meet the eligibility for this exception. Meaning: the player needs to suffer a “season-ending injury” to qualify for exception. What does a season-ending injury mean?

The CBA defines it as:

A “season-ending injury” is an injury that, in the opinion of a physician jointly selected by the NBA and Players Association, makes it substantially more likely than not that the player would be unable to play through the May 31 following the date of such injury;

Luka did not have a season-ending injury in 2025-2026. He missed his 65th game to attend a personal event.

Even Cade’s collapsed lung only sidelined him for 11 games and did not prevent him from playing through the rest of the season and into the playoffs.

So, Luka and Cade do not qualify for the awards, even after taking into account the exceptions defined in the rulebook.

So how come they got the award anyway?

The NBA and the players’ association agreed to ignore the rules and make an ad-hoc exception just for them:

“The NBA and NBPA agreed that, taking into account the totality of the circumstances for Cunningham and Doncic, each player qualified for awards.”

So, was Jaylen robbed?

He qualified fair and square. And two players were allowed in despite not meeting the pre-defined eligibility OR the pre-defined exceptions.
