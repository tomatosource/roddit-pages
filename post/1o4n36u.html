13 past teams have walked off a winner-takes-all game to advance to the next round of the playoffs. Only one has gone on to win the World Series.

Interestingly, the one team to win the World Series after such a walkoff win, the 2001 Arizona Diamondbacks in the NLDS, are themselves one of just 6 teams to walk off a winner-takes-all game to win the World Series.

1951 New York Giants - Bobby Thomson hits the “Shot Heard ‘Round the World” to walk off a 3-game pennant tiebreak series versus the Brooklyn Dodgers; the Giants ultimately fall to the Yankees in 6 games in the World Series.

1972 Cincinnati Reds - After a game-tying Johnny Bench solo homer in Game 5 of the NLCS, the Pirates’ Bob Moose throws a wild pitch to Hal McRae that scores George Foster from 3rd to win the Reds the pennant; the Reds ultimately fall to the A’s in Game 7 of the World Series.

1976 New York Yankees - After the Royals rallied to tie the game in the top of the 8th, Chris Chambliss hits a walk-off solo homer in Game 5 of the ALCS to win the pennant; the Yankees are ultimately swept by the Reds in the World Series.

1992 Atlanta Braves - After shutting out the Braves for 8 innings in Game 7 of the NLCS, the Pirates’ Doug Drabek loads the bases for Atlanta. Stan Belinda earns two outs, but Francisco Cabrera hits a single to score the game-tying and game-winning runs; the Braves ultimately fall to the Blue Jays in 6 games in the World Series.

1995 Seattle Mariners - Edgar Martínez hits “The Double” while trailing in the bottom of the 11th of Game 5 of the ALDS to complete the improbable series comeback against the Yankees in the Mariners’ first ever postseason appearance; the Mariners ultimately fall to the Indians in 6 games in the ALCS.

THE EXCEPTION: 2001 Arizona Diamondbacks - After alternating wins and losses against the Cardinals in the NLDS, they walk it off in Game 5 with a RBI single from Tony Womack. The Diamondbacks dispatch the Braves in 5 games in the NLCS and, despite trailing in the 9th inning of Game 7, ultimately win their first championship against the Yankees with a walk-off single from Luis Gonzalez.

2003 New York Yankees - In the ALCS, the Yankees meet the Red Sox in the postseason for just the second time. Boston forces a Game 7, which goes to extra innings after the Yankees recover from a 4-0 deficit, and which is ended in the 11th with a solo shot from Aaron Boone; the Yankees ultimately fall to the Marlins in 6 games in the World Series.

2007 Colorado Rockies - The Rockies catch fire in September, winning 13 of their last 14 scheduled games to force a one-game tiebreaker with the Padres. The game goes 13 innings, and though the Padres score 2 runs in the top of the 13th, Trevor Hoffman blows the save and Jamey Carroll hits the walk-off sac fly despite a controversial play at the plate; the Rockies sweep the Phillies and Diamondbacks to win the pennant, but are ultimately swept by the Red Sox in the World Series.

2009 Minnesota Twins - The Twins likewise get hot down the stretch, but a series loss to the Tigers at the very end of the season forces a tiebreaker for the division between the two teams. After threatening to do so in the 10th, the Twins finally walk it off in the 12th inning on a single from Alexi Casilla; the Twins are ultimately swept by the Yankees in the ALDS.

2011 Milwaukee Brewers - The Brewers go up 2-0 in the first 2 games of the NLDS, but the Diamondbacks score 18 runs in their next two games to force a decisive Game 5. The Diamondbacks tie it in the 9th inning of a low-scoring game, but a Nyjer Morgan single in the bottom of the 10th finishes them off; the Brewers ultimately fall to the Cardinals in 6 games in the NLCS.

2014 Kansas City Royals - In the wild card game vs. the A’s, the Royals come back from a 7-3 deficit to force extra innings in the 9th and, after allowing a run in the top of the 12th, rally to win it on a Christian Colon infield single and a Salvador Pérez walk-off base hit; the Royals sweep the Angels and the Orioles to win the pennant, but ultimately fall to the Giants in Game 7 of the World Series.

2016 Toronto Blue Jays - In another close wild card game, the Blue Jays force extra innings by shutting out the Orioles for 7 straight. Edwin Encarnación eventually hits the dramatic 3-run walk-off homer off not-Zach Britton; the Blue Jays sweep the Rangers in the ALDS, but ultimately fall to the Indians in 5 games in the ALCS.

2021 Los Angeles Dodgers - The winningest wild card team in MLB history play a tense pitchers’ duel versus the Cardinals in the final one-game NL wild card round, and finally pull through with a 2-run walk-off homer from Chris Taylor; the Dodgers beat the Giants in the NLDS, but ultimately fall to the Braves in 6 games in the NLCS.
