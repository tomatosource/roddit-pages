Ranking every team’s offseason by blended WAR. The Orioles led baseball at +4.7, the Yankees had the worst offseason in the AL East at -1.4, and Baltimore’s projection gap is the biggest in the league.

Apparently they pulled every roster-changing transaction from this offseason and ran each player through two frameworks. The first is historical WAR: an average of their FanGraphs fWAR over the last three seasons (2023-25). The second is Marcel projected WAR, which is Tom Tango’s forecasting method that weights recent seasons (5/4/3), regresses toward the mean, and applies an age curve that boosts players under 28 and penalizes players over 28.

The Orioles led all of baseball at +4.7 blended WAR.

The raw historical number is massive. +9.3 net WAR looks like one of the best offseasons in recent memory. But the projections tell a very different story. Alonso drops from 2.8 to 0.7. Bassitt goes from 2.4 to 0.2. Eflin from 2.6 to 0.1. That gap between the two lenses is the widest of any team in the league.

The reason is straightforward: every major Baltimore addition is on the wrong side of 30. Bassitt is entering his age-37 season. Ward, Alonso, and Eflin are all 32-33. The Marcel age curve hammers all of them, and regression pulls their forecasts toward replacement level based on typical career arcs for players their age.

That doesn’t mean the projections are necessarily right. Marcel is designed to be conservative and plenty of 32-year-olds outperform it. But it does mean Baltimore’s upside depends on veteran performance holding steady rather than young player development, which is a different bet than this organization has made in recent years.
