Ishbia on the Suns going younger

Eddie Johnson:

What was the mindset behind going younger and building that way?

Mat Ishbia:

When I bought the team, I felt like if I could come in and provide resources financially to let them go over the salary cap, luxury tax and go get these type of players, and then we go hire a championship coach, which we tried with Frank and Mike Budenholzer. And it didn’t work.

I decided that’s not how I wanna do it. We’re gonna go in a different way. We’re gonna go and build it the right way, from the beginning, and it might not happen in a year. It might take a couple years, but we also have a franchise player in Devin Booker, so we have a head start on most.

We’ve been able to put a lot of young players around him; Jalen Green, Mark Williams, Maluach, Rasheer Flemming, and Ryan Dunn.

And then we got a coach [in Jordan Ott] that’s looking forward and not looking back. Not looking at what worked in 2020, but what’s gonna work in 2030.

Ishbia on Devin Booker’s future

Justin Termine:

How confident are you that Devin Booker is going to be with the organization longterm and how much pressure do you feel to make sure that you win to keep him happy so he doesn’t ask out?

Mat Ishbia:

I don’t feel any pressure to win to keep Devin happy. Devin’s gonna be happy because we’re going to try to win. We’re gonna do things to be aligned with the vision and identity that he agrees with 1000%, and I agree with 1000%.

I don’t need any external pressure to try to win; We want to win every single day. We want to get better every single day. But the expectations have changed. When you have Kevin, Devin and Brad, people think championship or bust. And unfortunately, we busted 2 years in a row.

Last year, it was not as fun as a lot of people would have liked. It was not as entertaining, it was not energetic. We might not win every game this season, but we’re gonna play hard and compete.
