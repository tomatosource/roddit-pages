Ever since the 2017 Western Conference finals, when Golden State’s Zaza Pachulia slid under the ankles of a jump-shooting Kawhi Leonard and changed so much about the trajectory of Spurs history, the NBA has been hyper cognizant of close-out fouls.

Under NBA rules, shooters are entitled to a safe landing after releasing a jump shot. Defenders who invade an opponent’s landing space are almost automatically whistled for a foul.

If the close-out is deemed reckless, a flagrant foul can be assessed – even if the contact is minimal and unintentional. It is a rule aimed at protecting shooters that those shooters have since learned to bend even further to their advantage.

As much as the closeout rule has been a boon to shooters, it has been a bane to defenders. It is the only foul in the NBA rulebook that can be called without contact occurring.
