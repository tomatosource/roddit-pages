[OC]: These NBA players were ahead of their time: Part two

Around a week ago, I posted the first part of a series where I looked at players from past eras whose games I felt would translate today. This got a lot of good feedback/responses, and after going off a list of names I had in mind/taking some suggestions from the first post, had a few more players I found interesting. Since I’ll include six players here, the writeups will be a bit shorter.

Grant Hill: In the current NBA, we see a lot of bigger guards or wings as both the primary scorers in facilitators for their teams. Hill fit that mold. In his six years with the Pistons from ages 22-27, he averaged just under 22 points a game, 8 rebounds and six assists night while winning Rookie of the Year, and making 5 All-Star games, and being named to 5 All-NBA teams. Hill’s prime unfortunately was cut short as he dealt with an array of injuries in Orlando. Though he became a valuable role player in Phoenix, where he emerged as a threat on corner threes, and gained a reputation as a defender in his mid to late 30s. Given that he showed some growth as a three point shooter later in his career, to go along with the other skills he possessed and added spacing, I think he’d thrive today.

Lamar Odom: At 6’10”, Odom was ahead of his time with the versatility he offered as a player. With a reported 7’4” wingspan, he had the size, movement and length to defend an array of positions. Though also had the ability to score and create for others in a variety of ways. Lamar played some of his best basketball as a 6th man for the Lakers as in that time they won two straight championships, and he won 6th Man of The Year in 2011. Teams will always covet players who can do a wide array of things on both ends of the floor, and can impact winning. Odom happened to have stretches where he did that at a championship level.

Peja Stojakovic: Before doing research for this, I didn’t know Peja was 6’10. Though that blend of high end shooting and size would be highly coveted today. Peja wasn’t only just a three-point shooter offensively, but in today’s game I’d imagine he’s more of a stretch four, and while he never exceeded 7 three point attempts per game during his career, would imagine he’s taking 10 or so a night from every action you could think of now.

Reggie Miller: It’s hard to believe, but Reggie only had one season in his career where he took more than six threes a game. That wouldn’t be the case now, as his blend of shooting and off ball movement would be incredibly valuable. Miller was also incredibly consistent, as he had 15 straight seasons where he ranked among the top 10 in the NBA in true shooting percentage. On top of that, he was also a playoff riser, as his scoring increased in the postseason without a drastic efficiency drop off. In today’s era, he not only has more spacing at his disposal, but is probably taking 10-12 threes a night, and his blend of shooting and moving without the ball will be seen on basketball courts for the foreseeable future.

Mark Price: I knew of Price going into this, but was incredibly impressed once I got to watch more of his game. Price’s blend of shooting from three, to go with scoring and facilitating out of pick and roll in a variety of ways was well ahead of its time. Price through his peak attempted just under 4 threes a game, but would likely be much higher now. He was also the 2nd member of the 50/40/90 club, and his ability to split defenders in pick and roll, shoot off the dribble, and convert runners in the paint is on full display when watching games today.

Drazen Petrovic: Unfortunately, we only saw two years of Drazen in his peak at the NBA level as he was killed in a car accident at 28. Though in his two years as a full time starter with the Nets, he averaged over 21 points per game, shot over 44% from three (though on 3 attempts a game), could score in a variety of ways, and showed some facilitating ability as well. Drazen today would be taking 8-10 threes a night, and the added space and off ball movement would make him a very dangerous offensive player.

I’m interested to hear what others think, in terms of these players and also if they have any others they wanted to discuss who they think might fit this mold. I have a list going of guys to potentially discuss, but would always be interested to see what others think, and it could help bring back some nostalgia/celebrate these players. At the least this been a fun way to show the game and trends within it can change overtime, while looking at how different players of different eras can fit among them.
