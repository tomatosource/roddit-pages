If the Oklahoma City Thunder wins in Indianapolis on Thursday, they would be the first team to win a championship via all four iterations of winning a series (win in 4, win 5, win 6, win 7)

This has to be done by a championship team, since they have to win 4 series. This is only applicable for teams from 2003 onwards, as that was the first year the NBA employed the four 7-game series format.

&nbsp;

From 1984-2002, the first round was 5 games. Which is fair, you have to reward every playoff team with at least two home games. Before that, top seed teams only had to play 3 series, think Moses Malone’s famous quote “fo, fo, fo”, which meant they would win every series in four games. He only said it three times. They actually went four-five-four that year.

If you’re going to diminish Bill Russell’s 11 rings, the argument is not that they faced weaker competitions. They faced Wilt Chamberlain. With Hal Greer, Chet Walker, Billy Cunningham. They faced Oscar Robertson. With Wayne Embry, Jerry Lucas, Maurice Stokes. They faced an LA team with Jerry West and Elgin Baylor. The Celtics just had more household names because they kept winning. Think Klay Thompson being more popular than DeMar DeRozan. KC Jones is a hall of famer because he keeps winning but skill-wise he’s the Derek Fisher to Bill Russell’s Kobe Bryant. Anyway, I digress. But if you want to make an argument against Bill Russell’s 11 rings, it’s that he only had to win 2 series in most of them. There were less teams, only 8. Less talents at that time. But those teams were compact. If they did 20 teams at that time, each team would be diluted with talent.
