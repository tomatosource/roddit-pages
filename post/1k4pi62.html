Jonathan Kuminga’s benching is about fit, not talent

The numbers tell a clear story: Kuminga is not a good fit with Jimmy Butler on the floor. The Butler-Kuminga pairing was -20 in 125 minutes during the regular season, by far the worst of any pairing involving Butler:

Lineup Minutes Total +/-
J. Butler III - B. Podziemski 651 +227
J. Butler III - D. Green 742 +154
J. Butler III - Q. Post 358 +85
J. Butler III - M. Moody 622 +82
J. Butler III - S. Curry 529 +80
J. Butler III - G. Santos 144 +66
J. Butler III - B. Hield 347 +47
J. Butler III - K. Looney 176 +29
J. Butler III - G. Payton II 196 +4
J. Butler III - J. Kuminga 125 -20

This matches the eye test – Butler operates best when surrounded by shooters, and adding another non-shooter to Butler-Draymond bench lineups stalls them out offensively. If butler’s playing 40+ minutes, there’s not a lot of room for Kuminga to get playing time.

But what about the steph-only minutes, you ask? While the Kuminga-Steph minutes put up good numbers, the Gui-Steph numbers are much better on a per possession basis:

S. Curry - M. Moody +9.2
S. Curry - J. Butler III +8.3
S. Curry - K. Looney +5.9
S. Curry - G. Payton II +5.6

So Kuminga isn’t even the best PF to pair with Steph in the Steph-only minutes. Anecdotally, Gui seems to be better at navigating the organized chaos of Kerr’s movement offense, which shifts into overdrive whenever Butler is off the floor.

Kuminga is clearly a talented player, but the warriors have pieces that fit better with their two stars.
