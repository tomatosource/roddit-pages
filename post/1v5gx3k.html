[Krawcyznski] After missing out on LeBron James, McDaniels is unlikely to be the starting PF for Minnesota. Finch prefers him at the SF spot and has indicated to Lyles that he may have a “big role” next season: “Opportunity to be a starter after being out for a year. What, am I gonna say no?”

Finch also wants McDaniels at small forward so they can better use the improved shot creation he showed last season when he averaged career highs in points (14.8), 3-point percentage (41.2) and field goal attempts (11.1). With a pass-first player like Ball now at point guard, Finch believes McDaniels’ role in the offense can get even larger.

The coach also discussed adopting a more flexible approach to the starting lineup night in and night out next season, depending on the matchup.

“I see the opportunity to be creative with that, maybe, every night,” Finch said. “Maybe it’s a different person starting at the four based on who we’re playing. Why not?”

Lyles is listed at 6-9 and is the closest to a traditional power forward frame. He can stretch the floor from 3, which is important when sharing the frontcourt with Gobert. When he was discussing his decision on a vlog, Lyles indicated that the team at least mentioned the possibility of earning a big role next season.

“Opportunity to be a starter on an NBA team after being out for a year,” he said. “What, am I gonna say no?”

It would seem more likely that the Wolves give a longer look to some of their incumbents, including defensive specialist Jaylen Clark, offensive spark plug Terrence Shannon Jr. and the recently acquired Josh Green. All are 6-6 or under, which would potentially leave the Wolves a little vulnerable on the glass. The Wolves also experimented with 6-11 center Joan Beringer at power forward in summer league, though it would present major spacing concerns when sharing the court with Gobert.
