historical perspective as we consider the future path

Not sure exactly what year it was, maybe 1988 or 89, but before the start of the season Bird had season-ending surgery. I’m going on memory, so bear that in mind. And I got the impression that Ainge and McHale were looking forward to the season without Bird. It wasn’t that they wanted to see their teammate and friend hurt, they didn’t. It wasn’t that they though the team was better without Bird, of course they didn’t. There was just a sense that perhaps they could more fully develop without being in the shadow of the legend. I don’t recall what gave me that impression, but it was not just something that I was guessing at. There was an article, perhaps by Shank Shaughnessy or Bob Ryan. Maybe it was something Ainge or Kevin said. But I definitely got that impression.

We have to understand how good McHale was, and how much potential Ainge had when he entered the NBA. After 87, McHale was never himself again because of the broken foot, but I don’t think he was fully aware of that, and he expected a full recovery. And Kevin McHale was a truly dominant player in the NBA. Not like a Jordan…no one was like Jordan. But as a post up pan, it wasn’t until the arrival of Shaq that we’d see anyone better. Prime time McHale without Bird to eat up shots might have averaged 35 pts a game. Hard to say, because he wouldn’t have Bird feeding him the ball. But certainly Ainge and DJ could feed him. And he could score when triple teamed.

Ainge had entered the NBA with the reputation that he might be “the Larry Bird of guards”. I remember being so excited when we signed him and finally won the battle to wrestle him from the Blue Jays, who didn’t want to give him up. In my mind, I was imaging having the Bird of forwards and the Bird of guards. It seemed unstoppable.

But in reality even the Bird of forwards would not have developed the way he did if he came onto a team with John Havlicek or Bob Macadoo taking the bulk of shots. Bird came onto a team that had won only 29 games, and the closest thing to stars they had were Maxwell and Archibald. Good players, two of my favorite. But there was plenty of room for Bird to develop his own shot range, and he would get plenty of volume of shots. Same thing with Michael Jordan. It was different for Magic, but Magic took years to really develop an outside shot, and it was never in Bird’s league. Maybe it would have developed differently if he didn’t have Jabbar in the post taking most of the shots.

Ainge was no doubt aware that his potential as a scorer had been severely constrained because he came onto a team He even tells the story about how Bill Fitch told him in practice to stop taking open shots because it made more sense to toss it to McHale, Parrish or Bird down low. So he had to develop with that always in mind. If Jordan came onto the Celtics to start his career at that time, he no doubt would have been a great player, but I doubt he would have become Air Jordan scoring 63 points in a playoff game his second year in the league.

I mention these things because it’s only natural for Brown to feel like his development might be a bit constricted because of Tatum. Of course, Brown was here a year earlier, so he’s certainly had his opportunity. Imagine how Hugo Gonzalez might feel? He can’t even crack the lineup much now. So while Brown has no objective reason to complain, it’s also understandable to a degree.

The question is how Celtics management should factor this in as they do what should be, or least could be, a reset this summer. If they decide to use the 27 million dollar trade exception they have to bring in new talent and run it back, will Brown be disgruntled?

I have enormous respect for Brown, but in fairness, he does seem to have a bit of a natural tendency to be disgruntled. He was very angry this season about not being the leading candidate for MVP, which was not a rational position. There are simply better players, such as Jokic and SGA, Doncic and Wemby. But if Browns disgruntlement is not significant enough to impact his play or the team’s chemistry, then it shouldn’t matter. Only the Celtics are close enough to make that determination.

In my calculation, Tatum has more trade value, and he’s generally not played great in the playoffs, or even in showcase regular season games. So I would look to trade Tatum for the second pick, held by Indiana, or even the first pick, held by Washington. The team was just as successful without Tatum as with him…maybe more so. So give me an exciting young player as an addition and run it back. Keep Brown as the wily veteran. Would I consider trading both of them? Only if the return was an offer I couldn’t refuse, and no one is doing that for Brown. What if someone offered the 8th pick in this draft? I’d consider it, but it’s probably a bad idea to add two rookies to the rotation next season. It just puts us back in that basketball problem of there being only one ball.
