My stance on trading Jaylen Brown has not changed

Most regulars here know that I frequently get labelled a Jaylen Brown hater. Truthfully, I’ve supported this guy since draft day and kept receipts showing that my hope had always been that he’d develop into a star like Jimmy Butler. In some ways, he surpassed those expectations. I’m a Jaylen Brown fan. He’s obviously incredibly talented and we’ve won a lot of games with him. He will forever have a special place in my heart after helping Boston win the title even if, personally, I feel Jayson Tatum deserved FMVP.

It’s comments like that which earned me the hater label. There is no place of nuance on the internet. By admitting that Jaylen probably isn’t actually a top 10 player, consistently has weak advanced stats, has said some problematic things, and will never be as good as peak Tatum, the assumption I have been rooting for Jaylen to fail or want him gone.

This isn’t my first time going through something like this. People use to call me a Gerald Green hater, because I refused to pretend he was the next T-Mac. They called me a Rondo hater, because I didn’t believe he was on the same level as CP3 and advocated trading him for a young Steph Curry. I got heat for defending Ray Allen leaving. I got heat for celebrating for the KG/Pierce Brooklyn trade. I got heat for believing Fultz trade was the right move and Tatum would be our best prospect since Bird. I love these guys, but I also try to take some emotion out of it and see the big picture. My opinions aren’t always right, but I’ve never been too worried about them not aligning with the consensus.

That’s why it’s been kind of funny seeing the consensus opinion here shift over the past week as many of the things that got me the “label” hater (and some things way more harsh) are suddenly acceptable with people ready to run Jaylen Brown out of town.

I just want to say, my opinion on trading Brown really hasn’t changed. I still woudn’t trade him for the sake of trading him. There’s only been a couple rare instances where I advocated for it. I was against trading young Jaylen in a package for guys like Paul George, Jimmy Butler and Kawhi Leonard when they had become available. I was fully against trading then-26 year old Jaylen for 34 year old Durant. At no point have I ever suggested this guy is “bad” and that it would be “addition by subtraction” to dump him.

The only couple times where I really became open to trading him were pretty specific.

First one was in early 2019 when Anthony Davis had requested a trade from the Pelicans. At the time, the thought was that maybe pairing Kyrie + Davis would also lead to Durant coming to Boston (would have been a Hayward sign-and-trade) in the offseason and Kyrie, Durant, Davis, Horford could be the Warriors of the East. Remember at this point, Jaylen was in the midst of a year-3 regression where he had been averaging 10 points on dreadful shooting and had been moved to the bench. In retrospect, Brown himself admits he had wanted out as he felt he was capable of more elsewhere. To be frank, I wasn’t just open to the idea of moving JB, but reluctantly accepted that Tatum might be the centerpiece instead with guys like Rozier also at risk. I didn’t love it, but I understood it and went as far as to make a joke video set to “If you love something, set it free”. As we know Davis killed the possibility when it became clear he wouldn’t re-sign in Boston and despite the wedding talk by Ainge, Kyrie and Durant paired elsewhere.

The other time I became open to it was this past offseason. Not because I hated Jaylen, but because I understood the big picture that if Tatum wasn’t returning this likely was going to be a 1st round exit. To fully take advantage of a gap year, moving Brown, White and Pritchard for youth/picks potentially made sense if it also allowed the team to bottom out and get a top 5 pick in 2026 and potentially set the team up for max cap space. Obviously, getting a blue chip talent like Cooper Flagg or Dylan Harper would have been intriguing. Ultimately, it only made sense if it was someone we truly could count on to be can’t-miss. At the time, the belief was that achillies injuries could take up to 2 years for a player to fully return to 100% and I think it’s safe to say that both Flagg and Harper are the type of talent that will develop into impactful within the next couple years. It doesn’t seem like either was an option though. Rumor has it the best offer we got was Ainge in Utah offering the #5 pick (Ace Bailey). While Ace could make a leap in the next 2 years, it’s understandable why Boston felt that wasn’t enough. Instead, JB ended up having a career year leading the team to 36-22 record without Tatum, a 1st round exit and the 27th pick.

There’s been other times where I’ve offered up thought experiments or trade ideas mostly to engage discussion. Back in 2019 during that same JB regression season, I had openly asked if anyone loved Bradley Beal (at the time a 25 year old elite all-star scorer averaging 26, 6, 5 on 47.5%/35.1%/81% shooting) enough to move JB in a package. Most were against it. LIkewise last season I pointed out my Philly fan friend who said he would give up the #3 pick + Paul George for JB and I asked if anyone loved anyone at #3 enough (like Edgecombe or Knueppel) to take on George’s disaster contract. Similar this week where I asked if anyone LOVED Sengun enough to consider him + Sheppard/VanVleet/picks in a deal for JB. This generally isn’t me advocating for these moves it’s just me trying to get a better understanding of how to feel about these options.

All to say that, I’ve seen the numerous Giannis trade ideas and personally if I was given the choice of trading Jaylen Brown right now straight-up for him (presumably with Brown going to a 3rd team and picks/youth going to the Bucks), my answer is probably no unless Brown himself is demanding it. Yes, Giannis is obviously a better player. Yes, the age gap (29 vs 31) is much smaller than the prior KD trade ideas. Yes, I can see Tatum + Giannis be like pairing a rich man’s Kobe with a poor man’s Shaq. Still, the last time we tried to make an obvious star for star upgrade (Isaiah for Kyrie), it didn’t work out all that great. I understand this one, but it’s not something I’m pulling for. I’m especially 100% against this deal if it requires us giving up more than just Jaylen. Brown has 3 years left on his deal. Giannis is an expiring contract. Giannis has leverage to dictate where he goes by saying who he will re-sign with. Giving up a younger talent coming off a 1st team all-nba season + additional draft picks for a early 30s injury prone superstar given the contract situation would be ridiculous.

I do think that Boston has to do their due diligence to see if Brown’s career year has caused offers to improve beyond last year (where Utah had offered #5 for Brown). Ultimately this team has fallen on it’s face several times over the years aside from that one “easy path” title run. You have to explore what’s out there. If you’re getting obvious value and can’t miss young talent, you have to look hard at it. For instance, if a team like the Hawks (who own the Pelicans pick) lands the #1 pick and is desperate to bring Jaylen home to Atlanta to have an immediate upgrade on their playoff team, maybe you consider it. Likewise if a team like the Wizards, who shifted to win-now mode with Trae Young and Anthony Davis and are at risk of losing both to free agency if they don’t build a winner immediately, lands a top pick and wants to give it up along with some of their other intriguing youth, maybe you have to look at it. Like if the Wizards are offering a package built around Alex Sarr and a top 5 pick, you have to at least hear them out, right? You honestly might even have to hear them out if they are like “We’ll give you Davis/Trae’s expiring + our pick” depending on how you feel about this draft. Personally, I always prioritize toward landing draft prospects with 4 year rookie scale locked in, because I generally don’t think elite prospects take as long as people think to start making an impact (for example Tatum leading his team to the ECF as a rookie, or this year where we saw Knueppel helping the Hornets make a run and Edgecombe helping the Sixers defeat the Celtics), but fwiw that seems counter to Brad’s approach as he seems to avoid using draft picks as much as possible in favor of going for known assets. Bottom line is, he has to do his job and see what’s out there and

But I’m still firmly against the idea of trading Brown just for the sake of trading him.

I will say I fully agree with the consensus that his comments come across like someone who is sick of being the #2 option, is jealous of his co-star, and wants to be “the man”. But the thing is, there’s also a very obvious historical precedent for this. Scottie Pippen, one of the greatest players to ever life, spent most of his career underappreciated and in the shadow of Jordan. In 1994, Pippen “at the helm” was able to lead his team to 55 wins (only 2 less than the year before) without Jordan. That year, Pippen’s mentality completely mirrored Jaylens. He talked a lot about how it was his team, about how they didn’t need Jordan, about how the world was finally seeing what he was capable of and how they were still gunning for a 4-peat. In a lot of ways, he put his money where his mouth was and made 1st team all-nba. The team had a real shot but got knocked off in game 7 of the 2nd round. Pippen, as we know, never fully got rid of his bitterness and jealously toward Jordan, but once the franchise talent returned he managed to put that aside and go back to his 2nd banana role and help the team win 3 more titles. When I look at Jaylen right now, he looks like he clearly has Pippen syndrome. He clearly enjoyed being the man and probably dreams of leading his own team. But to be real, Boston has him under contract for 3 more years and he’ll ultimately have to just fall in line. We saw it in the playoffs where the 2 games he took 15-16 shots we won while the 3 games he took 23-27 shots, we lost. Ultimately, if Boston keeps him Jaylen will have to just bury his jealousy and accept his role. This is the most likely outcome. And personally, I’m fine with that. Jaylen doesn’t strike me as the kind of guy who will start fighting the coaches or going off script to gun for his own shots. He’ll just have to swallow his pride and go back to being Tatum’s sidekick. The good news, for him, is that he’ll still probably get 20 shots per night and average 26+ like he did in 2022-23 before Jrue/Porzingis showed up and ate some of the field goal attempts.

TL;DR: I still support my guy despite the negatives. I think the concerns about his mentality are valid, but he still will probably reluctantly accept his Pippen-role long-term. I still wouldn’t trade him for the sake of trading him, but as always you have to explore every option especially after a career year pumped his trade value.
