Which hyped-up defense for the 2025-26 season are you not buying?

Question is as simple as the title. Which hyped-up NBA defense for the 2025-26 season are you not buying?

There have been multiple defenses that have been garnering hype headed into the 2025-26 NBA season, but every team can’t finish as a top-10 defense. Someone is bound for a disappointing season where their expected top-10 defense finishes 16th. Which team(s), out of the most highly touted, do you think is mostly likely to fall flat defensively:

  • OKC Thunder

  • Houston Rockets

  • Los Angeles Lakers

  • Golden State Warriors

  • New York Knicks

  • Cleveland Cavaliers

  • Orlando Magic

  • Dallas Mavericks

  • San Antonio Spurs

  • Minnesota Timberwolves

———

I think the most obvious candidate is the Los Angeles Lakers. Maybe Laker fans being the vocal majority are responsible for 90% of the noise about being a top-10 defense next season, but I’ll still put them here as a team people are expecting to be much improved defensively. Losing Dorian Finney-Smith, who ranked in the 98th percentile in both overall defense and switchability metrics, is a massive blow. They’ve replaced his elite perimeter and interior presence with Jake LaRavia—who’s far more known for his offense than defense—and Marcus Smart, who’s still solid but no longer the All-Defense force he was in Boston and comes with significant durability concerns. Despite this, many Lakers fans seem to expect the team to return to being a top-tier defensive unit like they were for that brief two-month stretch last season. I think the loss of Finney-Smith is going to weigh heavily. They’ll be much better offensively, but defensively I don’t expect them to improve at all. In fact, I believe they’ll be worse.

A sneakier pick for me would be the Minnesota Timberwolves. With Mike Conley and Rudy Gobert both aging and the team losing Nickeil Alexander-Walker—who was outstanding defensively for them last season—they’re quietly in a tougher spot than people realize. Jaylen Clark was to be Nickeil Alexander-Walker’s replacement, but his offensive limitations could keep him off the floor in big moments. If that happens, they’ll be forced to lean more on guards like Rob Dillingham and Bones Hyland, both of whom are major liabilities on defense. That combination could quietly erode the defensive identity they built last season.
