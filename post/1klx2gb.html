History of NBA Achilles injuries

Achilles tears are among the most devastating injuries in basketball. They typically require long recoveries, and many players never fully regain their previous level of performance.

Here’s a rundown of notable cases in recent NBA history:

Kevin Durant (2019 Finals)

  • Ruptured Achilles in June 2019
  • Missed the entire 2019–20 season (about 18 months off)
  • Returned in 2020–21 and looked like himself — still elite
  • One of the few superstars to fully return to form
  • Age at injury: 30

Klay Thompson (2020 Offseason)

  • Tore Achilles while rehabbing a torn ACL
  • Missed two full seasons
  • Returned in January 2022
  • Remains an elite shooter, but mobility and defensive ability declined
  • Age at injury: 30

DeMarcus Cousins (2018)

  • Ruptured Achilles with the Pelicans
  • Missed about a year, came back with the Warriors
  • Never returned to All-Star form; lost mobility and consistency
  • Career has since been derailed by additional injuries
  • Age at injury: 27

Kobe Bryant (2013)

  • Tore Achilles in April 2013
  • Returned after about 8 months, but struggled with further injuries
  • Was never the same physically
  • Age at injury: 34

Wesley Matthews (2015)

  • Tore Achilles in March 2015
  • Returned for opening night the following season (~7 months)
  • Lost some athleticism but remained a solid 3-and-D player
  • Age at injury: 28

Brandon Jennings (2015)

  • Tore Achilles mid-season
  • Returned after about 11 months
  • Lost explosiveness and never regained previous form
  • Out of the league a few years later
  • Age at injury: 25

Rudy Gay (2017)

  • Ruptured Achilles with the Kings
  • Returned in about 9 months with the Spurs
  • Adapted well; became a productive bench scorer
  • Age at injury: 30

Elton Brand (2007)

  • Tore Achilles during preseason
  • Missed most of the season (about 9 months)
  • Returned but was no longer the same All-Star-caliber player
  • Age at injury: 28

Chauncey Billups (2012)

  • Tore Achilles with the Clippers
  • Returned after 10 months
  • Clearly lost a step; played only 41 more games in his career
  • Age at injury: 35

Dominique Wilkins (1992)

  • Tore Achilles at age 32
  • Returned the next season and averaged 29.9 PPG
  • Made the All-Star team and stayed productive
  • Very rare outcome; he’s the exception, not the rule
  • Age at injury: 32

Mario Chalmers (2016)

  • Tore Achilles with the Grizzlies
  • Missed a full season
  • Came back briefly but couldn’t reestablish himself
  • Age at injury: 29

Jon Leuer (2018)

  • Tore Achilles
  • Never played another NBA game
  • Was a rotation-level player beforehand

Key Takeaways:

  • Most players miss 10 to 18 months
  • Players under 30 tend to have better recoveries, but even then, the outcomes vary
  • Players who rely on quickness and agility often see lasting decline
  • Only Durant and Wilkins truly returned to their prior level
  • Many others (Cousins, Jennings, Brand, Chalmers) saw major drop-offs or career-ending trajectories

The Celtics will almost certainly be without Tatum for all of next season. And even if he returns in 2026, there’s no guarantee he regains the same level of agility, footwork, and rhythm that define his game.

Best-case scenario: a full recovery like Durant.
Worst-case: a long road back with permanent decline.
