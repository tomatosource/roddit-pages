Why old NBA players dribbled so differently: Rare films / analysis of how ball handling was actually taught (and enforced)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kb60e6Pet78

I worked on this video project the last few months because when I touched on it in the past it drew a decent amount of interest so I thought I’d create this deeper dive.

The point is to shine a light on that players that far back in time were operating under a much stricter interpretation of what counted as a legal dribble. In older footage, you consistently see players keeping the hand more on top of the ball, using shorter rhythm changes, tighter body angles, and quick direction changes without letting the ball come to a pause in the hand. A lot of what modern viewers interpret as “stiff” or “basic” ball handling was really players solving a different rule puzzle.

The video looks at things like how dribbling was taught in old instructional films.

  • Why players like Bob Cousy, Jerry West, Calvin Murphy, Nate Archibald, Pete Maravich, Earl Monroe, Archie Clark, and others handled the ball the way they did
  • How old hesitation moves worked without the modern hand-under-ball pause
  • Why the crossover and change-of-pace dribble evolved differently once carrying enforcement loosened
  • How big men like Wilt Chamberlain, Bill Russell, and Connie Hawkins handled in transition compared with modern bigs like Kevin Durant and Giannis
  • Why the modern game allows a wider “gather,” float, and pause than earlier generations were usually allowed to use

I made this because a lot of modern basketball discussion, including by current players and coaches even, reduces older players to “they couldn’t dribble,” (or weren’t skilled in other ways either) when I believe the truth is way more interesting: they had skill, but the skill was constrained by a different enforcement environment.

Curious what people here think: when watching older footage, do you or did you always see a lack of skill? After watching this, do you now see players adapting to a stricter dribbling standard? Or if you have your own take, what is it? Love to see some discussion on this.
