Breaking down some of the GOAT concepts used in arguments.

The Most Accomplished

This is probably the most straight forward. Most number of rings, Allstar, All-nba, All-defense, stat titles,

The greatest vs the best

This is the most controversial concept that gets thrown around. But i feel the distinction is a real one and an important one. Most people conflate the two so the argument is often about ignoring so evidence and not directly addressing others by changing the subject.

The Best

This is the most effective. The players combination of skills, talents, abilities, health, longevity, reliability, efficiency. Basically any first degree (direct causal) attribute that leads to winning. This is probably the aspect that is least context dependent

The Most Impressive

This is probably the most context dependent, and what people use the most to conflate with the greatest. Which is why I would prefer to call it what it is, the most impressive. Most people would agree that MJ is better than Steph Curry. But an argument can be made that Steph is more impressive. MJ is the master at midrange, but Steph is the master at the more difficult, more statistically impactful 3pt. At 6’6 with supreme athletic gifts, MJ is still not as impressive as Curry at 6’3

Another aspect here is regarding achievements. Lebron is both the beneficiary and victim of this. The 2016 come from down 3-1 win against the 73win warriors is easily one of the most impressive single season achievement in NBA history. But the 2011 finals loss teaming with 2 allstars, against a disfavored dallas team, while playing statistically below expectations, is one of the most disappointing expectation defying events in player history.

Being 6 of 6 with 2 3peats is not only a great achievement. Its an impresive achievement. And the fact that it happened consecutively adds to the aura, even if it doesnt really have more value than (for example) Kareem’s 6 rings or Horry’s 7. After all, a title is a title, doesnt matter when you win it. Each ring is equivalent in value even if the three peat circumstances makes it vastly more “impressive”.

Similarly, MJ’s 6 rings is objectively less than Bill Russells 11. But the circumstances (percieved or otherwise) makes 6 more “impressive” than 11

The Most ______

This is probably the hardest to pin down. Its something that gets thrown around often, but rarely clarified, which is why i dont really have a name for it. It’s a combination of multiple attributes but not easily embodied by a single one.

If you combine MJ’s/Lebron’s points assists, rebounds and defense, they still dont account for 1⁄2 of their team’s total offensive and defensive production. Basketball is a team game and the team has a bigger impact than any single player. What this concept tries to capture is how well a player facilitiates the maximization of the rest of the team.

It’s not as simple as assists, because every pass you try to take is a ball handling opportunity you take from someone else.

It’s not as simple as trade offs or weaknesses either. Like Shaq’s free throws, Lebron’s ball dominace, MJ and Kobe’s shot usage. While all of those are net positives, they have drawbacks that impact not just the team players, but the flexibility and team building philosophy as a whole. You dont just build around those weaknesses, you take on the opportunity cost of not allowing any other team construction.

Therefore, It’s not just versatility either, though its a closely related concept. Because versatility is about how a player’s skills allow other players to thrive. Its about how the whole package, impacts the team indirectly. Like how Steph’s skills allows a below average floor spacer like Draymond to be more effective. When in other team constructions, a Star like Lebron could in theory make Draymond better, but likely wont even get to thst point because of the overlap of their games.

To me the best player that embodies this concept is Robert Horry. He’s not the best scorer, nor best defender, nor best passer, but his combination of skills and portability allowed the construction of rosters that could consistently win.

It combines multiple attributes and intangibles like luck, leadership, non-abrasiveness with good or better players, lower salary, versatility, spacing ability all of which facilitate without directly impacting every player itself.

I’ll call it the the “Horry”iest player.

This might sound like an intentionally convoluted shoehorned concept, but it does impact GOAT tier player arguments. The best way to look at it is Bill Russell vs Wilt. Wilt was by all accounts the superior player in offense and skill. But Russell’s playstyle, leadership, lack of usage, and intangibles give him the ability to play under a more flexible roster construction that facilitated to collection of a slight talent advantage, but win consistently across more than a decade.

Highest Peak

We know peak as the best level of play any player has reached. That could be gauged by what you accomplish but considering that that’s a team outcome, its really more about individual peak performance.

But Nick Wright makes a great point here that no one talks about. How do we define peak? Is it a single season, or your whole prime?
