[Morganstern] CJ McCollum: “People who have been in the United States for 10 years, even 30 years, and are either being detained on farms or being detained outside of courthouses. Some people have even had court cases scheduled, then the court case gets dismissed and they’re arrested.”

Which is more frustrating, an NBA ref or U.S. Wine laws?

U.S. wine laws by far. A ref’s accuracy is like 90%. They get a lot of slack and they don’t get enough credit for their ability to execute in those tight environments. But these wine laws. A lot of them are from so long ago and some of them should be changed. We’re in a different time now.

You’ve joined the board of Oregon LIVE Certified. Why was that important to you?

Sustainability has always been important to my family, not just on the wine side but because we care about the environment. We want this place to be better than it is now. And better than it was when we found it after we leave. We care about the future of our society and especially, with having kids, we want them to be able to live in a safe, healthy environment.

We also want to make sure that the employees and people that are on the farms are paid well and treated well. Obviously lately there’s been a situation where a lot of the farm workers who are not from the United States are in difficult positions. People who have been in the United States for 10 years, even 30 years, and are either being detained on farms or being detained outside of courthouses. Some people have even had court cases scheduled, then the court case gets dismissed and they’re arrested.

How vital is this workforce to the wine industry?

Immigrants are vital to not only wine, but so many different parts of society. I think people are starting to realize that. But for those of us in the wine business, understanding the importance of harvest and farming land and how many people play a vital role in that — the business won’t survive without immigrants.

Who have been some of your favorite NBA colleagues to share wine with?

I’m from Ohio, so I’ve obviously drank wine with LeBron. Kevin Love has a great palate. He drinks a variety of wine. Channing Frye. Carmelo Anthony always brings some unique wines and he’s heavy on white burgundy. Terry Stotts has a good palate. Coach Willie Green enjoys his wine. I’ve shared some different Pinots with him. It’s cool to drink with players, but I would say I enjoy drinking wine with my wife the most. We’ve got special occasion bottles. Birthday and celebratory bottles. Birth year bottles. We enjoy opening a bottle and sharing stories. We’ve had some unique experiences over the course of our life.

Would you be happy if one day someone knew nothing about your sports career and said ‘you’re the guy that makes that wine?’

That would be cool. That’s something that takes time. Maybe 15 years from now someone will say ‘he played basketball? I thought he was the guy from that winery.’ That would be a great moment. One day I’ll just be Jacobi and Margaux’s dad and the guy who makes wine. That’s fine.
