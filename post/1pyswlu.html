Updated pitching standards shouldn’t apply to guys like Felix, Buehrle, or Hamels

I’ve seen a lot of takes about how we need to update Hall standards for starting pitching, which I agree with (and have argued for in the past). The simple fact of the matter is that modern pitchers aren’t gonna have the chance to get close to the traditional counting stats that marked the barriers to the HOF–3000 strikeouts will be an incredibly difficult number to reach and 300 wins is out of the question when many of today’s best pitchers might not retire with 100.

If we don’t update pitching standards, there might not be a single starter in the Hall to debut after 2020. Among 2010s debutants, it’s basically Cole and Sale and then, like, Wheeler in third (edit: forgot DeGrom for a moment, think he should be third above Wheeler). There should be serious questions about how to judge that generation.

What I don’t get is why these standards are being retroactively applied to players whose primes came before pitching transformed so much. Guys like Felix and Hamels with fringey cases are getting a lot of defenders arguing that we need to update hall standards. But why? The 2000s had plenty of obvious HOF starters who managed to accumulate great career numbers nonetheless. Verlander, Scherzer, Kershaw, Greinke, CC, Halladay–all of these guys should be first ballot HOFers, CC and Halladay already are, and all of them pitched through the same era as Felix, Buerhle, and Hamels.

That’s five starters to debut in the 2000s (plus Hallday who debuted in ‘98 but was still obviously a contemporary to Buehrle, Hamels, and Felix). That’s just not out of line with historical standards at all. Only three HOF starters debuted in the 90s and only four in the 80s. Steroids do complicate this (and Schilling obviously had HOF numbers and a Hall of Shame personality), but the 70s only saw two starters debut, and one of those starters was Jack Morris.

Now, you can argue that we should reconsider the standards of the 70s and 80s as well, but that seems to be a different conversation from the era-focused re-evaluation people are making now. I’m totally in favor of looking at the cases for guys like Stieb, Hershiser, or Saberhagen–all put up great numbers and none would be a shame to the Hall.

But the argument that we need to revise pitching standards or else no more starters will make the Hall shouldn’t apply to guys who played the bulk of their careers well before the era of 5-inning starts began. We have clear empirical evidence that plenty of guys could still build compelling cases in their era–if six starters could all have first-ballot HOF careers in the same environment they did, then it’s clear that the HOF standards were still perfectly reasonable.

One more thing: this isn’t to say that none of these guys deserve the Hall. I think you could make a fair case for all of them. (I think Hamels is the least compelling of the three, but I understand disagreement.) All I want to say is that they should be compared against their contemporaries, not against ours.
