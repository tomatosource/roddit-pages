Lefties who earned consecutive Cy Young Awards

Congrats to Tarik Skubal on his back-to-back Cy Young hardware!

He enters some rarefied air:

LHPs who won consecutive Cy Young Awards LHPs who should’ve won consecutive CYAs
Randy Johnson Randy Johnson
Clayton Kershaw Clayton Kershaw
Sandy Koufax Sandy Koufax
Tarik Skubal Tarik Skubal
Johan Santana

Notes:

  • Johnson won an unprecedented 4 in a row from age 35-39. Among this group of elite lefties (including Santana), only one other Cy Young was awarded to a man in his 30s - Koufax’s second consecutive award in his final season at age 30.

  • Kershaw and Koufax both won 3 within 4 years though no more than two in a row. Kershaw pitched well enough to win it in 2012 (which would’ve been 4 in a row for him), but R.A. Dickey was equally deserving that year.

  • Santana should have won three in a row, as he was clearly the best pitcher in the AL in 2004, 2005, and 2006. He won it unanimously in 2004 and 2006. His snub in 2005 was largely about Bartolo Colón hitting the 20-win milestone. With all due respect to Big Sexy (a national treasure in his own right), Santana was the best pitcher in the league that year and it’s not particularly close:

The AL Cy Young voting in 2005. The voters must’ve been deeply soured on even primitive analytics that year, longing for their simple and unadulterated 20th century baseball brains.

  • Remarkably, despite Colón’s higher win total and having more run support than Santana that year, the Angels were merely 22-11 in games started by Colón, whereas the Twins were 24-9 in games started by Santana, underlining both Santana’s merit and the inherent flukiness of pitcher wins.

  • Since Santana didn’t win consecutive awards, it means that Skubal is the only lefty to have done it in the AL. He’s also the only lefty to have done it in a league with the DH. All of the consecutive awards to Kershaw, Koufax, and Johnson were exclusively for NL clubs- though both Kershaw and Johnson pitched vs. the DH in interleague matchups. Johnson also won one AL Cy Young much earlier in his career with the Mariners.

  • Even as a Twins fan (could you tell?), I’m rooting for Skubal to three-peat. His pitch sequencing reminds me of a southpaw Greg Maddux - lots of finesse and off-the-charts efficiency. And his demeanor reminds me of a southpaw Max Scherzer - a genuinely nice and down-to-earth guy who morphs into an irate bull on the pitching mound every 5 days. A real generational talent.

  • Guys who almost made the table:

    • Steve Carlton: The former Twin - ahem, and Philly - won 4 CYAs overall, but never won them consecutively. He could have easily three-peated from 1980-1982, but finished third in a sensibly close 1981 vote.
    • C.C. Sabbathia: He definitely pitched well enough to win it in 2008 after winning in 2007 with Cleveland. However, he switched leagues at the trade deadline, complicating the matter. His dominant stretch with the Brewers in the season’s final two months (11-2 in 17 starts, 1.65 ERA, 128 Ks, 4.9 bWAR) was still enough to net him remarkable award consideration given such a small body of work.
    • Tom Glavine: He was a multi-time Cy Young winner whose inability to net consecutive awards was largely a function of his own teammates - especially Maddux.
    • Several great lefties preceded the Cy Young Award. Most notably: inner-circle Hall of Famer Lefty Grove.
