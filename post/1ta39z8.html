Inside a sterile room on the edge of Lake Michigan, Austin Ainge let out a “yes” that cut through the quiet.

“Well, I couldn’t just sit there and do nothing,” Ainge justified later, smirking.

The Utah Jazz’s president of basketball operations spent the last year trying to create NBA draft lottery luck in a place where none previously existed. Not only did he guide his team to 60 losses to secure the fourth-best odds, he personally surveyed every potential draft room representative to see who had the best fortune — only to see the battle scars of the past four years laid out in front of him.

“Who wants it?” Ainge asked among the Jazz’s front office executives. “Justin Zanik said he’d already gone and lost. [Danny Ainge] same thing. Ryan Smith went and didn’t get the pick. You’re all fired, I said. I’ll be the one to go.”

So it was Austin Ainge, fist-pumping and grinning ear to ear, who was the first to know the Jazz’s fate in Chicago.

For the first time in franchise history, Utah moved up in the draft with their own pick to go No. 2 overall. For a franchise always snakebitten by the lottery balls, the Jazz basically won the lottery twice.

After it was announced that Utah landed at two, the next draw also came up Utah’s way. The NBA had to re-spin the lotto balls, eventually giving Memphis the third pick. Ainge was so giddy he reached across the desk to fist bump Memphis General Manager Zach Kleiman.

“When you look at the playoff teams, most of them had the balls bounce their way at some point,” Ainge said.
