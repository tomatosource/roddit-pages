You’re chasing the dragon - A discussion on loot drops / higher quality loot / identified items

I know people asked for items to be identified all the time and instead increase the quality of the loot, while reducing the amount of loot dropped to compensate.

I also know that people generally don’t have a good grasp on statistics and the impact it would have on the statistical distribution of item power and on the actual gameplay loop.

Let’s say that the devs throw the towel and say: “fine, we drop 80% less items, but we buff the loot by 500%.”

At X point in time, let’s say that a dropped item has a ~0.1% chance of being an upgrade. When an item like this drops, you equip it making the new item stats the new threshold for your upgrade check. This has the effect of lowering the chances to find a new upgrade to ~0.08% (again, random numbers)

The rate of the items you drop has not changed, yet at point X+1 unit time, you’ll find more trash loot than before because there are less items that statistically go above your upgrade threshold. You still find upgrades, albeit less.

Yes you could argue that as you increase level, you unlock better tiers of stats, slightly increasing the probability of finding an upgrade, although with diminishing returns for each added tier as the lower tiers are not removed from the loot pool. But my hypothesis is that this isn’t enough to counteract the fundamental issue that as you progress on the item power bell curve, the items become increasingly trash, no matter how you increase the quality of the loot & lower its drop rate.

As a though experiment, imagine we drop the loot chance to almost 0, but increase the loot quality by 100 000. Now every items you find each 3 hours of gameplay is a almost BIS. What happens? Fast forward a week later, you’re at the same point, but you’re switching weapons for a sweet 1% more Crit damage or +1 strength. Welcome to Diablo 3 super end-game, where you’re chasing percents on specific spell damage stats on items, but everything else is trash.

Also, yes, you could find items that are trash to you, but good for someone else, but then you increase the “sharedness” of the item power bell curve with other players, so everyone gets to the same “1000 trash items no upgrade” point, but quicker.

It’s a statistical inevitability, you WILL be surrounded by trash loot, at one point or another, because you’re chasing the dragon that is peak performance.

People just need to accept the fact that you’re playing a game that by definition is exponential growth, with the fun being tied to finding statistically rare items. Those items become increasingly rare the further you’re at in the game and as everyone else is.

The only way I can see things changing would be to remove lower stat tiers at higher level to increase the statistical impact of the better tiers. This, however, would have the effect of increasing the speed at which your character hits the “almost full-power” threshold, increasing the impression of only finding trash loot.

I’d be curious to know if anyone with a high level of academia in statistics could pitch in and give their take on it. I’m only an undergrad in IT but so far everything I’ve learned in stats points toward that conclusion.

TL:DR: There are some things at play in the way the statistics of items works that are fundamental to the experience of an ARPG. Changing those in a significant way require people to either lower their standards regarding what fraction of the game the average joe can complete without spending significant time / effort OR accept that there will be a point that you’ll be surrounded by trash items as this is the price to pay for having a good character. I’m open for discussion.
