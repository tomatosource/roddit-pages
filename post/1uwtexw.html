Lebron should go play college ball

LeBron went straight from St. Vincent-St. Mary High School to the No. 1 overall pick in the 2003 NBA Draft, entirely bypassing the college experience. He has won NBA rings, MVP trophies, and Olympic gold medals. The only major basketball milestone completely missing from his resume is a National Championship. Imagine the legacy points. If LeBron could stroll into April, cut down the nets, and hoist the NCAA National Championship trophy, he would possess an absolutely bulletproof, multi-tiered basketball resume that literally no other modern player could ever touch. No sporting event on earth is as unpredictably chaotic as March Madness. Now, picture LeBron James leading a team like the Ohio State Buckeyes (the school he has historically supported) or USC (where his son Bronny played) through the bracket.  The TV ratings would shatter every sports broadcast record in history. Imagine LeBron—a literal mountain of a man with 20 years of elite pro experience—setting a screen on an 18-year-old freshman communications major from a 16-seed mid-major school. It would be a hilarious, terrifying, and deeply entertaining spectacle.If LeBron played a single season in the NCAA today, he wouldn’t just break college records—he would rewrite the physics of the game.  Against college defenders, a coast-to-coast LeBron dunk would look like a grown man playing on a mini-hoop. He would easily average a triple-double: something absurd like 38 points, 15 rebounds, and 12 assists per game, while shooting 75% from the field. I’m getting fully erect just thinking about it.
