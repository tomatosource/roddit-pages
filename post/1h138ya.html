The majority of active NBA players don’t remember a league without Lebron

My exams are coming up soon, so naturally I spent some time looking through the NBA roster to create some peak pre-christmas NBA-content.

To start off this post: The average age where childhood memories seize cease to be fragmented and start resembling a more continuous timeline is 4.7 years. Your absolute earliest memories are likely from somewhere between 3-4 years of age, but it can be younger if it’s an event of particular importance, say for example the birth of a sibling.

Now as I looked through the age sorted NBA roster (the link I gave earlier only sorts by full years by the way, so I sorted every 25 year old manually. Grant Williams is the oldest, born November 30th 1998, while Brandon Williams is the youngest, born November 22nd 1999.) I found out that the median age of all active NBA players is 25 years, 4 months and 15 days (Shout out to Bucks rookie center Liam Robbins, born July 12th 1999 who marks the exact cutoff where half the league is older than him and the other half is younger. Perhaps somewhat notably, Ja Morant and Zion Williamson are both still among the younger half of players in the league.)

Back to the point, whatever that point may be:

When Lebron was drafted, June 26th, 2003, Liam Robbins was 3 years, 11 months and 14 days old. Of course it isn’t impossible that Liam and several others of the younger NBA players watched the Spurs win their second championship in 2003, and may remember that. Liam would’ve been 3 years, 11 months and 3 days during Game 6 of the 2003 finals.

Of all the 25 year olds in the league, 18 of them are younger than Liam. If you are under 25, you would be at most 3 years, 6 months and 18 days during Game 6. I think that is a reasonable cut off where it is sufficiently improbable to have memories of the NBA at a younger age, though realistically this is is being generous. As such we only need a handful of the older half of NBA players to not have pre-Lebron NBA memories to make up for anyone in the younger half having early memories of the 2003 post-season.

In this regard, we are somewhat lucky that the 2003 NBA finals (and playoffs as a whole) had relatively few viewers (The highest it reached was 11.57 Million viewers in Game 6. For reference, the last games of the 2004 and 2002 finals had 21.84 and 16.56 Million viewers, respectively). In fact, it held the record for the fewest viewers as far back as reliable NBA finals viewership estimates are available, up until 2020 dethroned it. Thus, the 2003 season is the season where we have the best chance of Liam and other NBA players not having formed any early childhood memories of pre-Lebron NBA.

Taking this into account I therefore claim that it is highly likely that a majority of active NBA players have no memories of a Lebron-less NBA.

QED
