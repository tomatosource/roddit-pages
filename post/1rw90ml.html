Italian point of view on the Italian national team result

Hello fellow baseball fans, I am an Italian lurker of this sub, I have been recently active since the successful path of the Italian team in this WCB to comment under many posts. Some of you may think I am being salty, or a party pooper about it, you may be right, and I am here to explain my point of view. Please note, I didn’t use any AI to write this post, so please accept the wrong syntax or grammar errors.

Some users here are claiming that this result will boost the Italian baseball audience, and perhaps help the development of the sport in my country. I believe this statement is wrong, because the sport exists in Italy since old times, precisely since 1945, when the allies landed in a small town 60km south of Rome, called Nettuno. Along with first aid, the US soldiers gave bats, gloves and baseballs and explained the rules to the local kids. Since then, the sport has grown, overshadowed by football (soccer), and as of today the Italian baseball championship is held every year since 1948 (not immune to issues and controversies but this is not the point of my post). Being one of the European baseball pioneering countries, Italy collected 10 gold medals at the European championships, second only to the Netherlands. Here I would like to point out, that in some cities we do know the rules, we do like the sport. It is a minor sport for sure, it has a small group of fans for sure, but it is an historical reality in the country. I am one of the fans, and know many of them, we go to see the matches, we root for our teams, and every single baseball fan in Italy, did not feel attached to this rooster.

Do we hate you guys? Of course not, you invented the sport and bring it to us back in the day. Just we are used to people taking shortcuts and loopholes to circumvent the rules, and we can smell these things from miles away.

I would now like to point your attention to one Italian law, and one WBC eligibility rule:

-the so called “iure sanguinis” (right of blood): Italian citizenship by right of blood is obtained through descent from an Italian ancestor, regardless of place of birth, as long as the bloodline has not been interrupted. There is another little loophole, you can prove you have the right of blood also if none of your parent has the citizenship but you can prove that one of your grandparent did, and he was alive while their children were born (and one of those children being your parent), this means that having one Italian grandparent (they don’t have to be alive now) qualifies almost anyone to be an Italian citizen. DISCLAIMER, I am not expert in law, I am sure there are some caveats to this rule, and recently the law was modified a bit by the current prime minister party, but that is the core, for this reason there are loads of Italian citizen in Argentina, Venezuela, Uruguay. It is the easiest ticket to come to work/live in Europe without having to go through a VISA process.

-One of the the WBC eligibility criteria, states that “A player is eligible to participate on a WBC team if he is qualified for citizenship or to hold a passport under the laws of a nation represented by a team, but has not been granted citizenship or been issued a passport; in this case, the player may be made eligible by World Baseball Classic Inc. upon petition by the player or team.

Now, by crossing the two rules you can see the loophole: any current MLB player who has at least one grandparent (living or not) who had the Italian citizenship, qualifies to play in this Italian team. Moreover, Italian blood runs in many US and Venezuelan families, then I am not surprised how likely is that many good players not drafted for their main national teams found out other routes to take part in the tournament. It happened with team Israel (where if I’m not mistaken you are eligible to citizenship by religion), and team GB (I didn’t do any research here, feel free to explain it to me in the comments).

A good question I catch under other posts was “why you don’t root for your Italian diaspora”, other users pointed out that we don’t really keep contacts with our overseas relatives, but in my case, and in the case of other Italian baseball fans, the main reason I didn’t feel attached is that this was a missed opportunity to showcase the state of Baseball in Italy, and perhaps give some chances to our national players to be noticed.

Finally, I am not saying that all the Italian team players of this WBC were total scammers, I am not claiming that they aren’t proud to represent Italy or that they don’t feel Italian. I am sure many of them feel like that, but I am also sure that, given the chance to play in team USA (and in a lesser part Venezuela), they would have taken it.

I hope this will shed some light on my point of view, I cannot guarantee this is the opinion of all Italians, because at the end of the day it is true that baseball is unknown to the vast majority of Italians born and raised in Italy, but for sure many Italian baseball fans share it with me.
