I feel like it’s going to end up being almost completely forgotten that Luke Walton coached the Warriors for most of their record setting 73-9 regular season

The Warriors were 39-4 under Walton that season, but it’ll likely end up getting forgotten (and already has to some extent) for a couple of key reasons.

A. The NBA decided, on the spot, that interim coaching records would now automatically be credited to the head coach if they were absent

Prior to 2015, interim head coaches would, in some cases, get credit for wins and losses. It was decided on a case by case basis. After Walton led the Warriors on a 24 game win streak, the league made the executive decision that the wins would count for Kerr rather than Walton, and in all future such cases. This was a sudden, and meaningful change. When you look up the Warriors’ season, Kerr gets credit for 39 wins for which he was not there.

https://sg.news.yahoo.com/blogs/nba-ball-dont-lie/nba-rules-that-luke-walton-s-only-official-win-with-warriors-is-coach-of-the-month-210547134.html

B. Steve Kerr received the NBA’s coach of the year award, despite coaching for less than half the season, and technically having a worse record than Walton

This was always a weird one. Kerr missed 43 games due to an offseason back surgery, but was named the best coach of that NBA year. The Warriors went 34-5 after Kerr came back. Kerr gets the coach of the year and is credited for all 73 wins - people who didn’t watch will primarily look back and assume he was there for the whole time, instead of less than half of it.

C. That is the only good thing that Luke Walton did during his head coaching career

If Walton had gone on to become a championship winning head coach, it would have been cemented in NBA lore. Instead, Walton coached three years with the Lakers and three with the Kings, never winning more than 37 games, making him much more forgettable.

Since it’s reddit and people have a tendency to distort statements into something entirely different to make it seem dumb if the other person had actually said that thing, this does not mean Luke Walton was a better coach than Steve Kerr. It does not mean he would have had the same success without Kerr’s foundation. But it is pretty interesting that the man who led most of the best regular season in history will be ultimately pretty much forgotten, along with that entire chapter.
