Crafting guide for casuals (like me)

1. Use a Loot Filter

  • Go to 👉 https://poe2filter.com/
  • Create or copy a filter (use “presets” button) → sync it with your PoE2 account.
  • In-game: open settings → sync your account filter and select it.

📁 2. Organize Your Stash (seriously, do it)

  • Create labeled stash tabs like: Helmets, Chests, Gloves, Scepters, Jewelry, Flasks, etc.
  • Store normal / magic / rare items relevant to your build.

🧱 3. Gear Level Matters (a lot)

  • Example:
    • Lvl 30 sceptre = max +40% minion damage
    • Lvl 55 sceptre = max +75% minion damage
  • TLDR: Old gear = capped potential → don’t hoard it.
  • Jewelry / belts / charms matter less in terms of level scaling – keep only the best ones, but keeping all is still manageable – they take up little space.
  • Flasks are cheap to craft (they cap at magic), update for cheap every other level or even only when you run into this issue = button press does not really heal me.

🧹 4. Only Keep Armor and Weapons From the Last 2 Level Tiers

  • Rule of thumb:
    • Are you level 60? Then keep level 50–60 armor and weapons.
    • Dump anything lower: sell, salvage, or auction it.
  • Avoid stash clutter. You’ll thank yourself.

🧪 5. Flask Crafting Is Easy and Worth It

  • Keep and use the best flasks for your level. They’re dirt cheap. If you have good flasks and you are not in need for better (you heal and mana through boss fights easily), you can save blue orbs until you do run into issues.
  • Example cost for good flask: 1-2x Orb of Transmutation + 3-4x Orb of Augmentation per every 10 levels.
  • Only magic or unique flasks exist = simple upgrade logic.

🔷 6. Upgrade All Normal Items → Magic

  • Use Orb of Transmutation → convert all your normal to magic.
  • Upgrade only when you are in need of an upgrade. E.g. your chest piece is several tiers behind? Time to spend time on this piece of gear.
  • This is your baseline for improving items.

⚙️ 7. Upgrade Magic Items with Orb of Augmentation

  • Use Orb of Augmentation to add a 2nd mod.
  • Only upgrade if the item has useful existing mod.
  • Skip junk mods like reduce stat requirement – not worth it.

♻️ 8. Recycle Trash at the Salvage Bench

  • Use the Salvage Bench for bad magic items.
  • Doesn’t matter if the item has 1 or 2 mods – just dump it. Dumping three 2-mod items does not result in 2-mod recycled item (you can get 2-mod item, but you can also get 1-mod item – but I might be wrong and dumping three 2-mod items might increase the probability of getting a 2-mod recycled item).
  • Efficient and keeps stash clean.
  • If you know what mods YOU do not want, but other builds need – item is valuable for others, you can auction the item. But this is beyond my personal casual knowledge and I personally would spend a lot of time learning, managing and trading – which is not fun for me.

👑 9. Regal/Exalt Only When It’s Worth It

  • If a magic item has 1⁄2 great or 2⁄2 good mods, upgrade it using a Regal Orb.
  • If the third mod sucks → stash it and recycle later.
  • If the third mod is good →
    • Use up to 3x Exalted Orbs to max out the item to 6 mods.
    • Only if it’s crucial for build/endgame-worthy/you plan on keeping it for a longer time and has high-tier stats.

Summary: 💡 Generally speaking, you’re usually hunting for one crucial mod that enables your build – like +X to minion skill level or +% minion damage. Without it, your clearing power or survivability might take a serious hit.

The second mod is also important – ideally something like +X Health, but you can make do with useful utility stats based on your build (e.g. +X Spirit, regen, etc.).

Slamming a Regal + Exalts will either:

  • Create trash,
  • Give you something to store for niche cases (e.g. +% resistances for specific bosses/maps, or +% item rarity for farming),
  • Or, in lucky cases, give you a solid piece you can use until you hit progression walls in endgame.

Eventually, you’ll be chasing 3⁄6, 4⁄6, or even 5⁄6 strong mods on your gear. But early on? Just getting 2 solid ones is a win, unless you are depending on very specific multi-combinations of mods for a build to work.

Side notes. That’s it. No theorycrafting, no 30-minute YouTube deep dives. Just simple, practical steps to craft good-enough gear and avoid drowning in junk.
