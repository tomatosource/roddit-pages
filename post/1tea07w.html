Interludes and Cruel acts don’t have to be thrown away—maybe they could ease the league-start burnout instead

I don’t have a ton of free time. Work eats most of my energy, and I love Path of Exile 2, but the campaign every single league burns me out. I’ve barely played the last few because repeating the same acts feels like a chore. This idea came to me at work—not while playing. Just thinking about how much I respect GGG but wish the game respected my time a little more.

I’m not a dev or a streamer. I’m just someone who cares and thought maybe this could help. If not, that’s fine.

TL;DR:

· The very first time anyone plays PoE2, the full story campaign is mandatory. No shortcuts, no choices. You learn the game.
· After that, for every new league or new character, you make a single choice at the start.
· Story Path: All 6 acts as normal. Slower, but more total loot.
· Interlude Path: All 6 acts condensed into fewer, tougher zones. Faster to Maps, less total loot, but the loot that drops is from higher-level enemies, so it tends to be better quality.
· Power from skill points stays exactly the same either way.
· The path you don’t pick is locked until endgame, then becomes a tough, optional Cruel campaign.
· Cruel acts hold extra weapon set points (up to 24 more, spread across quests) and give Cruelty Stacks.
· Each Cruelty Stack makes the whole game harder for that character, but also more rewarding. It’s a risk you choose to take. Nothing forced.

-–

Your first campaign ever is the only mandatory one

When someone plays Path of Exile 2 for the very first time, they play the full six-act story campaign. No forks. No Interludes visible. That’s how you learn the game and experience the world.

Once you’ve finished that first campaign and reached endgame, everything changes.

For every new league and every new character you make from then on, you’ll have a choice. You’ve earned it.

One choice, two paths

At the start of a new character, you pick your entire route:

· Story Path: The full six-act campaign. All zones, side content, everything. It takes longer, but you fight more monsters, which means more drops overall. You reach Maps with a larger stash.
· Interlude Path: A condensed six-act campaign. Fewer zones, but the enemies in those zones are slightly higher level. You get to Maps faster, and while you’ll see fewer total drops, the loot you do get comes from tougher enemies, so it tends toward higher rarity. Less quantity, but potentially better quality.

No matter which path you pick, you get the exact same 24 weapon set points and all normal skill points. Your character’s power when you hit endgame is identical. The trade-off is time versus the type of loot progression you prefer.

The path you don’t pick stays locked until you finish the campaign. Once you reach endgame, that locked path opens up as a Cruel campaign. No switching mid-run.

Even on the Interlude path, you won’t hit Maps with empty slots. You just won’t have the same pile of backup gear, currency, or crafting mats. Veterans who know how to make the most of higher-rarity drops can get a head start. SSF players might still prefer the safer, quantity-heavy Story stash.

(For group play: the party leader picks the route and everyone follows. If a new player hasn’t unlocked the choice yet, the option is greyed out and you all run Story. Not perfect, but workable.)

The Cruel campaign is seriously tough

The Cruel version of the path you skipped is a significant step up in difficulty. A character fresh out of the campaign—even a veteran’s Interlude run—won’t be able to clear it right away. You’ll need real gear, endgame progression, a solid build. It’s aspirational content. You’re meant to see it, maybe try once, realize you’re not ready, and come back later.

No timer. No rush. Just a goal.

Across all six Cruel acts, quests and objectives would reward extra weapon set points—up to 24 more in total, distributed just like the normal campaign does. You go in and earn them. Finishing each Cruel act also applies a permanent Cruelty Stack to your character.

Cruelty Stacks make the whole game harder

Each Cruelty Stack permanently increases difficulty and reward quality for that character everywhere—not just endgame, but everywhere. Enemies hit harder, but loot gets better. It’s like earning wanted stars in GTA: you ask for more, and the world pushes back harder. No toggle, no backing out. You’re choosing to live in a more dangerous world.

Because the difficulty is global, the remaining Cruel acts get harder as you gain stacks. The challenge escalates naturally. With greater power comes greater risk.

Stay at zero stacks and stay comfortable. Push through to the maximum, and maybe something special happens: a visual badge, a cutscene, a new pinnacle encounter on your Atlas. Prestige you actually earned.

Why 48 points isn’t just power creep

The core endgame—Maps, normal pinnacles—could and should stay balanced around the original 24 weapon set points. The extra 24 points are for players who willingly take on a much harder game. You don’t get them all at once; they’re spread across brutally difficult, completely optional content that many players might never finish.

What could you do with more points? The passive tree and weapon set trees are huge. Most builds would likely grab a few new keystones or quality-of-life. The real potential is in weapon set trees—more points there could open complex dual-build setups that aren’t possible now.

On the numbers

I haven’t run any spreadsheets. The exact tuning—how much higher level Interlude enemies are, how much harder enemies get per stack, the precise loot rarity scaling—needs real testing by people who know the game inside out. This is just a structural idea. The numbers come later.

Why this might fit PoE2

No content rots. Acts and bosses stay relevant forever. A new player’s first run is pure. Extra power is locked behind genuinely difficult, optional challenges, and difficulty scales with your ambition. Best of all, the whole thing could be tested as a league mechanic first. Low risk.
