No, xWOBA is not biased against pull hitters

Only posting this shit because it’s such a boring and slow off season. Someone do something already. And fuck the Dodgers.

Anyway back to the topic, people often parrot how xWOBA is oh so biased against pull hitters. Yet a look at the numbers show that this bias is very exaggerated, and only exists on the upper end of pull rates.

Using savant data I looked at qualified hitters in the past 3 years (post shift ban) and grouped them by pull rate. The numbers are as follows:

&gt;= 40% Pull Rate

WOBA .335

xWOBA .334

&gt;= 45% Pull Rate

WOBA .336

xWOBA .334

&gt;= 50% Pull Rate

WOBA .349

xWOBA .336

As you can see you only start to get meaningful bias at the highest end of pull rate. We are talking about 8 hitters who were in that group in the last 3 years by the way. Extrapolating this bias at the extreme end toward all other hitters, even those with above average pull rate, is a mistake.

As for why people think there’s this huge bias for pull hitters, I have a couple theories. The first is selection bias. People will point to hitters like Paredes, Altuve, J-Ram and say see! They have high pull and pulled fly rates and consistently overperform their xWOBA!

Of course this ignores all the cases where the opposite is true. Batters like Salvador Perez or Michael Massey (sorry Royals) who have above average career pull and pulled fly ball rates…yet have a lower career WOBA than xWOBA. Or how about Seiya Suzuki, who changed his all fields approach and had an above average pulled fly rate this year. Yet this was actually the only year in his career when he *underperformed* his xWOBA. Go figure.

The second reason is likely because pulled fly balls do overperform their xWOBA by a significant amount. So people just extrapolate that to mean pull hitters must also be underrated by xWOBA! But clearly it’s not that simple, you can’t do a one to one comparison between batted balls and hitters.

Couple reasons for this. One is that when you pull the ball alot, some of those are gonna be on the ground, especially since it’s harder to lift pulled balls. And pulled groundballs actually underperform their xWOBA unlike pulled fly balls. This is true even post shift ban! Cancels out the bias from pulled fly balls abit.

Another is that it’s hard to consistently try and pull the ball and in the air. Only a small handful of hitters manage to pull the ball =&gt; 50%. And when you change your approach to try and pull more, you open yourself up more on certain parts of the plate. Strikeouts can also tick up, because while the ideal homerun stroke might be 36 inches out in front, for contact it’s 30 inches in front.

This is also why I caution jumping to the conclusion that a player would be better if they just upped their pull fly rate. I’ve read comments about how Soto or Torres for instance underperform their xWOBA because they just need to pull the ball in the air more! But there are always trade offs in changing approach as we see. And plenty of hitters who underperform their xWOBA despite great pull and pulled fly ball rates. And simply having a high pulled fly rate does not mean you will be successful. Michael Massey for instance has a career 81 wRC+. And Seiya Suzuki had a lower wRC+ compared to his 2023 and 2024 seasons. Pulling the ball more might have helped his homerun stroke but it didn’t make him a more productive hitter. He used to be a BABIP god but had a measly .282 BABIP this year. And you know what pull rate is negativity correlated with? Yep, BABIP.

TLDR:

- You only see meaningful bias at the highest upper end of pull rate.

- Plenty of hitters with above average pull and pulled fly rates actually underperform their xWOBA. You can not just assume high pull rate or lack there of is the reason for a discrepancy between WOBA and xWOBA.

- Pulled fly balls might be very good and overperform their xWOBA by a significant amount. This does *not* mean changing your approach to hit more pulled fly balls will automatically make you a more productive hitter. There are always trade offs.
