[Study - Overcoming Misaligned Incentives: Evidence from the NBA Heave Rule] Before the reform, players heaved on 58% of opportunities; reluctance was concentrated among efficient shooters and players in contract years, as the model predicts. After the reform, the heave rate jumped to 94%.

A new paper from two Texas Tech professors examines the effects of the new heave rule.

Abstract:

Performance metrics can misalign individual and organizational incentives. We study a clean case: an NBA player holding the ball as a quarter expires must choose between a low-probability “heave” that can only help his team and protecting his shooting statistics. We model this decision as a metric-driven principal-agent problem and test it using play-by-play data from 2015–16 through 2025–26, exploiting the 2025–26 Heave Rule, which removed the individual statistical penalty for end-of-quarter heaves. Before the reform, players heaved on 58 percent of opportunities; reluctance was concentrated among efficient shooters and players in contract years, as the model predicts. After the reform, the heave rate jumped to 94 percent, the efficiency gradient collapsed, and difference-in-differences estimates using the untreated fourth quarter confirm the effect is sharp, immediate, and smallest among the players with the least efficiency to protect. Removing a metric distortion realigned individual behavior with team objectives almost completely.

More:

There are three immediate implications for our results. First, our results show that in order to fix misaligned incentives, policymakers and executives must structurally shift risk away from the individual. Second, we show that when agents do not have to face reputational costs, they can be incentivized to engage in actions even if there is little-to-no chance of success. This demonstrates that structural decoupling mitigates information asymmetry, allowing for more efficient behavioral allocations. Finally, we further demonstrate that regulatory intervention dismantles behavior detrimental to the collective goals of the organization
