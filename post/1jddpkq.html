An NBA fan’s quick guide to NCAA Tournament prospects

The NCAA Tournament (March Madness) starts this week. There’s no shortage of information available about the field. If you wanted to, you could read a full page on Alabama State or Robert Morris’s roster.

But since this is an NBA sub, I wanted to quickly summarize the top pro prospects in the field. Notably: these prospects are all freshmen so I didn’t even bother including that class distinction. (Another note: some top prospects, like Rutgers’ freshmen Dylan Harper and Ace Bailey, did not qualify for the tournament so they are not included either.)

PF Cooper Flagg, Duke, ranked # 1 on ESPN

Coming into the year, I thought Cooper Flagg may have been a little overhyped – especially as an offensive centerpiece. Through his freshman year, he’s proven most of those doubters like me wrong with a great all-around season. He’s even shot the ball well (36.8% from three, 83.0% from FT) to go along with his basketball IQ and defensive impact (1.5 steals, 1.3 blocks). He may not have MVP upside, but he’s an all-around stud who doesn’t have many holes to his game. And what’s especially impressive about his polish is that he’s young for his class (a full year younger than many of these freshman peers.)

SG V.J. Edgecombe, Baylor, ranked # 4 on espn

After playing well for the Bahamian national team in Olympic qualifiers, V.J. Edgecombe has followed that up with a good freshman season for Baylor (15.0 PPG). His signature trait is his explosion and athleticism, which he’s used to garner an awesome 2.1 steals and 0.6 blocks from the SG position. The biggest question mark with him is whether he can still dominate with that athleticism in the NBA given his limited size (at 6’5”, 190).

SG Tre Johnson, Texas, ranked # 5 on espn

A baby-faced assassin, Tre Johnson has been lighting it up for 19.8 points per game on good splits (39.1% from three, 88.7% from FT). Pundits have been on his case about not doing much else (3.0 rebounds, 2.8 assists), but he does have better-than-you’d-think size at 6’6” so he appears to be a viable starting SG.

PG Kasparas Jakucionis, ranked # 6 on espn

The Lithuanian started off his freshman year red hot, with stats that could rival analytical darling Reed Sheppard from last year. Since then, he’s cooled off considerably (down to 32.9% from three), but he still projects as a starting lead guard. Your hope is that his size at 6’6” allows him to adjust to the NBA more easily than Sheppard did.

C Khaman Maluach, Duke, ranked # 7 on espn

The African gives you about what you’d expect from a 7’2” center: with averages of 8.2 points, 6.7 rebounds, and 1.2 blocks (in 20.8 minutes of action). His stats are about the same as what Mark Williams gave Duke as a freshman, although Maluach shows a hint of stretch potential and shouldn’t fail any physicals.

SG Kon Knueppel, Duke, ranked #8 on espn

Kon Knueppel (pronounced “ka-nipple”) looks the part of a stereotypical spacer. There was some thought that he may be more than that early in the season. ESPN draft guru Jonathan Givony watched the Blue Devils in training camp and said that Knueppel was “easily” their best player, even ahead of Cooper Flagg. But when the lights came on, Knueppel took a backseat and played like… the stereotypical spacer. He’s money from deep (39.7% from three, 91.5% from the line), but didn’t get the chance to do more much than that. But hey, if it turns out that he’s just a taller (6’7”) and sturdier Luke Kennard, then he’d still have value to an NBA team.

PG Jeremiah Fears, Oklahoma, ranked # 9 on espn

A fairly big (6’4”) and long-armed guard, Jeremiah Fears attacks the basket well and draws 6.3 free throws a game. In a world where Shai Gilgeous-Alexander may win MVP, that could be an appealing trait for teams. Fears has more downside than that though because his shooting (27.5% from deep) and PG instincts (4.1 AST / 3.5 TO) still lag behind his scoring.

SG Egor Demin, BYU, ranked # 10 on espn

Billed as a 6’9” point guard coming out of Russia, there was a lot of hype about Egor Demin to start the year. That’s cooled down some though, because there are doubts about his shooting (27.8% from three, 67.1% from the line) and about whether or not he’s a true point guard at all. There’s a natural comp to Josh Giddey right now, for better or worse.

C Derik Queen, Maryland, ranked # 11 on espn

The 6’10” freshman doesn’t jump off the screen with his athleticism, but he’s been among the most productive players in the country with averages of 15.7 points, 9.2 rebounds, 1.1 steals, and 1.0 blocks per game. He has a good feel for the game.

SF Liam McNeeley, UConn, ranked # 12 on espn

It’s required by law to comp a white shooter to another white shooter – and in Liam McNeeley’s case the most common comp of Corey Kispert probably does make sense. He’s a good shooter (86.0% from FT) who has solid height at 6’8”.

PF Asa Newell, Georgia, ranked # 20 on espn

A skinny springboard, the 6’10” forward has a good amount of energy and athleticism. His skills will need to be polished if he’s going to end up as a starter though.

SG Jase Richardson, Michigan State, ranked # 24 on espn

If you’re too young to remember former Michigan State SG and Dunk Contest champ Jason Richardson, you’re missing out, because he was lightning in a bottle and a poor man’s Vince Carter. But if you’re too young to remember him, you can live through it with his two sons. Older brother Jase has been an efficient scorer in his 24.3 minutes a night (and younger brother Jaxon is a high school stud coming down the pike later on.) Jase has been climbing the draft boards and could crack the top 10 with a good tournament.
