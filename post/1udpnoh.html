Lost in the Celtics roster discussion: player development

I am astounded by some of the negativity regarding the Celtics roster of late, and I think it’s reflective of recency bias following the ugly first round exit. There are some asserting that the Celtics are not contenders barring major changes in 2026-27, which I think is overly negative. The most obvious reason for optimism is the possibility for a fully healthy Tatum with no restrictions. But there is a second, more frequently overlooked, factor: the age of the Celtics’ key role players.

Please consider:

- Pritchard: Took a major leap last year and is 28. Signed through 2027-28.

- Gonzalez: Contributed almost immediately, proved he can defend multiple positions at an NBA level, and is 20. Signed through 2028-29.

- Walsh: Arguably the Celtics’ best on-ball defender, took a significant leap this past season, and is 22. Contract expires after this upcoming season.

- Scheierman: Similar, took major steps last season to become a key rotation player, and is 25. Signed through 2027-28.

- Queta: Was out of the rotation in 2024-25, and just finished his first season with real playing time in which he was discussed as one of the better starting centers in the league. Just experienced his first real NBA postseason. About to turn 27. Expires after this upcoming season.

A team with 1) two top-10 or top-15 players; 2) a supporting cast that includes White, Hauser, and the ascending young players mentioned above; and 3) whatever the team is able to add with the full MLE or trade exception, absolutely should have enough talent to compete for a championship. That’s especially the case if one assumes the continued development of those young role players, many of whom just completed their first real season in an NBA rotation.
