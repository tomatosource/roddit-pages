The death of the 14-year megadeal?

The way free agent megadeals have mostly worked is: you pay $25M per year for like 12 years. At the beginning of the deal you get a steal. At the end, the contract is underwater and hampers your team. That’s the ultimate root cause of “competitive windows”.

LAD and NYM ask: “Why should it work like that?”

Well, the player can get \(300M on the open market. And you're a billionaire, so you can pay him that. But you can't pay him what he's worth in his good years. In his good years he's worth like 6-8 WAR. That'd be \)50-70M per year. You can’t pay that.

LAD and NYM: “Why not?”

You’d spend 25% of your payroll before the luxury tax on one player. And you have to have 25 other guys. A baseball team needs like 5 star-level players to win. After you built the rest of the roster, the luxury tax bill would be insane. So you need to distribute the $300M out over a long time.
