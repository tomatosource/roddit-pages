Jose Ramirez’s new contract extension gives Cleveland some short-term payroll space. What will they do with it?

Per MLBTR:

Ramirez will earn an even \(25MM in each of the seven seasons from 2026-32, but with \)10MM in deferred money each year.  This means that the \(69MM Ramirez was initially slated to earn from 2026-28 has now been bumped down to \)45MM in upfront money, giving the Guardians some extra flexibility to perhaps make other short-term roster additions.

In other words, this deal gives them $24MM to spend over the next 3 years.

Total luxury tax payroll was at about \(102MM, down from \)125MM in 2025. Subtracting the \(8MM from the renegotiated extension, is it fair to assume that the Guardians have \)30MM in one-year payroll space for 2026? Has Ramirez signed another team-friendly deal knowing that the money will be used to build the team?

What they could use most is an outfielder and a middle infielder, especially one with a right-handed bat. The middle infield market is pretty barren at this point (Kiner-Falefa, Rengifo), but the outfield has some inexpensive bats that fit the bill (Andujar, Hays).

However, I can’t really see where they plan to reinvest the money this late into the offseason. Perhaps a two-year deal for Luis Arraez, who could play 2B passably alongside a good defensive shortstop like Arias/Rocchio and also pitch in at 1B/DH?

The trade market also exists, but nobody on it is making so much money that the Guardians had to up their payroll space from around \(22MM to around \)30MM.

They could also use the money to add to the strengths of their team, which would make their team pretty unbalanced but still improve it; the staff could use an ace, as could any, and there’s no harm in adding to the bullpen.

Finally, they could penny pinch and not use the money for anything. What could they do that makes sense?
