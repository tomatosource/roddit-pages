[OC] 99 Cent Store: forwards who may be potential bargains

The NBA offseason is always filled with exciting storylines like star free agents and blockbuster trades (and surprising GM firings!)

But rather than dwell on the obvious, this series intends to do the opposite: focus on the lower-profile free agents who may have some value to teams. No NBA player is actually “99 Cents,” of course, but these are all players who may be bargains based on their perceived market. We try to organize the listing in terms of tiers. “Featured items” may get $10M a year, while “clearance rack” listings tend to be players on the fringes of NBA rosters who may take minimum deals.

This “99 Cent Store” series has been open for business since 2018. In the past, we’ve highlighted reserves like Fred VanVleet and Isaiah Hartenstein among others prior to their breakouts. Not all of the items turn out to be gems, but the returns have been mostly positive so far. Let’s see if we can keep that legacy going this season.

######99 cent store

PF Jake LaRavia, Sacramento, UFA, 24 years old

NBA general managers don’t like college vets, so it’s impressive that Jake LaRavia managed to parlay 3 years on campus (2 at Indiana State and 1 at Wake Forest) into the 19th pick in his draft.

LaRavia hasn’t made many waves during his three years in the NBA so far. In fact, he’s only started 6 of his 136 games between his initial stint in Memphis and his recent trip to Sacramento (post midseason trade).

Still, you can squint and see the potential here. LaRavia doesn’t have a flashy game or one stand-out skill, but his calling card is being an all-around player. Per 36 minutes, he’s averaged a solid 13.2 points, 6.3 rebounds, 3.3 assists, and 1.4 steals, which is right around where he sat in college as well.

For LaRavia, the big “swing” skill is his scoring efficiency. He was below average during his first two seasons (53.1% and 54.0% true shooting) but took a big jump up this past season with 59.1%. Is that real? Or is that a mirage? It’s an important distinction. If it’s real, then he can potentially develop into a starter or at least a high-level rotation player. If he falls back to Earth, then he should fall back to the bench.

Clearly, the Grizzlies weren’t bowled over by him. They declined his extension option and sent him packing to Sacramento. Personally, I’m a little more optimistic. Sometimes, players who take a while to break out in college take some time to break out in the NBA as well. And what’s also appealing in regards to LaRavia is that the Grizz declining his option made him an unrestricted free agent (as opposed to RFA), opening up his freedom of movement this summer.

potential fits

SAC. The most likely scenario would be Jake LaRavia staying put. The Kings have gotten solid play from Trey Lyles over the last few years, but LaRavia may have more upside in the tank.

BKN. Rebuilding teams like Brooklyn and Washington can take a gamble on a player like LaRavia since they don’t have much to lose. He also fits with the ball-moving style they appear to be building.

LAC. It’s hard to see this working financially, but the Clippers could use a younger SF/PF to buoy the rotation. Nic Batum is 36 and may retire soon, and Kawhi Leonard isn’t exactly an iron man.

######clearance rack

SF Drew Peterson, Boston, RFA two-way, 25 years old

We’re jumping all the way down to the clearance rack, but staying on theme with another late bloomer college player of a particular shade. Drew Peterson averaged only 5 points per game as a freshman at Rice, but eventually worked his way up (after 3 years at Rice, 2 years at USC) to the NBA radar.

To be fair, he’s still barely a blip on the radar. He didn’t even get drafted. He isn’t even the world’s most famous “Drew Peterson” (still second to the murderer). But he’s intriguing nonetheless. Like Jake LaRavia, he’s a versatile 6’8” forward with an all-around game. This past season in the G-League (15 games), he averaged 20.0 points, 6.8 rebounds, and 5.9 assists.

Currently in the Boston system, Peterson would only get freed up if there’s a roster crunch (or if he’s traded and waived by a new team). Still, he’s a player to keep an eye on.

SF Jordan Nwora, Anadolu Efes, 26 years old

Drew Peterson is still waiting for his NBA chance, but Jordan Nwora already had – and may have blown – his own. He got almost 30 total starts in his first three years in the league, but didn’t make the most of them. Billed as a 6’8” 3+D wing out of Louisville, he mostly struggled on the defensive end. He always looked a step slow and a little out of shape (potentially injury related). In fact, Nwora has stumbled out of the league entirely.

However, there are still some desirable tools here. He’s a solid shooter (37.6% from 3 in the league) with some size. Since leaving the NBA, he’s played pretty well for Team Nigeria and for the Anadolu Efes (in the Turkish league). If you were casting a wide net and looking for an end-of-the-bench player, you could do worse.

SG/SF Dylan Windler, Perth Wildcats, 27 years old

Former NBA player Dylan Windler has followed a similar career path to Jordan Nwora. He had some opportunities early on, but he ended up falling out of the league. Windler may have had a better excuse, as he lost momentum after a serious leg injury in 2020. Nowadays, he’s playing overseas (and just re-signed with Perth in the Australian league).

I don’t know his buyout situation, but Windler is the type of player who could be worth a bench spot in the NBA. He’s only an OK shooter, but he tries hard and crashes the glass well (7.0 rebounds per 36) for a 6’6” wing. There are shades of a poor man’s John Konchar here. And again, if that’s not enough for you, then go shop on Madison Avenue, big shot, because this is the 99 Cent Store.
