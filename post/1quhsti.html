Attempting to roster the best historical team based on names or nicknames that have to do with weather phenomena. Is this it?

At first I thought to add a constraint that a qualifying name or nickname could only be used once, but if we did that we’d be without a catcher, shortstop, or second baseman. Having said that, the team below is chosen based on the following criteria:

  • Weather-ly name or nickname (obviously)
  • Every position must be represented, and the bench has to be more than just great pinch-hitters (e.g. everyone needs a backup, even if it’s someone rotating positions among starters to make room for a bench guy)
  • Starting pitchers must have been real starters when they were good
  • 26-man roster limits, including constraints on number of pitchers
  • 5-man rotation, regardless of an included pitcher’s era, and no openers (obviously some pitchers racked up huge WAR totals because the rotations were smaller, but I’m not calculating that issue)

#Notes

  • Perez and Marte made the team by default: there are no other catchers or second basemen, and in this case, both are nicknamed “El Niño”, which is a nickname doing a lot of work on this list.
  • Donaldson is the backup catcher because he had experience as a catcher (408 games in the minors) and there isn’t another catcher available.
  • Ramirez and Tatis Jr. are either the SS or backup SS, and both are generously backing up 2B (both have exactly 11 games total professional experience, all but one in the minors; even Donaldson only had 3 games in the minors). The infield on this team will hit, but it’s feast or famine for defense. Amed Rosario is yet another “El Niño” if you want more infield flexibility, but he’s bad enough (even on defense) that I’m willing to give up his flexibility.
  • Flood is the true CF backup, Crawford is yet another corner OF but can provide pinch-running and late defense support, and Werth is on the team because he plays all 3 OF spots and because he’s one of the few Sunshines.
  • RP were hard to come by, so it’s closer-by-committee, with Weathers as the veteran default (75 career saves). The rest of the pen is filled out with guys with experience as both starters or relievers, or else leftover starters (easily the biggest position pool). LaMarr Hoyt, for instance, had a short career but won a Cy Young award. Joe Williams has limited data, but is in the HOF.
  • There are, by my count, 3 HOFers, 1 shoo-in, and 1 or 2 snubs (depending on Flood).
