Ducking Under the Tax Threshold Prolongs Our Rise Back to Contention

With the report that we’re trying to get under the tax threshold, we’re looking at potentially 3 years until we’re contending again.

We’re repeat offenders and would need to be under the tax threshold at least two consecutive years to reset it. Next season the threshold is \(187M, and then \)206M the season after.

To get under the threshold this year we’d need to trade:

A) Simons (\(27M) -&gt; 2 roster spots left to fill \)14M

B) Hauser & Niang (\(10M & \)8M) -&gt; 3 roster spots left to fill $5M

C) Derrick/Jaylen (\(28M / \)53M) -&gt; 2 roster spots left to fill \(15M/\)40M

What some people are overlooking is that by ducking the tax, we’d have to dump our second most-valuable contracts all in the name of saving money. In two years when we can go over the tax again, we won’t have those contracts to trade for a high-level role player/third star. No smart GM will value minimum contracts like Queta, Baylor, or whoever we get back by dumping Simons/Hauser/Niang.

We’re going to have to rely on either trading our draft picks in a trade when we want to contend again, or start hitting our draft picks to infuse this team with cheap talent.

If you look at teams under the tax last season, it’s: Cavs, Pacers, and OKC who were real contenders. Celtics, Wolves, Knicks, Lakers, Nuggets, Warriors were all over the tax.

What do the Pacers and OKC have in common? Highly productive rookie-scale or lower-level contracts, and it took them a while to get to this point. Pacers and OKC were literally the first pair of non-taxpaying teams to make the finals.

In my opinion it’s kind of a bad sign that new ownership is immediately trying to duck the tax (assuming Himmelsbach report is credible), even if Tatum is out next season.
