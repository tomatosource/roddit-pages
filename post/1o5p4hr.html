Ready for their Close-Up (?) : 6 anonymous players who may be important this season

The upcoming season should has plenty of superstars to focus on, but here are some former no names who may step into the spotlight and provide a critical role themseves.

PG Ryan Rollins and SG A.J. Green, Milwaukee Bucks

The idea of Giannis Antetokounmpo wearing a New York Knicks jersey may partly be a media-created fantasy, but there’s no doubt that the Milwaukee Bucks and their star may be facing a crossroads. The Bucks won the title in 2022, but it’s been a slow decline since then. Now 30 years old, Antetokounmpo hasn’t shown many signs of aging yet himself, but the roster around him certainly has. The veteran supporting cast has withered and splintered and mostly been sent packing, leaving little in its place. In fact, the team has lost their last 4 playoff series (which means 3 straight R1 losses). Is there enough left here to contend?

The Bucks notably added Myles Turner to replace Brook Lopez, but they still needed plenty of help. And for the first time in quite a while, they’re going to tap into their farm system in order to provide it. Right now, PG Ryan Rollins and SG A.J. Green are slated to play a major role in saving basketball in Milwaukee. Neither were expected to be here. Rollins is a former R2 pick, while A.J. Green didn’t get drafted at all. Still, these no names from nowhere (Northern Iowa and Toledo, respectively) have shown flashes of skill in their three seasons in their limited roles. Rollins is a long-armed (6’9” wingspan) defender, while Green is a dead-eye shooter (42.1% from three in his career). If they can show provide some lift and some hope for the team going forward, then maybe a Antetokounmpo trade isn’t inevitable after all.

Of course, for that to happen, coach Doc Rivers will have to evolve and develop young players, which is something that he’s struggled with a lot over the past few decades. Right now, the roster doesn’t give him much other choice. To keep the title window open, they may have to trust the kids.

C Neemias Quata (BOS) and C Isaiah Jackson (IND)

The Milwaukee Bucks’ shelf life as a title contender had an obvious expiration date, given all the age around Giannis Antetokounmpo. In contrast, the young Boston Celtics and Indiana Pacers were supposed to have a wide open runway and 5+ years of greatness left in them.

That is: until their superstars got hurt. With Jayson Tatum and Tyrese Haliburton slated to miss the entire season, it’s shaping up to be a “lost year” for both Boston and Indiana. They may compete for the playoffs or play-in based on culture and muscle memory alone, but they may ultimately decide to throw in the towel and punt on 2025-26 as well.

That decision may depend heavily on two unheralded big men down low.

With Kristaps Porzingis and Al Horford gone, the Celtics may give starter’s minutes to Neemias Queta. And personally, I’m intrigued to see what he can do with them. Queta was one of the best rim protectors in college (at Utah State), and has shown flashes in the NBA so far. He’s averaged 12.9 points, 10.7 rebounds, and 2.0 blocks per 36. If he can stay out of foul trouble and come close to that, he’ll team with Chris Boucher for a lengthy frontcourt platoon. If those two can anchor the Celtics’ defense, then there’s enough talent elsewhere to stay in the playoff mix.

Similarly, Isaiah Jackson’s surprisingly been thrust into a large role himself in Indiana (after Myles Turner’s signing in Milwaukee). It may be asking a lot of the 23 year old. He was athletic but raw to start with, and now he’ll be coming off an Achilles injury to boot. If he can get back to 100%, maybe the Pacers can stay in the play-in range as well. If not, they’ll probably have to patch together minutes with Pascal Siakam or Obi Toppin at center, the latter of which isn’t ideal defensively.

PG Aaron Holiday (HOU) and C Jock Landale (MEM)

“Next man up”! That adage will be tested again, this time with some more unexpected and more recent injuries.

A few weeks ago, the Houston Rockets had the look of a title contender. They had an aggressive defense already baked in, and finally added some reliable half-court shooting with Kevin Durant. Unfortunately, PG Fred VanVleet got hurt and will now miss the season. That leaves the Rockets’ dangerously thin in terms of perimeter punch. KD is great and all, but the Rockets will now be without their top 3 three-point shooters from last season (FVV, Jalen Green, and Dillon Brooks). Someone needs to step up to help.

The most obvious candidate for that should be Reed Sheppard, the recent # 3 pick who had some of the best advanced stats of all time as a freshman at Kentucky. The theoretical Reed Sheppard (elite shooter and disruptive defender) is exactly what the doctor ordered here. The Reed Sheppard that showed up didn’t look like that as a rookie, seemingly overwhelmed by NBA athleticism. If Sheppard can’t take a sophomore leap, then a lot of that burden will fall on journeyman Aaron Holiday to hold the fort.

Is Holiday up to that task? At age 29, he’s only started 33 games in his career so far. He overcame a mediocre start to his career and developed into a solid but “low upside” backup (40% from 3 last year), but it’ll be interesting to see if he can do more than that when given the chance.

Solid and low-upside also describes the career of journeyman backup center Jock Landale. Some may have even forgotten the 29-year-old Australian was still in the league. He’s more of a heady, floor-bound veteran that most would consider a 3rd center. Memphis may have to test whether he can be more than that after injuries hit both Jaren Jackson Jr. and Zach Edey. We’ll probably see a lot of smallball centers like Brandon Clarke and maybe Santi Aldama in the meantime, but a traditional center like Landale may need to play a lot more than expected. And for Memphis’ purposes, he’ll need to play decently well. The Western Conference is going to be brutal, so there’s not much margin for error here.
