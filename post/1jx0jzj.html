They said they wanted to know what’s wrong with Armour before they could fix it. Well, I’ll just tell you!

Before I start, if for some reason you think Armour is OK even after I explain what’s wrong with it, I don’t know what to say. It’s so completely objectively indefensible that saying it’s OK just communicates to me that you’re extremely susceptible to Stockholm Syndrome or something. Feel free to do so, I don’t want to shut down any critiques of my take here, but just know that’s how I’m going to perceive it.

ANYWAY, Amour has two major problems.

Problem 1: It doesn’t work linearly enough to be viable in hybrid.

For those that don’t know, the Armour formula in PoE 2 works exactly the same as it does in Dark Souls / Elden Ring, where it’s called Defense. People usually misinterpret it as a “flat damage reduction” but it’s actually not.

The basic summary is that it’s designed to either punish small hits, or reward larger hits, depending on your perspective. Basically Armour reduces the damage of a hit by a larger percentage the smaller the hit is.

The issue is that it’s very exponential and works more on a breakpoint. A hit will do basically zero damage until a certain point where the formula will basically “let it go” and stops affecting it by much at all. (The formula is actually MORE than exponential.)

So a hit from 1 to 100 damage might be reduced by 99%, basically nullifying it, but then a hit that’s 120 damage might only have 65% taken off. So 120 damage seems like 20% more damage from 100, but in reality it’s nearly 42 TIMES LARGER. Then at 200 damage hit basically has 1% or less is reduced.

In practice, when a player is using it, it’s designed around reducing tons of tiny little mosquito bites.

The result is it working more on a breakpoint system, where either you have enough Armour to reduce the mosquito bites for the area you are in after heavy investment, or you don’t. Meaning a hybrid character is far more likely to never hit that breakpoint, rendering it useless even in the context of mosquito bites.

Compare this to Energy Shield and Evasion. Let’s say a robe has 400 ES and a leather tunic has 40% Evasion. If a hybrid piece has 200 ES and 20% evasion, you can see how those values are linearly scaling and it’s easy to wrap your head around how you’ll be protected.

4 out of the 6 characters using Armour will use it in hybrid.

(Another note on this, the developers have already said that the mosquito bite tier of enemy in PoE 2 almost doesn’t even exist, contributing to Armour being almost totally useless everywhere anyway.)

Problem 2: All the little conditionals add up.

Not only is Armour ONLY physical damage, and ONLY mosquito bites, and ONLY after heavy investment to reach that breakpoint, it ALSO gives you a MOVEMENT SPEED PENALTY.

Obviously switching to a more linearly scaling formula will solve most of those issues, but it being only physical hits with a movement speed penalty still make it too conditional.

Here’s my solution to reworking Armour:

Make the formula more linear. It can interact with accuracy in some way like Evasion if that’s desired. Then, add some kind of bleed and poison resistance to it, kind of like the inverse of Energy Shield.

You make it like that, where people can understand what the heck it’s doing and are confident it will work on their hybrid character, and also make it not so conditional with the extra chaos help, and I think people are going to be really happy with it.

Ultimately half the characters are going to use Armour at a baseline, and a casual player is going to look at the passive tree and see “more Armour” and think that node will make them die less, when in reality it’s one of the most convoluted and complex defensive layers in the entire game.

(And stop saying we need more defensive layers as a fix for Armour. More defensive layers just means ES and Evasion characters get extra defensive layers to choose from and Armour characters require them. Armour is a core defensive layer than needs to be fixed first. Also it HAS a second defensive layer in the form of BLOCK.)
