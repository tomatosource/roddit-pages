The most unlikely ultimate grand slams

TL;DR - the two 2026 ultimate grand slams were both the most likely and the most unlikely ones ever hit.

Yesterday, Kody Clemens, hit an ultimate grand slam with 2 outs and on an 0-2 count. It was immediately pointed out that this was the most dire situation you can find yourself in. Out of curiosity, I went digging to see how likely previous ultimate grand slams were.

First, I found counts/pitch sequences for as many of these as I could. I was successful in finding 33 counts and 32 pitch sequences (I couldn’t find the sequence for Del Crandall’s 1955 slam on a full count.) All my sources can be found in this Google Sheet I made.

Some notes. The most common counts for ultimate grand slams to occur on are 0 - 0 (12x, batters before 1970 loved jumping on that first pitch), 1 - 0 (5x), 2 - 2 (4x), and 3 - 2 (4x). The least common counts are 3 - 0 and 1 - 2, neither of which have ever been achieved.

I believe this distribution makes sense. The more strikes, the likelier it is that the pitcher is hard to hit. The more balls, the likelier it is that the pitcher is lacking in control. Too hard to hit and the batter is likely to not be able to hit a home run. Too lacking in control and the batter is likely to try for the certainty of a bases-loaded walk over a potentially luck-based ball in play. So the most ideal counts would hypothetically be where the two factors are balanced.

At the same time this is only a sample size of 35 homers so uhh, perhaps I’m just being small-sample-size’d. In any case, from there, as an informal estimate for how impressive an ultimate grand slam in a specific scenario is, I took the 2021-2024 run expectancy for outs given bases loaded courtesy of Fangraphs and the 2015 run expectancy for counts courtesy of Jim Albert, and I added them together for each slam.

Of course, this is not a scientific study of run expectancy in these situations. RE on different counts/outs almost certainly cannot be added independently, and also these values change year over year. Additionally, this is agnostic to the number of runs already given up by the pitcher. However, I’m only looking for what intuitively feels like the most impressive grand slams to us in the modern day, purely in a vacuum, and I believe this is a fair estimate. Here’s the table:

While there aren’t any general trends I can spot, there is something funny about the two 2026 ultimate grand slams: by this measure, the two such slams this season were both the most unlikely one we’ve ever had, and the most likely one we’ve ever had. And it will continue to be until we have someone crazy enough to decide to swing 3 - 0, bases loaded with none out.
