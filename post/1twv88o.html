What Happened to Julio Rodriguez’s Defense: An Analysis

In 2025, Julio Rodriguez was the 6th best defender in CF by both OAA (10) and DRS (9). While not in the tier of the elite CF defenders (PCA, Cedanne Rafaela, Victor Scott, Jacob Young), he was clearly a plus defender out there. The results were similar in 2024 (8 OAA, 3 DRS) and in 2023 (11 OAA, -5 DRS?). Over that entire time frame, from 2023-2025, he graded out as the 6th best defender in CF.

In 2026, he’s ranked as the worst CF defender by OAA (-5), and 7th worst by DRS (-4).

So what happened?

Before we start diving in, I should add that, while defensive stats take ~3 years to stabilize when evaluating a player’s true talent level, they do a pretty good job at telling us what has already happened. I don’t think Julio turned into a bad defender overnight. It would take a lot more innings to tell if that is the case, but by both the eye test and the stats, he’s been bad this year.

When trying to figure out what is happening with a player on defense, Statcast is the place to start. Julio has slowed down a little bit from last year, from a 29.2 ft/s spring speed down to 28.7 ft/s. He’s still a plus runner (88th percentile), but has slowed down from his elite speed days in his early career. Losing a little speed will hurt, but 88th percentile sprint speed is still plenty to be a good to great CF defender.

Then perhaps he’s getting bad reads on fly balls all of a sudden? But again, he’s been pretty in line with his career norms this season. His reaction has always been meh, but both his burst and his routes have been pluses. These numbers are all in line with what he did last year, although, like his sprint speed, they are down from his highs in 2023⁄24.

So he’s getting roughly the same jump on the ball and has roughly the same speed to get there. Last year, he was 0 or positive in run value moving every direction. This year, he’s 0 or negative in every direction. So this also isn’t a case of being bad at one type of batted ball and then getting an inordinate amount of those hit with the early-season small sample size constraints.

tl;dr Julio’s underlying defensive stats appear about the same as last year, but he has turned a number of easy catches into hits by attempting awkward or ill-advised catch motions that result in balls bouncing off his glove or landing when he is in a bad position.
