[OC] In Pursuit of Un-Mediocrity: Forecasting the Timberwolves’ Quest to No Longer Be the Worst Team in NBA History: Is This the Year?

This is a huge year for the Minnesota Timberwolves. Yes, we have our sights set on a third straight Western Conference Finals appearance and our first ever NBA Finals appearance, but we also have a goal that some may say is even more important: no longer being the worst team in league history. Many of you will recall that two years ago the Timberwolves passed the Tampa Bay Buccaneers in total regular-season win percentage, pulling us out of last place of all franchises in the Big 4 men’s leagues. With the NBA season kicking off tonight, we remain in last place for all NBA teams, but there’s a chance that could change this season. I figured I’d check in on our pursuit of greatness. Spoiler alert: it might come down to the wire.

The Timberwolves currently have a record of 1,196 and 1,680 losses, good for a winning percentage of .416. I suppose “good” isn’t the right word to use there, but I digress. Directly ahead of us stand the Nets (.425; 1,680-2,270), Clippers (.426; 1,893-2,549), and Hornets (.427; 1,193-1,602). Let’s run the numbers using the extremely scientific projection method of assuming each team wins the same amount of games this year and see how things could look by the end of the year:

Timberwolves projected 2025-2026 record: 49-33

Timberwolves updated win percentage: .4209 (1,245-1,713)

Nets projected 2025-2026 record: 26-56

Nets updated win percentage: .4231 (1,706-2,326)

Clippers projected 2025-2026 record: 50-32

Clippers updated win percentage: .4295 (1,943-2,581)

Hornets projected 2025-2026 record: 19-63

Hornets updated win percentage: .4213 (1,212-1,665)

So as you see, the Timberwolves have a very real shot at catching the Hornets, currently 4th worst, by the end of the year. When I originally did this math I was going to round the Timberwolves record to .421 but decided to keep it as .4209 because 420, but turns out in this projection that extra decimal really did matter, as otherwise I would have ended up with .421 for both teams. Obviously the teams could vary widely in how they play versus last year, currently the Clippers and Wolves are projected to be about as good as last year, while expectations for the Nets are slightly worse and for the Hornets slightly better. This will be an interesting side angle to keep track of this year, and now we know that as Timberwolves fans we have to tune in to root for whoever is playing the Hornets.

Other interesting wrinkles:

-As more longstanding teams with more total games, the Nets and Clippers win percentages are affected less by any one win or loss than the Timberwolves and Hornets. This means that every time the Clippers and Timberwolves both win, we will creep ever so slightly closer to them, but it also means that losses by the Nets don’t have as much impact on our quest as losses by the Hornets.

-This is just NBA games, so it doesn’t include the Nets’ ABA years, when they were one of the best teams in that league and won two championships. So even if we pass the Nets it still might be hard to make the case that they are the more pitiful franchise all-time. None of these 4 teams has an NBA championship.

-On a similar note, the Buccaneers have 2 Super Bowl wins, so it’s hard for us to talk trash to them. But you know who doesn’t have any Super Bowl wins? The Jacksonville Jaguars. Off to a 4-3 record this season, they currently boast an all-time record of .4196, well within striking distance.

-The obvious caveat for the Clippers is the pending Kawhii investigation. It seems unlikely to make a big difference this year, but if they get hit hard by the league that will ease our path towards overtaking them in the near future.
