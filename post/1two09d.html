Hexblast has been left behind for too long the skill needs a serious mechanical rework

I want to bring attention to Hexblast because after several leagues, it feels like the skill has completely fallen out of the game. This is not simply a balance issue or a matter of numerical tuning. The core problem is that Hexblast feels mechanically outdated, clunky, and unrewarding compared to almost every other endgame archetype currently available.

Since the major changes that removed the old curse detonation gameplay, Hexblast has never really recovered. While there have been some adjustments and small buffs over time, none of them addressed the fundamental issue: the skill is simply not enjoyable to play in modern PoE2 endgame.

The biggest problem is the amount of setup required for such a small reward.

To deal damage, a Hexblast player must:

  1. Apply a curse.
  2. Wait for the curse conditions to be met.
  3. Cast Hexblast.
  4. Stand still during a long cast animation.
  5. Hope the target remains in range and alive long enough for the payoff.

Meanwhile, most modern builds can simply press one button and instantly clear packs or deal boss damage while maintaining mobility and survivability.

This creates a massive gap in efficiency. Hexblast is effectively paying a “complexity tax” without receiving a meaningful reward in return.

The issue is not that Hexblast cannot technically clear content. With enough investment, almost any skill can complete the Atlas and kill bosses. The question should not be “Can it clear the game?” but rather “Why would anyone choose it over alternatives?”

Right now, there is almost no answer to that question.

The skill feels slow.

The skill feels awkward.

The skill feels delayed.

The skill feels unresponsive.

And perhaps most importantly, the skill no longer feels powerful.

Even after the increase to the maximum number of explosions, the gameplay loop remains fundamentally unchanged. The clear speed is still heavily limited by curse application and setup time. Bossing still feels significantly worse than many competing builds. Mapping feels like extra work for average results.

This is reflected in player adoption. Hexblast has become one of the least discussed and least represented spell archetypes in the endgame community. It is extremely rare to see Hexblast among popular ladder builds, build showcases, or meta discussions. The skill has effectively disappeared from the conversation.

When a skill remains unpopular for multiple leagues in a row, it is usually a sign that something deeper is wrong than simple damage numbers.

Many players who enjoyed Hexblast originally were attracted to its unique playstyle. It was a curse-based chaos spell with a satisfying payoff. It had a distinct identity. Today, that identity has largely been replaced by a gameplay loop that feels restrictive and frustrating.

Increasing damage by 10%, 20%, or even 30% would not solve the underlying issue.

The problem is mechanical.

Potential solutions could include:

  • Removing or significantly reducing the curse waiting requirement.
  • Reducing the cast time.
  • Allowing more reliable interactions with persistent curses.
  • Improving responsiveness and hit feedback.
  • Giving the skill a much stronger payoff for the amount of setup required.

A two-button skill should be rewarded for requiring additional actions. Currently, Hexblast asks the player to do more work for less reward.

PoE2 is at its best when skills feel impactful, fluid, and satisfying. Hexblast currently feels like the opposite of that design philosophy.

After roughly three leagues of low representation, poor player interest, and persistent feedback about clunky gameplay, I think it is time to take a serious look at the skill.

Hexblast does not need a small numerical buff.

Hexblast needs its gameplay to feel good again.

Because right now, the biggest reason players are not using Hexblast is not that it lacks damage.

It is that the skill simply does not feel worth playing.
