The lakers have had an All-NBA first team player on their team at some point for 65% of the franchise’s history

I’m talking about specifically an all nba first team player, not all nba in general.

1949-1954:

George Mikan was first team for the first 6 years the lakers were a franchise. Jim Pollard overlapped in 1954.

1955-1958: None

1959-1974:

This was primarily the Elgin Baylor and Jerry West era. Baylor made 10 first teams and West also made 10. They overlapped in 5 of those years.

Gail Goodrich was first team in 1974 to keep the streak to 16 years in a row.

1975-1991 (13 out of 17 years):

The Kareem and Magic era. They weren’t in a row here, there were 4 years where a player didn’t make first team. Kareem was first team 6 times in this span, and Magic 9 times. They overlapped in 2 of these years.

1992-1997: None, tied for the longest span in franchise history.

1998-2013 (14 out of 16 years):

The Kobe and Shaq era, only years one of them didn’t make it was 1999 and 2005. Shaq made 6 first teams and Kobe made 11 first teams. They overlapped in 3 of those years.

2014-2019: None, tied for second longest in franchise history.

2020: LeBron and AD were first team.

2021-2025: None

2026: Luka Doncic for the foreseeable future.

So 51 out of 78 years of existence the lakers have had an all nba first team player. In those 51 years, 12 have had 2 players on the lakers make first team. Accounting for around 12% of franchise history.

The closest franchise to this are the Spurs who in their 50 years of being in the nba (since 1977), have had a first team all nba player for 22⁄50 seasons. Totaling at 44% of their existence in the nba (not counting ABA years).

George Gervin —-&gt; David Robinson —-&gt; Tim Duncan —-&gt; Kawhi Leonard —-&gt; Victor Wembanyama for the foreseeable future.
