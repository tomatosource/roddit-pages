Do MLB scouts actually value intelligence when evaluating prospects?

One thing that I thought about recently: do MLB scouts and front offices actually care about a prospect’s intelligence - in the cognitive ability sense?

I’m not talking about who can solve a calculus problem or memorize the periodic table. I mean the kinds of cognitive skills that help someone process complex information, make good decisions under pressure, adapt quickly to changing situations, and learn new things fast. In the real world, IQ (or generalized cognitive ability) is one of the strongest predictors of success across a wide range of professions and life outcomes.

With baseball being a very complex strategic sport, where players are constantly analyzing situational math (pitchers, counts, launch angles, spin rates, defensive positioning, etc.) - intelligence definitely matter.

Old-school scouting language rarely uses terms like cognitive ability or processing speed. Instead, you see phrases like:

“He’s a smart player”

“He picks things up quickly”

“High baseball IQ”

My suspicion is teams have always been informally valuing some form of intelligence - even if it shows up as character evaluations, coachability reports, or verbal descriptors like “good makeup.” That’s basically a proxy for cognitive and learning ability.

But in the modern analytics era, when teams are trying to find any edge, you’d think they’d look for more objective metrics related to learning ability, pattern recognition, and mental decision-making - especially as the sport becomes more data-driven and complex.

Yet, I’ve never heard of a team publicly measuring intelligence like IQ or equivalent. Instead, we mostly hear about work ethic, grit, baseball IQ, or makeup - which are very fuzzy proxies.

There is some actual research touching this topic.

There’s a study that looked at general cognitive ability in professional baseball players and found meaningful relationships between cognitive test scores and on-field performance outcomes. It’s basically trying to answer: do players who process information better also perform better?

Similar work has been done in the NBA, where cognitive testing showed correlations with certain performance metrics. Again, not saying it’s destiny, but it suggests mental processing isn’t just noise.

Outside baseball, sports science research (especially in soccer) consistently shows that perceptual-cognitive skills - things like decision-making speed, attention control, and working memory - are strongly associated with high-level performance. Elite players tend to be better at these things than lower-level ones.
