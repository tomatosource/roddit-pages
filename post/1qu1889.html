The hardest thing in sports

Hitting a round ball with a round stick… The hardest thing to do in sports? For fifty years I have thought so and will point it out to anyone who dares disparage the game. Last night though, as sleep wouldn’t come to me, I had the thought that maybe hitting a baseball wasn’t the hardest thing to do in sports, but that maybe pitching a baseball was?

I know that being an MLB hitter is harder right now than it’s ever been. Velocity, spin rates, human umpires (ha), fresh arms over nine innings, the pressures of social media etc etc I sympathize with them and find it remarkable that anyone can actually barrel a ball. It’s truly one of the most amazing things that can physically be accomplished.

I’m not even really sure where the thought came from, maybe my subconscious was thinking about elite basketball players that can’t throw out a ceremonial first pitch without hitting the bull, or maybe I was in a bit of delirium as we haven’t had sunshine for a few days, but I did start thinking- and even remembered when I woke up this morning!

First, It’s a pretty unnatural thing to do. I remember seeing a picture of Dave Steib when I was a kid (a teenaged ss/pitcher myself), and I couldn’t believe what it looked like his elbow was doing. It was kind of gross. To do this thing, 50-100x per game, seems like you’re risking a catastrophic injury every pitch. It’s also amazing to think that they have to be as precise as they can be under the duress that they are in. To think that a tiny flaw could mean the difference between a grounded and a home run, missing your spot by millimeters could mean being sent down to AAA, all while having to deal with fatigue, all eyes being on you, starting the play every play… So much pressure!

As difficult as hitting is, the basics are still, see the ball, hit the ball (be the ball). It’s reactive, impressively so obviously. Pitchers on the other hand have to manage the whole game and strategize for nine different hitters, and yes, I know that analytics mean that coaches and catchers carry a lot of that load as well and that there are pitchers who are pretty much automatons and don’t think! But to know which pitch is working that day, which you’re struggling with, how you can’t find the inside party of the plate at what’s the alternative… There’s so much on a pitcher

It used to be said that if you failed 7x out of 10, that you’d sign a million dollar contract tomorrow,( not adjusted for inflation!). Great hitters succeed 3⁄10 times. A pitcher can throw 20 brilliant pitches but misplace one (and sometimes it doesn’t even have to be misplaced!), and the game is gone. I remember Greg Maddux talking about his 100 pitches - if he could throw 2⁄3 where he wanted to that left 34. About half of those would be terrible- spiked curveballs etc- which left 17 pitches that he had to get away with! A pitcher can make you look like a fool on two pitches but you can check swing the third into no man’s land and be a hero!

I do miss pitchers like Maddux as he was always a treat to watch. I don’t think the chase for velocity has ruined the game, although I do wonder about guys that scouts miss because they don’t hit 95, or teens that could have been contenders if they didn’t have the 13U coach trying to win and not develop. But I did love watching a guy with control, even if sometimes in watching those old highlights, you couldn’t believe the 8 in they were getting off the plate? Haha. This, to me, is something that makes pitching one of the hardest things to do- control. I’m amazed at how precise so many of these guys are still, even throwing like they do- Skubal, Skenes, Chapman…I mean, we have guys throwing with Bugs Bunny like movement as well, but man are these guys good- even the guys that aren’t!

Both are kind of physics defying acts of athleticism. Reacting to a pitch isn’t even the length of an eye blink, it’s insane to think about that that much can happen in milliseconds. So for that split moments, yes, maybe hitting is the most difficult, but pitching… More depending, more strategy, more complex, more grueling, more attritional.

But maybe, even after all that, it really does come down to seeing just how bad both normal people and super athletes from other sports are from the mound and front in front of the mound! I can’t wait for pitchers and catchers to report next week.
