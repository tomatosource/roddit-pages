Is it crazy to say Isaiah Hartenstein was the signing of the offseason?

When the GMs were surveyed before the season started, they were asked this question:

Which one player acquisition will make the biggest impact?

and the results were:

1. Paul George, Philadelphia – 60%
T-2. Mikal Bridges, New York – 13%
T-2. Karl-Anthony Towns, New York – 13%

  1. Alex Caruso, Oklahoma City – 7%
    » Also receiving votes: DeMar DeRozan, Sacramento; Isaiah Hartenstein, Oklahoma City

Obviously, KAT, Mikal, and Caruso were traded, not signed, thus ineligible for the overarching question. Hartenstein got some love, but it’s clear the GMs preferred the acquisition of PG, maybe DeMar if the last few results are ordered by number of votes.

Hartenstein has taken the Thunder from a great team to an elite team and has been invaluable to OKC especially with the frontcourt injuries throughout the year. The Thunder are 45-5 when Hartenstein plays this season. Is it that crazy to say that Hartenstein was the best signing of the offseason?

Players that you can technically make an argument for:

Malik Beasley (DET)

Tobias Harris (DET)

Paul George (PHI)

DeMar DeRozan (SAC) (sign and trade, so not sure whether to count it)

Chris Paul (SAS)

~~~~~~~~~~~

The ones you can really make arguments for are DeMar DeRozan and Paul George, maybe Chris Paul from a Wemby developmental standpoint. Paul George has been bad this year even when he has played. DeRozan is probably a better player than Hartenstein, but the Kings are in no-man’s land, and again, I’m not sure if sign & trades should count. I think overall, PG and DeRozan have both been disappointing, especially when compared to Hartenstein.

The Detroit guys (Harris and Beasley) are interesting shouts. I don’t think either were a better signing than Hartenstein, but maybe when you combine them there’s an argument.

What do you think?
