The only recorded 3-run strikeout in MLB history

Okay well, only recorded 3-run strikeout in the Retrosheet play-by-play archives going back to 1910.

This was July 26, 1990, Kansas City Royals @ Toronto Blue Jays. The day prior, the Royals had won 6-1 with George Brett hitting for his second career cycle. Going into the bottom of the 2nd of this game, they had already established a small 1-0 lead off of a Willie Wilson RBI groundout.

To start off the half-inning, the Royals’ Kevin Appier first got John Olerud to ground out, but he then gave up consecutive singles to Greg Myers and Mark Whiten. With runners on first and second and one out, on the 0-1 pitch Manuel Lee hits a grounder back to Appier, but Appier screws up the throw to 1B. Still one out, now with the bases loaded. Glenallen Hill hits a pop fly on the very first pitch, but of course with the error, it’s not over. Up to bat comes Jays leadoff hitter Mookie Wilson.

Wilson takes a couple of balls and hits a couple of fouls, but on the fifth pitch of the at-bat, he swings and he misses. This should be the end of the inning. It isn’t. Royals catcher Mike Macfarlane drops the ball and, instead of going for the force at home plate directly in front of him, then proceeds to airmail the throw into right field (video). Myers easily comes in to score, as does Whiten. As Wilson stops at 2nd, Lee rounds 3rd and he beats any attempt at a relay. The Royals had effectively allowed a bases-clearing double just from an errant throw.

This is, as far as I can tell, the only place in the Retrosheet play-by-play data that there is a 3-run strikeout. There have been plenty of bad strikeout throws allowing a runner from first to advance home, but this is the only time it has happened with the bases loaded.

While Tony Fernández would ground out to end the inning at the very next at-bat, the Jays would end up winning the game 7-5, meaning that those 3 unearned runs really did come back to bite the Royals. Ultimately it didn’t matter though as neither team made the playoffs, with the Jays sliding at the last minute to lose the division title. Nevertheless, I thought it was cool.

=====

By the way, for those curious, as far as I can tell there hasn’t ever been an “inside-the-park” strikeout with the batter making it all the way back home, but there are 26 instances of a batter reaching third on a strikeout. This is an unsurprisingly rare occurrence, and in fact there was a 37 season gap between 1923 and 1961 where it never happened.

The last time it happened was July 18, 2023, Arizona Diamondbacks @ Atlanta Braves, when Corbin Carroll struck out on a wild pitch from Bryce Elder but made it to 3rd after an errant throw. You can see video of it here.
