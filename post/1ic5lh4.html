Kel’el Ware in his last 5 Games averaged: 18.6 PTS, 9.8 REB, 1.8 BLK, 2 Threes, on 52.9% from the field, and 41.7% from 3

As a draft prospect, there were concerns about Ware’s thin frame, low motor and lack of passion on the court. However, Ware has recently become a key part of the Heat’s lineup thanks in large part to the team’s desperate need of size. Ware in a lot of ways is a perfect compliment to Bam. Ware at the center position has allowed Bam, who for years was an undersized 6’9” center, to shift more appropriately to the power forward position. Ware can also shoot 3’s, which has been a limitation of Bam’s game throughout his career, made even worse because the Heat’s best player for the past 5 years, Jimmy Butler, also couldn’t shoot 3’s. Ware still has lapses that you’d expect from a rookie like foul problems, questionable shot selection, and being too eager to get blocks. However, if he continues to play like he has in these past few games, he could very well be a pivotal piece in the Heat’s future.

It’ll be interesting to see what the Heat’s rotations looks like from now on, especially with Spo recently finding success with a lineup of Bam, Ware, and the 6’10” Nikola Jovic. The Heat’s small ball era may finally be over.
