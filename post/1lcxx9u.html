The Huge Uncomfortable Opportunity

This was a great team in 2023-24. They took a step back in 2024-25. It’s hard to imagine any necessary salary-cutting move would make them better in 2025-26 even with Tatum in the mix.

Fans also seem mostly in denial about what it means to lose a top 5 player for a season. That level of talent can genuinely be the difference between a 20 win team and a 60 win team and while we obviously still have Brown and White, we’ve seen countless examples of guys like that falling well short. Whether it be Westbrook without Durant failing to crack 50 wins, Kobe + Odom averaging 40 wins over 3 seasons, Pierce + Walker never once cracking 50 wins and missing the playoffs 5 out of 9 seasons, Ray + Rashard averaging 39 wins for the Sonics, on and on… you can have a decent also-ran built around a lower-tier star, but you don’t just remove a superstar from the mix and still win at a high level.

I think you got option 1 which is doing a couple salary cutting moves (Jrue and/or Porzingis), bringing back some cheaper role players, plodding along to 35-43 wins next season + a 1st round exit + a mid 1st, then coming out of it as a worse team than the one the Knicks beat in the 2nd round with a post-Injury Tatum + Brown + White + Pritchard + role players + minimal prospects of improving.

I remain in favor of using Tatum’s injury as an opportunity to be extremely aggressive in laying a new foundation with multiple chances at star talent to build around.

This is never going to be a popular opinion here. I’ve lived through fans throwing fits over the Walker trade, the KG/Pierce to Brooklyn trade, the Isaiah trade, the Fultz for Tatum trade, the Marcus Smart trade, etc. You wanna root for the guys you have in the moment. Most fans can’t or don’t want to see the big picture. Their vision doesn’t extend beyond the present and some even feel discussing trades is akin to insulting their chosen family or disgracing the honor of the guys they have vowed to support. There’s also this collective delusion, built in part because of Celtic miracles past, that if you believe - Anythiiiiing is Poosssssibbllle!!

There’s nothing wrong with that mindset. This is one of the greatest fandoms in all of sports and that blind faith is part of the charm. We’ve seen many examples of this team defying expectations over the years so the fanbase has earned the right to expect the unlikely.

I’ll just say that short of Tatum miraculously coming back 110% mid season, this team realistically has no chance next year and the idea of “running it back” just feels like a wasted opportunity to me.

I remain a Sam Hinkie apologist. I think think the 76ers botched several moves, but the heart of what they set out to do still holds logic. They had spent 25 years with only one fluke 50+ win season that only came as a result of a tankjob that landed them Allen Iverson. They were toiling around in mediocrity. It wasn’t some advanced thinking to realize “44% of top 5 picks end up all-stars… while that’s still not great odds, it’s better odds than not having a top 5 pick and if you have multiple top 5 picks, you increase those chances even more” … Clearly they wiffed on a number of their golden chances. Noel at #6 didn’t work out. Okafor at #3 didn’t work out. Simmons and Fultz over Brown and Tatum still haunt them. But ultimately, it just takes one guy. Embiid, flawed as he is, was that guy. The Embiid era, botched as it was, produced four 50+ win seasons and some relevance.

There are so many ways that could have gone so much better for them.

The way I look at it, when the dust settles on Horford’s retirement, Jrue/Zingis salary dumps, outside of Tatum we have: 1 all-star (Brown), 1 fringe star (White), 1 elite 6th man (Pritchard) + role players + no quality prospects + potential mid 1st in 2026

Alternatively, I think you could set this team up with numerous cracks at all-star level talent by just focusing on acquiring blue chip prospects on rookie scale deals, 1st round picks, and expiring contracts.

I’ve run through this before, but the Banes might give us additional insight.

Pritchard - 3 years left before unrestricted free agency. Could you move him to a team like Portland for the 11th pick and have him under cheaper contract 4 years + restricted free agency? You could get a crack at a star here.

White - Can you get a high lotto pick for him? A later lotto pick + multiple additional picks like Banes? One or more cracks at a star here.

Brown - His contract hinders his trade value, but in theory you can get a Banes-level deal for him. Or if we are to believe the pie-in-the-sky rumors, maybe a “cant’ miss” prospect in this draft + additional pieces (like Dylan Harper from the Spurs).

Jrue/Zingis - Outlined it before, but I think if you utilize multi-team trades you optimistically can come out of it with at least one future 1st rounder for each of them + no salary commitment beyond next season

Very important - You tank for a top 5 pick in 2026 and give yourself another 44% crack at an all-star

Maybe even more important - There’s a very real road map here where you can have Jayson Tatum (whatever his career is to become from here) + 5 or more recent lotto selections with star potential on rookie scale deals + multiple future 1sts + no major salary on the books beyond that leaving max cap room to target another all-star. Relevant names include Fox, JJJ, Trae, Beal and Luka

You just completely open the team up to maximum flexibility. You spend next season developing talent and hope the tank job results in a phenom. Maybe that 2026 pick ends up the Tim Duncan to Tatum’s David Robinson. Or maybe it ends up a weak player in a weak draft like 2007 when we were set to take Yi Jianlian, but flipped it for all-star Ray Allen. People bring up how Golden State tanked and only ended up with Wiseman, but that didn’t mean they didn’t have opportunities to trade Wiseman for a star. They just decided to keep him too long. Maybe the Celtics end up like the Thunder with multiple young budding stars. Or maybe they turn around and package some of those lotto guys for a 3rd star to pair with Tatum and a free agent?

It isn’t guaranteed to work. We know the guys we have are good and should remain good into their early 30s. We know that lotto selections can bust and prospects can fail to develop. You’re gambling either way, but if Tatum is set to miss a whole year, this is a real opportunity to give yourself multiple swings at a home run to pair alongside him or replace him as the new centerpiece if his recovery doesn’t go well.

TL;DR: Tatum + 5 or more star prospects + a top 5 pick + max cap room for an all-star seems better to me than Tatum + an allstar + a fringe star + an elite 6th man + a mid 1st + no elite prospects
