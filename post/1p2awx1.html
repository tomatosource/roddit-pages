With a potentially historic 3rd of the league tanking, Boston might have no choice but to win

Several times over the offseason I said this looked like a 35-43 win team on paper to me. I leaned more towards 35 expecting that guys like Brown and White wouldn’t play all 82 games. My go-to comp had been the 05 Lakers built around Kobe, Odom, Butler and Atkins that won 34 games. Part of that was because Kobe and Odom each missed about 16 games.

For their troubles, that 34 win Laker team ended up with the 10th pick and selected future all-star Andrew Bynum. Not a terrible outcome. However, one thing to keep in mind here is that 34 wins this season would project as the 12th worst record and in the play-in tourney over the past 5 years, we’ve seen teams as low as 33 wins get a play-in berth where two games then decide between lotto position and a likely 1st round exit with pick #15 being best possible prize.

Given this, there’s a large contingent of fans, myself included, who saw a possible Tatumless season as a wasted year and felt if he wasn’t returning the team should do everything possible to gun for a Top 5 pick which is astronomically more valuable than getting stuck with a lowly mid 1st.

For what it’s worth, I haven’t really seen anything this season to change my opinion of the team itself. You had to expect that some of our unknowns would put up some stats with default minutes. Some of these guys (Neemias, Walsh, Minott, Garza) look like they might earn consistent rotation minutes, which is nice progress for a team that doesn’t have any blue-chip prospects to develop this year. Pritchard/White look about as good as expected. Brown had been a 27ppg guy in games Tatum sat and so far his stats are very much in line with those games, though his efficiency has been a welcome surprise early on.

However, I think with 8 wins already, the vision of tanking for a top 5 pick is just about dead. The thing I’m keeping an eye on that’s a bit surprising is that there seems to be a crop of historically bad teams this year gunning for what is being called a historically great draft. Currently, 9 teams are under .300. That means there’s currently 9 teams projected to win 6-23 games.

That’s insane. For reference, in 2007 when we tanked we finished with 24 wins and the 2nd worst record in the league with only 22 win Memphis worse. 24 wins would be the 10th worst record this year if these projections stand. 2nd vs 10th is the difference between having a 52% chance at a top 4 pick and a 14% chance. The idea that you could finish this season with 24 wins and still be in line for the 10th pick is crazy.

There will likely be a couple more tankers joining them like Utah and Portland as the season progresses. So now you’re talking maybe 11 teams all actively trying to gun for this historically great draft. The also-ran squads like Boston and Phoenix could pick up 30 wins just from the teams trying to lose.

It’s early, but when we focus on what we’ve done so far, this still very much looks like 35-43 win team if Tatum doesn’t return. Of our 8 wins, 5 of them have come against bottomfeeders like the Nets, Wizards and Pelicans. Two more of our wins against Philly (24 wins last year) and Orlando (41 last year) are hard to currently measure. Our strength of schedule has been weak and we’re actually under-performing expected win/loss despite being a game over .500. Of our wins, only Cleveland is a quality team. Meanwhile, other than Utah, all of our losses have come against above .500 teams.

I think this next stretch of 6 games is going to give us a strong idea of what this team actually is. Our next game will be Brooklyn, which should be an easy win pushing us 2 games above .500. Then we’ll face Orlando, which finished .500 last year and hasn’t shown the expected improvement (that game can go either way). Then it’s 4 straight legit teams: Detroit, Minnesota, Cleveland, New York.

When the dust settles we’ll have a 21 game sample size and be coming off a stretch of games against real teams. The thing is, even if we were the play out a worst-case scenario over these next 6 games (Brooklyn = win, Orlando = loss, Detroit = loss, Minnesota = loss, Cleveland = loss, New York = loss), we’ll be looking at a 9-12 record and still not be in position for a top 5 pick.

On the flip side, we might come away from this stretch of games believing the ceiling for this team is higher than anticipated. Going 2-2 against DET, CLE, MIN, NY would be eye-opening. Certainly more-so if Jayson Tatum actually stands a chance of coming back this year at 100% for a playoff push. I’d take it a step further and say that with this many tanking teams, it also could result in a buyer’s market shaping up. I’ve posted threads before about how Simons + Hauser (38 mil total) + draft picks could position us to land another impact player to really tip the scales and make us a surprisingly late-season contender.

At this point, the Tatum returning + big splash trade is the outcome I’m hoping for. It’s hard to imagine we’d be able to catch any of those aggressively tanking teams in the loss column this year. You can go back to a season like 2022-23 where 33 wins was enough to land Portland in the bottom 5. During the the lotto drawing, they actually hopped up to the 3rd pick. If ultimately we’re only talking about a few wins separating a team from a Top 5 pick and a 15+ pick (as mentioned, 33 wins has also landed multiple teams in the play-in tourney in past season), tanking has a lot of appeal. But if this season continues it’s unprecedented path with 9+ teams all finishing under 24 wins, that route is looking more and more like a dead end.

TL;DR: The team looks about as good as expected, but the landscape around the team may have shifted substantially with 9+ teams currently projected to win under 24 games
