What does it mean to be MVP? An Australian’s take

Is it the best player in the league, the best player on the best team, someone who has done something statistically unique, or something else entirely?

I’ve been following the NBA for about six or seven years and it seems this debate happens every season. In the Australian Football League a rough equivalent of the MVP is the Brownlow Medal. After each game of AFL, the umpires vote for the top three players on the ground (across both teams), and award them 3/2/1 votes. At the end of the season, the player with the most votes wins the Medal. I’ve always thought this to be quite a simple but elegant system as players need to be consistently better than both their teammates and their opposition, and they need to play in as many games as possible.

Since it’s the offseason, and I wanted to practice coding, I thought it would be fun to apply this game-by-game approach to the NBA, and see if NBA Brownlow Medallists would be materially different to the winners of the MVP award.

Approach

Of course there’s no way I could get umpire votes, and given the discourse around Scott Foster et. al. it might be for the best, so I scored each player’s performance in each game according to the following rules:

  • ‘points’: 1.00,
  • ‘assists’: 2.25,
  • ‘blocks’: 3.00,
  • ‘steals’: 2.00,
  • ‘fieldGoalsAttempted’: -0.70,
  • ‘fieldGoalsMade’: 2.00,
  • ‘threePointersMade’: 0.75,
  • ‘freeThrowsAttempted’: -0.30,
  • ‘freeThrowsMade’: 0.60,
  • ‘reboundsDefensive’: 1.00,
  • ‘reboundsOffensive’: 1.25,
  • ‘turnovers’: -1.00,
  • ‘plusMinusPoints’: 0.30
  • WIN_MULTIPLIER = 1.25

*plusMinusPoints referes to box +/-,* and WIN_MULTIPLIER is applied to the ‘raw’ total score of all players on the winning team of a game

For each game, the player (across both teams) with the highest score received three votes, the second highest score two votes, and third highest score one vote. The player with the most votes each season was awarded the Brownlow Medal.

Results

Full results are below (I only included 1976 - present because I’m not sure how the data file treats players pre-merger).

In general the Medal is typically always won by an all-time great. This makes sense as basketball teams have much fewer players than an AFL side and are usually constructed to support the best player’s style, meaning they can consistently poll votes most games. However, players having outstanding seasons, and iron men that play in almost every game each season also regularly crack the top five.

While the Medallist and the MVP overlap in many seasons they’re not congruent. My approach, of course, overlooks many intangibles that don’t show up in the stat sheet that could may impact an MVP voter, it also standardises the impact of certain statistics - perhaps it’s wrong to say all field goals/assists/blocks etc. are made equal. However, it also removes any sentimentality that might be present in NBA MVP award voting such as season storylines, outsized individual highlights, or voter fatigue.

A few interesting insights and superlatives to call out from the results:

Most common Brownlow Medallist:

1.     LeBron James - 8

2.     Michael Jordan - 6

3.     = Nikola Jokic, = Larry Bird, = Kareem Abdul-Jabbar* - 4 (KAJ has some pre-1976 too)

Most successful individual polling seasons:

1.     = LeBron James 2010, 2009, = Michael Jordan 1988 - 207 votes

2.     = Shaquille O’Neal 2000, = Michael Jordan 1989 - 206 votes

3.     Hakeem Olajuwon 1993 - 201 votes

Closest Brownlow Medal race:

2019 - James Harden (175 votes) beating Giannis Antetokounmpo (174 votes) by a single vote

Key differences between MVP and Brownlow Medal winners:

  • Embiid, Westbrook, DRose, Kobe, Dirk, Nash, AI, Barkley, Walton all have won MVP but not the Brownlow
  • Chris Paul (2008) is the only player to win the Brownlow without ever having won MVP
  • Lebron has the biggest disparity between MVPs won (4) and Brownlows won (8)
