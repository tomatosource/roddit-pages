The (legendary) story of two Japanese baseball scouts

This is a long but emotionally rewarding story about two Japanese baseball scouts that, in retrospect, had significant historical impact. The rewarding part comes from the evolvement of the story, so I’d ask you to follow along and not quick scroll to the end.

Miwata Katsutoshi, born 1945, was a baseball ex-pro in Japan and became a scout for Orix Buffalos (named BlueWave back then) in 1971. He valued the player’s passion for the game and was dedicated to finding players with obscure backgrounds and understated potentials. Back in the day, Ichiro Suzuki - who desperately wanted to join his hometown team, the Chunichi Dragons and had been a Dragons fan since childhood - was actually recommended to the team by one of their own scouts. Yet the Dragons still turned him down. Even though he was already a very well-known high school player at the time, no team showed interest in him.

It was Orix scout Katsutoshi who traveled to Nagoya to find Ichiro and persistently pushed Orix to draft him. In the end, in the 1991 draft, Orix selected Ichiro with a 4th round pick - something that has since become part of baseball legend.

However, this outstanding scout chose to end his life in 1998. What came to light afterward exposed the darker side of professional baseball at the time. In the 1998 draft, Orix drafted pitcher Nagisa Arakaki, who Katsutoshi was responsible for scouting. But Arakaki refused to join the team and plainly stated he would only play for the Fukuoka Daiei Hawks, thus resulting in the wasting of the draft pick from Orix (not entirely wasted, but it’s still wasteful and bad for Orix).

Katsutoshi was harshly blamed by Orix management for this incident, and took his own life on November 27, 1998. Even afterward, the team’s leadership showed no sign of remorse. They even claimed that the player’s agent had deceived the scout and partially blamed this on Katsutoshi being reckless.

The 18-year-old pitcher Nagisa Arakaki, deeply affected by the incident, considered giving up baseball, believing that he had caused the scout’s death. But Katsutoshi’s wife did not blame him, and instead told him it’s not his fault and encouraged him to keep going with his baseball career.

Twenty-eight years have passed. Ichiro still visits Katsutoshi’s grave every year to pay respects to the man who scouted him. And the last player Katsutoshi scouted was Kazuo Yamaguchi, who joined Orix in 1999. Upon joining the team, Kazuo vowed, “I will repay my scout Miwata with my performance on the field.”

In 2016, Kazuo paid back to his scout in an unexpected way. It was 6 years after he retired as an Orix player, and his 4th year as a scout for Orix. With yet another 4th round pick, he drafted a 5’10” pitcher from a less competitive HS. The pitcher barely touched 91mph on his 18th birthday and had been injured for the past year, and his name was Yamamoto Yoshinobu.
