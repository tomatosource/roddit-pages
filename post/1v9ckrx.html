How the supporting cast won with defense

I’ve read a lot about how Jaylen Brown dragged a team of bums to 56 wins, and that without them they would have been a 25-win team. When you take a closer look though, he actually missed 7 games before Tatum’s return, during which the team of bums went 6-1 and outscored their opponents by 20 points a game. When he was on the floor, they went 35-20, which is a 52-win pace.

But maybe they were only playing bad teams. If we filter by teams that were .500 or better, they went 3-0 and outscored their opponents by 18. With Jaylen, they were 19-16 and outscored opponents by 1.

The reasons they were better were almost all related to defense. On the offensive side, their TS% was 54% compared to 57% in the 55 games Brown played, which they compensated for to some degree by increasing their offensive rebounding (more on that later). They chucked a lot of 3’s and rebounded the many misses. But on the defensive side, they only gave up 90 ppg.

To get a better picture of what’s going on, let’s look at Cleaning the Glass’s on/off differences in various categories for Jaylen last year. The #1 contributor looks to be that the opponent turnover rate decreases from 14.6% when he’s off the floor to 12.4% - league average is 14.4%. Beyond that, opponents shoot 1% better when he’s on the floor and take 2.4 more free throws per 10 possessions - however, in these measures, the team is still above average when he’s on the floor. I suspect that some of this is because he’s expending so much energy on offense that he has less for defense, but there’s also plenty of video evidence that he tends to lose his man when he’s off-ball.

What made this group such a good defensive team, despite the fact that Pritchard isn’t a particularly good defender? It actually starts with the offense. The team ex-Jaylen has a 11.9% TOV% on offense - this would lead the league (OKC is next at 12.4% and league average is 14.4%). Pritchard, in particular, has one of the lowest turnover rates in the league for a PG, but White is also better than average. They’re just safe, smart ball handlers (and tellingly, Mazzulla almost never takes both of them off the floor at the same time).

They also had a offensive rebounding rate that’s 5th in the league. When you don’t turn the ball over and you make it hard for the other team to get defensive rebounds, you don’t allow many transition opportunities. So the other team has to go against a set defense more often than usual.

Then you add a great individual perimeter defender (White) and a strong defender at center (Queta) and generally good team defensive rebounding (6th in the league) and you just have a defense that’s hard to score on. Boston gave up 114.8 points per possession with Jaylen on the floor (would have been 14th in the league) and 109.0 with Jaylen off (2nd).

Obviously, this group is not a championship contender without Tatum, because the offense probably isn’t sustainable against the top teams. But I think they would be a lot better than people give them credit for, particularly in the regular season.
