Missing the Tommy Energy

To the doomers ready to jump ship after one loss…go find another bandwagon. Fair weather fans aren’t welcome here.

Watching every Celtics minute humanly possible has me reflecting on Game 2 and some adjustments I’d like to see Joe make (and probably will, because he’s a great coach).

Game 2 was a necessary growing pain. The Sixers are a capable team when Maxey has help, and Edgecombe provided exactly that last night. What we’re missing, now that Joe has rightfully tightened the rotation for the playoffs, as all good coaches do. Is that Tommy Point x-factor energy.

I’d love to see Joe be more reactive to Hauser’s shooting. When he’s hot, like Game 1, he’s an x-factor. When he’s off, his impact is marginal. That’s not a knock on his defense, he’s improved tremendously on that end. But with the rotation cut, we’re missing the three-headed beast of Walsh, Scheierman, and González. If Hauser is cooking, sorry Hugo, you’re not getting minutes. But if you run a few plays for Hauser early and he’s cold, let Walsh, Scheierman, and Hugo eat those minutes. They bring that x-factor of effort and just ride whichever hot hand emerges.

I’m not saying bench Hauser. Rhythm matters. Give him run to start each half, and if he’s cold, put in the dogs.

Same logic applies to the Vooch/Garza swap. Vooch might be a solid double-double guy, but he doesn’t bring the effort x-factor Garza does. If Vooch is hitting threes early, ride him. If not, see if Garza’s feeling it from deep.

I’m obviously not a championship-caliber coach, Joe is. In Joe I trust. Game 2 was just the necessary pain he needed to tweak the rotation, because this team isn’t built like past ones. We might win as a committee rather than through a tight rotation.

Tear me apart, doomers. I’m glad we lost so we can come back stronger. ☘️☘️☘️
