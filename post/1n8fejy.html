Full 50 mil from Ballmer to Leonard accounted for?

Last post was removed, because I didn’t source.

As reported today by John Karalis at Boston Sports Journal:

The \(28 million deal was to be paid in quarterly installments over four years, but it was not the only compensation Leonard received. According to a high-level source, Leonard also cut a side deal with Aspiration to receive an additional **\)20 million in company stock.** The stock was to be paid out from Sanberg’s personal holdings in the company over four years.

That brought the total of promised compensation to Leonard to $48 million.

People seem to already be misunderstanding this. I’m seeing comments brushing this off by saying “he never really got the $20M since the stock went to zero.” But that’s not how stock grants work. When you’re issued equity, you receive it as an asset at the valuation set at the time. Whether or not it ends up being liquid or valuable later. If it goes to zero, you’ve still received the stock; it’s just worthless now.

Think of it like this: I recently left a tech company and exercised \(60K worth of pre-ipo stock options. I had to spend \)60,000 in real cash to buy them. On paper, I now own \(60K in pre-IPO stock in the tech company even though the company isn’t public and the shares aren’t tradable in the same way you can buy and sell something like Microsoft shares on the open market. If the company fails, that \)60K goes to zero, but it doesn’t mean I never had the stock… it just means I lost value. In-fact, the IRS would still let me treat that as a capital loss. Likely the same deal with Kawhi: he received $20M in pre-IPO Aspiration stock. While they note it was from Sanberg’s personal holdings, the directly aligns with Ballmer’s investment at the time, and if it became worthless, that’s a loss… not evidence he “never got it.”

That \(20M grant also lines up almost perfectly with Ballmer’s \)50M personal investment in the company. Without that infusion, the stock wouldn’t have been valued at that level in the first place. So even if it later went bust, at the time it was still a real transfer of value based on a real cash infusion into the company.

I also wanted to highlight something I haven’t seen discussed much. While some have made the obvious speculation that the final 2 million was a “kickback” to Aspiration for being involved, It’s worth noting that \(2M would be 4% of the \)50M investment — which just happens to be the maximum commission NBA agents are allowed to take on player contracts. Notably, on endorsement deals (real ones), agents typically take much higher commissions in the range of 10-20% since it isn’t capped by NBA rules. So it would seem the obvious next step would be to see if there were smaller payments made out to Leonard’s uncle or agent (Mitch Frankel), or more likely, LLCs that were set up by his representatives.
