Trentyn Flowers, currently on a two-way contract with the Chicago Bulls, is drawing interest from several power five college programs, a source told On3. Schools including Kentucky, Michigan, BYU, Kansas, Washington, Texas Tech, Florida, USC, LSU, SMU, Indiana, and Clemson are among those showing interest.

Flowers, a 6-foot-8 small forward in the 2023 class, was regarded as a top-25 caliber recruit coming out of high school and originally committed to Louisville. After spending part of the summer with the Cardinals, he opted to forgo college and pursue a professional path, signing with the Adelaide 36ers in Australia’s NBL.

He went undrafted in the 2024 NBA Draft but later signed a two-way deal with the Los Angeles Clippers. Flowers appeared in six NBA games during his time with the organization, while spending the bulk of the season in the G League, where he averaged 17.7 points per game. He was eventually waived and later signed a two-way contract with the Chicago Bulls in October 2025.

With the NCAA recently allowing former G League players to regain college eligibility in certain cases, some programs have begun testing the boundaries by targeting players on NBA two-way contracts, including Flowers. It is worth noting that a player who has appeared in NBA regular-season games, as Flowers has, has not yet been granted eligibility by the NCAA.

That said, the idea no longer feels far-fetched, as the NCAA has shown increased flexibility in recent decisions, including Baylor’s signing of former NBA Draft pick James Nnaji.
