Today is Jamie Moyer’s 63rd birthday. He once went 16 years between getting an RBI (1988-2004). He once went 19 years between hitting a double (1988-2007). He once went 20 years between wins against the Braves (May 23, 1987, and next beat them on May 25, 2007.

Jamie Moyer is the oldest pitcher to ever record a win (49 years and 150 days)

Speaking of wins, did you know that Jamie Moyer had more wins after he turned 38 (138 wins) than he did before he turned 38 (131 wins)?

By the time Jamie Moyer retired, he had faced almost 9% of all MLB players ever, and faced over 100 batters that were born after Moyer made his MLB debut.

When Jamie Moyer played for the Phillies in 2010, the team’s throwback alternate uniform was the same uniform that the Phillies wore against Jamie Moyer in his MLB debut (Moyer picked up the W, facing off against Steve Carlton).

Jamie Moyer gave up a record 522 home runs in his career, but did you know that Todd Frazier wasn’t even holding his bat when he “hit” number 522?

By the time he retired, Jamie Moyer had pitched in 50 Major League ballparks.

Jamie Moyer had Tommy John surgery. Tommy John was still an active MLB pitcher when Moyer made his MLB debut.

When Jamie Moyer retired, so did the list of players that had played at Wrigley Field before it had lights.

Craig Biggio hit 643 doubles during the stretch when Jamie Moyer didn’t hit any.

Ken Griffey, Jr. had 1,435 RBI during the stretch when Jamie Moyer didn’t have any.

Hilary Duff was born, did four seasons on a Disney sitcom, and recorded three albums during the stretch when Jamie Moyer didn’t beat the Braves.

Other people have entire careers during down periods for him.

One generation cometh and another goeth, but the Moyer abides.

Edit: Jamie Moyer won 19 games in MLB before “Phantom of The Opera” opened on Broadway. When he won his last game, Phantom was still running and had held the title of “longest running Broadway musical” for six years.

When Jamie Moyer made his MLB debut, the Fox network didn’t exist. When he retired, it had been two years since “The Simpsons” became the longest-running American scripted primetime television series.

Please keep telling the tales, don’t let the legend of Jamie Moyer get lost in time.
