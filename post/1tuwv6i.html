Dirk was already one of the greatest playoff performers’ ever before 2011 and the “soft choker” label was always nonsense.

This take has been floating around forever and it drives me nuts. People act like without the 2011 ring, Dirk would be remembered as some soft playoff fraud. I think that entire narrative was always way more about him being a 7-foot European than anything he actually did on the court. So let’s look at what the man actually did in the playoffs before 2011.

Dirk’s appearance’s in the playoffs: 2001-2010

From 2001 to 2010, across 103 playoff games and 19 series, Dirk averaged 25.6⁄10.9⁄2.6 on .458/.375/.883 splits. Those are all-time great numbers, especially considering it was the era of slow ball and ineficient basketball . He was bumping his scoring almost every postseason compared to the regular season, Dirk was a playoff riser for the entire decade.

In the 3 years right before the championship (2008–2010), across 21 playoff games, he put up 26.8⁄10.0/3.3 on .515/.351/.899. Over 50% from the field and almost 90% from the line as a #1 option getting schemed every night. His 2010 series against the Spurs was disgusting — 26.7⁄8.2⁄3.0 on .547/.571/.952 yet because his #2 in Jet decided to average 13/3/2, they lost in 6.

From 1999 to 2010, the WCF winner was the Spurs or Lakers in 11 out of 12 years. The only team to break through was the 2006 Mavs, led by Dirk, who beat the Spurs in 7 and the Suns in 6 to get to the Finals. Nobody else cracked that duopoly.

The “bad” series

Now let’s talk about the bad ones, because there were really only 3 across the entire decade, and all 3 had real context.

2005 Rockets — 21.3⁄8.9⁄3.3 on .352/.286/.846. Ugly shooting, no question. But they still won in 7.

2006 Finals — 22.8⁄10.8⁄2.5 on .390/.250/.891. Not great by his standards. But this wasn’t a normal series. Wade shot 97 free throws in 6 games. In Game 5, Wade went to the line 25 times — same number as the entire Dallas team. I don’t think this was at all a fairly called series, Wade was getting calls that the Mavs just were not getting. That said, Dirk still failed here, but it was his first finals appearance at the age of 27.

2007 Warriors — 19.7⁄11.3⁄2.3 on .383/.211/.840. The worst of the 3. But context matters,Don Nelson literally coached the Mavericks before this. He built that roster. He knew every tendency, every play they ran. Nelson specifically put smaller, quicker defenders on Dirk to speed him up and take him out of his comfort zone, and the Warriors had gone 4-0 against Dallas in the regular season after Nelson took over. Golden State was basically a hard-counter built specifically because their coach used to be Dallas’s coach.

The other 16

Those are the 3. The other 16 series range from good to absolutely dominant. He went 33.3⁄15.7 on .526/.727/.889 against KG’s Wolves, 29.9⁄8.9 on .521/.560/.900 to beat Portland in 7, 27.1⁄13.3 on .527 to beat the Spurs in 7, 31.3⁄7.8 on .506 to sweep the Grizzlies, 34.4⁄11.6⁄4.0 on .534/.385/.919 against Denver in ‘09, and 26.7⁄8.2 on .547/.571/.952 against SA in ‘10.

3⁄19 were bad but people took those series, slapped a “soft European” label on it, and retroactively rewrote an entire decade of elite playoff basketball. Dirk didn’t need the 2011 ring to prove he wasn’t a choker. The numbers already said it. The ring just forced people who weren’t paying attention to finally shut up.

Edit: A lot of comments are saying the choker narrative existed. I know it did, that’s not what this post is arguing. I’m saying the narrative was wrong.
