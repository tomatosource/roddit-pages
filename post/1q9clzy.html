How KAT Left an All-Time Career on the Table

Ask any Knicks or Timberwolves fan who has the worst whistle in the league, and they will immediately point to KAT. Unfortunately, it is no secret how you’re supposed to guard him. Ask his teammates, ask his coaches, ask his opponents, ask Jordyn Woods, ask the crowd, they will all tell you the same thing: initiate contact and be physical with KAT, and he will reciprocate that physicality tenfold.

For the same reason it’s so fun to annoy your little brother, you will always get a reaction from KAT. He has led the league in offensive fouls four of the last five years and would have made it five in a row if he hadn’t gotten injured during the 2022–23 season.

The player KAT should model his game after, where he would benefit the most, is Embiid. What makes Embiid so difficult to guard is his ridiculous physical traits combined with his unpredictability. Embiid will start the game with a rip-through foul, followed by a pump fake and jumping into his defender. At that point, you’re at his mercy because you’re questioning how to defend him. Then he’ll hit a step-back fadeaway in your face, bulldoze through you for an and-one, and channel his inner Hakeem by ballerina-ing around you with footwork.

KAT, on the other hand, will force the issue every time and use his off arm to throw the defender away (even though the defender initiated all the contact). He’ll complain to the ref, not get back on defense, and the cycle will repeat over and over. If Embiid were guarded by Booker the way KAT was defended last night in the first example, he’d be going to the line 20 times.

What’s frustrating is that this guy has been in the league for over 10 years, yet he hasn’t improved his basketball IQ once in the offseason. This isn’t a problem of KAT versus the refs. Every game, KAT is unfortunately in a battle with himself.
