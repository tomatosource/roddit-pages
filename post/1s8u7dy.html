The stats back up that Jaylen Brown has always been capable of this impressive season

Over the Summer when it was expected Tatum would miss the whole year, I tried to look at the past for clues on how it would play out. Brown’s first 3 seasons were up and down, but starting in year 4 (2019-20) he was an all-star 20ppg star-level talent. Continuing up through 2024-25, there was only a 35 game sample size of JB playing without Tatum.

In those games, JB averaged 28.3 points, 7.5 rebounds, and 4.3 assists while shooting 50%/33.6%. The team went 20-15 during those games (46.8 win pace). Pretty clear hint on how this season might play out.

On the flip side, during that stretch Tatum played 80 games without Brown, going 56-24 (57.4 win pace) and averaged an extremely similar 28.5 points, 7.8 rebounds, 4.4 assists with 46.6%/37.2% shooting.

However, that 6 season stretch also included other impact players like Kemba, Hayward, Smart, Horford, Timelord, Holiday, Porzingis, Kornet, etc. There was also the reality that during those seasons, JB never played more than 57-70 games. It seemed fair to expect him to miss around 15. It also seemed fair to expect lack of depth to be an issue. That was the crux of why a site like ESPN had put them at 40 expected wins focusing on lack of depth and questions around Garza as expected starting center.

My go-to comp was the post-Shaq 05 Lakers where Kobe (66 games played) alongside Odom/Butler won 34 games with Kobe averaging 27.6 points, 6 assists, 5.9 rebounds with a dip in efficiency (43%/33% shooting). Based on all of that, it seemed reasonable to expect Brown/White/Pritchard to win 35 games on the low end. Maybe 43 on the high end simply because nobody plays 82 games. I figured we’d see Brown, like Kobe, average 27+ with a dip in efficiency and make 2nd/3rd team all-nba.

My opinion has shifted a little throughout the season. Earlier this year when Brown was seemingly unstoppable (he averaged 30.1ppg on 51%/37% shooting first 32 games), I made several posts where I noted he was outperforming prime Kobe, was wildly surpassing the Jimmy Butler trajectory he’d been on, was outplaying guys like Paul Pierce in their prime and his surprisingly efficient play and reliance on mid-range fadeaways genuinely no-hyperbole was the closest I’d seen to mid-90s Michael Jordan.

At the time, I actually was of the mindset that Brown had actually taken a rare late-career leap and was underselling his own progress when he publicly claimed he was always capable of this. This was something I heard people like Bill Simmons and Zach Lowe also comment on about how exceptionally uncommon it was to see a guy take it up another level at almost 30 years old in his 10th season.

However, Brown then hit a lengthy stretch 20+ game where he was averaging 27ppg while shooting 42%/32% from the field and those numbers trickled down while his usage and shot attempts stayed highest in the league..

You can basically look at it as two halves of the season. First half culminating with his 50 point game.

- Up to 50 point game: 32 games - 30.1 ppg with 51%/37% shooting

- After 50 point game: 34 games - 27.1 points with 44%/31.6% shooting

Fair to note that in the 9 games he’s played with Tatum, his efficiency has been creeping back up shooting 47% in those games.

At this point, with the dust settling, it’s interesting to see that his numbers for the year as a whole are almost exactly the same as they had been in those 35 games he played without Tatum from 2019-2025: 28.6 points, 7 rebounds, 5.2 assists with 47.4%/34.2% shooting.

Ultimately, this is only 1 shot attempt more (21.6) than his prior career high (20.6 in 2023), only 2 points more (28.6) than his prior career high (26.6 in 2023), and just slightly less efficient (47.4%) than his career high (50% in 2024).

I’d still say he has exceeded expectations. To be clear, I expected him to average around 27ppg with 44% shooting (basically slightly better than Kobe) and make 2nd/3rd team. I was wrong. With Cade disqualified, he’s a virtual lock to make 1st Team All-NBA this year along with Jokic, Shai, Wemby and Luka. Absolutely impressive and worthy of praise.

All to say, Brown seems to have a valid point that he was always been capable of this. We had a limited sample size that showed him average 28.3 ppg/50% shooting without Tatum. Here he is averaging 28.6 ppg/47% shooting in a season without Tatum.

In-fact, he’s only averaging slightly more points/shots than he did in 2023, a season in which Tatum himself averaged 30+ on 21fga. I think for those worried about “who takes a back seat”, one major piece of context to keep in mind is that the difference isn’t actually with/without Tatum, it’s that in 2023-24 and 2024-25 we added Holiday and Porzingis. Prior to their arrivals in Boston, those two combined for 31 shots per night. Once the Celtics incorporated them, sacrifice was necessary in the same way we saw the Big 3 Celtics give up shots when the joined forces (KG = 17.6 to 13.9, Pierce = 18.1 to 13.7, Ray = 21.0 to 13.5). Likewise, with the addition of two more scorers, each player sacrificed: Porzingis = 15.7 to 13.2. Holiday = 15.3 to 10. Brown = 20.6 to 17.8. Tatum = 21.1 to 19.3.

You can see very clearly above, the reason we weren’t seeing Brown averaging 26-28 the last couple seasons wasn’t JT, it was that each of our scorers was giving up 2-5 shots per night to make room for Zingis/Holiday. With those two gone, it’s less of an issue.

But that, to me, is the main story here and I feel like sometimes my point on this is taken the wrong way. Brown is Brown. Brown has been elite for years and there was no reason to expect him not to remain elite. It was still a season where you could have reasonably expected a dud without Tatum, Holiday, Porzingis, Horford and Kornet. In prior seasons in the rare instances where both JT and JB missed games, the team barely played .500 and now they were losing the bulk of what had kept them afloat in those games. If I have to pinpoint a reason why we played at a 52 win pace with Brown this year (instead of 35-43), and why JB was able to have more success than someone like Kobe in 2005, it almost entirely comes down to the supporting cast. A bunch of guys on this roster nobody had any expectations of. Queta, the most notable, turned our biggest weakness (starting front court) into one of our biggest strengths (he’s lead the team in defensive rating and win shares). White and Pritchard proved doubters (myself included) wrong about their ability to step up and keep playing elite-level ball with heightened roles. I definitely did NOT account for Hugo, Walsh, Baylor and Garza to be impact role players. Truthfully, I thought all those guys were fringe g-league players. I would have assumed these guys would just get blown out in every game Brown missed, but that hasn’t been the case. While obviously they are better with Brown (I repeat - we are clearly better with Brown), we saw this team go 8-1 this year against mostly good teams even when Brown sat. That, to me, is notable and the most interesting aspect of the season that almost entirely explains why this team stayed at the top of the standings instead of puttering around .500 and making the play-in. I was not expecting to look at our plus/minus leaders at the end of the season and see White, Queta, Pritchard and Hauser in the top 4 with Hugo, Baylor, Garza and Walsh also heavily positive.

And now with Tatum back in the mix (we’ve played at a 67 win pace in the games he’s suited up including some monster wins against great teams), it still looks like a roster that still can have both the superstars take 20+ per night and average 26-29ppg moving forward, just as they did in 2023 when both players made the top 2 all-nba teams. On a personal level, one of my least favorite parts of Celtic discourse across social media is this perpetual narrative that “this proves Brown doesn’t need Tatum” or “this proves Tatum is overrated and JB has always been their best player” or “Tatum needs to take a back seat to Brown for this team to succeed”. That’s typically the part I push back against given Tatum is one of the most widely disrespected top 5 players in the history of the league. I think both of these guys are wildly talented. They clearly can co-exist. They clearly need each other and add to our success. Brown has a valid point that he could have been scoring more points all along, but when fans twist that to push the anti-Tatum narrative, I think it’s the root of a lot of arguments.

TL;DR: Stats back up that Brown has always been capable of an impressive season like this, but the supporting cast is the greatest “gap year” differentiator.
