Don’t blame the players for the state the NBA is in. Blame the rulebook and warped reffing norms for making it a viable way to win games.

NBA players are trying to win games and ultimately a championship. To win a championship, you need to maximize your chances of winning games, and that means doing everything within the rulebook to win. If you were making your living playing a game, and there was material and social benefits to winning that game as much as possible, you would understandably do everything within your power to win those games.

In a past era, the offensive player initiating contact to dislodge a defender was almost always called an offensive foul, which means SGA would likely lead the league in offensive fouls playing the style he does with his arm bar.

In a past era, jumping into a defender would have been in the best case scenario a no-call, at worst an offensive foul, which means that players like Austin Reaves, James Harden, and Luka Dončic would not be going to the line nearly as much as they do (see, Laker fans can be objective! 🤣)

In a past era, the gather step was enforced more strictly, you couldn’t just let the ball drift in a “sorta but not quite fully gathered” state for multiple steps, that would be a travel. That’s how players are able to run from the half court line to the basket while technically taking as few as one “real” dribble. If the gather was enforced in the older way, Giannis Antetokounmpo would probably lead the league in travels by a wide margin

In a past era, if the screener was moving when contact was made, it was an offensive foul. Now, screeners are allowed to move as long as a subjective assessment is made by the refs that the contact wasn’t severe enough, ignoring the fact that the illegal motion often determines whether a player gets there in time for the screen to happen in the first place, which in my view makes every moving screen one of consequence. If screens were called the way they once were, the Golden State Warriors would probably have led the league in offensive fouls every year since 2014.

If you watch the games, the refs are (for the most part) calling the games “correctly” in the sense that their calls are correct based on the rules and norms of the league, with some exceptions like inconsistent defensive calls depending on what team and player is involved.

The problem is that changes in the rules and officiating norms have created a situation where players are leaving a ton of potential advantage on the table if they don’t take advantage of the situation. They’re doing what their jobs are: to maximize their productivity and their team’s chance of winning.

If we want defenses to have a chance again, defenders need to be allowed to occupy space and contest. Offensive players that initiate contact and force violations shouldn’t be rewarded for it, they should be given offensive fouls. Screeners should not be allowed to move at the point of contact regardless of whether that screen actually knocks someone over or not. The hand check should be consistently called or not called, as opposed to the current situation where teams and players with good defensive reputations are allowed to hand check all day where other players cannot without getting called for fouls.

Fix the rules and fix the norms. Don’t blame the players (no matter what team they play for) for doing their jobs within the rules and norms of the game they play
