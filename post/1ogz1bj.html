How do you replace the talent we lost WITHOUT tanking?

Serious question for those who are still pushing for us to make this a competitive season as opposed to trading ourselves even further into a hole and locking in a top draft pick.

Even assuming Tatum is able to come back 100% the All-NBA 1st team player he once was, we still obviously lost some solid talent in Jrue, Porzingis, Horford and Kornet. Brad got basically nothing in return if we’re assuming we’re letting Simons walk.

A lot is made of how cutting salary and getting under the apron unlocks mechanisms we otherwise wouldn’t have like aggregating salaries and having access to the MLE, but it’s not like you’re going to replace a Jrue or Porzingis level talent by having access to an MLE.

Boston has one major trade exception (22.5 mil) that expires in July of 2026 so in theory if they were ready to add more salary again and attached picks to the exception they could land a key piece.

Simons will remain an interesting trade piece, but only up until this year’s trade deadline as you could in theory combine his 27.6 mil expiring contract along with someone like Hauser (10 mil) and a prospect like Hugo (2.7 mil) along with several draft picks to land a star-level talent making over 40 mil - so if they are dead set on avoiding a true tank job, maybe that’s the move they make and they start looking at guys like Sabonis (42.3 mil) or Markanenn (46 mil) as a mid season addition.

But truly, I just don’t see any real route where we can replace the guys we lost without tanking. We just aren’t going to have enough cap space or quality trade assets available to fill those holes. And even if you DID fill those holes, you’re looking at a ceiling being a team that is probably 90% of the one the Knicks beat in the playoffs last year.

Tanking, on the other hand, gives you a trade asset that is astronomically higher than anything we have on our roster. Case in point, this offseason the best offer we allegedly got for Derrick White was #9 and filler while the best offer we got for Jaylen Brown was #5 and filler. Consider the year we desperately tried trading up from the #16 pick to #9 pick. We were trying to give up 3 additional 1sts (one of which ended up the Jaylen Brown pick) and not even that was enough. It’s just far, FAR more valuable to have a top 5 pick than a mid 1st. For one, you could end up with a transcendent talent who can be the cornerstone of your rebuild. But even in a down draft, we’ve seen first hand how valuable those picks are. Consider in 2007 when we gave up the #5 pick, thought to be a weak pick (Yi Jianlian was our target) and it became the centerpiece in a trade for Ray Allen.

TL;DR: Explain the me the non-tank roadmap for bringing this team back to contending. I just don’t see it. Tanking, by far, kick starts either a quick retool to get us back to contending, or a centerpiece in the next era of Celtic basketball.
