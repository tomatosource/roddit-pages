Game 6 and the potential for the “reliever familiarity penalty”

For those who are unfamiliar, the “reliever familiarity penalty” (similar to the “third time through the order” penalty for starters) suggests that hitters gain an advantage in a playoff series the more times they face a particular reliever.

Going into Game 6, I wanted to look at how often certain matchups have occurred in the series so far. I tried to remove intentional walks, but it’s possible I may have missed some.

For both teams, I listed the projected starters first, close to the estimated batting order. It’s not perfect, try not to get hung up on the listed order.

Takeaways:

  • A lot of this is skewed by the 18-inning game on Tuesday. If Klein, Dreyer or Wrobleski are pitching in this game for the Dodgers (same with Lauer and Little for the Jays), familiarity is the least of their respective team’s worries.
  • I don’t think this data affects how either manager approaches high-leverage spots late in the game. Schneider will roll out Fluharty against Ohtani/Freeman, Dominguez will get Smith/Teoscar, Hoffman will get the 9th (maybe the 8th for an extended save if it’s the top of the lineup). Roberts will push Yamamoto as late as possible, and I’m guessing Sasaki would go multiple innings for the save.
