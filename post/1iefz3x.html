Who will win the Spurs spinoff of Succession?

Lottery luck aside, Gregg Popovich is one of the most successful coaches of all time. Not only did he reach the mountain with 5 separate NBA championships, he created a sustainable, long-lasting, Logan Roy-esque empire that spanned decades. He’s been the San Antonio Spurs coach since 1996 – which means he’s been at the helm for an incredible 29 straight years.

Unfortunately for Pop, he may not retake the reins for Season 30. There was already some question marks about his future given his age (at 76), but that’s only been exacerbated by his recent health scare. The Spurs don’t have to answer to a board of directors or shareholders like Waystar Royco, but it’d be a surprise if they don’t address this situation during the offseason to settle the debate and create some stability. Even if Popovich comes back for a ceremonial 30th year, the team should be lining up an official heir to take his place in the future.

Here’s a look at the possible successors.

Mitch Johnson aka Karolina. After Popovich took a leave, the Spurs promoted his faithful aide Mitch Johnson to hold the fort as the interim coach. At age 38, Johnson has the youthful energy and upside to potentially run the show for a long time to come. Still, you wonder if the Spurs would entrust the job long term to someone who only has “assistant” experience and has never led his own team before.

Brett Brown aka Kendall. Most of Popovich’s top lieutenants went on to successful coaching careers elsewhere and aren’t available for this job anymore – including names like Ime Udoka and Mike Budenholzer. But like Kendall Roy, Brett Brown is the wayward son who has come back into the fold after his own car/eer skidded into a lake. He’s been back with the organization since 2022. At the very least, he can serve as a veteran mentor for Johnson or whomever else takes the job.

Becky Hammon aka Shiv. Back when Becky Hammon started as an assistant for the Spurs there was talk about her taking over for Pop one day – in what would surely be a headline making, glass ceiling breaking storyline. I had always been skeptical of her chances back then because she hadn’t proven herself on her own yet. Shiv felt the same way and nearly led Gil to the presidency, but Hammon did even better than that – leading the Las Vegas Aces to two championships in the WNBA. She also has a modern approach to offense that would easily translate to the men’s game.

Chase Buford aka Roman. Originally thought to be a nepo baby himself (as the son of Spurs CEO RC Buford), Buford has actually racked up an impressive resume outside of the nest. He led the Wisconsin Herd to a first-place finish in the G-League, and then led the Sydney Kings to two championships in Australia. Like Roman, Chase Buford’s demeanor is more of a question mark than his intelligence, as he did get some flak for his frenzied ref bashing.

Monty Williams and Mike Brown aka Tom Wambsgans. You can quibble with Tom Wambsgans’ virtues as an executive, but he was… an executive. He fit the role. He fit the suit. He landed jobs. Similarly, former Spurs’ employees Monty Williams and Mike Brown have experience and some success on their resume. Like Brett Brown, they’d at least be viable lead assistants for new coaches.

James Borrego aka Cousin Greg. Wait, the old coach for Charlotte? The one who was a candidate for the Lakers this summer? How’s he related to our family again? Actually, Borrego has deep ties to the Spurs organization: he served as an assistant from 2003-10 and again from 2015-18.

Tim Duncan aka Connor. This is a disrespectful comp for the legend Tim Duncan, but like Connor, he’s a vestige of an earlier era of the dynasty, and seems content to do his own thing and live his own unconventional personal life. Duncan worked as an assistant for only one year and may not be interested in anything more than that. But if we were to cross metaphors here and make this a more timely Conclave post, perhaps you can make the argument that the man who most deserves the job is the one who least wants it.

Bill Self aka Lukas Mattson. In terms of outside the box, outside the “family” hires, Kansas coach Bill Self would be a name to keep an eye on. Among top college coaches, analysts think that Self (or Jay Wright) would be well suited to adjust to the NBA. There are even some specific connections between Self and the Spurs as well. In fact, Self’s son is the GM of the Spurs’ G-League team. Could a merger be on the horizon?
