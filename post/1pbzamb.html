Is xWOBA biased against pulled balls?

It’s an oft repeated statement and has an element of truth. But paints a very incomplete picture as a cursory analysis will show. Because xWOBA’s biggest bias is actually this: batted balls up the middle!

I decided to take a look at batted balls in the past 3 years post shift ban. And pulled balls did overperform their xWOBA. But what they don’t tell you is that oppo batted balls had a nearly equal bias:

WOBA - xWOBA:

Pulled: .035

Oppo: .033

I guess we should say xWOBA is biased against oppo hitters! But actually the biggest bias here is against balls up the middle and it’s not even close:

-.068

That’s a big bias. And it doesn’t matter the batted ball type, or even if it’s a barrel or hard hit. Straightaway batted balls underperform their xWOBA. Here’s the numbers for barreled fly balls based on direction:

WOBA - xWOBA:

Pulled: .412

Oppo: .028

Straightaway: -.414

Even when taking into account batted ball type, exit velo, and launch angle, the biggest bias is not against pulled balls but straightaway balls.

Furthermore, it’s important to break down oppo and pulled balls based on type. As pulled ground balls do not overperform their WOBA, only balls in the air. And for oppo balls it’s the opposite - the biggest bias is groundballs.

Oppo groundballs had a .372 WOBA compared to a .222 xWOBA good for a .150 difference. Hitters had a .405 AVG on said batted balls.

TLDR:

  • xWOBA’s biggest bias is against straightway batted balls which it overrates.

  • xWOBA has bias against pulled flyballs and oppo groundballs which it underrates.

  • In aggregate these all cancel out and the difference between WOBA and xWOBA in the last three years was -.002.

(One thing to note is that I looked at pulled balls and not pull hitters here for example. And we can’t assume the results would by the same if I grouped hitters by pull rate and looked at their difference WOBA and xWOBA. The bias with all pulled balls might not be the same as the bias with pull hitters specifically. Getting those numbers will be a little more difficult though and is for another time. I bring this up because Ben Clemens did such an analysis on the 2023 season and did not find much bias against pull hitters based on grouping them by quartile.)
