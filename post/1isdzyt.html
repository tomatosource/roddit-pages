[OC] The Rookie of the Year Conclave: a rare look behind the scenes at the voting

Most of the major awards votes are decided by the media. However, it’s a little known fact that the Rookie of the Year vote is actually decided by the candidates themselves, sequestered in a good ol’ fashioned conclave. Over the All-Star break, they were tasked with deciding which among them would be most worthy.

We’re lucky enough to have obtained the audio of the proceedings, transcribed below.

STEPHON CASTLE: “Sede vacante. The throne of the Rookie of the Year is vacant. So thank you, dear brothers, for joining me today as we elect a new winner.”

DONOVAN CLINGAN: “Uh no offense, Cardinal Castle – you’re my UConn teammate and I respect all we did to win that championship together. But who put you in charge of this? Bill Simmons?”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Brother, I’m not in charge of anything. I’m simply run this conclave as Dean, because I have no desire to win the trophy myself. Some were chosen to be shepherds –”

REED SHEPPARD: “Present.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Oh no, sorry, Reed, I was talking allegorically. Some were chosen to be shepherds – and some to manage a farm. Apparently, I’m a manager. According to the Spurs, it’s merely my mission this season to support the other stars around me like Wemby and CP3 – and now De’Aaron Fox too. It is not my time to win glory or awards bigger than Rising Stars. But surely, one among you will be deserving. I know, outside these conclave walls, the world has criticized this class. But I see before me today several good candidates – college stars – and lottery picks – and –”

BUB CARRINGTON: “Excuse me, Cardinal Castle. I’m sorry to interrupt. But there’s a problem with our numbers.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Uh oh. Have we lost one? Another ACL tear?”

BUB CARRINGTON: “No, Your Eminence. We seem to have acquired one. Another cardinal has just turned up. A second rounder…”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Oh god, no. Don’t tell me they let Bronny in.”

BUB CARRINGTON: “No, his name is Jaylen Wells. He’s an Archibishop of Washington State. And before that, stationed at Sonoma State.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Archbishop of where?”

BUB CARRINGTON “Sonoma State. Apparently, it’s a real college. It’s been kept quiet, but the media has recently elevated him to official candidate.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Very well. He’ll be allowed to take part in the conclave.”

JAYLEN WELLS: “Thank you. It’s a great honor to be included in the discussion. Even in this class.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Now, if you may bow your heads, and allow me to start with a prayer. Cari fratelli, in questo momento di grande incertezza nella storia del associazione, dobbiamo pensare innanzitutto all’ultimo ‘Rookie of the Year,’ il cui gioco brillante è stato un dono di Dio. Ma ora dobbiamo chiederci di inviare un nuovo ‘Rookie of the Year’ e dobbiamo pregare affinché ci guidi nella scelta giusta –

ALEX SARR: “Excusez-moi, but if we’re doing a different language for the prayer – shouldn’t it be in French? There are a lot of us here. Me, Cardinal Risacher, Cardinal Salaun…”

TIDJANE SALAUN: “Bonjour. Italian – French – doesn’t matter to me. But can we hurry this up a little? You’re dragging it out so long it may as well be broadcast on TNT.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Sorry for keeping you here, Tidjane. I know you must have a busy schedule. Is there a G-League game starting soon?”

TIDJANE SALAUN: “Va te faire foutre.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “What’s that mean?”

TIDJANE SALAUN: “Go ask Wemby, encúle.”

KYLE FILIPOWSKI: “Sorry, guys, but I’m with Salaun here. You know I love this church stuff as much as anyone, but I think this vote should be obvious. The best rookie has been my buddy from Duke, Jared McCain. He lit it up with 15.3 points per game, 38.3% from three…”

RON HOLLAND: “And hitting shots in this league is not easy… trust me.”

CODY WILLIAMS: “Believe me, I know.”

MATAS BUZELIS: “Heck, it’s even hard to make shots in the dunk contest…”

RON HOLLAND: “So given that, we’re all in favor of McCain.”

JARED McCAIN: “Appreciate you all, thanks. It’d be an honor to accept this –”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Wait, brothers. I didn’t want to influence the vote in any way, but I feel like I must say something… Last night, I overheard something troubling. A voice, in Cardinal McCain’s room. A female voice.”

JARED McCAIN: “That’s just my falsetto…”

STEPHON CASTLE: “No, Cardinal, it wasn’t. I investigated the matter myself and discovered it was… a team doctor. She confirmed the truth to me. You will not be returning to the court this year. And I don’t think it’s right for this class to elect a candidate who only played 23 games.”

JARED McCAIN: “Please please please –”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Is that your counter argument or —”

JARED McCAIN: “Sabrina Carpenter. Told you about my falsetto.”

DONOVAN CLINGAN: “I have an idea, brothers. It’s obvious that there’s no clear favorite emerging here. So how about we just default to the preseason favorite? Zach Edey? He’s averaging respectable 9.5 points, 7.6 rebounds… and he’s starting on a good team. Winning should matter.”

REED SHEPPARD: “Are we really going to fall back on outdated traditions like wins and losses? I want this congregation to push to the future. To modernize. To think about shooting – and analytics. No offense to you and all the bigs here, Father Clingan, but we’d set this sport back 30 years if we pick some big oafish center from Europe.”

DONOVAN CLINGAN: “I’m from Connecticut…”

KEL’EL WARE: “And I’m from Arkansas.”

ZACH EDEY: “I’m from Canada.”

REED SHEPPARD: “Wait, Edey, you’re Canadian? Seriously?”

ZACH EDEY: “Yep.”

REED SHEPPARD. “Huh, weird. ‘Cause I watched Team Canada in the Olympics and I didn’t see you on the roster. But if you’re all comfortable electing a ROY who can’t even beat out Dwight Powell, then go ahead –”

ZACH EDEY: “At least I play in the NBA, Reed. How are your precious analytics nerds viewing you now?”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Brothers, brothers, let’s not quarrel. Perhaps we can find a compromise between the modern contingent and the old traditions. How about Cardinal Risacher? He’s a shooter – and a # 1 pick. Historically, they’re the most likely to win. Cardinal Risacher, are you here? Where are you?”

ZAC RISACHER: “I’m here. Hi.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Oh, sorry, didn’t see you standing over in the corner there. Is there something you want to say? Something you want to do to impress us?”

ZAC RISACHER: “Nah, I’ll just stand over here and blend in, thanks.”

REED SHEPPARD: “This is getting ridiculous, Cardinal Castle. If I can’t win to represent the guards, why don’t you throw your hat into the ring yourself? You have the athleticism. The star potential.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “I don’t wish to compromise my neutrality as Dean.”

REED SHEPPARD: “Too late. Every cardinal has that desire. Every rookie, deep down inside, has already practiced their ‘Rookie of the Year’ speech. They’ve already decided who they’d thank first on that podium.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Well I haven’t.”

REED SHEPPARD: “Deny if it you like. But search your heart and then tell me it isn’t so. Tell me who deserves it more than you?”

STEPHON CASTLE: “How about Cardinal Sarr? People doubted him, and he’s proven them wrong. He’s more advanced than we expected. He’s started every game – and averaged 11.4 points, 6.6 rebounds, 1.6 blocks…”

DONOVAN CLINGAN: “Starting isn’t everything… but winning is. And are we really going to elect a Rookie of the Year from the worst team in the league? From one of the worst team of the entire 2000s?? Doesn’t this institution get enough criticism already? Enough scandal? Or are we really going to learn nothing from Michael Carter-Williams?”

STEPHON CASTLE: “But do we have a choice? Outside these walls, the media and fan bases are laughing at us. Mocking us. They already called us the worst class in decades, and now, whomever we pick will surely be called the worst Rookie of the Year as well.”

REED SHEPPARD: “So take it, Cardinal Castle. Admit it. You want to be Rookie of the Year.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “… …. My mother.”

ALEX SARR: “Excusez-moi?”

STEPHON CASTLE: “… My mother. That’s who I’d thank first. On the podium. So for her sake, and for the sake of our congregation, perhaps I’ll accept a nomination after all…”

DALTON KNECHT: “Uh, is anyone else uncomfortable with how much of the media and this post is focused on Cardinal Castle? No disrespect – you’re a good athlete and all – but you’re not efficient at scoring. I know we all have doubts in our heart, but I’d at least like a candidate who knows what to do when the ball’s in their hands.”

BUB CARRINGTON: “I fear this task may be impossible. We’ll never find a candidate who doesn’t have some kind of black mark against them! We are not aliens – nor unicorns – we are mortal men! We serve an ideal – we cannot always be ideal.”

JAYLEN WELLS: “Brothers, may I say something? I know this is my first time amongst you and I suppose it will be my last. These last few hours we have shown ourselves to be small and petty men. We have seemed concerned only with ourselves, with our stats. But this is not the way the award should be. The award is not a tradition. It is not the past. The award is what we do next.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “I… really have no idea what that meant, but I know it was supposed to be profound. So how about we just give up and elect Jaylen Wells? The ultimate outsider? Wouldn’t he be the most fitting winner of our unconventional class?”

DONOVAN CLINGAN: “He is on a winning team…”

REED SHEPPARD: “And he does have solid efficiency. 58% true shooting…”

STEPHON CASTLE: “There’s also some precedent for it. Sure, he’s only averaging 11.2 points. But Mike Miller won Rookie of the Year averaging 11.9 points for a playoff team… Malcolm Brogdon won averaging 10.2 points for a playoff team – and he didn’t even start that year. At least Wells has been starting, contributing, defending, for a top seed. Are we all okay with that?”

DALTON KNECHT: “Works for me.”

ZACH EDEY: “Fine.”

TIDJANE SALAUN: “Je vous déteste tous.”

STEPHON CASTLE: “Well then, it’s been decided. I’d love to conclude this conclave with some wild and unrealistic twist ending – but fitting of our class, we’ll have to go home disappointed.”
