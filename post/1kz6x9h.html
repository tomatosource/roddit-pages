How do the difficulty of the playoff runs of this year’s potential champions compare with the previous 42 years?

Read the next 2 paragraphs for the answer to my rather click-baity title… For a deeper dive into how championship runs have changed by opponent regular season performance and opponent health over the decades, keep reading!

If OKC wins the championship this year, they will have faced opponents with an average regular season net rating of 4.475 (if they face the Knicks) or 3.975 (if they face the Pacers). Where do these numbers rank all time? Well at least over the last 42 years, that would be the 17th or 22nd most difficult championship run by opponent net rating. In just the last decade, either of those numbers would rank 4th. OKC has only played 1 playoff game so far where their opponent was missing a top 3 player (much more below on why this is a somewhat flawed metric). Those are the “healthiest” opponents a champion has faced in the first 3 rounds since the 2019 Raptors. And if they HOPEFULLY face a healthy opponent in the finals, it would be the fewest games missed by opponents during a championship run since the 2016 Cavs.

What about if the Knicks or Pacers win it all? Either would be an unprecedented championship run. Both runs would be the highest average opponent net rating of the last 42 years (Knicks: 6.575, Pacers: 7.25). They would also have the toughest opponent by net rating any champion has ever faced (OKC: 12.8). AND the toughest opponent a champion has ever faced in getting to the finals in either the Cavs (9.5) or Celtics (9.4). They both got a fair deal of injury luck in the first 2 rounds, but the games missed by opponents’ top 3 players would be about on par with other champions in the first 2 rounds so far this decade.

For those wanting a deeper dive:

It feels like the eventual champion’s run through the playoffs the last few years has been defined by relatively weak competition, both in terms of the regular season performances of the teams they face as well as the high amounts of injuries in those opposing teams. I took a look at every championship run since 1984 to see just how true this perception is. The results are fairly damning… Making me a little depressed about the level of playoff basketball we’ve seen recently, but also optimistic that this is more of a blip than a new normal.

Games missed (GM) = number of games missed in a playoff series/run by a team’s top 3 players in MPG during the regular season

Net Rating (NR) = regular season net rating from Basketball Reference

See My methodology below for more details

Main takeaways:

Health has always been super important for winning championships and that is especially true recently

Over 41 years of data, 78% of champions did not have a SINGLE game missed by a top 3 player throughout their whole playoff run. Only a single champion had more than 2 games missed (Chris Bosh missed 9 games for the Heat in 2012). Compare that to the teams they faced during those championship runs. Just 34% of runs faced teams with zero games missed among top 3 players. And 6 championship runs saw 10+ games missed from opposing teams (the highest? The 2015 Warriors with 17. Thanks in large part to the 11 combined games missed from Kevin Love and Kyrie Irving in the Finals). Overall, champions experience just half a missed game (0.46) on average from a top 3 player throughout their whole playoff run, and teams that they face average just over 4 (4.05) games missed. When looking at each decade though, the numbers get even crazier.

80s – 0.17 GM for champions, 4.17 for their opponents

90s – 0 GM for champions, 0.9 for their opponents (no one got injured in the 90s, apparently)

00s – 0.3 GM for champions, 3.5 for opponents

10s – 1.3 GM for champions, 5.3 for opponents (injuries start creeping up…)

20s – 0.4 GM for champions, 8.8 for opponents

Champions are basically just as durable as they’ve always been (Giannis’ 2 games missed in 2021 are the only GM for a champion since 2017), but their opponents are racking up crazy amounts of injuries. The last time a champion faced an opponent who didn’t have a game missed from a single top 3 player was the 2011 Mavericks.

Outside of injuries, opponent competition remains high as of recent, other than 3 outlier years

Turning now to regular season net rating. If you average the net ratings of all opponents faced during a championship run, the lowest of the last 41 years were the 2023 Nuggets, with just a 0.6. The 3^(rd) lowest was the 2020 Lakers: 1.7, and the 6^(th) lowest was the 2024 Celtics: 2.375. This may seem like a trend, but the 2022 Warriors had the 10^(th) highest average opponent net rating (4.725) and the 2019 Raptors were not too far behind (4.55). The ’21 Bucks (3.2), ’17 Warriors (3.675), and ’18 Warriors (3.5) were all pretty much middle of the pack. As for the champions themselves, over all 41 years, the average NR was 7.46. Just for the 20s? 6.4. Though that is buoyed quite a bit by the +11.6 ’24 Celtics.

For reference, these are the 5 hardest championship runs by opponent NR:

1995 Rockets: 6.525

2001 Lakers: 6

1997 Bulls: 5.95

2002 Lakers: 5.625

2011 Mavericks: 5.15

Looking at the average NR in opponents faced by round, the 20’s look pretty weak.

1^(st) round opponents average net rating (20s): 0.6

The 3 decades prior were all over 1.15 (though the 80s were a paltry -2.15… the bottom of the league, especially the West, was WEAK in the 80s).

2^(nd) round opponents NR (20s): 3.5

Right on par with other decades

Conference finals opponents NR (20s): 2.32

For all decades this is 5.21. A whopping 6.86 in the 2000s (the West was TOUGH in the 2000s).

Championship runner-ups NR (20s): 3.66

Compared to 5.6 for all years and 6.84 in the 90s.

So, champions have faced pretty weak (as far as regular season net rating goes) opponents in the playoffs in the 20s, but I think that has mainly been due to 3 (hopefully) outlier seasons.

Smaller takeaways:

  • As I mentioned, being healthy in the playoffs is HUGE for championship teams, but having a good regular season net rating is pretty important as well. Only 6 teams have won a championship in the last 41 years with a regular season NR below 5.

1995 Rockets – 2.2 (Pacers would tie this if they won it all this year)

2023 Nuggets – 3.4

2001 Lakers – 3.6 (wild for such a dominant and difficult playoff run)

2006 Heat – 4.2

1994 Rockets – 4.5

2011 Mavericks – 4.6

  • The 90s Bulls were incredible. Everyone knows this, but the numbers definitely confirm it. 1996 and ’97 had the highest net ratings of any champion (13.4 and 12, respectively). ’97, ’94, and ’93 all had top 10 playoff runs in terms of opponent NR (5.95, 4.925, and 4.75 respectively), and none of those opponents had a single game missed by a top 3 player either.
  • The ’08 Celtics had a whopping 11.2 net rating and crazy conference finals and finals competition (8.4 NR and 0 GM for the Pistons, 7.6 NR and 0 GM for the Lakers). But they got taken to 7 games in the 1^(st) AND 2^(nd) rounds by 2 teams with NEGATIVE regular season net ratings (Hawks and Cavs).

My methodology:

Using Basketball Reference for all data, I looked up the net rating of every champion since 1984 (before this there were only 3 playoff rounds… and I was getting tired), as well as the net ratings of every team they faced en route to their championship. Why net rating? I don’t know, sometimes I feel it’s a little more predictive than overall record and isn’t as affected by shortened seasons. But you could probably use win percentage and the trends would be very similar. I also wanted to look at how injuries impacted each of these teams. In an effort to keep things simple, I took the top 3 players in minutes per game during the regular season for each team and noted how many games they missed in each playoff series (I excluded anyone who had &lt; 20 games played during the regular season, or who was traded off of the team midway through the season). This is rather imperfect. It doesn’t take into account players who played through injury or missed the majority of a game due to injury. It counts best players on teams at the same level of importance as role players who simply play a lot of minutes. It doesn’t account for injuries further down the roster. And I looked solely at number of games missed, so it doesn’t account for the length of a series. Some examples of how things may be skewed – the Brooklyn Nets only have 6 games missed in the 2021 ECSF (3 for Kyrie and 3 for Harden), but that doesn’t take into account Harden being severely limited when he did play. The 2016 Warriors only have 1 game missed (Draymond’s suspension) in the finals, which doesn’t account for Curry being limited by injury and Andrew Bogut’s injury. In the 2017 WCF, the Spurs only have 3 games missed, which is the same number of games missed as the Jazz in the first round of the 2009 WC playoffs. Kawhi missing essentially that entire 4 game series was probably a little more impactful than Mehmet Okur’s 3 missed games out of 5 in ’09. All that said, it was an easy enough calculation that gives a good overall view of health luck during a playoff run.

Summary/TL;DR:

All champions are deserving, you can only play who’s in front of you. But not all championship runs are created equal. Champions have always been remarkably healthy in the playoffs, but so far this decade, their opponents during these runs are experiencing more injuries than ever before. Opponent net ratings during championship runs have also been extremely low thus far in the 20s, but that will likely normalize after 3 outlier years (’20, ’23, and ’24).
