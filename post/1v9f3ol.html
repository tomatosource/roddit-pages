Mitchell Robinson will be more than just an offensive rebounder here in Boston, and Jayson Tatum will be main the reason why.

I want to preface this post by saying that this post has nothing to do with whether I believe Robinson will start over Queta. I know many fans want and believe that Queta will start. This post is for those worried that Robinson will be a negative factor outside of his offense his rebounding. I don’t think that’ll be the case regardless of if he starts or not and here’s why:

The context:
It is valid to be concerned about Mitchell’s lack of scoring
Last season, he only averaged 5.7 points per game.
That’s only 10.4 points per 36 minutes.
Which ranked 43rd among qualifying centers and 217th for qualifying players.
Or 83rd of all centers and 490th among all players.

That’s bad no matter how you slice it.
He only averaged 1 unassisted basket per game and 1.5 assisted baskets per game.
And he hasn’t averaged more than 7 points per game since the 2022-2023 season.
His career high is 9.7 points per game, which was all all the way back in his year 2 season. (2019-2020).

I think this changes in Boston because now he’ll be playing with Jayson Tatum.

Tatum has quietly played a huge role in helping interior big men get buckets.
I can still remember the chemistry he built with Kornet before he got paid by the Spurs.
Then I saw something similar with Queta at the end of last season.

So I decided to look at the numbers to see if there was any truth to it. Guess what? There is.
There’s clearly a Tatum-effect at play for low-volume, interior Celtics big men

Here are the numbers comparing Queta & Kornet while sharing the court with Tatum vs not (last 2 seasons):

Queta 2025 - 2026

per 36 minutes scoring:
-with Tatum: 15.6 points on 68.2%
-without Tatum: 12.9 points on 61.4%

Percentage of FGs Made: assisted
-with Tatum: 81.4%
-without Tatum: 58.7%

Kornet 2024-2025

Per 36 minutes scoring:
-with Tatum: 11.2 points on 74.6%
-without Tatum: 9.4 points on 66.1%

Percentage of FGs Made: assisted
-With Tatum: 84.8%
-Without Tatum: 62.3%

Queta 2024-2025

Per 36 minutes:
-With Tatum: 14.8 points on 67.9%
-without Tatum: 11.1 points on 59.3%

Percentage of FGs Made: assisted
-With Tatum: 78.2%
-Without Tatum: 49.1%

Tatum has demonstrably been our best pick & roll ball handler, but these numbers are pretty significant. Too me, his effect on big men is clear with the eye test alone, but it actually might be more glaring when you dive into the numbers.

Tatum not only gets our interior big men more space to operate, but also delivers better passes in better positions.
His gravity and playmaking ability allows them to take more shots AND easier shots.
And Mitchell Robinson is going to benefit from that just like Queta and Kornet did.

Between Robinson’s putbacks on offensive rebounds and the increased volume of high-quality looks from playing with Tatum, I wouldn’t be surprised if he nearly doubles his scoring this season, jumping from 5.7 points to around 10 or 11. He’ll could easily set a career high in scoring, while not playing career high minutes. And best of all, he could do this while keeping his FG% around 72% or better.

Of course, the raw counting numbers will be heavily dependent on how many minutes he plays, which will probably be tied to if he starts or not, but either way I expect a noticeable leap in production for our new addition to the big man rotation.

He and Queta could easily combine for 18-20 points a night, which would be huge for our center position.

Is this just green-team cope or do these numbers give you optimism?

Will Mitchell see a jump in scoring or do you expect more of the same?

Let me know your thoughts. Go Cs! ☘️
