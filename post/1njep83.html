Ideas for Expanding PoE2’s Atlas: Traversal Systems, Thematic Regions, and Progression Mechanics

Hey Exiles! I’ve been thinking about how *Path of Exile 2* could expand the Atlas overworld with immersive traversal and progression mechanics. Inspired by campaign acts and thematic regions, here are 10 ideas that could add depth, variety, and exploration to endgame mapping.

1. Cave System Tunnels Fast Travel

Inspired by Act 1’s Druid’s Grotto, players explore cave-themed maps filled with blocked passages and hidden tunnels. After defeating the cave boss, players exit through a tunnel that connects to a new region. Using Expedition-style explosives, players can clear boulders and cave-ins to unlock alternate routes, secret chambers, or fast-travel exits.
Exit Point: Tunnel exit enables fast travel to other cave-connected maps, creating a subterranean network across the Atlas.

2. Desert Caravan Fast Travel

Inspired by Act 2’s caravan encounter, players battle through harsh desert maps with new dynamic weather elements, leading to a stationary, but moveable caravan. Upon defeating the boss stationed on or near the caravan, it becomes a fast-travel method. The caravan visually traverses a vast desert landscape in the Atlas overworld, unlocking access to distant desert-themed map clusters.
Exit Point: Caravan acts as a fast-travel vehicle across desert zones, reinforcing thematic progression and regional exploration.

3. Sarn Sewer Network Fast Travel

Inspired by Act 3’s urban underbelly, players complete city-themed maps and exit via a sewer grate hidden beneath the final area. This unlocks a sewer traversal system, allowing players to travel between completed Sarn-style maps. The sewer network adds a gritty, interconnected feel to zones and could include unique encounters or modifiers.
Exit Point: Sewer grate enables fast travel between city-based map clusters, forming a hidden underground route across the Atlas.

4. Boatyard and Oceanic Passage Fast Travel

Inspired by Act 4’s boat system, players fight through coastal or dockyard maps and defeat a boss guarding a vessel. Once cleared, the boat becomes a fast-travel method across the Atlas ocean from port to port, or maybe even upstream rivers. This unlocks access to island-based maps, port towns, and sea-themed regions.
Exit Point: Boat acts as a fast-travel method to coastal and island maps, enabling oceanic exploration and progression.

5. Tower Glider Fast Travel

Players can use a Glider Stone on completed tower-type maps to glide across the Atlas to nearby uncompleted towers within a set radius. This mechanic encourages players to complete tower clusters and rewards them with a unique movement system across the atlas.
Exit Point: Glider enables fast travel between tower maps, reinforcing horizontal throughout the atlas progression and regional connectivity.

6. Atlas Zoom and Map Spacing Enhancement

To support traversal mechanics and improve clarity, increase the spacing between Atlas maps and allow a slightly greater zoom-out level. This change enhances the sense of scale, helps players visualize travel routes (caravans, boats, tunnels, gliders), and makes the overworld feel more expansive and immersive.
It also improves strategic planning and regional awareness during progression.

7. Corruption Spread Mechanic

After every 3–5 maps completed, corruption begins to spread across nearby regions. This corruption alters map modifiers, increases enemy aggression, and introduces unique threats or visual changes. Players must locate and eliminate the corruption source to cleanse affected areas.
This mechanic adds urgency, dynamic difficulty, and a layer of strategic map management to endgame progression.

8. Map-Dependent Trade Items

Introduce thematic loot tied to specific map types that can be sold for gold. For example, pirate maps may drop *doubloons* worth 10,000 gold, while cave maps yield *precious ores* valued at 5,000 gold, sought after by regions like Kingsmarch.
These items are not used for crafting but purely for trade, adding economic incentive to explore diverse environments.

9. Bounty Contracts

Optional contracts can be picked up from bounty boards in main hubs or hideouts. These endgame-only objectives include tasks like *“Kill X monsters of a specific type”* or *“Defeat X map bosses.”* Completing contracts grants bonus XP and possibly gold.
They’re non-mandatory and add variety, structure, and short-term goals to mapping without disrupting core progression.

10. Map Memory Echoes

After completing a map, players may trigger a Memory Echo—a ghostly remnant of a past event tied to that location. These echoes may appear as shimmering portals, temporary Atlas nodes, or altered versions of the map with spectral enemies and corrupted terrain. Echoes offer short, high-reward encounters and add replayability through unpredictable post-map events.

Echoes could feature unique enemy variants or mini-bosses, ore fragments or visual flashbacks. High-reward loot or gold caches.

Would love to hear thoughts—what would you add, change, or expand on? Feedback welcome!
