Something that never gets brought up from the 2017 Sixers Trade.

So, the 2017 Draft Trade the Celtics had w/ the Sixers has been discussed ad nauseam. How we got our guy at #3 and got a 1st round pick via the Kings from the Sixers in “the process”, and how that pick then turned into Romeo Langford and was then part of the Derrick White trade etc.

What pretty much never gets brought up and is a credit to Danny Ainge’s genius is: by drafting Jayson Tatum 3rd instead of 1st, is how much cap space Ainge benefited from in that trade.

The NBA drafted has structured salaries based on their draft position. Drafting Tatum 3rd instead of 1st looks like this on the salary sheet:

Fultz vs. Tatum Salary:

  • 1st year: \(7,026,240 vs \)5,645,400 (-$1,380,840)
  • 2nd year: \(8,339,880 vs \)6,700,800 (-$1,639,080)
  • 3rd year: \(9,745,200 vs \)7,830,000 (-$1,915,200)
  • 4th year: \(12,288,697 vs \)9,897,120 (-$2,391,577)

That’s \(7,326,697 of total cap space the 1st four years of their NBA careers, averaging \)1.83 million per year for Ainge to work with.

Just to get this part out of the way: I don’t like advocating for the Billionaires (owners) getting over on the millionaires (players), but the only team that would have drafted Tatum #1 was the Celtics. There also not might have been a team drafting him higher than #3 other than the Celtics.

So Tatum benefited from Ainge and the Celtics recognizing him as the top talent in the draft, and he could have earned more with the Celtics if they stayed put and drafted him 1st, but he also might have earned far less by dropping in the draft, if not for the Ainge and the Celtics.

Either way, Ainge giving himself another $2 million-ish/year in cap space to work with, was an under the radar genius part of the trade that I haven’t seen anyone discuss.
