The State of Self-Cast Spells in PoE 2 is Worrying (the last spell class has been revealed)

With the recent Druid reveal, we now have a complete picture of the “spellcaster” archetypes available at launch. The Druid brings 4 new spells, which look fun, but since all remaining unrevealed classes are confirmed to be melee-focused, we can now assess the full roster of magic.

And honestly I am extremely concerned about the state of self-cast spells in PoE 2.

1. The “Main Skill” Shortage If we look at the classes designed to cast spells, the pool of viable “main damage skills” is shockingly small.

  • The Witch: More than 50% of her kit is dedicated to Minions.
  • The Druid: 4 spells, Offers interesting hybrids, but leans heavily into melee/forms.
  • The Sorceress: This should be the premier spellcasting class. However, looking at her kit, it feels like 75% of her spells are designed as “utility,” “setup,” or “triggers” rather than damage dealers.

2. The Early Game “Utility Trap” Let’s look at the first 6 spells available to the Sorceress. It paints a bleak picture for anyone wanting a smooth leveling experience:

  • Ice Nova: Cannot effectively be used as a main damage source.
  • Flame Wall: Pure utility/buff.
  • Frost Bomb: Long Cooldown.
  • Orb of Storms: Utility/Secondary damage.
  • Frostbolt: Low damage, mostly used as a setup for Nova.
  • Ember Fusillade: weird targeting/setup mechanics.
  • Spark: Literally the only skill in the early roster that feels like a “Main Skill.”

This is disappointing. You have a master of elements, but you are forced into one specific playstyle if you want consistency.

3. The “Cool” Spells are Clunky When you finally unlock the visually impressive spells, they come with massive downsides that kill the fun:

  • Flameblast: Looks incredible, but the Cooldown (or channel time/release mechanic) makes it awkward to use as a primary clear skill.
  • Comet: Amazing visual, but the massive cast time combined with the forced “jump back” animation makes it horrible for flow and positioning.
  • Incinerate: clunky usage.

4. The Death of Build Variety & The “Rainbow Soup” Gameplay My biggest issue after testing the Sorceress is the lack of identity. Because so many spells have cooldowns, downsides, or low damage, you are forced to rotate through everything.

You don’t feel like a Pyromancer or a Frost Mage. You feel like a “Cooldown Manager.” You end up casting a soup of Fire, Cold, and Lightning spells just to deal reasonable DPS. This destroys build variety. Instead of specializing, every Sorceress ends up playing the same “piano” rotation of 5 different elements just to function (at least during campaign).

5. Infusions feel bad On top of the clunkiness, the Infusion system adds friction without enough reward. Chasing little floating orbs for a damage boost breaks the combat flow and simply isn’t fun.

Conclusion While Attack-based classes (Bows, Crossbows, Spears) and Melee (Monk, Warrior, Bear-Druid) look fluid, impactful, and fast, pure Spells feel stuck in a weird place.

We need a revamp of the spell philosophy before launch.

  • Stop putting massive downsides/CDs on every cool spell.
  • Make “utility” spells viable as main damage dealers if built correctly.
  • Reduce the “clunkiness” so we can actually specialize in an element rather than playing “Rainbow UI Rotation.”

Am I the only one feeling this, or are other spellcaster mains worried?
