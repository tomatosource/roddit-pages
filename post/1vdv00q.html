Feedback & Suggestions: Ideas for the Future of Tablets & Waystones

I want to talk about the current state of the Tablet and Waystone system.

I like the idea behind Tablets, but the amount of micromanagement they create is becoming exhausting.

Each Tablet can roll four different modifiers, and those modifiers can completely change the value of a farming strategy. Effects like Abyssal Overrun, extra Breach rares, or additional Ritual rerolls can easily make one setup 3-4x more rewarding than another. Because of that, optimizing isn’t optional.

The biggest issues are:

  • 10-use limit & no loadouts: Every 10 maps I have to stop, open my stash, find the correct Tablet with the correct modifier, and replace it manually. There are no Atlas presets or Tablet loadouts to make this easier.
  • Trading is frustrating: In PoE 1 I could bulk-buy Scarabs and maps, then play for hours. In PoE 2 I’m constantly jumping between hideouts buying individual Tablets and Waystones. Finding specific Tablet modifiers is often difficult, expensive, or simply impossible. I literally have a notepad filled with dozens of saved poe 2 trade links just to search for specific tablets.
  • Waystone crafting isn’t sustainable: Optimized Waystones require Omens, but Ritual doesn’t provide nearly enough of them to sustain mapping.
  • Tablet management becomes a mess: Different farms consume Tablets differently. Regular maps use three, Cities use four, and switching farming strategies constantly desynchronizes Tablet charges. Before long, your stash is full of partially used Tablets with random remaining charges.

Even the Tablet Stash Tab doesn’t solve the management problem—it only solves storage. It still takes too many clicks to find the specific Tablet you need, making it far from ideal to use between maps.

  • Experimentation is punished: If I try a new farming strategy and don’t enjoy it, my partially used Tablets immediately lose a huge portion of their value, making experimentation unnecessarily expensive.

I don’t think Tablets should be removed, they add depth to the endgame. However, maintaining a farming setup currently requires far too much time compared to actually playing the game. In PoE 2, I often spend more time managing Tablets, Waystones, and trades than actually mapping, and sometimes I just log off instead.

My Proposed Solutions:

To fix this, I think GGG should look into one of these approaches:

1. Permanent Tablets Fueled by Salvage

Remove the 10-use limit entirely. You craft a specific tablet once, and it slots into your device permanently. To “power” or refill it, you consume a specific new currency. You would get this currency simply by salvaging the junk tablets you find while mapping. This completely solves stash clutter and the issue of partial charges losing trade value.

2. The PoE 1 Scarab Approach with Atlas Integration

Make Tablets single-use and stackable, similar to PoE 1 Scarabs, but move their modifiers to the Atlas Tree.

Each mechanic would have 2 generic modifiers (such as increased effectiveness or pack size) and 2 mechanic-specific modifiers (for example, cheaper Ritual rerolls or additional Breach rares). Players would allocate these four passives on the Atlas Tree, then simply use stackable Tablets to activate the mechanic.

This keeps the strategic choices while eliminating the need to constantly hunt for specific 4-mod Tablets every 10 maps.

3. Weighted Tablets Tied to the Atlas System

Instead of Tablets directly determining which four modifiers you get, let players choose those modifiers on the Atlas Tree, while Tablets only determine how much power is allocated to each one.

Every Tablet would still roll four values that always total 100% (for example, 35% / 30% / 20% / 15%). Those values would be applied to the four Atlas passives you’ve selected for that mechanic.

This keeps randomness and item hunting alive, but dramatically reduces the gap between a “perfect” and a “bad” Tablet. Buying a “good enough” Tablet would become much easier and cheaper, while players would still have meaningful optimization goals.

4. Allow Delirium Emotions to Craft Tablets

With 0.5, Delirium Emotions can now be applied to Jewels, which is a nice addition. However, in practice only a small handful of Emotions are actually desirable for Jewel crafting, while many others have little to no demand.

Allowing Emotions to also guarantee one Tablet modifier during crafting would give those underused Emotions another meaningful purpose.

This would make crafting a decent Tablet far more realistic while still leaving plenty of room for optimization. It would also give many underused Emotions an actual purpose and create a valuable new currency sink instead of letting them sit unused in players’ stashes.

5. Increase Waystone Omen Supply

Even without changing the Waystone system itself, simply increasing the number of Waystone crafting Omens dropped by Ritual would go a long way toward making Waystone sustain feel more reasonable.

At the moment, sustaining optimized Waystones often requires constant trading because Ritual doesn’t provide enough Omens to keep up with mapping.

6. Atlas Tablet Loadouts

Regardless of whether any of the ideas above are implemented, the Atlas Device desperately needs a Tablet Loadout system.

Players should be able to save multiple Tablet presets (for example, Breach, Ritual, Delirium, City farming, etc.) and switch between them with a single click instead of manually replacing every Tablet one by one.

It wouldn’t solve all of the underlying issues, but it would significantly reduce the constant stash management and make switching between farming strategies much smoother.

7. A Permanent Multi-League Tablet Crafting System (My Favorite Solution)

Create a permanent Tablet crafting system that combines several league mechanics into a single progression path.

  • Abyss: Add a Desecrated modifier to the Tablet.
  • Delirium: Use Emotions to guarantee one generic (prefix) modifier.
  • Expedition: Use Expedition Alloys to guarantee one league-specific (suffix) modifier.
  • Ritual: Introduce a new Omen that limits the next Tablet craft to Prefixes, Suffixes, or rerolls the Implicit (league-specific modifiers such as Ritual Tribute, Breach Hiveblood, Expedition Explosion Radius, Abyss Density, Delirium Fracturing Mirror Multiplier, Temple Additional Crystallized Monsters, etc.).
  • Breach: Increase the Tablet’s Quality.
  • Temple: Increase the maximum Quality to 30% or corrupt the Tablet for an additional crafting outcome.

Unlike the current system, a Tablet would only need to be crafted once and would remain permanently socketed in your Atlas Device. If you wanted to switch farming strategies, you could simply swap to another Tablet using the Atlas Tablet Loadout system suggested above. Instead of replacing Tablets every 10 maps, they would simply be recharged by salvaging unwanted Tablets found while mapping.

One of the reasons I like this idea is that it fits naturally into PoE’s design philosophy. Almost every major gameplay system has its own crafting or progression mechanics. Tablets arguably deserve one more than any other item, because while your gear determines how your character plays, your Tablets determine how you play the endgame itself. They define your farming strategy, your league mechanics, and ultimately where your rewards come from.

The goal isn’t to make Tablet crafting easier—it’s to make it less repetitive. The crafting process itself would be deeper than it is now, but the complexity would come from building and improving a permanent Tablet rather than repeatedly buying, crafting, and replacing temporary ones every 10 maps.

Another goal is to reduce the enormous gap between average and perfect Tablets. Right now, a perfect Tablet setup can easily generate 4-5x more loot than an average one, making certain modifier combinations feel almost mandatory.

With this system, league currencies would allow players to craft a solid, functional Tablet without an unreasonable investment, while still leaving plenty of room for long-term optimization. Players who want to invest heavily could continue improving their Tablets through crafting, creating valuable sinks for league currencies and Divine Orbs.

A Tablet crafted after a few hours shouldn’t be equal to one that’s been perfected over weeks of investment—but the difference also shouldn’t be several hundred percent. A well-designed progression system could reduce that gap to something like 20-30%, rewarding optimization without making average Tablets feel worthless.

Overall, this shifts the complexity from repetitive maintenance to meaningful progression, allowing players to spend more time actually playing the game instead of constantly managing Tablets, trading for replacements, or rebuilding the same setup over and over again.

Randomness Should Still Matter

I also don’t think Tablets should become completely deterministic. Part of what makes PoE’s endgame enjoyable is that no two maps feel exactly the same. A permanent Tablet system should still leave room for randomness, but in ways that create gameplay variety rather than simply multiplying rewards.

Instead of another generic reward modifier, Tablets could have one additional Wild Event modifier. Rather than granting more loot directly, it would determine the chance for a random gameplay event to occur in maps affected by that Tablet.

For example:

  • 20–30% chance for an Additional Breach Wild Event
  • 20–30% chance for an Additional Abyss Wild Event
  • 20–30% chance for an Additional Ritual Wild Event
  • 20–30% chance for an Additional Delirium Wild Event
  • 20–30% chance for an Additional Temple Wild Event
  • 20–30% chance for an Additional Corruption Wild Event
  • 30–50% chance for a Random Wild Event

When a Wild Event is triggered, it would randomly select from a pool of gameplay modifiers within that category. For example, a Breach Wild Event could spawn Abyss monsters inside the Breach, an Abyss Wild Event could open a Breach from an Abyss, Rituals could summon monsters from other league mechanics, Corrupted monster packs could appear, Temple encounters could manifest in maps, or other unique crossover events with their own exclusive rewards.

This keeps the excitement of randomness alive while shifting it away from raw loot multipliers and toward gameplay variety. Players would optimize the category they enjoy, but the exact encounter would remain unpredictable, ensuring that maps continue to feel fresh.

Summary

I know this is a large proposal, and I don’t expect every suggestion to be implemented. The main goal is simply to reduce repetitive Tablet and Waystone management while preserving the depth of the endgame.

Even a few smaller changes, such as Tablet Loadouts or better Waystone sustain, would already make a huge difference. I just want players to spend more time mapping and less time managing their stash, trading, and rebuilding the same setup every few maps.
