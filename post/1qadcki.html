After playing a Plant Oracle and Wolf Shaman this league, I think I get GGG’s stance on the importance of impactful combat now.

From a thematic point of view, I’m the EXACT target audience for both builds. When given a class choice in any tabletop or RPG, ​I have always played Druids. When given the choice between humans or fluffballs/scalies, I pick the more questionable option. You can imagine my hype when GGG finally announced 0.4.

Both builds feels completely different to each other. That good and expected. It’s Druid, after all - Druids are known for diverse gameplay and flexibility.

Yet after playing both to level 95, I’m surprised how unexpected that difference appears:

- Plant Oracle is a 2-button build whose skills, however, have almost zero impactful feeling. You cast it and it damages (+slows/pins) everything around you, but there is a lack of visuals and/or acoustic feedback that ​​make you *feel* like pressing buttons does something. And that’s despite there only being two skills (Entangle + Vines, since rain and debuffs are automated) and despite, objectively, the build doing enough damage. For some reason, this is exhausting since you do almost nothing and it feels like your skills do almost nothing, yet this gameplay experience doesn’t represent the actual mathematical efficiency.

- Wolf Shaman is the exact opposite. You have 6 active skills you frequently use (2 damage options + apply mark via pounce + 2 buffs + warcry), all have great visual and acoustic feedback and it’s ironically a lot less exhausting to play. I mean: That frozen shattering alone is chef’s kiss. Yet, purely mathematically speaking, my Wolf is less efficient than the Plant Oracle,​ but the gameplay experience totally contradicts that.

It’s the first time in years of playing PoE1/2 that I can genuinely *get* what GGG is saying when they try to explain how important it is (for PoE2) to not reduce everything to 1-2 button builds.
