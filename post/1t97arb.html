Keep them both, trade them both, trade Brown or Trade Tatum?

It’s always best to simplify your decision tree. If you are Brad Stevens, those are your 4 choices:

  1. keep them both

  2. trade them both

  3. trade Brown

  4. trade Tatum

Let’s assume the goal is to be competitive enough to put fans in the seats while still trying to have a serious chance at becoming a true contender in the next few years.

(1) keep them both

This would absolutely keep them competitive. Will it make them a contender? Stevens admitted right out of the gate that they need a significant injection of talent. They have the 27th pick. No real cap room, but a 28 million dollar trade exemption. Could that trade exemption be combined with marketable talent on the roster outside the Jays be enough to add a difference maker? Maybe. But we’re probably talking about grabbing a fading star, like Durant.

The biggest problem with continuing to ride the Jays is this: a) they aren’t getting any better. They’ve reached their peak. It’s a good peak, don’t get me wrong. Daily game stats put them as both top-ten type players. In a league where there are probably 25 top-ten type players. That sounds odd, but I think you know what I mean. There are a lot of very good players in the league now, and some players, like Cooper Flagg, that will be there sooner rather than later.
b) Against most playoff teams, the Jays are not as good as the opponent’s best two players. This is something that Celtics fans have a hard time swallowing, but it’s true. An elite player really earns his worth at the end of games. In fourth quarters, the last 5 minutes, the last minute, the last possession. Both Tatum and Brown are surprisingly and distinctly awful in these situations. The higher the pressure the more likely they are to turn the ball over, take a low percentage shot, or miss a wide open shot so badly(this is more Tatum than Brown) that you find yourself stunned at what you are watching. It’s not unusual to see an open twelve footer result in a shot that barely touches rim. Despite numerous opportunities over several years, we still see this. It has not improved and at this point is not going to.

It’s virtually impossible to win close playoff games when the opponent’s best 2 players are both better than yours, or when your best player can’t perform at an elite level in key moments. This is what the Celtics are looking at, and the sooner people recognize it the better. It is what it is.

So for me, option (1) is off the table if you want to contend. Let’s look at the next.

(2) trade them both

This should be at least explored, though it might not be the preferred option, IMO. If a king’s ransom was the return, one which included multiple lottery picks, I’d be listening. The current roster without the Jays is hard-playing and competitive. Hard to see them making the playoffs, but if you add one or two lottery players from this year’s stocked draft, maybe that puts them into the playoffs while setting them up for a longer run.

I’d prefer to keep one of the Jays and end up with a high lottery pick that might represent the future.

(3) trade Tatum or (4) Brown

As to which to trade, it’s whomever gets the biggest return, meaning the highest lottery pick. If I can get a top 2 pick this year for Tatum, I’m jumping on it. Indiana has the second pick. I can’t see why they wouldn’t take Tatum for that pick, unless they are worried about his Achilles.

So for me, the ideal situation is keeping Brown and trading Tatum for the second pick and maybe another first round pick on top of that. This year’s bunch, without Tatum, was going to win more than 50 games. And they have young players…Hugo and Walsh…who will likely continue to make solid improvements. Hugo might even make a legitimate leap. So that Celtic team…which will include a top-two player from the draft…should win 55 to 60 games, and have a player with the potential to develop into what we now know Tatum and Brown are not: clutch players.
