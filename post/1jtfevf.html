GGG, I understand your vision for the size of the maps, but here is why I don’t agree with it. Some actual game feedback and criticism.

I reached the end of the campaign tonight after about 20 hours. This was my third time doing so. When the league launched, I started as a Huntress but quit after 4 hours. Then I remembered Chaos spells got buffed, so I tried Witch (ED/Contagion) for the first time. The fun returned, and I chipped away at the campaign over two days, often taking breaks after just one area.

This got me thinking: Why did I burn out so quickly?

Marco Pierre White (a famous chef) says that perfection is “doing a lot of small things right”—a philosophy I’ve heard echoed by Jonathan. You guys focus on micro-level design: the feel of a spear, damage sound effects, incremental character growth. Meanwhile, players experience the game at a macro level.

What is Path of Exile from a micro-view? Kill monsters -&gt; Drop loot -&gt; Get stronger -&gt; Repeat.

This is the reason why you guys see larger zones as strictly positive (more space = more monsters = more loot = more game). But players push back because you’re missing the psychological weight of pacing. Let me try to explain this better…

In your recent post, you said:

“Many players are reporting that areas feel like they are too large. Normally when this is the case, the main reason is due to other concerns such as monster life or player damage being incorrect, or just a lack of entertaining content in the area. However, we are checking to see if there are any outliers that players are spending too long in to see if any changes to area size need to happen.”

This response shows how you are approaching this issue: if players complain about zone size, the *real* issue must be something micro (monster stats, damage values). But the actual problem is macro, it’s human psychology craves: clear milestones; more frequent dopamine hits; the satisfaction of closing a tab in a mental task list.

The first goal on a new league start is simple: finish the campaign. Smaller objectives (gear upgrades, ascendancies, gem levels) do exist, but you only truly relax after finishing that main objective. This is the reason why you often see streamers take their first big break after finishing the campaign, they’ve finished their first goal.

After that, for me at least, my objective becomes to complete my atlas and only after that I finally rest and think “ok, it’s time to choose a farming strategy”.

In PoE 1, finishing that first goal usually takes 6 hours (for me). After that, I spend another 12 doing the second one (atlas). And, finally, after that, I reach that sweet spot where I have many roads open in front of me that I can choose from.

Bigger zones don’t just affect efficiency; they stretch the psychological distance between rewards. When you double a zone’s size but keep the same number of key encounters, you’re not giving players “more content”, you’re making the existing content *feel* slower.

I’m not suggesting you abandon micro-design (those details make PoE 2 awesome). But when players consistently report a macro-level issue (“zones feel too big”), the answer isn’t just to tweak monster life, it’s to ask: How does this change affect the player’s sense of progression through their main objectives?

Sometimes, the best way to perfect “many small things” is to first ensure they’re building toward the right bigger picture.
