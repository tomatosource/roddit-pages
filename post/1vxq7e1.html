How to prevent your city from being extorted by the NBA

Owners are organized. Players are organized. Fans aren’t.

Every NBA city gets the same offer: pay for the owner’s arena, or watch your team shop for a city that will. Teams have billed the public $33 billion for their buildings, about a billion dollars per market, and the bill comes back sooner every cycle: arenas used to last thirty-five years, and Portland’s was declared aging at thirty. The league wrote rules to protect everyone it needs. Owners have a constitution. Players have a union and half the money, guaranteed. The people who pay for the arenas the teams run have nothing. Five rules fix that, and they fit on one page.

  1. Show us the numbers.
  2. Owners put in real money.
  3. The public gets paid.
  4. Prove relocation threats, or drop them.
  5. Same rules in every city.

Portland just signed an arena deal without these rules. Over twenty years it will leave the public about $1 billion short of a market deal. One deal, one city. Seattle told the league no in 2008, alone; eighteen years later it has a privately built arena and still no team.

In Europe they don’t have this problem, because the threat that powers it doesn’t exist there. The leagues are open and the clubs are rooted: promotion and relegation mean no city bids to keep one of thirty monopoly slots, and a club is its city. In Germany, fans hold majority voting control of their clubs by rule. European law treats public money for a club as state aid that Brussels can order repaid. And the one time owners tried to build an American-style closed league, in 2021, European fans destroyed it in seventy-two hours. Britain answered by writing fan power into law. American fans have never once held the same rules in all thirty markets. That is the entire difference.

If you want to change it let the NBA know here: https://www.ripcitynotripoff.com/compact
