How to fix the infinite atlas at the current stage?!

I noticed how some may dislike the infinite atlas idea and prefer the alternative. Here’s my quick pitch before I dive into my current thoughts on the atlas:
1-Introduces randomness and unpredictability to what otherwise would be quite static.
2-When done right, it simulates sense of adventure not captured by poe 1’s atlas. Offers a different dynamic to poe 1’s atlas.
3-Promotes a dynamic framework of exploration that can be built on in later leagues (lore-wise or mechanic-wise).

Onto the main topic, here are what I believe to be the current issues:

- Too much unpredictable randomness by the allocation of maps and biomes, discouraging agency and control.
- Anti-synergies between concepts - towers and expanding atlas.
Regarding endgame and towers, I haven’t seen enough so I can’t say for certain somebody mentioned it, but I haven’t seen it broken down to conceptual level and linking it to an ever expanding atlas yet. 

My take is that there is an *anti-synergy* between current iteration of towers and infinite atlas. The former wants concentration and static while the latter wants continuity and dynamic. To make towers worth it mean you have to stack as much as possible, but the atlas wants to to explore to get to other regions more! Thus, this makes map/atlas traversal over unjuiced and unstacked maps extremely tedious. If you think that walking across dead zones is bad, moving across “dead maps” is much much worse, like the current state of endgame.

Solutions:

  1. Definitely don’t make stacking towers (or a similar mechanic) worth it, because the down/dead time to set those juiced maps again was a proven bad concept in both 1 and 2, so let’s explore about concepts that synergize with infinite atlas. There needs to be a consider

  2. Ask these questions - “Why should I explore more maps? And how should I explore more maps?”
    I argue that PoE 1 has offered its own set of answers for the concepts of the atlas it gave, but poe 2 hasn’t.
    Imho, encourage more movement and exploration of the atlas, by incentivising the different manners of explorations with a similar (but not the same) benefits. Note - Exploring maps only to get to the same list of maps with the same benefits would get boring quite fast with the infinite atlas; if this was the case, then just make the atlas finite! So, make the explorations intentional and meaningful.
    E.g. I’ll use the 4 conquerors here as an example. Let’s say that each of the C occupies a region - N/S/E/W. You may explore each direction by traversing maps in a particular order to control the order of how to navigate/unlock the Conquerors (i.e. N-W-S-E as one continuous line will unlock a certain order, and N-E-S-W with another list.) Then, with multiple mechanics mapped onto the atlas, you then might have to make choices - How should I explore? Should I priortise exploring the vicinity or move far far away?

  3. You may get rid of towers entirely, or repurpose towers. Towers might become one of the vectors to offer exploration benefits, such as a ‘stop shop’ after traverse across certain maps (i.e. horizontal # of maps or vertical # of maps) to receive different benefits that may not be available should you choose another. You can also add incentives to either keep the current pathway/explored route or change it.

Note: horizontal means maps with the same biome and vertical means maps with different biomes.

  1. Zones: the atlas should have less random and more deterministic generation of zones with the same biomes. The horizontal maps can cluster together in an area (with appropriate terrain differences as seen on the beautiful atlas map) and then later meet a crossroads to cross to other biomes. Hence, the randomness is still there, but gives the player more agency over the random environments (Also makes local knowledge more worthwhile as an atlas node). Then, after zonal occupation is introduced/fixed, you could develop more map configurations and movements as leagues in the future to allow more customisation and diversity. 
    One last note - the atlas maps don’t need to be so far apart. You can certainly scale it down (helps with less scrolling as well, unless moving really really far is your goal).

Ty for reading the long post, but hopefully some of the pain points can be addressed soon.
