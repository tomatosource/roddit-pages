OC: David and Goliath, an analysis of the height of finals team’s leaders through NBA history.

I had the realization that Wemby and Brunson, the two best players in the Finals and the best player for each team, were at least 14 inches apart from each other. Given Brunson is 6’2” and Wemby is 7’4”. I actually think it’s maybe like 16-18 inches, as Brunson is probably 6’1”, and Wemby appears 7’5” at least.

Anyways, I was curious to see if that had ever happened before, and about which team that would favor historically, so I went on basketballreference and decided to look at the height gaps between the best player on each team in the Finals throughout NBA history, and this is all the data that I collected. Enjoy!

Some fun facts:

14 inches is the biggest gap of this kind in history. The next biggest gap is one instance of 13 inches, which was in the 2001 finals with Shaq at 7’1” being 13 inches taller than Iverson at 6’0”.

The average gap is 4.225 inches.

The tallest player on the list is Wemby at 7’4”. The shortest is Allen Iverson at 6’0” (though there are a couple other 6’0” players listed as HMs to be their team’s best player)

The average best player on a Finals team is 6’7.8”. For Finals winners it’s 6’8.5”, for Finals losers it’s 6’7.1”.

Ignoring instances where teams’ best players were the same height, the team with the taller leader won 47⁄74 times, or about 64% of the time. In cases where the gap was at least 9 inches, the team with the taller leader has won 4⁄5 times. (After this series it will either be 5⁄6 or 4⁄6)

Below is the full list of height gaps between each team’s best player in Finals history. The best player on the winning team is listed first for each year.

“Best player” is obviously subjective, so I tried to streamline it by using the metric of either leading scorer in that post season or leader in WS that postseason, with some exceptions. I did make some judgement calls and go away from those stats, but very rarely, and in older years I pretty much just went by those stats as I don’t have much knowledge to pull from.

I put an asterisk next to a name in cases where the “best player” given for a team wasn’t overwhelmingly clear based on consensus, WS, leading scorer and FMVP voting. In cases where I had to pick one of those metrics only and I don’t have knowledge on consensus, I leaned towards leading scorer of that playoff run.
