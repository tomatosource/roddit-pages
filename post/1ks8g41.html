How Executive of the Year Sam Presti Turned Rashard Lewis into an MVP and a Championship Core

This might be the greatest value chain in modern NBA front office history, a masterclass in long-term asset management. In 2007, Rashard Lewis left the Seattle SuperSonics in a sign-and-trade to Orlando. What Sam Presti got in return seemed minor at the time: a trade exception. But that move was the first domino in a series that brought OKC an MVP in Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and a war chest of picks fueling their championship window.

Here’s the full trade tree:

1. Rashard Lewis (via sign-and-trade) → trade exception

In 2007, Lewis went to Orlando. Presti used the trade exception to absorb Kurt Thomas’s contract from Phoenix, but more importantly, he got two first-round picks in the deal.

2. One of those picks became the 24th pick in 2008 → Serge Ibaka

This was Presti’s first real gem. Ibaka became a key part of the Durant-Westbrook-Harden core that reached the 2012 Finals. But his ultimate value came in a later trade.

3. Serge Ibaka → Victor Oladipo + Ersan Ilyasova + draft rights to Domantas Sabonis (2016)

Orlando bet big on Ibaka. OKC got a high-upside guard in Oladipo and a lottery big in Sabonis. Presti cashed in again just a year later.

4. Oladipo + Sabonis → Paul George (2017)

Another high-risk move. PG was seen as a flight risk, but Presti got a star player for two guys who hadn’t broken out yet. While the George-Westbrook duo didn’t deliver playoff success, it set up the most important trade of all.

5. Paul George → Shai Gilgeous-Alexander + Danilo Gallinari + FIVE first-round picks + TWO pick swaps (2019)

The Clippers were basically trading for PG and Kawhi here but I’m sure if they knew they were giving away an MVP they would’ve reconsidered. Plus, the picks from this deal continue to shape OKC’s future, they’ve already been used or traded for Jalen Williams, Cason Wallace, and others.

End result:

From Rashard Lewis in 2007, OKC has eventually acquired:

  • MVP Shai Gilgeous-Alexander
  • 5 first-round picks (used or flipped for Jalen Williams, Cason Wallace, etc.)
  • 2 pick swaps (2023, 2025)
  • And a top-tier championship core

What started as a sign-and-trade to recoup value on a departing Rashard Lewis has become a 15+ year arc that led to arguably the best rebuild in NBA history.

This is why Sam Presti is the longest tenured GM in the league and the Executive of the Year, and his moves will be studied by front offices for decades to come.
