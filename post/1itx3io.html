The search for an “American” face of the NBA and talking heads’ weird narrative on Jayson Tatum

Recently, Stephen A. Smith made a bold (clickbaity?) claim that the “face of the NBA” can not be a player who is not American. The funny thing about it is SAS and his colleagues in the NBA mainstream media played a hand in not making an American the “face of the NBA” by helping build a negative narrative on Jayson Tatum. Tatum’s resume has the elements that media can spin as “face of the NBA”-worthy if they actually want to.

- Played for a popular college team in Duke

- Has led the league’s winningest franchise in scoring since age 21

- At age 25, has been to two NBA Finals and won one, has garnered one 3rd Team All NBA selection and three straight 1st Team All NBA selection

- Was the youngest #1 option in a championship winning NBA team since Dwyane Wade in his first championship season with Miami

But the loud voices in the NBA media keep on pushing the narrative that despite the accolades, Tatum is not a marquee player because, as SAS puts it, he supposedly does not have the “persona” of a superstar even if, as SAS even admits, his numbers say so. SAS goes on a detailed explanation.

“You have guys that are just about their business. Grab my lunch pail, show up to work, handle my business, do my job, go home. That’s a lot of players. Then, you have special dudes that you can tell gravitate to being the marquee. They want to be the marquee.”

But what does this actually mean? If Tatum does not have “persona,” why is he marketable? Let’s look at jersey sales. For the first half of this season, Tatum is 3rd in jersey sales, just behind Steph and LeBron. In 2023-24, the Celtics’ championship season, he was 2nd in jersey sales, outselling even LeBron. In 2022-23, he was 3rd in jersey sales, again just behind Steph and LeBron. Is that a guy who is not marketable?

Media people like SAS could whine all they want about not having an American “face of the NBA.” But there is an American player selling jerseys just behind Steph and LeBron who has the stats, accolades, and even a title to support his case but for some reason the media do not want to build a positive narrative around him. One almost gets the impression that for media guys like SAS, the “face of the NBA” should still play like Mike and have an asshole competitive attitude like Mike, and that is the only guy they can anoint as having the “persona,” actual achievements and on-court play be damned.
