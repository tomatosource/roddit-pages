3-PT Analysis on the loss

This loss was honestly not that bad if you consider the 3-PT discrepancy mathematically. Yes, cold streaks and slumps do happen, but if you consider a statistical analysis on this game, you get the following results.

Knicks: Usually have 36.9% 3PT rate. Expected to make 13.65 3-PTs out of 37, they ran 3.35 3-PTs above expected. Variance over 37 3PA is .369 * .631 * 37 = 8.62.

Celtics: Usually have 36.8% 3PT rate. Expected to make 22.08 3-PTs out of 60, they ran 7.08 3-PTs below expected. Variance over 60 3PA is .368 * .632 * 60 = 13.95.

A distribution over large numbers becomes Gaussian. Overall, the 3-PT discrepency vs. normal is -10.43 (yes, this means a game like today would have likely been a 30-point blowout if it was remotely close to average). The overall variance is 8.62 + 13.95 = 22.57, so the overall standard deviation would be approximately 4.75 3-PT shots, making today’s Z-score for the Celtics -2.19. A Z-score of -2.19 lies in the bottom 1.8 percentile.

This means that all other stats equal (to how they played out), if we keep the 3PT rates the same but re-simulate the Knicks and Celtics 3-PTs, we will on average have gotten over 30 more points from 3PTs that we would make and/or the Knicks would not make. And if we made two more threes, we would have handily won, despite being at the bottom 3.8% percentile. To put this in perspective, on average we would have won at least 25 out of 26 games that gone down like today’s just from gameplay, and we were immensely lucky.
