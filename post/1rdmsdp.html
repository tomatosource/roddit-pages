How Frequently Did MLB Teams Wear Grey on the Road in 2025?

The idea of the away team wearing grey in baseball stems back to the team on the road not having easy access to washing machines, so grey was deemed a better alterative to white. Over the years since then, baseball and uniforms have evolved. In the 70s and 80s, powder blue became a popular road alternative for several teams, with teams using color pants to match the top. In the 90s, black was frequently used as a team’s jersey color. In the 2000s the vest phenomenon was alive and well.

Currently, I’ve noticed a very high uptick in teams wearing colored tops. Additionally, this lends to instances of both teams wearing colored tops, or no team wearing either white or grey. With the advent of City Connect jerseys now and teams wearing throwbacks frequently, the aesthetic of the game has made a shift once again. Personally, I like a monochromatic look and enjoy the pants matching the top, regardless of color (actually more points and cheers to teams in orange/red/black/yellow/blue pants). Yet, until I control a team (or the league), the trend may only further continue.

I decided to crunch the numbers to see what the trends for grey uniforms on the road were in 2025. The table below has the team, the number of games in grey and the percentage of games in grey. UniformLineup.com was my source for this information. If this is interesting to anyone, I can see the numbers for 2015 and compare them against these to see if there is in fact a trend.

Otherwise, enjoy!
