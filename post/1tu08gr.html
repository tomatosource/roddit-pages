The story of the on-court Larry O’Brien Trophy is basically Spurs lore

https://preview.redd.it/njmwm722mp4h1.jpg?width=2804&format=pjpg&auto=webp&s=d6ebab80f993480dd7f6b138a318f951bb142889

Hey y’all! Not sure if this has been posted here before, but I just came out of Reddit hibernation to share an interesting tidbit regarding the relationship between the on-court Larry O’Brien Trophy and the Spurs’ runs to the NBA Finals.

As many of you know by now, the 2026 NBA Finals will see the return of the Larry O’Brien Trophy painted at center court—a welcome sight that many die-hard fans have been yearning for over the past decade.

The history of the on-court trophy began during the 2005 NBA Finals, a seven-game slugfest between the league’s two best defensive teams: the San Antonio Spurs and Detroit Pistons. What a way to make a first impression. Not only did we get a full seven-game showcase of the logo, but who else remembers the giant Larry O’Brien Trophy replica the players actually stood on during the starting lineup introductions?

Two years later, the NBA refreshed the design of the Larry logo for both the court and the Finals jersey patches. Once again, the new look debuted in San Antonio as the Spurs gave a young LeBron James a harsh lesson in just how difficult winning a championship really is (let alone six of them) with a four-game sweep of the Cleveland Cavaliers.

Unfortunately for us fans, the tradition didn’t last long. We only got two more Finals with the updated design before the league ultimately scrapped the on-court trophy in the name of player safety. Sigh.

Fast forward to Wednesday’s Finals: the long-awaited rematch of the NBA Cup. (Somewhere, Adam Silver is smiling big ear-to-big ear as one of his money-grabbing creations accidentally produced a Finals preview).

Before the season even began, it was reported that the iconic Larry O’Brien Trophy would finally return to center court. And coincidentally, the franchise chosen to debut the newest version of the logo just happens to be the same team from the Alamo City.

You know, that team many predicted would be fighting for a Play-In spot. Too young. Too inexperienced. Needed another year or two before being taken seriously as a contender.

Instead, they ended a seven-year playoff drought, snapped a twelve-year Finals drought, and now get to introduce the latest version of the Larry O’Brien Trophy on the NBA’s biggest stage.

Because this is tradition.

From the SBC Center to the AT&T Center and now the Frost Bank Center, the Larry O’Brien Trophy and Spurs Finals basketball have somehow remained linked across multiple generations.

The NBA Finals are finally going to feel right again.

Go Spurs Go!

Extra tidbit: The last time the NBA had proper Playoffs and Finals court signage (and no, I don’t consider the recent Google, YouTube TV, and other corporate-sponsored sideline slop) was in 2014 - the final year before we got this new NBA from Adolf Silver.

Coincidentally, that was also the Spurs’ most recent championship: a historically dominant Finals performance that sent LeBron James right back to Cleveland.

I’m just saying… maybe the basketball gods have a preferred aesthetic.
