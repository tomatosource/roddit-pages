Hugo Gonzalez is in elite company

When González started his rookie season with incredible plus-minus numbers, most people assumed he would come back to earth eventually. After all, net ratings of 15 and over are typically reserved for high-impact stars — such as SGA and Jokić, or LeBron James in his prime — and the elite role players on the best teams, such as Alex Caruso.

But González is still going strong, five months into the season.

Even the metrics that tame the noise by pushing the ratings towards zero — including RAPM — have been overwhelmed by the strength of González’s on-off numbers, now that we have a better sample size.

The upshot: González ranks seventh in the NBA in single-season RAPM, and fifth in the three-year version.

Even with the NBA introducing better individual stats, there are still blind spots. For example, we don’t have data on the frequency and effectiveness of weakside screens. And a player whose pesky defense forces an 8-second violation doesn’t get any credit.

González is the type of player the box score and even these new categories fail to capture — the type who shines in impact metrics, regardless of his stats, much like Shane Battier, the original No-Stats All-Star.
