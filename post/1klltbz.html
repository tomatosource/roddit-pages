Unless Zanik and Ainge have an ace up their sleeve, this Utah Jazz tank has been a disaster so far

Rudy Gobert and Donovan Mitchell were traded in the offseason in 2022 after a lackluster playoff exit that convinced the Utah Jazz FO that the team had reached their ceiling. I’m not about to debate that. But unless Justin Zanik and Danny Ainge know something we and the rest of the league don’t, then what has happened since then has been a very poorly executed tank.

It started in the 2022-23 season, when the Jazz, led by freshman coach Will Hardy, accidentally got out to a 10-3 start thanks to impressive play by Lauri Markkanen, the veteran leadership of Mike Conley, and the league’s inability to game plan for the new roster and coach.

The hot streak didn’t last, but the damage was done. The Jazz were close to .500 by the trade deadline—far better than they wanted to be—which prompted the FO to clean house again, sending Conley, Vanderbilt, Beasley, and Alexander-Walker out the door (all guys who appeared in the playoffs this season, in addition of course to Mitchell and Gobert).

Unfortunately the Jazz still managed to eke out 10 more wins, landing them the 9th, 16th, and 28th picks, with which they picked Taylor Hendricks, Keyonte George, and Brice Sensabaugh. These players have some promise but none of them have moved the needle yet, and Hendricks missed most of the season (and opportunity to develop) with a broken fibula.

The 2023-24 season started worse but despite the FO’s best efforts, the team was once again at .500 at the trade deadline, forcing the team to trade Olynyk, Agbaji, and Fontecchio at the deadline. This one worked out better, as the Jazz only won 5 more games after that, and went on a masterful 13-game losing streak.

The 2024 draft saw the Jazz picking 10th, 29th, and 32nd. They ended up with Cody Williams, Isaiah Collier, and Kyle Filipowski. Once again, no standouts here, although Collier’s playmaking has been encouraging as of late.

The Jazz had an opportunity to trade Markkanen or to subject him to another year of tanking, and they opted for the latter. Probably for fear that they’d once again win too many games, the FO went all in on tanking this season, sitting healthy players (especially Markkanen and Kessler) at a shocking rate, incurring fines from the league and frustrating fans.

And all that effort has not paid off, as the Jazz unexpectedly fell to 5th in the draft lottery, losing out on Cooper Flagg and other top prospects.

The Jazz roster construction is weird and they have more picks than they can possibly develop. Something really must be done this offseason or Jazz fans are looking at yet another year of watching Markkanen and Kessler waste their lives away, and seeing Collin Sexton give 110% to a team that doesn’t want to win, and wondering why we’re wasting a John Collins renaissance on intentional mediocrity.

TL;DR: tanking sucks and it’s hard to watch the Cavs and the Wolves play in the second round while the Mavs, who beat your team so badly that they blew it up, get Cooper Flagg
