[OC]: The Era of Passing Big Men Is Only Beginning

Whether it is basketball or other parts of culture, trends always seem to have a history of coming back in style. I think this is something we’re starting to see in basketball as teams run more offense through big men. Though this time, it comes with a bit of a twist.

If you watch games now, you’ll see a lot of teams playing “five out” offense that features all five players playing behind the three point line. Though in prior eras, we saw a lot of “inside out” where teams ran their offense through big men posting up, who if they didn’t have looks to score would kick it out to teammates to reset, or potentially find cutters or perimeter shooters. Your bigs like Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Shaq and Hakeem Olajuwon were all dominant in this regard, and all three to varying degrees showed passing.

To start the 2010’s, we still had bigs who could facilitate. Tim Duncan, the Gasol brothers, Joakim Noah and even Blake Griffin were examples of this. Though, towards the middle of the decade, we also saw a shift in teams incorporating handoffs. Handoffs can be very useful as a big doesn’t have to be a great passer to incorporate them, it can create mismatches and space for a player to attack downhill, and also act as a ball screen.

In 2015-16 (Nikola Jokic’s rookie year), only eight teams per Synergy incorporated handoffs on at least 5% of their offensive possessions (the Nuggets were one of them). Though by the 2019-20 season, nearly 20 teams incorporated them at that rate. Some in that span even incorporated handoffs or DHO’s on around 10% of possessions. Though in recent years that trend has stabilized to a little over half the league incorporating those at this rate.

Teams still have bigs score and facilitate out of the post. Players like Jokic and and Embiid are utilized out of the post at a high rate, while guys like Giannis, AD, and Bam Adebayo are also incorporated there a decent amount. Though bigs with the game being played in more space are very capable of bringing the ball up to facilitate offense, making quick decisions out of short rolls, passing off the dribble when attacking closeouts, or even running pick and rolls.

A tipping point in this to me was when Jason Kidd, as the then-coach of the Bucks gave Giannis the keys to be the lead ball handler/facilitator. Teams have always coveted bigger guards for a variety of reasons. It can create mismatches as scorers, and they might see different things than a shorter/more traditional point guard might not. Kidd (a bigger point guard for his era) realized this. Giving Giannis this opportunity not only let him get out in transition more, but he got more reps in how to read opposing defenses, and I think this is an underrated part of why he’s the player you see today.

Nikola Jokic of course doesn’t run like Giannis, but is a blend of all the skills mentioned. He’s also a ridiculous scorer out of the post with his touch and footwork, is a deadly shooter, and can score out of essentially any action. Though his ability to read angles and think a step ahead of opposing defenses makes him one of the best passers we’ve ever seen.

A fun thing about basketball is that no matter how old you are, you can watch various players and try to incorporate some of their skills into your game. Some of the recent draft picks even mention guys drafted just 2-3 years ago when asked about this. Though Jokic has now been at minimum an All-Star caliber player for the better part of a decade. So, between guys starting as guards/growing into their bodies and big men growing up watching him, we’re seeing more guys incorporate elements of his game. This could go from someone who is already an All-Star like Alperen Sengun, to a rookie in Yang Hansen.

I’m interested to hear what others think, in terms of if we’ll continue to see more big men facilitating offense in the future. With the league continuing to emphasize versatility/quick decision making, I feel more bigs enter the leagues knowing they have to at minimum, make quick decisions with the ball. Outside of your Jokic’s and Embiid’s, we already have players like Sengun and Domantas Sabonis operating as offensive hubs, though the same level of scorers. Though we also have younger players like Evan Mobley among others, who can put the ball on the floor and facilitate in a variety of ways as well. At the least, I think we’re going to see more secondary facilitators of various sizes, and while we might never see “the next Jokic”, his impact as a passer will be seen in the game for years to come.
