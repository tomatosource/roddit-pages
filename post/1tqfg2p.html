Under the new set of draft rules, it would still be possible for the Utah Jazz to draft in the top 5 for a 3rd consecutive year

If you want to understand why the Grizzlies voted against the new draft rules, which prohibits the 2027 Jazz pick they acquired in the JJJ trade from landing in the top 5, you have to consider how stupid the new provision is that picks tied to the original teams cannot draft in top 5 in three consecutive years. Consider the following scenarios in which the Jazz can still get a top 5 pick:

Scenario 1) The Jazz trade with a team that needs cap relief or young prospects like the Kings or Pelicans for their 2027 pick which lands in the new lottery as bottom 16 teams. And if the Jazz acquire another 2027 pick and it lands in the top 5, they get a 3rd consecutive top 5 pick.

Scenario 2) The Jazz get the 2nd most favorable of the TWolves, Cavs and Jazz pick in the 2027 draft. Lets say the TWolves and Cavs deal with injuries or turmoil and enter the new lottery as bottom 16 teams. If both end up in the top 5, the Jazz will get a third consecutive top 5 pick.

I’m not saying either of these scenarios are likely to happen, and in the 2nd scenario the Grizzlies would also get a top 5 pick. But I think it just shows how incredibly easy it is to get around the spirit of this rule, and how the current implementation does not prevent it from happening

If they wanted to actually prevent it they would have just made it so the teams actually drafting each pick cannot have three consecutive top 5 picks or just not included it at all since the new flattened odds make it unlikely to do that anyway.
