Infield In

I dig the strategic side of baseball, and generally I think I get why certain defensive alignments make sense (no doubles, outfield shallow), but I keep struggling to understand the real value of “infield in”. Late in close games, it makes a little more sense - try to prevent runs from scoring. I don’t always think it makes sense to bring the infield in with runners at 2nd and 3rd, but maybe there is data to back it up.

A few days ago, the Phillies and Mets were playing. It was the 6th inning of a tie game, and the Mets loaded the bases with 1 out. The Phillies played the infield in, and I cannot for the life of me understand why. Contact in the infield is almost guaranteed to be a double play at normal depth, so you wouldn’t need to come home at all. Were they worried about the speed of the batter? Their own offense not being able to match the Mets one possible run? The Mets hit a single up the middle, two scored, and that was the pivotal moment of the game. And I’m scratching my head at the rationale.

Can someone explain to me why this made sense?
