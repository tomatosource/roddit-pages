Eastern Conference Breakout players and X-factors

Trying to figure out what NBA players as breakout players or x-factors every season is one of my favorite offseason activities. Though everyone has different definitions of a player “breaking out”, so I also wanted to look at X-Factors who could alter how their respective teams performed this upcoming season. With that, here are some of my favorites.

Matas Buzelis: Given the way teams covet big wings who can score in a variety of ways, I still have trouble believing Buzelis fell to the later lottery in the 2024 draft like he did. Though for a 20-year-old, he showed very nice movement off the ball scoring off cuts and screens, while also being very dangerous out in transition. Buzelis averaged 13 points a game in 31 games as a starter last year, and I’m really interested to see how he performs as he gets more on-ball reps, and he adds some streangth/draws more fouls. Despite being subpar on his open threes off the catch per Synergy, Buzelis was still a league average shooter from 3 which is encouraging, assuming he improves as well. I’m not expecting him to become an All-Star in year two, but wouldn’t be stunned if the foundation that shows he’ll become one in the future.

Ron Holland: If the Pistons are going to build off their drastic improvement last year, Holland will play a major role in that happening. The 5th pick of the 2024 draft is a dynamic athlete who competed defensively, and was very dangerous when driving downhill or out in transition. Though like Ausar Thompson, Holland’s shooting is a concern, and one of them is going to have to improve if they’re going to share the floor. Given that Ron was actually a decent free throw shooter, he’s my bet to make that jump, and showed some flashes in the very small Summer League sample. Adding even league average shooting from three would also be a drastic boost to him attacking close outs given his ability to draw fouls and get downhill.

Jay Huff: Prior to last season, Huff had played in 31 NBA games across three seasons with three different teams. Now, he could play a vital role for the defending Eastern Conference champions. Huff last year with the Grizzlies played just 12 minutes a game, but he was very productive from deep, making 40% of his threes while attempting 3 a game. Jay was making these shots in pick and pop, transition, or on spot ups, where can attack a close out on occasion too. Huff also averaged nearly a block a game, which made him one of the league’s more productive shot blockers on a per-minute basis. Given that the Pacers are losing Myles Turner’s blend of outside shooting and shot blocking, The Pacers acquired Huff in a trade, where he’ll get to showcase those same skills, and should play a valuable role over the next few seasons in Indiana.

Ryan Rollins: Prior to last season, the 2nd round pick in the 2022 draft had appeared in just 25 NBA games. Though Rollins made the most of his opportunity with the Bucks. The young guard started for them down the stretch in the regular season, where he shot over 40% from three, and while he excelled on spot ups, can make them off the dribble too. Rollins is also a competitive defender, and with a 6’10” wingspan, showed he is very active forcing steals, altering shots and causing deflections. He’s also a serviceable secondary playmaker, so I wouldn’t be stunned if the new deal he signed with the Bucks’ this past offseason turns into one of the league’s better values.

Anthony Black: We know about the Magic’s primary core of Paolo Banchero, Franz Wagner, Jalen Suggs, and Desmond Bane who they acquired this offseason. Though some of the role players will need to make a leap if the Magic are going to truly become threats in the East, and Black could make the biggest difference. Anthony is a very talented defender, and can make quick decisions with the ball. Though he’s had struggles shooting from three and inconsistency finishing at the rim in his first two seasons. Part of the shooting woes last year was due to a brutal three month stretch, though he did progress as the year went on. Black will take on a larger role off the bench this season, and could provide a positive impact given the versatile defense and quick decision-making alone. Though an offensive leap could be a huge boost for the Magic’s present and future.

I have some other players who I felt fit this mold, such as Jared McCain, though these were just a few examples of who intrigued me the most. I’m also interested to hear what players in the East you feel could breakout or be x-factors for their teams this year as well. This conference looks like it will be wide open, so it’s going to be fascinating to see how things play out over the coming season.
