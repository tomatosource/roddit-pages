September 5th might be a key date

Correct me if I’m wrong, but Anfernee Simons can’t be traded in combination with other players for salary-matching purposes until 60 days after he’s acquired. If his official acquisition date was July 7, that would make September 5 the first day Boston could aggregate him with other contracts.

That could be a key date to watch, because you could do something like combine Simons (\(27.6M) + Hauser (\)10M) + Tillman (\(2.5M) = **\)40.1M outgoing**.

If the receiving team is over the cap but below the 1st apron, they can take back up to 125% of their outgoing salary + $100k. That means a team in that range would only need to send out about $31.92M to take in Boston’s \(40.1M — letting Boston shed about \)8M in salary, potentially getting them under the first apron. There are several teams who can absorb that extra ~$8M without crossing the first apron: https://www.spotrac.com/nba/apron/_/year/2025

Taking it a step further — purely as an illustration — suppose the Chicago Bulls sent out Vucevic (\(21M) + Okoro (\)11M) = $32M, taking back Simons + Hauser + Tillman.

Boston could then keep Vucevic and re-route Okoro + a 2nd-rounder to a team that can fit his salary into existing trade exceptions or cap space (Pelicans, Hawks, Pistons, Jazz, Wizards, Heat): https://www.spotrac.com/nba/transactions/trade-exceptions

Net result: Boston goes from \(40.1M (Simons + Hauser + Tillman) to just a \)21M expiring (Vucevic) — shedding about $19M and getting under the luxury tax entirely.

That’s just one example to illustrate the mechanics — you can play with the numbers and find versions that make more basketball sense for both teams.
