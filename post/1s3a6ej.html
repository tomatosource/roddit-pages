A couple weeks ago, I made a post on here about every 80-game stretch where a player batted .400+. A few comments pointed out that this was unfair to anyone who maybe batted over .400 for a longer stretch, but not for exactly 80 games. Here’s some justice for Chipper Jones and the rest of the guys who accomplished it. And yes, Chone Figgins returns.

I included the plate appearances column because to qualify for a batting title, you need 3.1 plate appearances per team game. This list is filtered only on qualified streaks. To win a batting title in a162-game season, you of course don’t need to play in all 162. You just need 502 plate appearances. With that being said, there are nine streaks here that, if they were somehow extracted out of this stretch of games and became the only games they played in a single season, would have qualified for batting .400 in a full season. I think Todd Helton gets a special shoutout for doing it in a single calendar year.

There’s a lot of overlap here with the 80-game list that I put out, but I would say the most surprising addition here is 36-year-old Tony Fernandez.

Tony Gwynn shows up on here three times, so I was curious what his total batting average was from the beginning to the end of those streaks. From 6/10/1993 to 7/14/1997, Gwynn hit .380 (767⁄2018). Pretty unreal in modern times.

If you’re curious who had the longest streak, including the old times, that would be Rogers Hornsby, who hit .400 over 786 games from 7/21/1920 to 5/12/1926.
