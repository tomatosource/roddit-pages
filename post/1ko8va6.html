Examples of multi-star teams who lost a superstar for a season

I’ve seen a lot of comments about Boston’s depth and the potential for Jaylen Brown to keep this team as a contender next season without Tatum. I wanted to look at some historical examples to get a sense of how realistic this truly is for a multi-star 61 win team to remain competitive after a 1st Team All-NBA superstar went down.

The best case scenario, to me, seems to the 1993-94 Bulls which saw their team go from a 57 win champion with Jordan + PIppen + Grant, to a 55 win 2nd round exit contender with Pippen + Grant during MJ’s first retirement. Only 2 wins less, which is remarkable considering how many “superstars” in the league struggle to crack 50 wins solo. Pippen and Grant both saw their output increase with the heightened roles. It is, however, important to point out that Pippen was a 7x All-NBA superstar (making 1st team 3 times) as well as a 10x All-Defense (8x first team). This was not merely an all-star level player. He was as elite as they come. Grant himself was a borderline all-star (1x) and 4x All-defense big. Additionally, the Bulls had a boost by adding Kukoc that season. The Jordan-stans also like to point out that the Bulls were only 2 seasons removed from being a 67 win team and merely looking at the 57-&gt;55 win drop undersells how much worse they were without him.

To be clear, I really do see Jaylen Brown, despite not being All-NBA the past two seasons, as someone very capable of averaging 27+ nightly and making one of the All-NBA teams with a heightened role. He’s legitimately an elite-level talent and nobody is disputing that. He’s averaged about 27.3 points in the 39 games Tatum has missed and Boston has a 22-17 record in those games (46 win pace), but much of those games predate this contender iteration of the team - some of which were alongside other stars like Kyrie.

I’d go further to say that Derrick White has shown the ability to step up in games Tatum has missed. For example, the March 25th game against Portland where he dropped 41 points and then Game 5 of this Knicks series where he had 34. The hope with him is that he’s reached such a point in his career arc that it would almost be a “reverse big 3” where instead of seeing Pierce and Ray sacrifice shots for the greater good, White’s stats would see a bump with greater shot attempts.

Examples:

  • Ray 2007 (w/Rashard): 26.4 ppg 43.8%/37.2% on 21 FGA per game
  • Ray 2008 (w/Big 3): 17.4 ppg 44.5%/39.8% on 13.5 FGA per game
  • Pierce 2007 (w/Al Jeff): 25ppg 43.9%/38.9% on 18.1 FGA per game
  • Pierce 2008 (w/Big 3): 19.6ppg with 46.4%/39.2% on 13.7 FGA per game

If you do that math in reverse and look at White’s stats over the past couple year (15.8 points, 45.1%/38.9% on 12.1 FGA per game) and consider that Ray and Pierce had an average drop of about 28% in points when sacrificing for the greater good, then increase White’s 15.8 points by 28%, it’s completely reasonable to imagine 31 year old White scoring 20-24ppg next season with a slight dip in efficiency.

That theoretically would leave Boston with one legitimate All-NBA level talent (Brown) and one legitimate borderline all-star (White). I’ve seen it often mentioned that Boston’s season might play out similar to the 2009 post-championship Celtics after losing KG. The surface-level thinking there, despite KG going down, they only saw their record drop from 66 wins to 62. However, important to point out here that KG only missed 25 regular season games that year. And while our record in those 25 games (62 win pace) wasn’t remarkably different from the games he DID play, there’s a couple factors to consider, One, that this had already been a season of regression for the aging 32 year old Garnett. Even when healthy he was now averaging 15.8 points and looking a step slower than his prime as the toll of that season saw them drop from a team that started 27-2 to one that was losing ground. Two, any regression to the BIg 3 was being countered by the rise of Rondo who had gone from a role player who was more or less along for the ride during the 2008 title run, to being one of the leaders of the team. 21 year old 2008 Playoff Rondo: 10 points, 6.6 assists, 4.1 rebounds, 1.7 steals to 22 year old 2009 Playoff Rondo 17 points, 9.8 assists, 9.7 rebounds, 2.5 steals isn’t the same player.

So while it’s convenient to look at that second round exit team and say “Boston was fine without KG because Ray/Pierce stepped up… so too will be the Tatumless Celtics with Brown/White stepping up”, there’s no such “young rising star” on this team who can unexpectedly make a leap from role player to All-star. 28 year old Payton Pritchard could conceivably give us a boost with a backup to starter transition, or he might see himself struggle facing starter-level defenses permanently.

In-fact, all signs to this roster actually regressing next season. Jrue Holiday is widely considered to be a salary dump given potential 500 million tax bill. The team seems destined to move on from Porzingis (final year of his deal), even if it means getting expiring contracts and a late 1st. Horford, who has been crucial and underrated (he started 42 of his 60 games) maybe hangs it up, or maybe he goes elsewhere to a team with more promising championship aspirations, but even if he stays he’s been steadily regressing for years. Kornet is an unrestricted free agent. Hauser is a likely candidate to be salary dumped for cap relief and some form of future draft compensation. Our routes to improve the roster are slim. No offense to Scheierman, Walsh and Davison, but there isn’t anything remotely resembling an up-and-coming rising prospect on this team.

So, if on one spectrum you have a “bast case” spotty example of 55-62 win 93 Bulls/09 Celtics 2nd round exits, on the other end you have to look at the sobering reality of the the more reasonable outcome of Brown + White + Pritchard (minus Zingis and Holiday) + low-level prospects/role players trying to dig out a winning season.

Post-Shaq MVP-level Kobe Bryant had Lamar Odom alongside him (certainly a White-level fringe star) and they won an average of 40 game per season in the 3 years prior to David Stern gifting them Pau Gasol. Kobe went from 56 wins/Finals to 34 wins and the lotto that first season carrying the Lakers.

Paul Pierce (Brown-comp) played alongside Antoine Walker and never once cracked 50 wins. Missed the playoffs in 5 of his 9 season. Scoff at Walker all you want, but he was a 4x All-Star (and eventual 3rd best player on a Miami title team) who was ahead of his time as a floor-spreading Big man. A 6’9 power forward playing 81 games and averaging 23.4 points, 9 rebounds, 5.5 assists, 1.7 steals with 2.7 three per game on 36.7% 3P% would be considered a prototypical big by today’s standards. He and Pierce (25ppg) won 36 games that season.

Ray Allen in his 5 seasons with the Sonics, much of with alongside 22ppg 2x All-star Rashard Lewis won an average of 40 games per season.

Russell Westbrook, an MVP level player, saw his team go from a 59 win WCF team in 2013-14 alongside Kevin Durant, to a 45 win lotto team in 2014-15 with Durant missing 55 games (they went 27-28 in games Durant missed). Later, when Durant left OKC, even with the addition of all-star Paul George alongside Westbrook, they failed to ever crack 50 wins and were bounced in the first round both years despite Paul George literally being 1st-Team All NBA in one of those seasons.

We can look Anthony Davis on the 2017-18 Pelicans winning 48 games alongside Boogie Cousins only to follow it up with 33 wins the next season without Boogie despite having prime Jrue Holiday and Julius Randle alongside him

Dwyane Wade and Chris Bosh saw their team go from a 54 win Finals participant in 2013-14 to 37 win lotto team in 2014-15 team once they saw LeBron leave and replaced him essentially with Luol Deng.

There’s several examples we can point to involving teams bottoming in between All-NBA departures/arrivals. Whether it be Kyrie leading a 33 win Cavs team the year prior to LeBron joining (53 wins and Finals appearance), the Warriors going from a 57 win team to 15 win team the year Steph missed, the 2010 Cavs going from a 61 win team to 19 win team without LeBron, the 76ers going from playoff participant to 24 win team with Embiid hobbled this season, and so many others.

I see all this and think to myself the simple reality is that losing an All-NBA 1st Team Superstar is astronomically impactful even if you have a strong supporting team with star talent. I’ve said that, on paper, this seems like a 36-43 win season without Tatum, which has gotten me some heat, but I truly feel that’s accurate.

Perhaps the good news - there’s some rumblings that maybe, just maybe, Tatum will return next season. While Durant (the only real example of a player remaining elite post-injury in sea of destroyed careers) skipped a full season and took 18 months to rehab (and recently admitted he didn’t feel like himself for 2 years), there’s examples like Kobe who tore his Achilles in April and was back by December at the start of the season 8 months later. And while Kobe, who was older, and only played 6 games before fracturing his knee, most seem to think this was a fluke and not the result of coming back too early. Medical advancements point to other more recent examples with extremely optimistic timeframes in as little as 4-6 months depending on severity of injury, age and work ethic of player, and type of surgery. Best-case, Tatum has all of this on his side.

Could Boston reasonably expect a return post All-star break in the Feburary time-frame (9 months) and gear up for a playoff push? If so, we’d only need this middling team to tread water until his return to incorporate him for a proper championship run.

If, however, signs point to Tatum going the Durant route and skipping a full year where he may or may not return to 100%, I strongly question the value in an also-ran season that nets us a mid-1st. I read the word “retooling” so often here suggesting in the midst of our salary dumping, we somehow swap out fringe-star talent like Jrue/Porzingis for veteran talent that keeps us competitive. That seems like a losing battle, imo. By far the most practical “re-tooling” step the team could take (barring an expected Tatum return) would be tank the 2025-26 season and do everything possible to net the highest possible pick in the 2026 draft. Best-case, you get lucky like the 97 Spurs and land a transcendent superstar on a rookie contract (unparalleled value-add). Worst-case, you net a Top 5 pick in a weak draft like the 2007 Celtics, but utilize that monster asset to make an immediate upgrade (as Boston did when they brought in all-star Ray Allen as a precursor to adding KG).

Recently I outlined a (highly unlikely, understandably unpopular, and admittedly fantastical) Ainge-esque “nuclear option” of gutting the roster entirely with a big picture focus on acquiring elite prospects, high lotto picks, and significant cap space to maximize free agent/trade flexibility and chances of developing young supporting talent. It really boiled down to the following hypothetical. Would you rather head into 2026 with:

  • Option 1: Tatum + Brown + White + Payton Pritchard + Hauser + nothing else meaningful
  • Option 2: Tatum + 5 recent high lotto selections on cost controlled contracts + top 5 pick from tanking the 2025-26 season + 3 additional 1sts acquired through trades + 52 mil in cap space to chase a star in FA class of 2026 (Luka, Fox, Jaren Jackson Jr, etc)

This was me taking things to the hypothetical extremes, but I do think more realistically (again, assuming no Tatum returning), I’d be looking at a middle ground of trying to acquire picks/cap space/youth for Jrue, Zingis, Hauser and perhaps going the 2006-07 Paul Pierce route of shutting down Brown early and giving youth a season of development all with the primary goal of maximize our chances of bottoming out and increasing the trade value of our prospects. I just think long-term that puts us in a far, far better position to come out of this ahead vs the idea of fumbling along to a 43 win season, a 1st round exit, and a FAR less valuable mid 1st.

TL;DR: Next season without Tatum Boston may struggle to remain a true contender. Historical comps (like Pippen’s Bulls or post-KG Celtics) are rare and usually involved either another All-NBA star or a breakout young talent—neither of which Boston clearly has beyond Brown. White might step up, but the supporting cast is aging or likely to be traded. Most realistic outcome: low playoff seed and mid 1st draft pick. Unless Tatum returns late, a soft reset or tank for 2026 might be the smarter long-term play.
