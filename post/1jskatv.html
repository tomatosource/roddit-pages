Johnathon’s theory and response to layout complaints are flawed.

In two of the past interviews, interviewers asked Johnathan and mark about layout size complaints and how they felt about them.

In response, Jonathan answered that they dont think its an issue of size but an issue of content and that adding more content to zones would fix the player perception (for example, the new azmeri spirit that can appear in any zone).

I think this idea is completely flawed, and here is why:

In Campaign

During campaign, your main way of progressing is by moving through zones and finishing quests. you dont really care for loot beside enough to help you keep up with levels.

larger zones, longer time to finish campaign, longer time suffering with low power, less loot and not interacting with the endgame.

I dont care if i am getting 10 exalts per act (which doesnt even happen, i got 1 exalt during the entirety of acts 1,2 this league) as the expectation for most of my gear is to be makeshift and temporary.

In poe1, the common consensus is that the campaign is a sort of tutorial, and that the real game starts with endgame as thats when you have enough tools to make something interesting and plenty of content to choose and try

In Endgame

Even in endgame, the amount of zones (maps) you clear per min is a huge component of your rate of progress as finishing a map is literally what makes you progress, and guess what, the new atlas system incentives traversing it as fast as possible (even if you stay at the same tier!!) as you get to reach more towers, corruption and explore more of the map for better content.

i am not advocating for a campaign skip, i have been playing poe1 for 6 years (3.0) and i have leveled multiple characters every league since then. i get the argument for char progression and it makes sense.

meanwhile, on poe1 league start, i kill kitava in 4-5 hours and maybe reach 90 in the next 10 and the pace of progression just feels right during the whole thing.

slower more meaningful combat does not mean having to make the game a slog fest.

in conclusion: i think reducing zone sizes (and appropriately increasing xp) in both campaign and endgame will help solve the current issues with the game progression pace without affecting combat pace or char movement speed.

TLDR: smaller zones = faster progress, same combat, more fun.
