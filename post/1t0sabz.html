Birdseye View of the Team

NBA trends.

History, both recent and long term, suggests the Celtics will win game 7. The home team has generally won about 75% of the time in NBA history.

In recent years, there’s been a marked change in the overall winning percentage of home teams. While it used to be that the home team won 65 to 70% of the time, it’s now only 55 to 60%.

Finally, this was hard to find useful numbers on, but often it’s the team with their backs against the wall that wins. The overall percentage on that is actually 30%, but that’s a little deceptive, because usually in those situations the team ahead in the series is a distinctly better team. But IMO, if both teams are relatively equal in talent and performance, the team with their backs against the wall will win more often then not.

After game 6, what do we know? Embiid missed the first three games of the series, but it was in game 5 that he became unstoppable in the low post. It’s a problem the Celtics simply have to solve to win. In game 6, even though they lost, it did seem that either the Celtics stopped Embiid from scoring in the low post, or he simply chose not to post up as much. The Celtics were certainly double teaming him down low, so this could explain why the Sixers won so easily. Embiid simply found the open man.

But my impression was that the Sixers, backs against the wall, simply played harder defense, much harder, and the home crowd helped fire up the shooting percentage of Philly’s younger players.

Are there other lessons to learn? Well, people here will disagree with me on this, but here goes. It’s certainly not the Sixers bench that gave them an edge. The Celtics did not utilize their bench as much, and the bench did not dominate like it had in other games, particularly game 4, but it’s also a sure thing that the Celtics bench is better than Philly’s. Grimes and Drummond have certainly done their damage, but to my eye, Pritchard has been by far the best bench player in the series, and Baylor Scheirman, Jordan Walshe, Luka Garza and Vukavich have all been consistent contributors.

I expect Boston to rise up and win Game 7. They just have too many good players. Brown has carried them all season, and I believe he is likely to rise to the occasion in the hard parts of the game, and Tatum will ride waves when the team is on a run, putting pressure on Philly to keep up despite the hostile crowd.

But if Philly wins, the problem with the Celtics must be located with the starters, not the bench. The question is whether it will be Philly’s 1 through 5 starting lineup that gave them the advantage, or whether their stars were simply better than our stars.

In basketball at all levels, it usually comes down to a battle of the best player or players. In close, pressure games, basketball teams, whether in pickup ball or any organized level, generally turn to their best player in key moments of the game. There are certainly other factors. Having key role players and using them properly is very important on the road to NBA championships. And if one team has a markedly better overall starting lineup, unless the best player on the weaker team is truly exceptional, it will come down to overall lineups.

Who has the better starting lineup here? Embiid gives them a decided edge at center. Edgecome is a rookie, and though he’s had his moments, he’s certainly not a star yet, and White should be the better player.

How about Oubre vs Hauser? Hauser has played decently on defense, so there’s not as much difference in that regard as people expect. But on offence, while he’s been somewhat effective and Philly can’t allow him to get off clean shots, he has so far not had one of those explosions of threes where he really impacts a game. Last night he was one for 5 from beyond. Oubre is far more athletic. His scoring has been erratic, averaging 9 pts. I would say this matchup has been close to even. Oubre plays a lot more minutes.

Paul George is Philly’s third best player. If we go best three against best three, it’s Embiid, Maxey and George vs Tatum, Brown and White. If it’s two on two, it’s Embiid and Maxey vs Brown and Tatum.

Maxey has stood out as the best player on the floor. Trust your eye, not stats. He’s been streaky as he always can be, but he’s had more dominant games. Embiid was the best player in game 5, so the recent MVP is still capable of rising to that level. But Maxey has outshone Tatum and Brown, who not only disappear for long stretches, but tend to disappear in the last five minutes or the 4th quarter of a close game.

We’ll really have to see how game 7 plays out before drawing conclusive lessons. If Tatum explodes for another 51 pts, as he did a few years ago on the road for a game 7 in Philly, that’s what we’ll be talking about. But if the Celtics lose, it’s hard to imagine the Celtics will not explore shipping out either Tatum or Brown this summer. Most people assume it’s Brown that will be cut loose. But Brown led the Celtics, without Tatum, to becoming a top-two team in the NBA. With most of their players being young and Brown still well within his prime, there’s no reason to think a Tatum-less Celtics would not be even better next year than it was this year. Hugo should make a big leap, Walshe and Baylor should continue to show solid growth, and Garza will improve. And if they land a windfall of good picks for Tatum, the team would be able set up for the long run with a regular infusion of talent. It would be very hard for new ownership to resist that scenario.
