[OC] The Insurance Adjuster is in: How Long Will Each Team Rebuild Take?

We live in the Pacific Palisades, which isn’t having the best week. If you hadn’t heard, there was a brutal wildfire that ravaged 20,000 acres and destroyed countless homes. Even in a best case scenario, you’re dealing with nasty smoke damage and nastier insurance companies. And in a worst case scenario, your home is rubble. It may take 2-3 years to rebuild it – if you can even find the money or workers to take the endeavor on.

It’s hard to tell exactly how long each will take, because they haven’t allowed us to even go back in to check on the damage. Insurance adjusters will eventually do that. They’ll assess damage – and attempt to make their best guess on the timeline.

The idea of a “rebuild” naturally led me to think about the NBA (as a distraction), and the franchises that are undergoing that long road to recovery themselves. There are 7 teams with a record under .333, mired in some form of that process already. So for today, I get to play the role of the insurance company, and assess the timeline for each to get back up to playoff relevance.

######STRUCTURE STILL STANDING: may take less than a year

New Orleans Pelicans

Entering the season, the Pelicans had an expected win total of 46.5 in Vegas. Instead, they sit at the bottom of the West at 9-32. It’s been a disaster all the way around, from the uncertainty about Brandon Ingram’s contract extension to the uncertainty about Zion Williamson’s calorie intake.

Still, if this team was healthy, they have a decent roster. After all, they did go 49-33 last season. They do have solid starters in Dejounte Murray, Trey Murphy III, and Herbert Jones (all between the ages of 24-28). Rookie Yves Missi has been better than expected and may lock down another starting spot long term.

For New Orleans, the question mark is at the top. In our analogy, it’s a damaged roof. The ceiling raiser of a true star. The franchise must not think that’s Brandon Ingram, and they must be losing hope that it’s Zion Williamson. But IF (big IF) Williamson stays healthy – or the team wins the lottery – then they have the rest of the pieces in place to jump back into the 45 win range as soon as next season.

######REBUILD IN PROGRESS: may take 2-3 years

Charlotte Hornets

The Charlotte Hornets almost have the opposite problem as New Orleans. They have two young scorers with All-Star potential – but the rest of the house needs work. The depth needs a boost. It’s a thin team overall, made thinner by the fact that they selected a rookie in Tidjane Salaun (4.9 PPG, 32.1% FG) who may need several years to get up to code.

But again, this is a star-driven league, so having two foundational pieces helps a lot. In his fifth year in the league, LaMelo Ball is still only 23 years old. Brandon Miller is only 22. The other good quality about those two specifically is that they’re easier to add talent around. They’re shooters. (Clearly, based on their combined 24 three-point attempts per game). Given that, their third banana doesn’t necessarily need to be a shooter/scorer. It can be a stud defender, or savvy glue guy. There happens to be some kid nearby in Durham who would fit that need to a T.

Toronto Raptors

Some home renovation experts like to do a complete teardown and start fresh. Some like to remodel instead.

Masai Ujiri must be the latter. After peaking with the old core, he resisted the urge to blow it all up and tank to the bottom. Instead, he brought in young veterans like Jakob Poeltl (now age 29), R.J. Barrett (age 24), and Immanuel Quickly (age 25). If the goal is to make the play-in, that’s not a bad approach.

Of course, if the goal is to make the play-in, you actually need to win games, too. This young-ish core has been disappointing this year, with a 9-31 record overall. Unlike Charlotte, their best players Scottie Barnes and R.J. Barrett are NOT spacers – meaning they’re going to be harder to mold with newer players.

If there’s any silver lining with a terrible record – it’s that Toronto has a better shot at getting lucky in the lottery. In that sense, going 20-62 is better than going 30-52. But my fear for this team is that they may be closer to a 30 win team in terms of true talent, which means Ujiri either needs a shakeup or a lucky rabbit’s foot on lottery day.

Portland Trail Blazers

The Portland Trail Blazers have a decent supporting cast for a future star. They have some young shooters (efficient or not) in Anfernee Simons and Shaedon Sharpe. They have starter-level forwards in Jerami Grant and Deni Avdija. They even have two solid young centers in Deandre Ayton (still only 26) and Donovan Clingan. All they need now is the superstar who brings it all together.

Of course, that superstar was supposed to be Scoot Henderson. We may forget the sins of our scouting past in hindsight, but there were plenty of folks who thought he’d be a surefire All-Star: the next Russell Westbrook. In home rebuild terms, he came in like a contractor with a 5.0-star rating on yelp. Only, instead of fixing your kitchen, he came over and took a dump on the living room floor instead. It sounds harsh, but Henderson has been that disappointing. Prior to his awesome game last night, he was shooting under 40% from the field and under 30% from three. Worse, he doesn’t demonstrate the natural feel and basketball IQ that makes non-shooting PGs great.

If you want to be the ultimate optimist, you can argue that Scoot Henderson’s struggles may actually be a positive for the future. Maybe he can take a giant leap. And if Henderson doesn’t get better, there’s some “addition by subtraction” potential here. If you replace his minutes with an average PG (say Tyus Jones) perhaps this team is closer to .500 than they appear. Henderson hasn’t been as bad as Killian Hayes, but it’s no coincidence that the Pistons’ rebuild finally took off after they remediated the damage from that pick.

######DEMOLITION DERBY: may take 4+ years

Brooklyn Nets

The Brooklyn Nets had a problem in their plan to blow it up and rebuild: there was too much talent on the team. Even after trading away Kevin Durant. Even after trading Mikal Bridges. The plucky squad even started the year 9-10, threatening to make the play-in.

Still, the demolition crew is undeterred. The Nets are still intent on blowing this up and selling away any spare pieces left in the rubble. Dorian Finney-Smith is gone. Cam Johnson may be next out the door. We’re lumping the Nets into this last category – this deepest rebuilt – because they haven’t even finished with the bulldozer yet. One they strip away all the talent, the true rebuild may start again. And traditionally, that’s a process that can take 4-5 years.

However, there is a quality unique to Brooklyn that may change their fate. They have a big market. They have cap space (after Ben Simmons’ contract expires this summer.) If they want to pivot and add a superstar, then it’s possible for them to flip the switch and get back to .500 sooner than later. However, by all indications, they’re drawing up the blueprints instead.

Washington Wizards

Make no mistake, the Washington Wizards are the worst team in the NBA. They’re chasing rare air: ranking 30th (dead last) in SRS. 30th in offensive efficiency. 30th in defensive efficiency. They’re straight up awful.

Of course, that’s by design. Led by Michael Winger, the new administration is committed to a deep rebuild that may potentially go 5 years or more.

Along the way, the team has started to quietly build the structure up again. Maligned # 2 pick Alex Sarr has been showing flashes – and is actually the betting favorite for Rookie of the Year right now. Fellow Frenchman Bilal Coulibaly looks like he belongs as well. Better yet, these two are super young (19 and 20, respectively). They’re also easy to fit into various models given their length and defensive potential. Neither have to be the alpha dog scorer to contribute to a winning team. Of course, that winning team is a far way away.

Utah Jazz

After trading away Donovan Mitchell and Rudy Gobert, the Utah Jazz were destined for a Washington Wizards-level tank job. A long “process”, if you will. Instead, coach Will Hardy and Lauri Markkanen had other ideas: helping the team overachieve with 37-45 and 31-51 records the last two years.

As mentioned in the Toronto Raptors section, it’s almost the worst place to be in the entire NBA. Too good for the lottery, too bad for the playoffs.

GM Danny Ainge hasn’t fully realized that and started the long process of blowing it up, but it’s hard to see a scenario where that’s not the eventual outcome (outside of getting lucky with a top 2 pick). Ainge may be licking his chops waiting for that opportunity. After all, he did a decent job rebuilding Boston before. Hopefully he gets the chance to dust off his hard hat and get back to work again.

(Not to be schmaltzy, but hopefully the Palisades and L.A. all enjoy a successful road to recovery as well, even if it takes 5+ years. I know some people online don’t have much sympathy because they think it’s all rich celebrities out there, but that wasn’t my personal experience. My neighbors were nice older couples – one who lived there for 30 years, another for 40 years. They raised their kids there. Now they’ve lost not only their houses and everything they own, but the community that they’ve loved. That sense of community is something that we tend to lack in modern society, so hopefully we can get it back one day.)
