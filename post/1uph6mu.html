I wrote this last night, decided to sleep on it, and now Brad is alluding to it, even Bill Simmons mentioned it on his latest pod. I’m going to post it anyway.

The C’s are at the cutting edge of the CBA, and the 2nd Apron is working just as it was designed to.

A team that ties up the vast majority of its cap in 2-3 players will have a hard time staying under the tax and an even harder time sustaining winning. Early evidence suggests no team can or wants to afford to operate as a tax-paying team for very long, and even fewer want anything to do with the 2nd apron. The Celtics just got here first. They built their title team just after the current CBA was negotiated, ironic, since Jaylen Brown and Grant Williams were repping the players’ union at the time. The C’s had a short window of opportunity to jump through before the whole apron nightmare took hold; they jumped through it, and we’re all glad they did. OKC will be in this space very soon. SGA, Holmgren, and J Will will consume 75-90% of the cap in most of the next 5 years, peaking around the 2027-28 seasons, when all three deals are active with SGA’s supermax at full tilt. They will want/have to move off at least one of them. What might save them is that they have 10,000 draft picks banked. The projected 2026-27 cap is around $165M. Philly has over 90% of their entire cap owed to Jaylen, Embiid, and Maxey for the next 3 years. What might save them is that both Embiid and Jaylen very likely will not be there at the end of that stretch. Assuming Wemby, Castle, and Harper stay healthy, the Spurs will have to pay them, trade at least one of them, or be a tax-paying team in 3 years.

Let me put on my green glasses: The C’s were, I don’t want to say first, but certainly an early adopter, of the 5-out, bombs-away, 3-point-shooter-at-every-position style of play, and they won a title playing that way. They are likewise one of the first to have to deal with the worst of the CBA. Since their title year, they ran it back, lost twice, and subsequently put themselves in a position to have real flexibility by the 2027-28 season. Other teams will face this same music, and Boston will be there waiting, out of the repeater tax, with a very large, soon-to-be-expiring contract and lots of draft capital.
