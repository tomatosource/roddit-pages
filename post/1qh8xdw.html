The Knicks are currently sitting at 2-8 in their last 10 games. Historically, about 20% of NBA Finals consist of a team who has gone 2-8 at some point of the season, but only 6 have turned around to win the NBA Championship

CBS Sports wrote an article about the Knicks current slide to 2-8 in their last 10 games and the historical comparisons for their end goals of making the Finals and winning a championship. Historically speaking, 17⁄79 (21.5%) NBA Finals have consisted of a team that has similarly struggled at one point of their season (both teams playing in the 1978 Finals went 2-8 at one point of their season). Only 6 actually went all the way to winning it all though.

I took the list from CBS Sports and did a bit of research into what caused each of these teams to slide and what turned their season around.

#1948 Baltimore Bullets

Any real basketball fan knows that the loss of future Rookie of the Year Paul Hoffman going out injured on December 6th against the Chicago Stags led to this slide in the standings. That’s why the team instantly came back to life when he returned on January 3rd against the Knicks.

The Bullets were 3rd in Pts/G and 2nd in Opp Pts/G (Net Rating not available); Paul Hoffman is unofficially recognized as ROTY but the records were lost by the league office

#1952 New York Knicks

Knicks had a few rotation players go down for a handful of games in Ray Lumpp and Ernie Vandeweghe, but the main hole in their rotation was a stretch of injuries by backup forward George Kaftan. With star shooting guard Carl Braun away from the team for military service for the second season in a row, the thin rotation got even thinner.

#1957 St. Louis Hawks

The 2-8 slide was actually a 1-9 slide, with a run that would eventually get future Hall of Fame Knicks coach Red Holzman fired 33 games into the season. During this stretch the Hawks made two key moves. On Dec 12, they traded Willie Naulls to the Knicks for Slater Martin. And on Dec 17, they signed Alex Hannum as a free agent right before the skid concluded. When Holzman was fired on Jan 7th, Martin was given the role of player coach. However, Martin did not care for the extra responsibility of both roles, and gave coaching responsibility to Hannum after 1 game (and the official title a few games later). So the Hawks actually traded for two head coaches within the span of a few days, all in the same season. At the end of the season, Hannum would retire and eventually take on a full coaching career that would get him into the Hall of Fame. (Fun fact - Alex Hannum is the only head coach to ever defeat Bill Russell in the postseason; he was the head coach of the 1958 Hawks and the 1967 Sixers).

#1959 Minneapolis Lakers

Which one? The Lakers went 1-9 between Nov 8 and Nov 24th early in the season before winning 4 straight. Immediately following that starting with a triple OT loss to the Royals, they lost 4 straight, won 2, and then lost 5 straight. Fast forward a few more weeks, They went on another 2-8 slide from Jan 4th to Jan 16th losing 3 and 5 straight. Other than 14 games missed throughout the season (once overlapping with one of those slides) by starting point guard Slick Leonard (future originator of the Indiana Pacers’ “Boom Baby” catchphrase), nothing really stuck out for the up and down play. This was simply a struggling team transitioning from the remnants of the Mikan dynasty into the hands of rookie Elgin Baylor’s franchise. The team would still go on to be a 2 seed in the West, as the only stand out teams in the league were the Hawks in the West and Celtics in the East.

#1963 Los Angeles Lakers

Jerry West missed last 7 weeks of the regular season with a hamstring injury on February 2nd. He returned for the playoffs March 31st but wasn’t 100 percent (only averaged a mere 27.8⁄8.2⁄4.7 that postseason)

#1967 San Francisco Warriors

Nate Thurmond (future MVP runner up that season to Wilt) broke his left hand in a double OT loss to Boston on Feb 10th, returned to the lineup March 10th still finding footing.

#1970 Los Angeles Lakers

Zooming out it was actually a 2-10 slide that started with a 129-97 loss to the Bullets at home. But within this context, we’re starting with their 100-81 loss at Milwaukee. First year head coach Joe Mullaney was tasked with trying to lead the West/Baylor/Chamberlain core to a championship after the all time Game 7 collapse at home to Boston in 1969. Chamberlain ruptured his patellar tendon in the 9th game of the year and would not return until Game 80 of 82. Baylor missed games to start this stretch, followed by a few games of overlap were West also missed games. The slide stopped in Game 31 when West returned to give the aging Baylor back up.

#1971 Baltimore Bullets

The Bullets struggled with injury all year, highlighted most by an aging Gus Johnson (not the announcer) who’s knees had deteriorated so much over the previous few seasons that he was taking injections and pain killers just to be able to bend his knees. Wes Unseld missed the final 8 games of the season with a twisted ankle, and 6th man Eddie Miles pulled his Achilles tendon 5 days before the slide.

#1975 Golden State Warriors

With the retirements of Oscar Robertson and Jerry West, the West was looked at as not standing a chance against the East in 1975. While the retirements of Willis Reed and Dave DeBusschere knocked down one of the dominant East teams in the Knicks, the defending champion Celtics and the now Washington Bullets were thought to be head and shoulders above everybody (with Bob McAdoo’s Buffalo Braves shortly behind). That didn’t stop the Warriors from making a bold move to trade Nate Thurmond in the offseason and starting the year a surprising 27-13. The “Cardiac Kids” Warriors eventually cooled off a bit mid season before getting back on track to win the Pacific Division.

#1976 Phoenix Suns

The better question is which 2-8 stretch are we talking about. From Dec 19th to January 31st the Suns went 3-19. They had a 4 game losing streak, followed by a 7 game losing streak, and then a 2 and 3 game losing streak to cap it off before winning 4 straight. During that stretch they only managed to hold their opponent under 100 points 4 times (league average 104.3), with the most recent time being 3 weeks prior. The Suns traded for forward Gar Heard on Feb 1st who made an immediate impact in his first game 5 days later, having a 17⁄13 night over the defending champion Warriors in what would be a 23-13 finish to the season (including a 7 game win streak in March). Gar Heard would also hit “The Shot Heard Round the World” to send Game 5 of the NBA Finals against the Celtics (“The Greatest Game Ever Played”) into triple OT.

#1978 Washington Bullets

Not to be outdone by their future Finals opponents, the Bullets also struggled out the gate losing 6 out of 10 to start the season. As for the 2-8 stretch, 3x All Star shooting guard Phil Chenier had a back injury on on January 8th that required season ending surgery. Starting point guard Tom Henderson and starting forward Mitch Kupchak (yes that one) also missed games in this stretch.

#1978 Seattle SuperSonics

Another case of “which slide are we talking about”, the Sonics started the season with 4 straight losses. From Opening Night on October 19th to November 29th, the Sonics started the season at 5-17 behind first year head coach Bob Hopkins who had been an assistant with the Sonics for 3 years prior under Bill Russell. This would be Bob Hopkins last NBA head coaching job; he was fired 22 games into the year. He briefly served as an assistant coach for the Knicks before moving on to a Collegiate Basketball Hall of Fame career. He was replaced by future Hall of Fame head coach (and player) Lenny Wilkens, who immediately stepped in to lead the team to a 6 game win streak followed by a 5 game win streak (and a 7 game win streak a few weeks later).

#1981 Houston Rockets

Starting point guard Calvin Murphy missed a few games in this stretch, and starting power forward Rudy Tomjanovich started to show his age in what would become his final season in the league

#2004 Detroit Pistons

Despite ripping off a 13 game win streak in late December into mid January, the Pistons were starting to lose a hold of their season. A 3 game losing streak came immediately after, followed by a 4 game winning streak that required two separate over times. Immediately following that was a 6 game losing streak. Approaching the trade deadline fast, the Pistons made a 3 team deal to bring in Rasheed Wallace. The rest is history.

#2011 Dallas Mavericks

The sky isn’t always falling in a rough patch. When Dirk sprained his knee on Dec 28th, the Mavs went on to lose 7 out of 9 with him out. But in the 2011 season they also went on: A 4 game win streak, a 12 game win streak, a 5 game win streak, a 10 game win streak, an 8 game win streak (the 10 and 8 game win streak separated by a 1 point loss on the second half of a back to back), a 5 game win streak, and ended the season with another 4 game win streak.

#2015 Cleveland Cavaliers

The 2015 Cavaliers overhauled everything, bringing in first time head coach David Blatt to try to lead the new Big 3 of Lebron James, Kyrie Irving, and Kevin Love to a championship. Lebron dealing with knee and back strains in late December/early January led to him missing 8 straight games where the Cavs lost 7.

#2018 Cleveland Cavaliers

The 3 time reigning Eastern Conference Champion Cavaliers were adjusting to life without Kyrie Irving in 2018 following trading him to the Celtics in the offseason. Despite a 13 and a 5 game winning streak before the turn of the calendar, the season began to go off the rails in early January. Following a loss on Christmas Day, the Cavs went 6-11 through the month of January. On Feb 8th the Cavaliers famously traded 6 players on their roster hours before the trade deadline to completely restructure their team.

#2022 Golden State Warriors

Considered the favorite for Defensive Player of the Year at the time and anchoring the number 1 defense in the league, Draymond Green went down for 31 games with a back injury in early January not returning until March 14th. The Warriors also took time adjusting to the return of Klay Thompson to the starting lineup, who had missed the previous two seasons due to injury.

##Summary

The Knicks currently have the #3 Offense, #19 Defense, and #8 Net Rating in the league 42 games into the season. History is by no means on the Knicks’ side in regards to winning the championship or making the Finals. But 20% of NBA Finals consisting of a team that has similarly struggled isn’t exactly insignificantly low either. At least not low enough to write them off as a viable contender if you previously thought they were capable of going to the Finals before that slump.

The Knicks current slide started in Game 33 of the season on December 31st. Assuming they can turn things around, this would put them about dead middle of teams that have similarly fallen in the regular season but ran their way to the NBA Finals. 9 of the 18 teams on this list slid in December/January.

Like many of the teams on this list, they also still have the Trade Deadline ahead to make a move to get things back on the right track. As was the case with the 1976 Suns trading for Gar Heard, the 2004 Pistons trading for Rasheed Wallace, or the 2018 Cavaliers trading anybody not named Lebron James.

The Knicks do also have the health aspect to look at historically. In that 2-8 stretch, Josh Hart missed 7 games. Mitchell Robinson has sat out for 3 games on back to backs (all losses). Landry Shamet returned from injury, but was missing for 8. And Jalen Brunson has missed the most recent 2 games as of writing this (both losses). Of course every team goes through stretches of injury, but if you were taking an optimistic approach that also would point to the ability for things to get back on track.

All in all, there is reason for concern with the Knicks recent record. It’s not nothing. But there’s also enough of a sample size of NBA history to suggest that the idea that a true contender wouldn’t ever have a 2-8 stretch in their season isn’t really true. Doesn’t mean they will get there. Just means Knicks fans should maybe take a step or two away from the ledge (for now) with the trade deadline and All Star Break still ahead.
