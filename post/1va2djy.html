My rarity on gear during the Arbiter farm was 143%, but it doesn’t matter for the Lineage Support gems, as it doesn’t affect pinnacle boss loot table drops. I’ve also tested the Aberration with both 15% rarity and 300% rarity (+600% rarity procs from Cadiro’s Gambit quiver), and the drops were actually better with 15% rarity, so it doesn’t matter.

I actually ran 425 Arbiter of Divinity encounters, but only tracked 300 of them. All statistics below are based on those 300 tracked runs.

Setup

  • Cost per run: 9d
  • Total investment: 2,700d
  • Time per run: Approximately 100 seconds
  • Total runtime: Approximately 8 hours and 20 minutes, excluding breaks
  • I purchased the Origin Cradle and Origin Spark separately and combined them, saving approximately 150d compared with buying completed Origin Cores.
  • I one-shot both phases, so most of each run was spent travelling or waiting for encounter animations.
  • Unique items were valued at 0d in the main calculation.
  • I made an additional ~200–300d from corrupted spears and boots, but that revenue is not included.
  • I only picked up Lineage Supports, raw currency, and unique items. I ignored magic items, rare items, and raw currency drops worth less than 1d.
  • Currency drops like Perfect Exalted Orbs were converted to their Divine-equivalent value.

Variance

The variance was brutal.

I was up 1,450d after the first 183 runs, but runs 184–300 lost 601d, reducing the final profit to 849d.

The first 183 runs produced:

  • 4 Seraphs
  • 8 Declarations
  • 12 total Lineage Supports

The final 117 runs produced:

  • 0 Seraphs
  • 2 Declarations
  • 2 total Lineage Supports

This included 76 consecutive runs without a single Lineage Support, from runs 204 through 279.

If the observed 4.67% Lineage rate were the true independent drop rate, the chance of encountering at least one 76-run dry streak somewhere within 300 runs would be approximately 28%. It was an awful streak, but not an implausible one.

Comparison with Kulemak and the Aberration

I’ve also run ~300 Kulemak encounters and more than 400 Aberrations.

Kulemak

Kulemak is the safest and most consistent of the three farms because one of the three bones is guaranteed.

Using equal drop rates (in my experience, they are equal) and current bone prices of 4d, 3.8d, and 2.9d, the average revenue per run is 3.57d.

At a cost of 2.9d per run:

Metric Result
Investment per 100 runs 290d
Revenue per 100 runs 356.67d
Profit per 100 runs 66.67d
Profit per run 0.67d
Best realistic time per run Approximately 60 seconds
Maximum practical profit per hour Approximately 40d

At these prices, the cheapest bone covers the entry cost, while the other two generate profit. The problem is scalability. Even if you one-shot the boss, the encounter animations and waiting time make approximately 60 seconds per run the realistic ceiling.

Final thoughts

Based on my results, Arbiter of Divinity had the highest hourly return, but it also required by far the most capital and produced the most extreme variance. A single long dry streak can erase hundreds of Divines in profit.

Kulemak is the safest option. The guaranteed bone makes the returns predictable, but the encounter itself caps how many runs you can complete per hour.

The Aberration has the best capital efficiency by a huge margin. It costs almost nothing to run, but its actual profit varies considerably depending on Emergent Possibility and Olroth’s Conviction drops.

There is no objectively best farm:

  • Kulemak offers consistency.
  • The Aberration offers extremely low entry costs and excellent capital efficiency.
  • Arbiter of Divinity offers the highest potential hourly return if you have enough currency and can tolerate severe variance.
