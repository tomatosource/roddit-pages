Hard capped by the apron: Why the Warriors are almost definitely not getting Jimmy Butler or Cam Johnson

The content of this post has come up recently in the context of potential trades for Jimmy or Cam Johnson, but it’s relevant league-wide and seems like information not many people know, so I thought it would be good to lay it out clearly.

As most people know, there’s a list of transactions that are restricted if a team is over the first apron, and an even longer list if a team is over the second apron. What people seem unaware of is that if a team engages in any of those transactions, they may not exceed the applicable apron level during the remainder of the salary cap year. That is, if you engage in a transaction that is not permitted for first apron teams, you are hard capped under the first apron for the rest of the season.

For example, the Warriors signed De’Anthony Melton using the non-taxpayer mid-level exception, which is not allowed for first apron teams. This means the Warriors are hard capped at the first apron level of \(178,132,000. Their current apron salary is \)177,801,591, meaning they have \(330,409 of available space. That means, even though they can bundle salaries and do all sorts of complicated trade stuff, they ultimately can't take back more than an additional \)330k in any trade. (By the way, I checked and I believe they are still locked under the first apron even though Melton is no longer on the team).

Here’s a list of the Warriors’ top salaries:

  • Steph 55.8
  • Wiggins 26.3
  • Draymond 24.1
  • Schroder 13.0
  • GPII 9.1
  • Anderson 8.8
  • Hield 8.8
  • Looney 8
  • Kuminga 7.6

Jimmy is making $48.8 million this year, so the Warriors would need to send back Wiggins plus at least two other contracts (assuming Steph and Dray are off the table). They would also need to sign free agents to replace the players they lost, because of the 14 player roster minimum, and those contracts can’t take them over the first apron, either.

Cam Johnson is making \(22.5 million this year, which seems more manageable, however he has unlikely incentives that *could* take his contract up to \)27 million this year. Unlikely incentives don’t count toward salary, but they do count toward the apron. This is referred to as ‘apron salary’ on Spotrac, for those interested. So the Warriors would need to match $27 million to get a trade for Cam Johnson to work. Assuming they don’t want to just swap Wiggins for Cam Johnson, that means they need to bundle at least three players to match salaries. And again, they would need to replace the missing players on their roster.

TL;DR Locking yourself under the apron is a hard cap, which makes trading hard.
