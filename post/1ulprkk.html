If there’s anybody to blame: It’s the new CBA

It’s been almost 24 hours and I’m still trying to wrap my head around it not necessarily because of who was traded, but because of the why.

I always imagined Jaylen and JT finishing their careers in Celtics green. But I’m old enough to know that’s not how the NBA works anymore. What I can’t comprehend is why teams are essentially punished for drafting and developing homegrown stars. Jaylen was a Celtics pick, just like Jayson. Is it really the organization’s fault that both turned into legit All‑NBA players?

Forget the debates about who’s better or who’s “the top dog.” The fact is these two were premier talents, drafted and raised by Boston, and neither ever publicly hinted at wanting out or wanting the other out. In a perfect world, they’d have wanted to be A1 and A1 together for a long time. Both are not only elite players but also high‑character guys — credit to Danny Ainge and Brad Stevens for building that foundation. And yet, here we are.

We just lost one of Boston’s sports pillars in Jaylen Brown. I’m all for parity in the league, but can’t the CBA architects find a way to reward great drafting teams? Imagine if homegrown stars had a buffer or exemption against the cap hit. I’m no contract analyst, but I’m sure Brad wouldn’t have pulled the trigger if that were the case. In the end, this feels like nothing more than a high‑profile “I have no choice” salary dump.

Jaylen deserves that supermax after last season. I admire players who take pay cuts, but I know it’s bad for them and the league. Giving him the deal would’ve crippled roster flexibility, and Brad had to make the painful call. I’ve been a JB fan since day one — thrilled when they picked him over Buddy Hield and even Sabonis back in my senior year in college. This one stings, and it’ll sting for a long time.

I love PG, I believe he can still produce, and I’m not writing him off. I still trust Brad. If he ever reads this, I hope he knows I understand — it was a painful necessity, like having a gun to your head. I’m sure he’s hurting too, but that’s the job. As for the new ownership, I’m holding reservations. We’ll see in a couple of years if they truly embody Celtics culture.

To JB:

I hope you get to read this — I’m all the way from MNL. I saw you when you visited the PH last August for the 741’s, and this trade still hurts. But man, I’m excited to see you step into that full‑time bus driver role, leading with two young dogs beside you. Goddamn, I wouldn’t be surprised if it’s Philly in the Finals in the next couple of years. The Process is over, you’re the SOLUTION.Thank you for ten incredible years in Celtics green. One day, I hope you find your way back to Beantown to close out your career. And when you drop 50 on us at the Garden, know that I’ll be cursing you out of love. LOVE YOU BROTHER! All glory to the most high!

-Much Love from Manila.
