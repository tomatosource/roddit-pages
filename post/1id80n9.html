[OC] The 40⁄20 Rule: Predicting This Season’s True Title Contenders

We’re now past the halfway point of the season, and the true contenders are starting to separate from the pack.

Legendary coach Phil Jackson once proposed a simple yet powerful rule to identify elite championship contenders: If a team reaches 40 wins before losing 20 (meaning at least 40 wins in their first 59 games), they should be considered a legitimate title threat.

But does this rule actually hold up? Let’s dive into the numbers:

  • Since the 1983-84 season (when the NBA expanded to a 16-team playoff format), 656 teams have made the playoffs.
  • 27% of all playoff teams have hit the 40⁄20 mark.
  • 47% of conference semifinalists met the rule.
  • 68% of conference finalists met the rule.
  • 76% of NBA Finalists met the rule.
  • And most importantly—90% of NBA Champions have passed the 40⁄20 test.

While not a perfect predictor, it’s clear that the 40⁄20 rule is a strong indicator of elite teams capable of making deep playoff runs.

How I Calculated the Odds

To estimate each team’s chances of hitting 40 wins before 20 losses, I calculated the probability of them winning each remaining game based on their winning percentage and the strength of their opponents.

Then, I simulated 10,000 scenarios to see how often each team would reach the 40-win mark in 59 games.

Of course, this isn’t perfect—it doesn’t factor in momentum, injuries, or specific matchups—but it gives us a directional look at which teams are most likely to hit this elite milestone.
