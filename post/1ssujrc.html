3 Guards, 3 Wings, 3 Bigs for the 27th pick

No intro, lets get right to the picks

&nbsp;

Guards

&nbsp;

Christian Anderson - 6’3’’ PG (generous) 20 years old

Stats at Texas Tech: 18.5ppg, 3.6rpg, 7.4apg, 1.2spg, on 47/42/81 shooting

Breakdown: Christian Anderson codes 2 things, shoot, and playmake. He did both at an elite level at Texas Tech last season. Christian hit 3.3 of his 7.9 3’s this year so there was some really great volume there. Ball dominant players have struggled in our offense in the past, but Anderson’s ability to shoot will allow him to earn minutes while he earns the confidence of the coaching staff. Anderson is a smart offensive player with his 7.4 apg. Given his size and lack of vertical, he’s not going to be able to get to the rim like he did at college, and similarly he’ll be hunted on D. This puts a ceiling on him we’re he’s not going to be an All Star. Boston needs to find someone to replace Payton Pritchard who is going to (rightfully) be hunting for a massive contract soon. We’ll either need to let him walk, or extend and trade him, but unless we’re moving Derrick White or Jaylen Brown, PP is heading out within the next 2 seasons. Christian Anderson could be trained up as a replacement.

Archetype: Tyrese Haliburton but small, Tyus Jones

&nbsp;

Dash Daniels - 6’5’’ G/F 18 years old

Stats in NBL: 4.1ppg, 2.4rpg, .9apg, .7spg, on 43/37/36 shooting

Breakdown: Dash Daniels spent is pre NBA year at home in Australia playing in the NBL. This is a similar path as the Euroleague. Younger players typically ride the bench behind the experienced guys. If Dash was a sponge, this could be a really valuable experience for him, but his stats look pretty terrible. Similar to his brother Dyson, Dash projects as a high level defender who can harass ball handlers up and down the court. Offensively he looks like he has a workable 3 point shot, has good vision as a distributor. This really checks all the boxes for a Joe Mazzula offense where he can defend, move the ball, and hit 3’s at a reasonable clip. With Dash it’s less about the stats and more about if he can receive coaching and be ready after a year in Maine. If there is a team willing to invest in his development, he could be an elite defender and ancillary offensive player.

Comp: Alex Caruso, Dyson Daniels

&nbsp;

Tounde Yessoufou - 6’5’’ G/F 19 years old

Stats Baylor: 17.8ppg, 5.9rpg, 1.6apg, 2.0spg on 47/29/75 shooting

Breakdown: Yessoufou is essentially a freight train on both ends, plowing through contact and throwing it down on offense, and being an absolute wall on defense. Like a lot of prospects his age, the 3 point shot is a question. He only shot 29% in his 1 season at Baylor, but his free throw shooting was 75% and he’s reportedly grown by leaps and bounds offensively. He’s one of the few prospects that gives reason to think they’ll be able to develop a shot, vs guys who haven’t shown they can learn and grow. 3 point shooting will always be a secondary tool for Tounde though, his main attribute is attacking the rim and scoring through contact. He averaged almost 4 FTA’s per game and can absolutely be a transition weapon. Speaking of transition, Yessoufou is a turnover generator, averaging 2 steals and being one of those hustle beasts who will disrupt offensive possessions with deflections. He only averaged 1.6apg, but nobody on Baylor averaged more than 3.3apg last year, which could be a reflection of their offense then Yessoufou’s ability to pass. Scouts think he’s a solid passer, and the evolution of his offense is going to come from mixing his aggressive drives with kicking out for an even better shot. Defensively you can’t say enough. He’s engaged and has great physical tools. He’s a guy you’d expect to guard the opposing team’s best player and can guard up a position.

Comp: Lu Dort, Victor Oladipo

&nbsp;

Wings

&nbsp;

Dailyn Swain - 6’7’’ F 20. years old

Stats at Texas : 17.3ppg, 7.5rpg, 3.6apg, 1.6spg on 52/34/82 shooting

Breakdown: Dailyn Swain offers a lot on the offensive end. He can operate as a fast break scorer, with his superior size and coordination. In the half court, he can operate as a secondary ball handler. Swain operated at a meh level shooting the 3 last season, but has made massive strides since his freshman year where he shot fewer than 1 per game at 15%. His 82% free throw shooting indicates he can become the 3 point shooter he needs to be. Swain is aggressive getting to the rim and playing through contact, averaging 5.6 fta per game. He’s also a solid rebounder for his position. Swain’s biggest shortcomings are motor and defensive IQ. He has all the tools to be a plus defender, but he’s been known to make some mental errors and can get caught with movement away from the ball. He also doesn’t have the mentality to be THE MAN and can disappear for stretches. This puts a ceiling on him, but if the Celtics are looking for a Jayson Tatum back up, this could be the guy.

Comp: Jeff Green, Kelly Oubre Jr.

&nbsp;

Allen Graves - 6’9’’ F 19 years old

Stats at Santa Clara: 11.8ppg, 6.5rpg. 1.8apg, 1.9spg, .9bpg on 51/42/78 shooting

Breakdown: Allen Graves is a smooth and well rounded player who projects to be a kind of jack of all trades master of none. As a former PG, Graves displays the ability to run an offense for stretches, as well as elite 3 point shooting. He is a middling athlete, while not explosive he’s solid. He’s also showcased great defensive awareness averaging nearly 3 stocks with his long arms and smart positioning. He’s a great glue guy on both ends. On offense he can keep the ball moving and make reads other guys his size just can’t, both out of the high post and in the pick and roll. Defensively he’s just always in the right place. Graves hasn’t showcased the ability to attack the basket effectively, and will often defer to teammates, which could impact his ability to effectively run NBA level pick and roll. The weird thing about Graves, is everything says he should have dominated college ball, but he didn’t start at the relatively small school in Santa Clara. While he was their 3rd leading scorer, he wasn’t part of the staring 5. This could indicate a low ceiling, but this also could indicate he’s able and willing to sacrifice and fit a specific role, which is kind of ideal for a later 1st round pick.

Comp: Kyle Anderson, 6’9’’ Derrick White

&nbsp;

Isaiah Evans - 6’6’’ F/G 20 years old

Stats at Duke: 15ppg, 3.2rpg, 1.3apg, on 43/36/86

Breakdown: Step 1, draft Isaiah Evans, Step 2, get Cooper Flagg in FA in like 5 years. For real though, Dallas fans desperately want this guy as a former Flagg teammate, it’s kinda funny. Isaiah Evans is touted as one of the best pure shooters in the draft. The guy can catch absolute fire from 3 and shift the energy in a game by himself. That being said, for every flamethrower game, there are a handful of games where you just don’t hear from him. His coaches have all complimented him saying that he’s really sacrificed for the good of the team, so despite the reputation as being a bit of a chucker, Evans is lauded as a great teammate, kind of reminding me of Jordan Clarkson. He has great length which allows him to also pull up over smaller defenders in the mid range. Despite the archetype of a swaggy shooter, Evans has shown to be a solid defender not bothered by the moment. While not elite, he holds his own and has good effort on D. Duke really limited him to a shooter this year, not allowing him to dominate the ball like he could have in a lesser college program. While he has good length, he lacks the strength to take the ball into stronger defenders. He’ll probably never be a power finisher, and needs to be crafty to draw fouls and get to the line. Evans has a higher ceiling, but also a lower floor than a lot of the guys on this list. Like he could be a 2 time all star and average 20+ppg, but he could also not adapt to NBA physicality and just be a streaky bench shooter.

Comp: Rodney Hood, Jordan Clarkson, Rip Hamilton

Bigs

&nbsp;

Joshua Jefferson - 6’8’’ F 23 years old

Stats Iowa State: 16.4ppg, 7.4rpg, 4.8apg, 1.6rpg, on 47/35/70

Breakdown: Joshua Jefferson is a high IQ player with great passing instincts and just enough shooting to keep defenses honest. Jefferson offers great intensity at the defensive end, and is quick enough to guard wings and strong enough to not get completely destroyed in the paint. Jefferson is an excellent rebounder for his size, and Joe has shown he really loves his aggressive rebounders regardless of position. Jefferson has also demonstrated great hands in the post, being able to find teammates, and get accurate shots off around the basket. His 6’8’’ stature makes it unlikely he’ll play a lot of C at the NBA level, and the C’s are definitely looking for someone who can eat minutes there. Despite being a solid post scorer in college, this doesn’t always translate to the NBA where players are bigger and faster. Is Jefferson a 23 year old bullying college bodies, or is he a legit interior scorer. He also lacks some of the burst athleticism to be able to beat guys off the dribble or explode to the basket, putting a ceiling on his potential. That being said, his combination of IQ and effort make up a lot of ground, especially in Joe’s system.

Comp: Boris Diaw, Kyle Anderson

&nbsp;

Morez Johnson - 6’9’’ F/C 20 years old

Stats at Michigan: 13.1ppg, 7.3rpg, 1.2apg, 1.1bpg on 62/34/78 shooting

Breakdown: Morez Johnson does the dirty work on both ends, being an aggressive and switchable defender, while running to the rim on offense. Morez is a little undersized but has the athleticism and wingspan that makes up ground. Being a little shorter plays to his benefit, where he’s not stuck in the paint on defense and can guard guys outside the paint. He’s shown to be a great finisher above the rim, but can also get shots off without needing to dunk. He’s also shown some ability to be a low volume 3 point shooter. His biggest weakness is moving with the ball. I wouldn’t ever expect him to be beating guys off the dribble. He’ll either be cutting to the rim, or catching entry passes, which he excels at. Defensively the man has an unquestionable motor and can guard a lot of different types of players. The only time he’ll be hunted is against the biggest bruisers in the league, and even then he has good hands and effort.

Comp: Rob Williams, Moussa Diabate

&nbsp;

Henri Veesaar - 7’0’’ C 22 years old

Stats at UNC: 17ppg, 8.7rpg, 2.1apg, 1.2bpg, on 61/43/62 shooting

Breakdown: Veesaar offers a unique combination of size, mobility, and shooting that every team could use. Offensively Veesaar is a solid transition big who can either run straight to the rim, or flare out for a 3. Veesaar’s mobility and 3 point shooting allows him to play PF, meaning he could play next to Queta, and Veesaar has shown some talent passing to other bigs. He’s a solid vertical threat and does everything you want a modern big man to do. Defensively he’s a solid weakside shot blocker as well, making things difficult for drivers. Veesaar’s biggest issue is his mentality. There will be stretches in a game where Veesaar disappears offensively. He wouldn’t be expected to do much early in Boston, but if he’s not inserting himself into the action and setting screens and rolling, he’s putting a ceiling on himself. He also will have mental gaps on defense or on the board, which I imagine he’d be in Joe’s dog house if he doesn’t iron out. You can’t teach 7 feet though and with a legit 3 point shot and fluid mobility, Veesaar could be a very good player for a long time.

Comp: Kyle Filipowski, Brook Lopez
