Consistent and reliable progression is what makes PoE1 great. PoE2 is too “spiky”, both in balance and in design

Title. The contrast between PoE1 and PoE2 shows how much PoE2 relies too much on high highs and boring, lengthy lows:

Atlas Progression

  • PoE1: Every map gives incremental gains towards your Atlas bonus objective count. It’s not just limited to 115⁄115 maps, as there are favorited map slots, Maven/Eldritch questlines, and optional league challenges to unlock every league.
  • PoE2: Running 15+ maps for 2 Atlas Passive points that are not very impactful. Getting Atlas passives related to content (e.g. Breach) takes WAY too long, as you realistically want the passives to help unlock the passives. This leads to most people just buying the keys (e.g. Breachstones) so the league mechanics don’t feel like a waste of time.
  • Potential Solutions: GGG already announced a change to the corruption nexus chase, which is a step in the right direction. The Arbiter of Ash / citadel questline needs some sort of meta progression tied to it, since I feel inclined to sell my crisis fragments right out the gate. Similar to favorited maps - maybe a flat base increase to the amount of waystones that drop once you beat each pinnacle boss at difficulty 0? I should feel inclined to engage in all the content the game has to offer, even if it’s only for a short stint for progression only.

Juicing Maps

  • PoE1: Players have agency to set up low-to-no investment strategies relatively quickly. Generally, a player can invest any amount of juice into maps with the expectation that they will at least return their investment.
  • PoE2: For consistent loot to drop, players must set up 2-3 overlapping towers with high-value tablets, have sufficient rarity on their gear, high rarity/quant waystones, 4-8 Atlas passives in the content of their choosing, and a favorable map layout (although kudos to GGG for making delirium 2.5x longer so layout is less of an issue). Anything outside of using all of these multipliers results in terrible loot. I ended up taking the ‘100% increased rarity of Waystones’ node on the Atlas tree - using an Orb of Alchemy or Exalted Orbs on your non-juiced maps is consistently a net negative. This leads to lots of boring setup gameplay with small spurts of “I get to finally play the game now”.
  • Potential Solutions: Quant/Rarity should also be tied to suffix mods, especially since waystones are a guaranteed drop now - it doesn’t make sense why difficulty is decoupled from rewards. Player rarity is too mandatory of a stat and shouldn’t exist in the first place. Exalts and Alchemy Orbs should probably drop more consistently. Chase items need to exist - otherwise, there is no carrot at the end of the stick. Also, something something fix the base loot GGG

Crafting

  • PoE1: There are a lot more options for mid-game crafting that are achievable by the player without trade. “Can have up to 3 crafted modifiers” from the crafting bench is a perfect example - I get to “finish” my item, even though it might be relatively inefficient from a cost perspective.
  • PoE2: Anything past 3-4 modifiers is a pipe dream with the current crafting tools we have. Gear quickly becomes near impossible to upgrade without hoping someone who won the slot machine lists an item on trade instead. Either that or you bash your head against the gambling machine until you win big someday.
  • Potential Solutions: Tiered rare drops need to be more plentiful and powerful. Divines need a metacrafting purpose to smooth out mods 4-6. Higher ilvl items should cull some lower tier modifiers (partially what tiered rares do).

Axes of Difficulty / Gameplay

  • PoE1: The Atlas Passive tree allows tailored difficulty to match what your build excels at. Harder content is rewarding enough that you are incentivized to keep iterating on your build, but you also have the option to run the same content faster.
  • PoE2: The limitation of movement in PoE2 makes clear speed difficult to scale to the moon on your character. Once you’re able to one-shot T16-T18 packs / rares, the game gives limited options to challenge your character further. Doesn’t help that many builds are either in a state of “ez pz” or “completely miserable” with little in between. Also, lots of waystone modifiers are just straight bricks (looking at you, Temporal Chains) and don’t provide any worthwhile incentive to run them.
  • Potential Solutions: Once again, Quant/Rarity should also be tied to suffix mods. Build meta should hopefully smooth out over time. All league mechanics need more ways to scale difficulty even higher to reap even better rewards (e.g. making Azmeri Spirited rares have more toughness / modifiers / damage in return for increased base loot / talismans / chance to release a sacred spirit).

PoE2 has such a great template in PoE1 to work off of… I trust GGG to create elegant solutions and iterate on the game - I just hope the changes don’t come too late.
