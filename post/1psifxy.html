Guide to Profit Crafting - How I made 2 mirrors in 5 days.

Before I continue, this post will NOT contain the exact crafts, recipes and steps I used to make the currency I have made. Partially because I want to protect my margins, but also because it detracts from the point of this post. This isn’t about learning an exact craft, but a methodology you can use to find gaps in the market, and how to take advantage when you do. There will be advice in here that you probably have already heard, and there will also be advice which I believe even the most prolific crafters can use.

For context, I am a complete POE noob. I never touched POE1, and was roped into POE2 due to couch co-op sounding like a fun activity for me to play with my girlfriend. In all the previous leagues, I generally made it to around 1 mirror of net worth by endleague - in 0.1 because I bought 3 temporalis at 30d, didn’t play too much 0.2 and made it in 0.3 mainly running sekhemas with a few lucky drops and a bit of crafting on the side. It’s been 9 days at time of writing since league start, and I spent the first 3-4 days making it to maps, figuring out my build and making a bit of currency (maybe had around 5-10d networth to start).

This was the league I decided to really try my hand at profit crafting. Within 5 days, my stash is sitting at 1 mirror and 150 divines, with around 300-400 divines worth of items yet to sell in my shop.

I am not the best crafter, nor the most knowledgable, but I do believe I have one edge over other crafters - which I will explain later on. So here’s what I have learnt profit crafting in this game.

  1. Identifying markets.

Generally, I try to avoid crafting items for the mega popular builds. This league, it’s poison arrow PF and druid talismans. This is because these markets, whereas often very profitable, is also often highly competitive. All the best crafters want to operate in these markets, and I do not believe, as a solo player, I have the liquidity nor the skill to outcompete them. The markets I target tend to be in the 3-10% play rate bracket - enough players to buy my crafts, but not popular enough to attract the attention of the massive crafting groups. That’s not to say that these markets aren’t profitable - it’s just a bit harder to find an edge on the market. This league, these builds are things like shield wall, spark, ice shot, minions/djinn, flicker strike etc. Check on poe ninja what the top players of these builds are running, what setups they use, and what stats they need. Check on trade site what stats seem to scale the price of the item - DPS, flat crit, sockets, ES/EV/Armour, resistances etc.

Spend some time reading about what crafting options there are. Fracturing, desecrations, essences, omens, there are actually a ton of ways to get to a final outcome with complete or high determinism. Often, the new currencies are the least well understood, so there are often massive market edges to be found utilising them - this league, for instance, was the vaal cultivation orb. I was lucky enough to stock up a few before they 20x’d in price.

Your crafts don’t have to be 6xT1 mirror tier, they just have to be cheaper to make than what you can sell them for. For instance, as is already well known, a very profitable craft at league start was crafting bows for poison PF with greater essence of abrasion and a T2 %inc phys magic base, resulting in a decent, but far from perfect bow with T2 %inc phys and T3 flat and often very mediocre suffixes. However, it was good enough for plenty of players to use, and had pretty healthy margins at league start.

Not all your crafts have to start from white bases. Think about whether there are any convenient starting points with specific modifier/rarity/ilvl combinations, and see how expensive they are.

  1. Testing the markets, the MVP.

Once you’ve identified a market, and figured out a way you can make an item players want to buy for cheaper than they’d be willing to pay, the first step is then to test it out. In business, this is called the Minimum Viable Product. Do NOT go all in on a craft before you know that this is actually something people are willing to pay for - this is how you go broke sitting on wands nobody wants for their build. Craft one or two items, and price them accordingly. See how fast they sell, if at all. A craft isn’t profitable until a player has bought your item, so wait until that first sale comes through before making a decision on whether or not it is worth your time.

  1. Pressing your edge.

This is the fun part. Once you’ve found a craft, and are sure it is profitable, now it’s time to press your market edge as much as possible. I also believe this is where even some of the best crafters get things wrong, and where my edge over other crafters lie. I haven’t seen this advice anywhere else, and it has worked extremely well for me while also making the market more efficient, so I see it as a win-win.

The common advice with pricing items is to price it a bit higher than your competitors, then to drop the price every so often until it sells. This is great for your map blasters who think “liquidity” is the name of that esports organisation, but is probably the worst thing for profit crafters to do. Sure, you can spend your 10d crafting a 50d amulet, letting it sit in your shop waiting to sell. You priced it slightly higher than the slight downgrade at 40d, and a bit lower than the slight upgrade at 60d, and in two days time someone will come by looking for exactly a 50d amulet and purchase it. Alternatively, you can price it at 25d, undercut everyone by half selling better items for cheaper, sell it within the hour, use that 25d to craft 5 more amulets and sell them all for 25d each within the next two.

By raising your price, you make more money per sale, but many crafters forget that by lowering your price, you make more sales as more people can afford your item. My general principle isn’t that I should be pricing my items at a “fair” price relative to others - I price my items such that it shouldn’t make sense to even consider buying the other items on the market. Even if this cuts into my per-sale margins, I will sell 10x more than the person who prices “fairly”, and make 5x more profit

Here’s my sale history from 3 days ago, where I had around a 50d networth at the time. All of these were between 2 different crafts. I priced them in a way such that my item was around 20-30% cheaper than the next worse item on the market, and all of them sold within 3 hours of listing. That liquidity allowed me to then craft more, and sell more, growing my net worth exponentially. This crafting session took place over the course of around 4-5 hours, and everything I made sold within a day.

Keep in mind, this is also very ethical. You’re allowing more players to buy good items for cheaper, while also making a bunch of currency yourself (market efficiency). The only people you’re screwing over are the other crafters in the market, who are wondering why their items that used to sell for 80d+ are now only being sold for 30d.

Obviously, this depends on the craft. If the bases for the craft are hard to come by, then you want to maximise your per-sale margins as much as possible. But if the bases are cheap and common, then you want to press your market edge as hard as possible - you do this by flooding the market with better crafts half the price of your competitors. This also depends on your liquidity and short-term cash flow. If you’re not in need of that currency right now, then it’s fine to leave it locked up in your stash for a few extra hours to extract a bit more currency from it. From my experience, this is not the case most the time.

  1. Knowing when to stop.

Gaps in the market don’t last forever. Maybe a youtuber/streamer made a video on it, maybe more people caught onto your crafting method, or maybe the item devalued over time and isn’t as valuable anymore (like rare high res belts). Knowing when the market edge is gone, cut losses and liquidate stock will save you from making items that nobody wants anymore, forcing yourself into miniscule or even negative margins. Over the course of this league, I used 5 different crafts. 3 of them are no longer profitable.

Anyways, that’s probably all the advice I have for people wanting to profit craft. Keep in mind, this doesn’t need to be a full-time job, you can do some profit crafting between maps to make some currency while enjoying the game itself. I did all this while pushing and upgrading my character and having a good time. My character is currently lvl 96, wearing around 200d in gear. I still need a few more upgrades though, as I’m still struggling a bit with scaling difficulty abyss maps + bad mods. Having a great time this league, and all this extra currency definitely helps.

Thanks for taking the time to read all this, and hope you might have learnt something. I’ll edit this post if I remember anything else I wanted to say.
